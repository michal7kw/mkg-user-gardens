<h1>Vitamin B6 (Pyridoxal-5'-Phosphate)</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Vitamin B6 exists in several interconvertible vitamers; the biologically active coenzyme is pyridoxal-5'-phosphate (PLP), which serves as a cofactor for over 150 enzymatic reactions, including amino acid metabolism, neurotransmitter synthesis, and the transsulfuration of homocysteine. The standard laboratory test measures plasma PLP concentration, considered the most informative single indicator of long-term B6 status.</p>
<p>The sample is plasma obtained by venipuncture and analyzed by HPLC or immunoassay; PLP is light-sensitive, so specimens require protection from light and prompt processing. The test is ordered to investigate suspected deficiency (peripheral neuropathy, dermatitis, sideroblastic anemia, seizures in infants), to monitor repletion therapy, and as part of micronutrient and homocysteine work-ups.</p>

<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>Low PLP indicates vitamin B6 deficiency, which may arise from poor intake, malabsorption, alcoholism, chronic kidney disease, or use of certain medications (isoniazid, penicillamine, oral contraceptives). Deficiency is associated with peripheral neuropathy, seborrheic dermatitis, glossitis, microcytic/sideroblastic anemia, and elevated homocysteine. Elevated PLP is uncommon and usually reflects supplementation; chronically very high B6 intake can paradoxically cause sensory neuropathy. The test is also used to monitor adequacy of repletion in patients receiving B6 supplementation.</p>
