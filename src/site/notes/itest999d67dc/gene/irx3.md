<h1>IRX3</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>IRX3 (Iroquois homeobox 3) encodes a homeodomain transcription factor of the Iroquois family that governs cell-fate decisions during development. Although physically distant from the FTO obesity-risk variants, IRX3 is a primary functional target of that locus: the BMI-associated SNPs in the first intron of FTO sit within long-range enhancers that loop to and regulate IRX3 expression rather than acting through FTO itself.</p>
<p>In human adipocyte precursors, the obesity-risk allele increases IRX3 (and IRX5) expression during early adipocyte differentiation, repressing mitochondrial thermogenesis and biasing cells away from energy-dissipating beige/brown-like adipocytes toward energy-storing white adipocytes. This cell-autonomous shift in adipocyte fate provides a mechanistic explanation for how a non-coding regulatory variant translates into measurable differences in body weight.</p>
<h2>Genomic Context</h2>
<p>IRX3 maps to chromosome 16q12.2, near the FTO gene. The functional connection is topological: chromatin looping brings the FTO-intronic enhancers into contact with the IRX3 promoter, making IRX3 a long-range target of regulatory variation at a locus named for a different gene.</p>
<h2>Functional Role</h2>
<p>As an Iroquois-family homeodomain transcription factor, IRX3 controls developmental patterning and, in adipose tissue, the browning-versus-whitening differentiation program of adipocyte precursors. Its activity influences mitochondrial biogenesis and thermogenic gene expression, linking it directly to whole-body energy balance.</p>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>Increased IRX3 expression driven by the FTO obesity-risk allele is associated with higher BMI and downstream cardiometabolic disease through reduced adipocyte thermogenesis. IRX3 is therefore considered a candidate effector gene of the most strongly replicated common-variant obesity locus and a potential therapeutic target for metabolic disease such as type 2 diabetes.</p>
