<h1>Breast Neoplasms</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Breast cancer is the <strong>most common cancer in women worldwide</strong> (~2.3 million new cases annually) and the leading cause of cancer death in women globally. In developed countries, the 5-year survival rate is ~90% due to early detection through mammographic screening and advances in multimodal treatment. The molecular classification of breast cancer has transformed the approach to therapy:</p>
<p><strong>Molecular subtypes:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Luminal A (ER+/PR+, HER2-, low Ki-67):</strong> ~40%; best prognosis; treated with endocrine therapy</li>
<li><strong>Luminal B (ER+/PR+, HER2+/-, high Ki-67):</strong> ~20%; more aggressive; endocrine therapy + chemotherapy + CDK4/6 inhibitors</li>
<li><strong>HER2-enriched (ER-, PR-, HER2+):</strong> ~15%; treated with anti-HER2 therapy (trastuzumab, pertuzumab)</li>
<li><strong>Triple-negative (ER-, PR-, HER2-):</strong> ~15%; most aggressive; treated with chemotherapy + immunotherapy (pembrolizumab); BRCA1-associated</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>BRCA1/BRCA2 testing</strong> is recommended for all patients with: diagnosis &lt;45, triple-negative &lt;60, male breast cancer, ovarian cancer history, Ashkenazi Jewish heritage, or family history suggestive of hereditary breast/ovarian cancer.</p>
<p><strong>Screening guidelines (USPSTF 2016/2019 update):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Women 40-49:</strong> individualized decision-making (biennial mammography)</li>
<li><strong>Women 50-74:</strong> biennial mammography</li>
<li><strong>High-risk (BRCA, prior chest radiation, LCIS):</strong> annual MRI + mammography starting earlier (25-30)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Surgical:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Breast-conserving surgery (lumpectomy)</strong> + radiation (equivalent survival to mastectomy for early-stage)</li>
<li><strong>Mastectomy</strong> (simple, skin-sparing, nipple-sparing; for large tumors, multicentric disease, BRCA carriers, or patient preference)</li>
<li><strong>Sentinel lymph node biopsy</strong> (replaced axillary dissection for staging; much less morbidity)</li>
<li><strong>Reconstruction</strong> (immediate or delayed; implant or autologous: DIEP flap)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Radiation:</strong> whole-breast (after lumpectomy; reduces local recurrence from ~30% to &lt;5%); partial-breast (APBI; selected early-stage); post-mastectomy (for high-risk: &gt;=T3, &gt;=N2, positive margins)</p>
<p><strong>Systemic therapy (stage-dependent):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>ER/PR+ (luminal):</strong> endocrine therapy (tamoxifen for premenopausal; aromatase inhibitors for postmenopausal) x 5-10 years; CDK4/6 inhibitors for metastatic</li>
<li><strong>HER2+:</strong> anti-HER2 therapy (trastuzumab +/- pertuzumab x 1 year); T-DM1 or T-DXd for metastatic</li>
<li><strong>Triple-negative:</strong> chemotherapy (platinum + taxane); immunotherapy (pembrolizumab; KEYNOTE-522 for neoadjuvant)</li>
<li><strong>BRCA-mutated:</strong> PARP inhibitors (olaparib; SOLO-1 for prevention; OlympiAD for metastatic)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Neoadjuvant therapy</strong> (before surgery): increasingly used for large or HER2+ tumors; allows assessment of treatment response (pathologic complete response = excellent prognosis)</p>
