<h1>Diabetic Kidney Disease</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Diabetic kidney disease (DKD, also called diabetic nephropathy) is a <strong>microvascular complication of diabetes</strong> characterized by progressive glomerular damage that culminates in chronic kidney disease and, if untreated, end-stage renal disease (ESRD). DKD is the <strong>single largest cause of ESRD worldwide</strong>, accounting for <strong>~50% of incident dialysis patients</strong> in developed countries. It affects approximately <strong>30-40% of people with type 1 and type 2 diabetes</strong> over their lifetime, although the modern era of renoprotective therapy (renin-angiotensin blockade, SGLT2 inhibitors, finerenone) is reducing progression rates.</p>
<p>The pathophysiology begins with <strong>hyperglycemia-driven glomerular hyperfiltration</strong>, which raises intraglomerular pressure and injures the glomerulus mechanistically and biochemically. Sustained hyperglycemia drives <strong>formation of advanced glycation end products (AGEs)</strong>, activation of <strong>protein kinase C</strong>, and flux through the <strong>polyol pathway</strong>, producing reactive oxygen species and <strong>TGF-beta</strong>-mediated extracellular matrix accumulation. Histologically this manifests as <strong>mesangial expansion, glomerular basement membrane thickening, and the pathognomonic Kimmelstiel-Wilson nodules (nodular glomerulosclerosis)</strong>. Podocyte loss and tubulointerstitial fibrosis follow, with progressive proteinuria and GFR decline.</p>
<p>The clinical course is staged by albuminuria and GFR:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Hyperfiltration</strong> (GFR elevated &gt;120 mL/min/1.73m2) -- earliest functional change</li>
<li><strong>Microalbuminuria</strong> (UACR 30-300 mg/g; &quot;moderately increased albuminuria&quot;) -- still potentially reversible with treatment</li>
<li><strong>Macroalbuminuria</strong> (UACR &gt;300 mg/g; &quot;severely increased albuminuria&quot;; overt nephropathy) -- GFR begins to decline</li>
<li><strong>Declining GFR</strong> -- progression to ESRD at variable rates; average ~10 mL/min/year untreated</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Cardiovascular disease</strong> is the leading cause of death in DKD; the presence of CKD roughly doubles cardiovascular mortality. This is why management must target <strong>both renal and cardiovascular risk</strong> simultaneously -- and the newer therapies (SGLT2 inhibitors, GLP-1 agonists, finerenone) do exactly that.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Foundational (all patients):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Glycemic control</strong> -- HbA1c <strong>&lt;7%</strong> in most (DCCT in T1DM reduced DKD ~50%; UKPDS in T2DM similar); individualize in elderly/frail</li>
<li><strong>Blood pressure</strong> -- <strong>&lt;130/80 mmHg</strong> (SPRINT); <strong>ACE inhibitor or ARB first-line</strong> in all patients with hypertension or albuminuria (RENOPROTECTIVE: reduces proteinuria ~30-50% and slows GFR decline; DO NOT combine ACEi + ARB -- increases AKI and hyperkalemia)</li>
<li><strong>Lipid management</strong> -- <strong>high-intensity statin</strong> (atorvastatin 40-80 mg, rosuvastatin 20-40 mg); target LDL &lt;70 mg/dL</li>
<li><strong>Smoking cessation, weight management, exercise</strong> (150 min/week moderate)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Renoprotective pharmacotherapy (the new core -- kidney AND cardiovascular protection):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>SGLT2 inhibitors</strong> -- <strong>dapagliflozin, empagliflozin</strong>. Reduce hard kidney outcomes (dialysis, doubling creatinine, renal death) by <strong>~30-40%</strong> and cardiovascular events ~20%, independent of glucose lowering. Now recommended in <strong>all T2DM patients with eGFR &gt;=20</strong> and albuminuria (or even without -- DAPA-CKD and EMPA-KIDNEY broadened indications)</li>
<li><strong>Finerenone</strong> (non-steroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist) -- FIDELIO-DKD showed <strong>18% reduction in kidney/CV outcomes</strong> in T2DM CKD on maximal RAS blockade; less hyperkalemia than spironolactone. Approved 2021</li>
<li><strong>GLP-1 receptor agonists</strong> (semaglutide, dulaglutide, liraglutide) -- reduce albuminuria and slow DKD progression (FLOW trial with semaglutide positive 2024); preferred add-on when SGLT2i insufficient or contraindicated</li>
<li><strong>Diet</strong> -- protein <strong>0.8 g/kg/day</strong> (excess accelerates GFR decline); sodium <strong>&lt;2 g/day</strong></li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Manage CKD complications as GFR declines:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Anemia</strong> -- iron studies; ESA (epoetin, darbepoetin) when Hb &lt;10 g/dL (target 10-11.5; avoid &gt;11.5)</li>
<li><strong>CKD-MBD</strong> -- phosphate binders (calcium-based, sevelamer, lanthanum) for hyperphosphatemia; <strong>vitamin D analog</strong> (paricalcitol, calcitriol); calcimimetic (cinacalcet, etelcalcetide) for secondary hyperparathyroidism</li>
<li><strong>Metabolic acidosis</strong> -- oral sodium bicarbonate to maintain bicarbonate &gt;=22</li>
<li><strong>Hyperkalemia</strong> -- dietary potassium restriction; patiromer or sodium zirconium cyclosilicate; adjust ACEi/ARB dose cautiously</li>
<li><strong>Hypertension</strong> -- add calcium channel blocker or diuretic as needed</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Renal replacement therapy planning:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Begin education and access planning when <strong>eGFR approaches 30 mL/min/1.73m2</strong></li>
<li><strong>Arteriovenous fistula</strong> created ~6 months before anticipated dialysis need</li>
<li><strong>Transplant evaluation</strong> for appropriate candidates (best survival and quality of life); pre-emptive transplant (before dialysis) preferred</li>
</ul>
