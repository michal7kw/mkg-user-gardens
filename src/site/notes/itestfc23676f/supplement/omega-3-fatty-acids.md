<h1>Omega-3 Fatty Acids</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Omega-3 fatty acids are a family of long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) defined by a carbon-carbon double bond three positions from the methyl (omega) end of the chain. The two species of greatest clinical relevance are eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA, 20:5n-3) and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA, 22:6n-3), both derived predominantly from marine sources such as cold-water fatty fish (salmon, mackerel, sardines, anchovies) and algae. The plant-derived precursor alpha-linolenic acid (ALA, 18:3n-3) is converted to EPA and DHA only inefficiently in humans (typically &lt;10% to EPA and &lt;1% to DHA), which is why direct marine intake or supplementation is the practical route to meaningful tissue levels.</p>
<p>These fatty acids are considered conditionally essential because the body cannot synthesize them de novo and depends on dietary supply. They are incorporated into cell membrane phospholipids throughout the body, where they govern membrane fluidity, receptor function, and the substrate pool for lipid-signaling mediators. DHA is especially enriched in the central nervous system and retina, comprising a large fraction of the polyunsaturated fatty acids in neuronal and photoreceptor membranes, while EPA is the dominant precursor for anti-inflammatory eicosanoids and specialized pro-resolving mediators.</p>
<p>Clinically, omega-3 fatty acids are among the most extensively studied supplements, with the strongest, most reproducible evidence in lipid management — particularly triglyceride lowering — and in cardiovascular risk reduction at pharmacological doses. They are also studied for inflammation, mood disorders, cognitive aging, joint health, and ocular surface disease, with evidence ranging from strong to preliminary depending on the indication.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level:</strong> 5/5 (triglyceride lowering and Omega-3 Index); 4/5 (inflammation, cardiovascular outcomes at high dose); 3/5 (mood and cognition).</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>Omega-3 fatty acids exert effects through several complementary mechanisms. First, EPA and DHA are incorporated into membrane phospholipids, displacing arachidonic acid (an omega-6 PUFA) and thereby shifting eicosanoid production toward less inflammatory series-3 prostaglandins and series-5 leukotrienes. Second, EPA and DHA serve as substrates for specialized pro-resolving mediators — resolvins, protectins, and maresins — which actively terminate inflammation and promote tissue repair rather than merely blunting its initiation.</p>
<p>In lipid metabolism, omega-3 fatty acids reduce hepatic synthesis and secretion of very-low-density lipoprotein (VLDL), in part by suppressing the transcription factor SREBP-1c and by enhancing fatty-acid beta-oxidation, which lowers the triglyceride substrate available for VLDL assembly. This is the basis for the robust, dose-dependent triglyceride reduction observed at 2-4 g/day of combined EPA+DHA. Omega-3 fatty acids also activate the G-protein-coupled receptor GPR120 (FFAR4), which mediates potent anti-inflammatory and insulin-sensitizing signaling in macrophages and adipocytes.</p>
<p>In the nervous system, DHA supports neuronal membrane fluidity, synaptic plasticity, and brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) expression, while EPA appears to be the principal driver of antidepressant effects, likely through reductions in neuroinflammation. Both fatty acids modulate serotonergic and dopaminergic neurotransmission and improve endothelial function, contributing to modest blood-pressure reductions at higher doses.</p>
<h2>Research Summary</h2>
<p>The triglyceride-lowering effect is the best-established benefit, with reductions of roughly 25-30% at doses of 4 g/day, underpinning FDA-approved indications for severe hypertriglyceridemia and the American Heart Association science advisory on omega-3 for hypertriglyceridemia (PMID 31422671). For hard cardiovascular outcomes, the REDUCE-IT trial demonstrated a 25% relative reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events with 4 g/day of icosapent ethyl (purified EPA) in high-risk statin-treated patients (PMID 30415628). Pooled meta-analyses of mixed EPA/DHA supplementation show smaller, dose-dependent reductions in cardiac mortality and coronary events, with benefit concentrated at higher doses (PMID 34505026), while broad Cochrane syntheses report only modest effects on overall cardiovascular events for low-to-moderate supplemental doses (PMID 32114706).</p>
<p>Anti-inflammatory effects are supported by reductions in hs-CRP, IL-6, and TNF-alpha, generally most pronounced in populations with elevated baseline inflammation or metabolic syndrome. In psychiatry, meta-analyses indicate that EPA-predominant formulations (EPA ≥60% of total) produce small-to-moderate improvements in depressive symptoms (PMID 31383846), with additional evidence for prevention of depression and anxiety symptoms (PMID 31647041). Evidence for cognitive decline prevention and dry-eye disease is more mixed and indication-dependent.</p>
<h2>Administration Guide</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>General health / maintenance:</strong> 250-500 mg combined EPA+DHA daily, achievable through 2 servings of fatty fish per week or a standard fish-oil capsule.</li>
<li><strong>Triglyceride reduction:</strong> 2-4 g/day combined EPA+DHA (prescription-grade or high-concentration products), under clinical supervision.</li>
<li><strong>Mood support:</strong> 1-2 g/day with an EPA-predominant ratio (EPA:DHA roughly 2:1 or higher).</li>
<li><strong>Timing &amp; form:</strong> Take with a fat-containing meal to maximize absorption. Triglyceride and phospholipid (krill) forms are better absorbed than ethyl-ester forms, though ethyl esters used in prescription products are effective at therapeutic doses. Enteric-coated capsules and freezing capsules can reduce fishy reflux.</li>
<li><strong>Omega-3 Index target:</strong> Aim for an erythrocyte Omega-3 Index above 8% for cardiovascular protection; levels typically rise 4-8 percentage points over 4-8 weeks of consistent supplementation.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Safety Information</h2>
<p>Omega-3 fatty acids are generally well tolerated. The most common adverse effects are a fishy aftertaste, eructation, and gastrointestinal upset (nausea, bloating, loose stools), all of which are dose-related. High doses (&gt;3 g/day) can modestly prolong bleeding time, so caution is warranted in patients on anticoagulants or antiplatelet agents and in the perioperative period (high-dose supplementation is often discontinued ~2 weeks before surgery). Some randomized data have associated high-dose omega-3 with a small increased incidence of atrial fibrillation, particularly in susceptible individuals, which should be weighed against benefits. Fish-oil products are contraindicated in those with fish allergy (algal DHA/EPA is an alternative).</p>
<h2>Quality Considerations</h2>
<p>Product quality varies considerably. Prefer products that are third-party tested (e.g., IFOS, USP, NSF) for oxidation (low peroxide and anisidine values), heavy metals, PCBs, and dioxins. Concentration matters more than total capsule weight — check the per-serving EPA+DHA content rather than total fish oil. Triglyceride-form and re-esterified triglyceride products generally offer higher bioavailability than ethyl-ester forms. Algal oil is a sustainable, fish-free source suitable for vegetarians and those with fish allergy, providing DHA (and increasingly EPA).</p>
