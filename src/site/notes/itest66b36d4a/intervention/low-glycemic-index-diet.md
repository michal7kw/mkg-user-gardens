<h1>Low Glycemic Index Diet</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Dietary pattern emphasizing foods with a glycemic index below 55, promoting stable blood sugar and improved insulin sensitivity.<br />
<strong>Type</strong>: diet</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>Low-GI foods produce a slower, more gradual rise in blood glucose, reducing insulin demand, promoting satiety, and lowering postprandial lipemia. Fiber and resistant starch slow gastric emptying.</p>
<h2>Protocol</h2>
<p>Choose carbohydrate foods with a glycemic index below ~55 and, more importantly, a low glycemic <em>load</em>:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Swap</strong> high-GI staples first: white bread → wholegrain/sourdough; white rice → basmati, parboiled, or legumes; white potato → sweet potato, legumes, or intact grains (barley, oats, quinoa).</li>
<li><strong>Pair</strong> carbohydrate with protein, healthy fat, and fibre to blunt the post-meal glucose rise.</li>
<li><strong>Emphasise</strong> non-starchy vegetables, legumes, nuts, intact whole grains, and low-GI fruit (berries, apple, pear, citrus).</li>
<li><strong>Limit</strong> refined sugar, sugary drinks, and highly processed starches.</li>
<li><strong>Macro guide:</strong> ~45–50% carbohydrate (low-GI sources), ~30% fat, ~20–25% protein.</li>
<li><strong>Transition</strong> over 1–2 weeks by replacing the highest-GI staples first.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Safety Information</h2>
<h3>Contraindications</h3>
<ul>
<li>may not suit high-performance athletes needing rapid glycogen replenishment</li>
<li>individuals with gastroparesis (high fiber may worsen symptoms)</li>
</ul>

<h2>Research Support</h2>
<p>Low-GI and low-glycemic-load dietary patterns improve glycemic control (fasting glucose, HbA1c) and fasting triglycerides in randomized trials and meta-analyses, with the largest benefit in people with type 2 diabetes or insulin resistance.</p>
