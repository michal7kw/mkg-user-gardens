<h1>Sun Exposure</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Sensible <strong>sun exposure</strong> is the primary <em>natural</em> route to raising vitamin D status: UVB light converts 7-dehydrocholesterol in the skin to vitamin D3, which is then hydroxylated to the measured <strong>25-hydroxyvitamin D</strong>. Short, regular, non-burning exposures (duration depends on skin type, latitude, season, and UV index) meaningfully raise 25-OH vitamin D, complementing oral supplementation. In Poland's latitude, effective cutaneous synthesis is limited in the autumn-winter months, so sun exposure is a warm-season lever paired with vitamin D3 supplementation year-round.</p>
<p>Beyond vitamin D, morning light exposure entrains the circadian clock (supporting a healthy cortisol rhythm and sleep) and supports mood. Balanced against skin-cancer risk, the recommendation is <em>sensible</em> exposure — enough for synthesis, short of burning.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>UVB (290-315 nm) converts cutaneous 7-dehydrocholesterol to previtamin D3, which isomerizes to vitamin D3 and is hydroxylated in the liver to 25-OH vitamin D. Synthesis depends on latitude, season, time of day, skin pigmentation, age, and sunscreen. Morning light also entrains the suprachiasmatic circadian pacemaker via melanopsin retinal ganglion cells and raises serotonergic tone.</p>
<h2>Protocol</h2>
<h3>Administration</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Timing</strong>: midday sun for maximal UVB / vitamin D; morning light for circadian entrainment.</li>
<li><strong>Frequency</strong>: short, non-burning exposures several times per week (minutes to tens of minutes depending on skin type and UV index).</li>
<li><strong>Duration</strong>: stop well before any skin reddening.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Safety Information</h2>
<h3>Contraindications</h3>
<ul>
<li>History of skin cancer or photosensitivity disorder</li>
<li>Photosensitizing medications</li>
<li>Never allow burning; fair skin and high UV index shorten the safe window</li>
</ul>
