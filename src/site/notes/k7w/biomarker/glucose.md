<h1>Glucose</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Blood glucose is the body's primary circulating energy substrate, supplying fuel to the brain, muscle, and other tissues. Its concentration is held within a narrow range by the opposing actions of insulin (lowering glucose) and glucagon, cortisol, and catecholamines (raising it). Because chronic dysregulation precedes overt disease, glucose is one of the most widely used screening biomarkers for metabolic health.</p>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Elevated (hyperglycemia).</strong> Sustained high glucose indicates impaired insulin action or secretion and is the basis for diagnosing prediabetes and diabetes. Diagnostic thresholds (fasting): normal &lt; 100 mg/dL; prediabetes 100–125 mg/dL; diabetes ≥ 126 mg/dL on repeat testing. Chronic hyperglycemia damages the vasculature, nerves, kidneys, and retina.</li>
<li><strong>Low (hypoglycemia).</strong> Glucose below ~70 mg/dL can cause tremor, sweating, confusion, and, if severe, loss of consciousness. Causes include excess insulin or sulfonylureas, insulinoma, adrenal insufficiency, and advanced liver disease.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Measurement</h2>
<p>Measured in blood (mg/dL or mmol/L; 1 mmol/L ≈ 18 mg/dL). A random sample is acceptable for screening, but a <strong>fasting</strong> sample (8+ hours) is preferred for diagnostic comparison. For longer-term control, glucose is complemented by <a href="/garden/k7w/labtest/hba1c/" class="internal-link">HbA1c</a>, which reflects average glycemia over ~3 months.</p>
