<h1>GDF15</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Growth differentiation factor 15 (GDF15, also called MIC-1) is a distant, divergent member of the TGF-beta superfamily that behaves as a cell-stress cytokine. Unlike most metabolic hormones, GDF15 has a single known central receptor — GFRAL, restricted to the hindbrain (area postrema and nucleus tractus solitarius) — through which it suppresses appetite and conveys a signal of &quot;somatic distress&quot; to the brain. Its expression is induced by mitochondrial dysfunction, the integrated stress response, inflammation, tissue injury, and metabolic stress.</p>
<p>Clinically, GDF15 is notable on two fronts: it is among the <strong>strongest single-protein predictors of all-cause mortality and biological aging</strong>, and it is the mediator of nausea/appetite-loss in several contexts (pregnancy, mitochondrial disease, and metformin therapy). This dual identity — robust aging/stress biomarker and actionable appetite axis — makes it a focus of both longevity and anti-obesity research.</p>

<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>Elevated GDF15 marks <a href="/garden/k7w/pathway/mitochondrial-dynamics/" class="internal-link">Mitochondrial Dynamics</a> dysfunction and the integrated stress response, and independently predicts cardiovascular events, <a href="/garden/k7w/condition/type-2-diabetes/" class="internal-link">Type 2 Diabetes</a>, heart failure, frailty, and all-cause mortality. It is elevated in <a href="/garden/k7w/condition/insulin-resistance/" class="internal-link">Insulin Resistance</a> and <a href="/garden/k7w/condition/metabolic-syndrome/" class="internal-link">Metabolic Syndrome</a>. As a biomarker of biological aging it complements <a href="/garden/k7w/biomarker/klotho/" class="internal-link">Klotho</a> (which falls with age while GDF15 rises). Pharmacological modulation of the GDF15-GFRAL axis is under active investigation for obesity and cachexia.</p>
