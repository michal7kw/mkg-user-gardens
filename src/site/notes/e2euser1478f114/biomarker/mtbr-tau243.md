<h1>MTBR-tau243</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>MTBR-tau243 (microtubule-binding region tau peptide 243) is a 24-residue peptide fragment derived from the microtubule-binding repeat domain (MTBR) of the tau protein. It was identified by Barthelemy and colleagues at Washington University (St. Louis) using a targeted mass-spectrometry workflow (immunoaffinity enrichment followed by LC-MS/MS) and first reported in <em>Molecular Neurodegeneration</em> in 2020. The fragment corresponds to amino acids 254-277 of full-length tau (0N4R isoform numbering), spanning the second microtubule-binding repeat -- a region that forms the core of paired helical filaments in Alzheimer's neurofibrillary tangles.</p>
<p>Mechanistically, MTBR-tau243 is hypothesized to be a proteolytic product of tangle-resident tau, released into CSF as tangles turn over. Unlike total tau (which reflects any axonal injury) and p-tau (which is largely amyloid-driven and may plateau later in disease), MTBR-tau243 appears to track the actual tangle burden in the brain. Initial cross-sectional and longitudinal studies suggest it correlates more tightly with tau-PET signal than other CSF tau biomarkers and continues to rise throughout the symptomatic phase, making it a candidate marker for staging and treatment-response monitoring in anti-tangle therapies.</p>
<p>The biomarker is currently used in research settings only; it has not been incorporated into clinical guidelines (NIA-AA AT(N) framework still uses t-tau and p-tau181/217) and is not FDA-cleared. Plasma assays emerged in 2024 and are in active development. The principal investigator group is the Bateman lab at Washington University, with collaborations across the DIAN and ADNI cohorts.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level: 3/5</strong> (strong mechanistic foundation; multiple replication studies; not yet in clinical practice).</p>

<h2>Factors Affecting Levels</h2>
<p><strong>Increase MTBR-tau243:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Alzheimer's disease across the entire clinicopathological continuum</li>
<li>Other tauopathies (corticobasal degeneration, progressive supranuclear palsy, frontotemporal dementia with tau pathology -- less well-characterized)</li>
<li>Chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE) -- emerging research application</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Decrease MTBR-tau243:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Successful anti-tangle or anti-amyloid therapy (hypothesis under investigation)</li>
<li>Slowing of neurofibrillary degeneration</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p><strong>Elevated levels are associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Alzheimer's disease:</strong> CSF MTBR-tau243 correlates more tightly with tau-PET SUVR than CSF p-tau217 or p-tau181 (Horie et al., <em>Nat Med</em> 2022). Continues to rise through symptomatic stages -- a property p-tau markers may lose in advanced disease</li>
<li><strong>Tau tangle burden:</strong> directly reflects tangle pathology rather than amyloid or nonspecific neuronal injury</li>
<li><strong>Other tauopathies:</strong> less well-characterized; ongoing studies in PSP, CBD, CTE</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Limitations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Not clinically available</strong> outside research contexts (no FDA-cleared commercial assay as of 2025)</li>
<li>IP-LC-MS/MS is technically demanding; inter-laboratory standardization incomplete</li>
<li>Plasma assay only emerging; clinical cutoffs years away</li>
<li>Limited longitudinal data compared to t-tau or p-tau181</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Monitoring use cases:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Research: staging in observational AD cohorts (ADNI, DIAN, A4)</li>
<li>Research: treatment-response biomarker for anti-tangle therapies (e.g., semorinemab, tilavonemab)</li>
<li>Emerging clinical use expected 2026+ as commercial assays and clinical cutoffs mature</li>
</ul>
<h2>Strategies to Manage</h2>
<p>MTBR-tau243 is a tissue-state biomarker and is not directly modifiable. Management targets the underlying tauopathy:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Alzheimer's disease:</strong> integrate into the AT(N) framework; consider anti-amyloid therapy (lecanemab, donanemab) early in disease</li>
<li><strong>Anti-tangle therapy trials:</strong> enrollment and response monitoring (research)</li>
<li><strong>Lifestyle observational signals:</strong> physical exercise, Mediterranean/MIND diet, cognitive engagement, and cardiovascular risk factor control are epidemiologically associated with slower tau accumulation on PET; presumed to also influence CSF MTBR-tau243 (unconfirmed)</li>
</ol>
