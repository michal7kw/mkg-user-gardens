<h1>Pterostilbene</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Pterostilbene is a naturally occurring stilbenoid — a close structural cousin of <a href="/garden/itestc7363ec9/supplement/resveratrol/" class="internal-link">Resveratrol</a> — concentrated in blueberries and grapes. The key practical difference is pharmacokinetic: replacing two of resveratrol's hydroxyl groups with methoxy groups makes pterostilbene far more lipophilic and metabolically stable, giving it substantially higher oral bioavailability and a longer half-life. This addresses resveratrol's principal weakness (poor absorption and rapid conjugation) while preserving the longevity-relevant signaling.</p>
<p>It is studied as a caloric-restriction mimetic and neuroprotective agent, often combined with NAD+ precursors because, like resveratrol, its sirtuin effects depend on adequate NAD+.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>Pterostilbene activates the <a href="/garden/itestc7363ec9/pathway/sirtuin-signaling-pathway/" class="internal-link">Sirtuin Signaling Pathway</a> (SIRT1) and engages the <a href="/garden/itestc7363ec9/pathway/ampk-pathway/" class="internal-link">AMPK Pathway</a>, promoting mitochondrial biogenesis and the metabolic adaptations associated with caloric restriction. It is a strong inducer of the <a href="/garden/itestc7363ec9/pathway/nrf2-pathway/" class="internal-link">Nrf2 Pathway</a>, upregulating endogenous antioxidant defenses, and it suppresses NF-kB-driven inflammation. In preclinical cognitive-aging models it increases <a href="/garden/itestc7363ec9/biomarker/bdnf/" class="internal-link">BDNF</a> and protects against oxidative and neuroinflammatory damage. Because its sirtuin activity requires NAD+ as cofactor, it is mechanistically synergistic with NAD+-boosting strategies (see <a href="/garden/itestc7363ec9/metabolite/nad/" class="internal-link">NAD+</a>, <a href="/garden/itestc7363ec9/supplement/nmn/" class="internal-link">NMN</a>).</p>
<h2>Dosage</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Typical dose:</strong> 50-250 mg/day; many human studies use ~50-125 mg/day.</li>
<li>Higher doses (250 mg/day) have been associated with modest LDL cholesterol increases in some trials — monitor lipids with prolonged high-dose use.</li>
<li>Often co-supplemented with resveratrol or NAD+ precursors.</li>
</ul>
