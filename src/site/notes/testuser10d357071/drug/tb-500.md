<h1>TB-500</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>TB-500 is a synthetic peptide based on a fragment of <strong>Thymosin Beta-4</strong>, a naturally occurring actin-regulating peptide. Like <a href="/garden/testuser10d357071/drug/bpc-157/" class="internal-link">BPC-157</a>, it is sold as a &quot;research peptide&quot; and promoted for tissue repair, tendon and muscle recovery, and general regeneration. It is <strong>not approved for human use</strong> by the FDA, EMA, MHRA or Poland's URPL, and is a <strong>WADA-prohibited substance</strong> in competitive sport.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>Thymosin Beta-4 is a small peptide that sequesters actin monomers and regulates actin polymerisation — a process central to cell migration and wound closure. In animal models, TB-500 is reported to promote <strong>cell migration, angiogenesis and extracellular-matrix remodelling</strong>, supporting the repair of skin, tendon, muscle and even cardiac tissue. As with BPC-157, these effects come from preclinical work and are not validated in controlled human studies.</p>
<h2>Clinical Evidence</h2>
<p>There are <strong>no large-scale human randomised controlled trials</strong> of TB-500. The literature consists of animal experiments and reviews that classify it as investigational. Its <code>evidence_level</code> is therefore <strong>1</strong> — present for education and mechanism, and intentionally not eligible as a health recommendation.</p>
<h2>Safety</h2>
<p>The dominant risks mirror other injectable gray-market peptides: injection-site reactions, and contamination/infection from non-sterile self-reconstituted product. Long-term human safety is unknown, and the pro-migratory, pro-angiogenic mechanism raises the same theoretical oncological concern noted for BPC-157. It is banned in sport and unauthorised as a medicine in Poland.</p>
