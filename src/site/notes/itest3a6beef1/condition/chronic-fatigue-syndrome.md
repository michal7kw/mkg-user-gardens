<h1>Chronic Fatigue Syndrome</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Complex, multi-system illness characterized by profound and persistent fatigue not relieved by rest, post-exertional malaise (PEM), unrefreshing sleep, cognitive dysfunction (&quot;brain fog&quot;), and orthostatic intolerance. Not explained by another medical condition. Also known as Myalgic Encephalomyelitis (ME/CFS). Diagnosis is clinical; pathophysiology is incompletely understood but likely involves immune dysregulation, mitochondrial dysfunction, autonomic nervous system abnormalities, and HPA-axis disturbance.</p>
<h2>Diagnostic Criteria</h2>
<p>The 2015 Institute of Medicine (NAM) criteria require:</p>
<ol>
<li>Substantial reduction in pre-illness activity, lasting &gt;6 months, accompanied by profound fatigue</li>
<li>Post-exertional malaise (PEM)</li>
<li>Unrefreshing sleep</li>
<li><strong>Plus</strong> at least one of: cognitive impairment OR orthostatic intolerance</li>
</ol>
<h2>Treatment Approaches</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Pacing</strong> (activity management to stay within the energy envelope, avoiding PEM)</li>
<li>Treatment of comorbidities (orthostatic intolerance, sleep disorders, IBS, pain)</li>
<li>Low-dose naltrexone (off-label for immune modulation)</li>
<li>Cognitive behavioral therapy for coping (controversial as primary treatment after PACE trial reanalysis)</li>
<li>Mitochondrial support: CoQ10, NAD+ precursors, B-vitamins, magnesium</li>
<li>B12 supplementation if deficient (frequent comorbid finding)</li>
</ul>
