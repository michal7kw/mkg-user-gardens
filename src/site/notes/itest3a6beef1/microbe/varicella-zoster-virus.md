<h1>Varicella Zoster Virus</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Varicella zoster virus (VZV), also known as human herpesvirus 3 (HHV-3), is an enveloped double-stranded DNA alphaherpesvirus. Primary infection causes varicella (chickenpox), a generalized vesicular rash. The virus then establishes lifelong latency in dorsal root and cranial nerve ganglia. Reactivation, typically with waning cell-mediated immunity in older adults, produces herpes zoster (shingles) — a painful dermatomal rash — and can lead to chronic post-herpetic neuralgia.</p>

<h2>Ecology &amp; Transmission</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Body Sites:</strong> skin (vesicular lesions), nervous system (latency in sensory ganglia)</li>
<li><strong>Transmission:</strong> respiratory droplets and direct contact with vesicle fluid; highly contagious during varicella</li>
<li><strong>Genome:</strong> ~125 kb linear dsDNA</li>
</ul>
<h2>Pathogenesis &amp; Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>VZV is neurotropic: after cutaneous and viremic spread during primary infection, it travels retrograde to sensory ganglia and persists latently. Declining VZV-specific cell-mediated immunity (aging, immunosuppression) permits reactivation along a single dermatome, producing herpes zoster. Vaccination — childhood varicella vaccine and the adjuvanted recombinant zoster vaccine (Shingrix) in older adults — substantially reduces disease and its complications.</p>
