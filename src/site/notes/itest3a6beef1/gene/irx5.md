<h1>IRX5</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>IRX5 (Iroquois homeobox 5) encodes an Iroquois-family homeodomain transcription factor that, alongside its paralog IRX3, mediates the metabolic effects of the FTO obesity-risk locus. The BMI-associated common variants within FTO act through long-range enhancers that co-regulate IRX3 and IRX5, rather than altering FTO function directly.</p>
<p>In adipocyte precursor cells, the obesity-risk allele increases IRX5 expression during early differentiation, suppressing mitochondrial thermogenic programs and shifting cell fate from energy-dissipating beige adipocytes toward energy-storing white adipocytes. This provides a cell-autonomous mechanism connecting a non-coding regulatory variant to increased adiposity and body weight.</p>
<h2>Genomic Context</h2>
<p>IRX5 lies on chromosome 16q12.2 in the same regulatory neighborhood as IRX3 and FTO. Chromatin looping links the FTO-intronic enhancers to the IRX5 locus, so IRX5 is co-targeted with IRX3 by the same obesity-associated regulatory variation.</p>
<h2>Functional Role</h2>
<p>IRX5 functions as a developmental transcription factor controlling patterning and, in adipose tissue, the thermogenic differentiation program. Its co-regulation with IRX3 modulates mitochondrial biogenesis and uncoupling, contributing to the balance between white and beige adipocyte fates and thereby to whole-body energy expenditure.</p>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>Elevated IRX5 expression driven by the FTO obesity-risk allele is associated with higher BMI and cardiometabolic disease via reduced adipocyte thermogenesis. IRX5 is regarded as a co-effector of the FTO locus and, with IRX3, a candidate target for interventions that promote adipocyte browning.</p>
