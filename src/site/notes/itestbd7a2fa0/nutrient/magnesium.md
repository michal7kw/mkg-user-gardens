<h1>Magnesium</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Magnesium is an essential mineral that serves as a cofactor for over 300 enzymatic reactions in the body, including those involved in ATP production, DNA and protein synthesis, muscle contraction, nerve transmission, and blood glucose regulation. It is the fourth most abundant mineral in the body, with approximately 60% stored in bone, 39% in soft tissues, and only 1% in blood serum.</p>
<p>Subclinical magnesium deficiency is remarkably common, estimated to affect 50-80% of Americans, driven by depleted soil minerals, processed food consumption, and increased urinary losses from stress, caffeine, and alcohol. Serum magnesium, the most commonly ordered test, is a poor indicator of total body magnesium status because it represents less than 1% of total stores. Red blood cell (RBC) magnesium provides a more reliable assessment.</p>
<p>Magnesium plays a critical role in cardiovascular health by regulating vascular tone, preventing arterial calcification, and maintaining normal cardiac rhythm. It is also essential for insulin signaling and glucose homeostasis, with strong epidemiological evidence linking higher magnesium intake to reduced risk of type 2 diabetes. Different supplemental forms have varying bioavailability and therapeutic applications: glycinate for sleep and anxiety, threonate for cognition, taurate for cardiovascular support, and citrate for constipation.</p>
<h2>Key Functions</h2>
<ul>
<li>Serves as a cofactor for 300+ enzymes including those producing ATP</li>
<li>Regulates muscle contraction and relaxation (natural calcium antagonist)</li>
<li>Maintains normal cardiac rhythm and vascular smooth muscle tone</li>
<li>Supports insulin signaling and glucose metabolism</li>
<li>Enables DNA, RNA, and protein synthesis</li>
<li>Modulates NMDA glutamate receptors, influencing mood and sleep</li>
</ul>

<li>Nuts (almonds, cashews, Brazil nuts)</li>

<li>Avocado</li>

<li>Whole grains (brown rice, oats)</li>
<li>Bananas</li>
</ul>
<h2>Deficiency and Excess</h2>
<p>Magnesium deficiency manifests as muscle cramps and spasms, insomnia, anxiety, heart palpitations, fatigue, headaches, and constipation. Severe deficiency can cause tetany, seizures, and cardiac arrhythmias. Chronic low magnesium is associated with hypertension, type 2 diabetes, osteoporosis, and migraine headaches. Medications such as proton pump inhibitors and diuretics increase the risk of deficiency.</p>
<p>Toxicity from dietary magnesium is virtually impossible in individuals with normal kidney function, as the kidneys efficiently excrete excess. Supplemental magnesium in excess can cause osmotic diarrhea (especially oxide and citrate forms), nausea, and abdominal cramping. In rare cases of extreme supplementation with impaired renal function, hypermagnesemia can cause hypotension, respiratory depression, and cardiac arrest.</p>
