<h1>Vitamin D Deficiency</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Vitamin D deficiency is a widespread condition defined by low serum <strong>25-hydroxyvitamin D</strong> (25-OH-D), the standard marker of vitamin D status. It impairs intestinal calcium absorption and bone mineralization and is associated with musculoskeletal weakness, increased fracture and fall risk, and—observationally—immune and metabolic effects. Deficiency is commonly defined as 25-OH-D &lt; 20 ng/mL (50 nmol/L) and insufficiency as 20–30 ng/mL, though thresholds vary by guideline.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Cholecalciferol (D3)</strong> — maintenance 800–2000 IU/day for most adults; higher loading doses for documented deficiency, then maintenance</li>
<li><strong>Recheck 25-OH-D</strong> after ~3 months of supplementation to confirm repletion</li>
<li><strong>Co-factors</strong> — adequate magnesium (required for vitamin D activation) and calcium intake; vitamin K2 often paired</li>
<li><strong>Address causes</strong> — malabsorption, obesity, limited sun exposure</li>
</ul>
