<h1>Cystatin C</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Cystatin C is a small protein produced at a constant rate by all nucleated cells and freely filtered by the renal glomerulus, then reabsorbed and catabolized by the tubules. Serum cystatin C rises as glomerular filtration declines, making it a sensitive marker of kidney function. Unlike creatinine, it is largely independent of muscle mass, age, sex, and diet, so it improves eGFR estimation—particularly in the elderly, in patients with low or high muscle mass, and where early detection of impairment matters. It is frequently combined with creatinine in modern eGFR equations.</p>
