<h1>Potassium</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Potassium is the principal intracellular cation, with approximately 98% of the body's potassium residing inside cells. It is essential for maintaining the electrochemical gradient across cell membranes via the Na+/K+-ATPase pump, which exchanges three sodium ions out of the cell for two potassium ions in. This gradient is fundamental to nerve impulse transmission, muscle contraction, cardiac rhythm, and cellular fluid balance.</p>
<p>Potassium plays a critical role in blood pressure regulation by promoting renal sodium excretion (natriuresis) and relaxing vascular smooth muscle. The DASH diet's blood pressure-lowering effect is largely attributable to its high potassium content (4,700 mg/day). Epidemiological studies consistently show that higher potassium intake is associated with reduced risk of stroke, hypertension, kidney stones, and osteoporosis.</p>
<p>Despite the adequate intake recommendation of 2,600-3,400 mg/day, most adults consume only about 50% of this amount, making potassium one of the most under-consumed nutrients. Processing and cooking leach potassium from foods, and modern diets high in processed foods and low in fruits and vegetables exacerbate the shortfall. Potassium citrate is well-absorbed as a supplement and also alkalinizes urine, helping prevent calcium oxalate kidney stones.</p>
<h2>Key Functions</h2>
<ul>
<li>Maintains resting membrane potential via the Na+/K+-ATPase pump</li>
<li>Enables nerve impulse conduction and action potential propagation</li>
<li>Facilitates skeletal and smooth muscle contraction and relaxation</li>
<li>Regulates cardiac rhythm and contractility</li>
<li>Promotes renal sodium excretion, lowering blood pressure</li>
<li>Supports acid-base balance and cellular fluid homeostasis</li>
</ul>

<h2>Deficiency and Excess</h2>
<p>Hypokalemia (serum potassium &lt;3.5 mEq/L) causes muscle weakness, cramps, fatigue, constipation, and in severe cases, life-threatening cardiac arrhythmias (including torsades de pointes) and paralytic ileus. Chronic low potassium intake is associated with hypertension, increased stroke risk, kidney stones, and bone demineralization. Diuretics, chronic diarrhea, and excessive sweating are common causes of potassium depletion.</p>
<p>Hyperkalemia (serum potassium &gt;5.0 mEq/L) is a medical emergency that can cause fatal cardiac arrhythmias (peaked T-waves, widened QRS, ventricular fibrillation). It occurs primarily in individuals with impaired renal function, those taking potassium-sparing diuretics or ACE inhibitors, or with excessive supplementation. Patients with chronic kidney disease must carefully limit dietary potassium intake.</p>
