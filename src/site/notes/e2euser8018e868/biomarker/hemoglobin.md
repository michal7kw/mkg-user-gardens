<h1>Hemoglobin</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Hemoglobin (Hb) is the iron-containing oxygen-transport metalloprotein in red blood cells. It is a tetramer composed of <strong>four globin polypeptide chains</strong> (in adults: 2 alpha + 2 beta chains in HbA, which is ~97% of adult hemoglobin), each bound to a <strong>heme group</strong> containing a central iron atom (Fe2+) that reversibly binds one O2 molecule. Each hemoglobin molecule can therefore carry up to four oxygen molecules, making it essential for tissue oxygenation. Hemoglobin concentration in whole blood is the most widely used screening test for anemia and polycythemia worldwide -- it is part of every complete blood count (CBC) and is the WHO-defined marker for anemia diagnosis.</p>
<p><strong>Low hemoglobin (anemia)</strong> affects an estimated 1.8 billion people globally (~25% of the world's population), with <strong>iron deficiency the most common cause</strong> (~50% of cases). Symptoms include fatigue, weakness, pallor, dyspnea on exertion, reduced exercise capacity, headache, dizziness, and cold extremities. Anemia is associated with worse outcomes in chronic disease (CKD, heart failure, cancer) and increased perioperative morbidity. The WHO anemia threshold is Hb &lt;13.0 g/dL in men, &lt;12.0 g/dL in non-pregnant women, and &lt;11.0 g/dL in pregnant women.</p>
<p><strong>High hemoglobin (polycythemia)</strong> increases blood viscosity and the risk of thrombosis. Causes include polycythemia vera (a myeloproliferative neoplasm with JAK2 mutation), chronic hypoxia (COPD, sleep apnea, high altitude, cyanotic congenital heart disease), dehydration (relative increase), testosterone therapy, erythropoietin-secreting tumors, and smoking. Symptoms include headache, dizziness, hypertension, and thrombotic events.</p>
<p>Hemoglobin is measured as part of a complete blood count (CBC) and reflects the total oxygen-carrying capacity of blood. It is distinct from hematocrit (Hct; the proportion of blood volume occupied by RBCs), though the two are closely correlated (Hb ≈ Hct / 3).</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level: 5/5</strong> (universally measured; WHO essential laboratory test; foundational diagnostic biomarker).</p>

<p><strong>Caveats:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Values vary by altitude (higher at elevation due to physiologic compensation)</li>
<li>Smokers have higher Hb (carboxyhemoglobin + chronic hypoxia)</li>
<li>African American populations have Hb 0.5-0.7 g/dL lower than European-descent populations (genetic)</li>
<li>Hydration status affects Hb (dehydration falsely elevates; over-hydration falsely lowers)</li>
<li>Pregnancy: Hb falls 1-2 g/dL due to hemodilution</li>
<li>Athletic training at altitude raises Hb</li>
</ul>

<h2>Factors Affecting Levels</h2>
<p><strong>Increase Hb (polycythemia):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Polycythemia vera</strong> (myeloproliferative neoplasm; JAK2 V617F mutation)</li>
<li><strong>Chronic hypoxia</strong> (COPD, sleep apnea, high altitude, cyanotic congenital heart disease, hypoventilation)</li>
<li><strong>Dehydration</strong> (relative increase; plasma volume contraction)</li>
<li><strong>Erythropoietin-secreting tumors</strong> (renal cell carcinoma, hemangioblastoma)</li>
<li><strong>Testosterone therapy</strong> (erythropoietin stimulation)</li>
<li><strong>Smoking</strong> (chronic CO exposure + chronic hypoxia)</li>
<li><strong>Post-splenectomy</strong> (transient polycythemia)</li>
<li><strong>Erythropoietin doping</strong> (athletes)</li>
<li><strong>Hemoconcentration</strong> (burns, severe diarrhea, third spacing)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Decrease Hb (anemia):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Iron deficiency</strong> (most common cause worldwide; dietary, malabsorption, blood loss)</li>
<li><strong>Vitamin B12 deficiency</strong> (pernicious anemia, malabsorption, gastric bypass, vegan diet, metformin)</li>
<li><strong>Folate deficiency</strong> (poor diet, alcoholism, pregnancy, methotrexate, phenytoin)</li>
<li><strong>Anemia of chronic disease</strong> (RA, SLE, IBD, CKD, chronic infection, malignancy)</li>
<li><strong>Acute or chronic blood loss</strong> (GI bleeding, menorrhagia, trauma)</li>
<li><strong>Bone marrow disorders</strong> (aplastic anemia, MDS, leukemia, myelofibrosis)</li>
<li><strong>Hemolytic anemias</strong> (sickle cell, thalassemia, autoimmune, G6PD deficiency, microangiopathic)</li>
<li><strong>Anemia of CKD</strong> (erythropoietin deficiency)</li>
<li><strong>Anemia of pregnancy</strong> (hemodilution + iron demand)</li>
<li><strong>Hypothyroidism, hypogonadism</strong></li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p><strong>Elevated Hb (polycythemia) is associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Polycythemia vera:</strong> JAK2 mutation; elevated Hb, Hct, platelets, WBC; thrombosis risk; treated with phlebotomy, hydroxyurea, JAK2 inhibitors (ruxolitinib)</li>
<li><strong>Secondary polycythemia:</strong> chronic hypoxia-driven erythropoietin elevation; treat underlying cause (oxygen therapy for COPD; CPAP for sleep apnea)</li>
<li><strong>Apparent polycythemia</strong> (dehydration): relative increase; rehydrate</li>
<li><strong>Thrombosis:</strong> high Hb increases blood viscosity; thrombotic events (DVT, PE, stroke)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Reduced Hb (anemia) is associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Symptoms:</strong> fatigue, weakness, pallor, dyspnea, dizziness, headache, cognitive impairment, reduced exercise capacity</li>
<li><strong>Cardiovascular:</strong> tachycardia, heart failure exacerbation (especially in CKD, HF)</li>
<li><strong>Perioperative:</strong> increased transfusion need, perioperative morbidity</li>
<li><strong>Quality of life:</strong> impaired in CKD, cancer, IBD</li>
<li><strong>Pregnancy:</strong> prematurity, low birth weight, postpartum depression</li>
<li><strong>Pediatric:</strong> impaired cognitive development</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Limitations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Single Hb value is non-specific</strong> -- always interpret with MCV, MCH, RDW, reticulocyte count, peripheral smear</li>
<li><strong>Hydration status affects Hb</strong> (dehydration falsely elevates; over-hydration falsely lowers)</li>
<li><strong>Smoking, altitude</strong> raise Hb (physiologic compensation)</li>
<li><strong>Population differences</strong> -- African American populations have Hb 0.5-0.7 g/dL lower than European-descent populations (genetic; not pathological)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Monitoring use cases:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Anemia screening (annual physical, pregnancy, perioperative, CKD/dialysis, cancer)</li>
<li>Iron deficiency monitoring (with ferritin, TIBC, transferrin saturation)</li>
<li>B12/folate deficiency monitoring (with MCV, B12, folate)</li>
<li>Chemotherapy/dialysis monitoring (CBC q1-4 weeks)</li>
<li>Blood loss monitoring (GI bleeding, trauma)</li>
<li>Hematologic malignancy workup</li>
<li>Polycythemia vera monitoring (with Hct, JAK2)</li>
<li>Transfusion threshold monitoring (typical transfusion threshold: Hb &lt;7 g/dL stable inpatient; &lt;8 g/dL symptomatic or CV disease)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Strategies to Manage</h2>
<p>Hb reflects underlying erythropoiesis; management targets the cause:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>For elevated Hb (polycythemia):</strong>
<ul>
<li><strong>Polycythemia vera:</strong> phlebotomy (target Hct &lt;45% men, &lt;42% women); hydroxyurea, JAK2 inhibitor (ruxolitinib) for high-risk; low-dose aspirin</li>
<li><strong>Secondary polycythemia:</strong> treat underlying cause (oxygen therapy, CPAP); <strong>avoid unnecessary phlebotomy</strong> unless symptomatic</li>
<li><strong>Dehydration:</strong> IV fluids</li>
<li><strong>Stop exogenous erythropoietin or testosterone</strong> if applicable</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>For low Hb (anemia):</strong>
<ul>
<li><strong>Iron deficiency:</strong> oral ferrous sulfate 325 mg TID on empty stomach (Stoffel 2017 alt-day); IV iron for malabsorption, severe deficiency, or intolerance; <strong>investigate underlying cause</strong> (GI workup for occult bleeding, celiac screen)</li>
<li><strong>Vitamin B12 deficiency:</strong> methylcobalamin 1000 mcg IM daily x 1 week, then weekly x 1 month, then monthly; or oral 1000-2000 mcg daily</li>
<li><strong>Folate deficiency:</strong> methylfolate 400-1000 mcg daily</li>
<li><strong>Anemia of CKD:</strong> erythropoiesis-stimulating agents (epoetin alfa, darbepoetin); IV iron</li>
<li><strong>Anemia of chronic disease:</strong> treat underlying cause; consider IV iron for select cases</li>
<li><strong>Hemolytic anemia:</strong> treat underlying cause (autoimmune: corticosteroids, rituximab; sickle cell: hydroxyurea, transfusion)</li>
<li><strong>Aplastic anemia, MDS, leukemia:</strong> hematology referral; transfusion support; bone marrow transplant for select cases</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Transfusion</strong> for symptomatic anemia (Hb &lt;7 g/dL stable inpatient; &lt;8 g/dL with CV disease or symptomatic)</li>
<li><strong>Lifestyle interventions:</strong>
<ul>
<li><strong>Vitamin C</strong> 200-500 mg with iron enhances absorption</li>
<li><strong>Heme-iron dietary emphasis:</strong> red meat, organ meats, shellfish</li>
<li><strong>Cast-iron cookware</strong> for acidic foods</li>
<li><strong>Avoid calcium, coffee, tea, bran</strong> at the same meal as iron</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Pre-operative optimization:</strong> iron therapy before elective surgery to raise Hb &gt;12 g/dL and reduce transfusion need (perioperative anemia bundle)</li>
</ol>
