<h1>COVID-19</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>COVID-19 (coronavirus disease 2019) is an infectious disease caused by SARS-CoV-2, a novel betacoronavirus first identified in late 2019. The ensuing pandemic, declared by the World Health Organization in March 2020, became one of the most consequential global health events in modern history, causing hundreds of millions of infections and millions of deaths and reshaping health systems and societies worldwide. Although primarily a respiratory infection, COVID-19 is fundamentally a multisystem disease.</p>
<p>SARS-CoV-2 is transmitted chiefly through respiratory aerosols and droplets and gains entry to cells by binding the ACE2 receptor, abundantly expressed in the respiratory epithelium and vascular endothelium. The clinical course is biphasic in severe cases: an early viral-replicative phase, in which antiviral therapy is most useful, followed in a subset of patients by a dysregulated host inflammatory and prothrombotic response that drives lung injury, microvascular thrombosis, and organ damage — the rationale for immunomodulatory and anticoagulant strategies in advanced disease.</p>
<p>The spectrum of illness is exceptionally wide. Many infections are asymptomatic or cause only mild upper-respiratory symptoms, while others progress to viral pneumonia, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), and multi-organ failure. Outcomes are strongly stratified by age and comorbidity, and were transformed by the rapid development of effective vaccines and oral antivirals. A meaningful fraction of survivors experience persistent symptoms — long COVID — long after the acute infection resolves.</p>
<h2>Clinical Presentation</h2>
<p>Common symptoms include fever, cough, fatigue, sore throat, nasal congestion, headache, and myalgia; loss of taste or smell was a relatively specific early feature. Gastrointestinal symptoms occur in a minority. Progression to moderate or severe disease is heralded by dyspnea, chest tightness, and hypoxemia, often around the end of the first week. Silent hypoxemia — low oxygen saturation with minimal subjective distress — is a recognized and dangerous feature warranting objective pulse-oximetry assessment.</p>
<h2>Diagnostic Information</h2>
<p>The reference diagnostic test is detection of SARS-CoV-2 RNA by RT-PCR from a respiratory specimen; rapid antigen tests are widely used for screening and point-of-care diagnosis, with lower sensitivity. Severity is assessed clinically and with pulse oximetry, inflammatory markers, and chest imaging — CT classically showing bilateral peripheral ground-glass opacities.</p>
<h2>Risk Factors</h2>
<p>The most powerful determinant of severe disease and death is advancing age. Major comorbid risks include obesity, diabetes, hypertension and cardiovascular disease, chronic lung, kidney, and liver disease, immunocompromise, and malignancy. Absence of vaccination or prior immunity markedly increases the risk of severe outcomes.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p>Management is stratified by severity. Mild disease in low-risk individuals requires only supportive care. For patients at risk of progression, antiviral therapy (such as nirmatrelvir-ritonavir or remdesivir) is most beneficial early in the course. Patients requiring supplemental oxygen benefit from dexamethasone, and those with severe inflammatory disease may receive immunomodulators such as tocilizumab or baricitinib. Oxygen and escalating respiratory support — up to mechanical ventilation — are provided as needed, along with thromboprophylaxis. Vaccination remains the cornerstone of prevention.</p>
<h2>Complications</h2>
<p>Severe COVID-19 can cause ARDS and respiratory failure, a hypercoagulable state with venous and arterial thromboembolism, acute kidney injury, cardiac injury and myocarditis, and secondary infections. A substantial proportion of recovered patients develop long COVID, a post-acute syndrome of fatigue, dyspnea, cognitive impairment, and other persistent symptoms.</p>
<h2>Prognosis</h2>
<p>Most patients recover fully, often without specific treatment. Prognosis worsens sharply with age, comorbidity burden, and the development of ARDS. Vaccination and antiviral therapy have substantially reduced rates of severe disease and death since the early pandemic.</p>
<h2>Epidemiology</h2>
<p>SARS-CoV-2 spread globally within months of emergence, producing successive waves driven by evolving variants of concern. Severity and mortality have varied with variant, population immunity, vaccination coverage, and access to care, with the heaviest burden falling on older and medically vulnerable populations.</p>
