<h1>Acid-Base Imbalance</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Acid-base balance is the maintenance of arterial blood pH within the narrow physiological range of <strong>7.35-7.45</strong>. The body regulates pH through three systems:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Chemical buffers</strong> (bicarbonate, phosphate, proteins; act within seconds)</li>
<li><strong>Respiratory system</strong> (adjusts CO2 via ventilation; acts within minutes to hours)</li>
<li><strong>Renal system</strong> (adjusts bicarbonate reabsorption and acid excretion; acts within hours to days)</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>The Henderson-Hasselbalch equation:</strong> pH = 6.1 + log([HCO3-] / (0.03 x PaCO2))</p>
<p><strong>Classification of acid-base disorders:</strong></p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Disorder</th>
<th>Primary Change</th>
<th>pH</th>
<th>Compensatory Response</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Metabolic acidosis</td>
<td>HCO3- low</td>
<td>&lt;7.35</td>
<td>Hyperventilation (low PaCO2)</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Metabolic alkalosis</td>
<td>HCO3- high</td>
<td>&gt;7.45</td>
<td>Hypoventilation (high PaCO2)</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Respiratory acidosis</td>
<td>PaCO2 high</td>
<td>&lt;7.35</td>
<td>Kidney increases HCO3- (slow)</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Respiratory alkalosis</td>
<td>PaCO2 low</td>
<td>&gt;7.45</td>
<td>Kidney decreases HCO3- (slow)</td>
</tr>

<p><strong>Metabolic acidosis causes (anion gap approach):</strong></p>
<p><strong>High anion gap metabolic acidosis (AGMA; AG &gt;12):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>MUDPILES:</strong> <strong>M</strong>ethanol, <strong>U</strong>remia, <strong>D</strong>KA, <strong>P</strong>ropylene glycol/phenformin, <strong>I</strong>ron/INH, <strong>L</strong>actic acidosis, <strong>E</strong>thylene glycol, <strong>S</strong>alicylates</li>
<li>Lactic acidosis is the most common cause (sepsis, shock, tissue hypoxia)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Normal anion gap (hyperchloremic) metabolic acidosis:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>GI loss of bicarbonate: severe diarrhea, pancreatic fistula</li>
<li>Renal: renal tubular acidosis (Types 1, 2, 4)</li>
<li>Iatrogenic: normal saline infusion (hyperchloremic)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Metabolic alkalosis causes:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Volume contraction:</strong> vomiting, NG suction (loss of HCl)</li>
<li><strong>Diuretics:</strong> thiazides, loop diuretics (contraction alkalosis; hypokalemia)</li>
<li><strong>Mineralocorticoid excess:</strong> hyperaldosteronism (Conn), Cushing syndrome</li>
<li><strong>Excess bicarbonate intake:</strong> antacid abuse (milk-alkali syndrome)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Respiratory acidosis:</strong> hypoventilation -&gt; CO2 retention</p>
<ul>
<li>COPD, opioid overdose, neuromuscular disease, obesity hypoventilation, sleep apnea</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Respiratory alkalosis:</strong> hyperventilation -&gt; CO2 washout</p>
<ul>
<li>Anxiety/panic attacks, pain, fever, sepsis, high altitude, pulmonary embolism, salicylate toxicity (early)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Systematic approach to any acid-base disorder:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Look at pH:</strong> acidemia (&lt;7.35) or alkalemia (&gt;7.45)?</li>
<li><strong>Look at PaCO2 and HCO3-:</strong> which is abnormal in the direction of the pH change? This identifies the <strong>primary disorder</strong></li>
<li><strong>Assess compensation:</strong> expected compensation using formulas (e.g., Winter's formula for metabolic acidosis: expected PaCO2 = 1.5 x HCO3- + 8 +/- 2)</li>
<li><strong>Calculate anion gap:</strong> AG = Na - (Cl + HCO3); normal 8-12</li>
<li><strong>Calculate delta-delta</strong> (if AGMA): compares delta-AG to delta-HCO3 to detect mixed disorders</li>
<li><strong>Assess for mixed disorders</strong> (multiple primary disturbances can coexist)</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Treatment principles:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Treat the underlying cause</strong> (the primary approach for all acid-base disorders)</li>
<li><strong>Metabolic acidosis:</strong> IV sodium bicarbonate (only if pH &lt;7.1 or HCO3- &lt;10; controversial for AGMA; goal pH &gt;7.15)</li>
<li><strong>Metabolic alkalosis:</strong> normal saline volume resuscitation; K+ correction; stop diuretics/vomiting; acetazolamide (carbonic anhydrase inhibitor; for refractory)</li>
<li><strong>Respiratory acidosis:</strong> non-invasive ventilation (BiPAP for COPD); intubation for severe hypoventilation; naloxone for opioid overdose</li>
<li><strong>Respiratory alkalosis:</strong> treat underlying cause (pain, anxiety, fever); rebreathing for symptomatic hypocalcemia</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Electrolyte management (critical in acid-base disorders):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Acidosis</strong> -&gt; K+ shifts extracellularly (hyperkalemia)</li>
<li><strong>Alkalosis</strong> -&gt; K+ shifts intracellularly (hypokalemia)</li>
<li><strong>Alkalosis</strong> -&gt; increased Ca2+ binding to albumin (ionized hypocalcemia; tetany, Chvostek/Trousseau signs)</li>
</ul>
