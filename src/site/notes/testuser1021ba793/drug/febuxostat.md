<h1>Febuxostat</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Febuxostat (Uloric in the US, Adenuric in the EU) is a non-purine, selective xanthine oxidase inhibitor approved in 2009 (US) and 2008 (EU) for chronic gout as urate-lowering therapy. It was the first mechanistic alternative to allopurinol in over 40 years. Chemically distinct from allopurinol (a purine analogue that inhibits only the reduced form of xanthine oxidase and requires metabolic activation to oxypurinol), febuxostat selectively inhibits both the oxidized and reduced forms of the enzyme, requires no activation, and does not interfere with other enzymes in purine or pyrimidine metabolism.</p>
<p>Clinically, febuxostat is more potent than allopurinol 300 mg at lowering serum urate and -- importantly -- does not require dose adjustment in mild-to-moderate renal impairment (CrCl 30-89 mL/min), making it an important option for the substantial fraction of gout patients with chronic kidney disease. The landmark CONFIRMS trial (Becker et al., 2010) demonstrated that febuxostat 80 mg achieved target serum urate (&lt;6 mg/dL) in 67% of patients vs 42% with allopurinol 300 mg, with comparable safety in patients with normal renal function.</p>
<p>However, the CARES trial (White et al., 2018; NEJM) -- a large FDA-mandated postmarketing CV safety study -- found a higher rate of cardiovascular death with febuxostat vs allopurinol (HR 1.34, 0.39% vs 0.29% per 100 patient-years), prompting an FDA boxed warning in 2019 and updated prescribing guidance to reserve febuxostat for patients who have failed or have contraindications to allopurinol. A subsequent European FAST trial (2020) did not confirm the CV mortality signal, leaving the question contentious; the FDA guidance nonetheless remains in force. Concurrent use with azathioprine or mercaptopurine is absolutely contraindicated due to risk of fatal myelosuppression (xanthine oxidase metabolizes these thiopurines to inactive 6-thiouric acid).</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level: 4/5</strong> (multiple high-quality RCTs and one large CV safety trial; debate on CV safety is unresolved).</p>
<h2>Pharmacology</h2>
<p><strong>Mechanism of Action:</strong><br />
Febuxostat is a selective non-purine inhibitor of xanthine oxidase (XO), the enzyme that catalyzes the final two steps in purine degradation: hypoxanthine -&gt; xanthine -&gt; uric acid. By blocking both the oxidized (Fe(III)) and reduced (Fe(II)) forms of XO, febuxostat reduces uric acid production at its source. Reduced substrate (hypoxanthine, xanthine) is more soluble than uric acid and is renally cleared or recycled by hypoxanthine-guanine phosphoribosyltransferase (HGPRT) into nucleotide pools.</p>
<p><strong>Pharmacokinetics:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Absorption: Oral bioavailability ~85%; peak 1-1.5 h; high-fat meal modestly reduces Cmax (no clinically meaningful effect)</li>
<li>Distribution: Vd ~50 L; protein binding 99.2% (mainly albumin)</li>
<li>Metabolism: Hepatic via CYP1A2, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, UGTs (conjugation); active metabolites present</li>
<li>Elimination: Balanced -- ~50% hepatic (feces), ~40% renal (urine); half-life 5-8 h</li>
<li>Renal impairment: No dose adjustment for CrCl 30-89; limited data in severe CKD</li>
<li>Hepatic impairment: No adjustment in mild-moderate; not studied in severe</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Key Drug Interactions:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Azathioprine / mercaptopurine: ABSOLUTELY CONTRAINDICATED</strong> -- XO inhibition blocks 6-mercaptopurine metabolism, causing fatal myelosuppression</li>
<li><strong>Theophylline:</strong> significantly elevates theophylline levels (XO is a minor theophylline clearance pathway); avoid or halve dose and monitor levels</li>
<li><strong>Warfarin:</strong> may slightly increase INR; monitor</li>
<li><strong>Didanosine (ddI):</strong> elevates ddI levels (mechanism analogous to theophylline)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Use</h2>
<p><strong>Primary Indication:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Chronic gout -- urate-lowering therapy with target serum urate &lt;6 mg/dL (or &lt;5 mg/dL for tophaceous gout)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Position in Therapy (post-CARES):</strong><br />
Per 2020 ACR gout management guidelines and FDA boxed warning:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>First-line:</strong> Allopurinol (preferred for all gout patients, including CKD 3-5)</li>
<li><strong>Second-line (febuxostat reserved for):</strong> Allopurinol failure (intolerance or failure to reach target serum urate at max tolerated dose), contraindication to allopurinol, or patient preference after informed CV risk discussion</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Dosing:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Start 40 mg PO once daily</li>
<li>Check serum urate at 2 weeks</li>
<li>If serum urate ≥6 mg/dL, increase to 80 mg once daily</li>
<li>If still above target, may increase to 120 mg/day (approved in EU; off-label in US)</li>
<li><strong>Do not start during an acute gout flare</strong> (continue existing flare therapy; ULT can be initiated ≥2 weeks post-flare resolution)</li>
<li><strong>Co-prescribe flare prophylaxis</strong> (colchicine 0.6 mg daily or low-dose NSAID) for at least 6 months after ULT initiation -- reduces the frequency of flares triggered by mobilization of urate pools</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Monitoring:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Serum urate: q2-4 weeks during titration, then q6-12 months once at target</li>
<li>LFTs at baseline and periodically (transaminitis ~5-10%, usually mild)</li>
<li>CV status: counsel patients on CV symptoms; reconsider therapy if CV events occur</li>
<li>Renal function (CrCl) annually</li>
<li>Gout flare frequency and tophi</li>
</ul>
<h2>Safety Information</h2>
<p><strong>Contraindications:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Concurrent azathioprine or mercaptopurine (absolute -- fatal myelosuppression)</li>
<li>Concurrent theophylline (significant interaction)</li>
<li>Known hypersensitivity</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>FDA Boxed Warning (2019):</strong><br />
&quot;Cardiovascular Death: Based on the CARES trial, febuxostat had a higher rate of cardiovascular death than allopurinol. Healthcare professionals should reserve febuxostat for patients who have an inadequate response to a maximum medically appropriate allopurinol dose, or for whom allopurinol is otherwise contraindicated.&quot;</p>
