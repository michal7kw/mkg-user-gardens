<h1>Multiple Myeloma</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Multiple myeloma (MM) is a malignancy of <strong>clonal plasma cells</strong> in the bone marrow that produce a monoclonal protein (M-protein / paraprotein). It is the <strong>second most common hematologic malignancy</strong> (after non-Hodgkin lymphoma), with ~35,000 new cases annually in the US. The median age at diagnosis is <strong>69 years</strong>. African Americans have approximately <strong>2x higher incidence</strong> than Caucasians.</p>
<p><strong>Disease spectrum:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li><strong>MGUS (monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance):</strong> M-protein &lt;3 g/dL, bone marrow plasma cells &lt;10%, no end-organ damage. Prevalence ~3% of adults &gt;50. Progression to MM at ~1% per year.</li>
<li><strong>Smoldering multiple myeloma (SMM):</strong> M-protein &gt;=3 g/dL and/or bone marrow plasma cells &gt;=10%, but no end-organ damage. Progression to MM: ~10% per year for first 5 years.</li>
<li><strong>Multiple myeloma (MM):</strong> requires <strong>CRAB</strong> criteria or <strong>myeloma-defining events</strong> (&gt;=1 bone lesion, &gt;=60% plasma cells on marrow, serum FLC ratio &gt;=100, &gt;1 focal lesion on MRI).</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>CRAB criteria (end-organ damage from myeloma):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>C</strong>alcium elevation (&gt;11 mg/dL or &gt;1 mg/dL above ULN)</li>
<li><strong>R</strong>enal insufficiency (creatinine &gt;2 mg/dL or CrCl &lt;40 mL/min)</li>
<li><strong>A</strong>nemia (Hb &lt;10 g/dL or 2 g/dL below LLN)</li>
<li><strong>B</strong>one lesions (lytic lesions or osteoporosis with compression fractures)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Pathophysiology:</strong><br />
Malignant plasma cells accumulate in the bone marrow, secreting monoclonal immunoglobulin and cytokines (RANKL, DKK1) that promote osteoclast activity and suppress osteoblast function -&gt; <strong>bone destruction</strong> (lytic lesions, pathologic fractures). Free light chains are filtered by glomeruli and are directly toxic to renal tubules -&gt; <strong>cast nephropathy</strong> (myeloma kidney).</p>
<p><strong>Treatment revolution:</strong> The introduction of proteasome inhibitors (bortezomib; 2003), immunomodulatory drugs (lenalidomide; 2004), anti-CD38 antibodies (daratumumab; 2015), bispecific antibodies (teclistamab; 2022), and CAR-T cell therapy (ide-cel; 2021) has dramatically improved median overall survival from <strong>~3 years</strong> (pre-2000) to <strong>&gt;8-10 years</strong> for newly diagnosed patients.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Newly diagnosed MM (transplant-eligible: typically &lt;65-70 years):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Induction:</strong> Daratumumab + Bortezomib + Lenalidomide + Dexamethasone (Dara-VRd) or VRd</li>
<li><strong>Autologous stem cell transplant</strong> (ASCT) after 3-4 cycles of induction</li>
<li><strong>Consolidation/maintenance:</strong> Lenalidomide until progression (or VR for high-risk)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Newly diagnosed MM (transplant-ineligible: typically &gt;65-70 or frail):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Daratumumab + Lenalidomide + Dexamethasone</strong> (Dara-Rd) or <strong>VRd-lite</strong></li>
<li>Continue until progression</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Relapsed/refractory MM (RRMM):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Daratumumab</strong> +/- pomalidomide + dexamethasone</li>
<li><strong>Carfilzomib + dexamethasone</strong> (Kd)</li>
<li><strong>Isatuximab + pomalidomide + dexamethasone</strong></li>
<li><strong>Bispecific antibodies:</strong> teclistamab (BCMA x CD3), elranatamab (BCMA x CD3)</li>
<li><strong>CAR-T cell therapy:</strong> idecabtagene vicleucel (ide-cel; BCMA-targeting), ciltacabtagene autoleucel (cilta-cel)</li>
<li><strong>Selinexor</strong> (XPO1 inhibitor; for penta-refractory)</li>
<li><strong>Bortezomib</strong> re-treatment (if not resistant)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Supportive care:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Bone disease:</strong> zoledronic acid 4 mg IV monthly OR denosumab 120 mg SC monthly (reduces skeletal-related events)</li>
<li><strong>Renal protection:</strong> aggressive hydration; bortezomib-based therapy (rapid FLC reduction); consider plasma exchange for severe cast nephropathy</li>
<li><strong>Hypercalcemia:</strong> IV fluids, bisphosphonates, denosumab, calcitonin</li>
<li><strong>Infection prophylaxis:</strong> PCP prophylaxis (TMP-SMX); VZV prophylaxis (acyclovir); IVIG for hypogammaglobulinemia with recurrent infections</li>
<li><strong>VTE prophylaxis:</strong> aspirin or LMWH (for patients on lenalidomide/pomalidomide-based regimens)</li>
</ul>
