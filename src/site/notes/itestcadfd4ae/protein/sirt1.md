<h1>SIRT1</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Sirtuin 1 (SIRT1) is the best-characterized member of the human sirtuin family, a group of seven NAD+-dependent (class III) deacylases descended from the yeast Sir2 gene. The 747-amino-acid, ~81.7 kDa enzyme uses nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) as an obligatory co-substrate to remove acetyl groups from lysine residues on both histones and a large set of non-histone proteins. Because each catalytic cycle consumes one molecule of NAD+ and releases nicotinamide and O-acetyl-ADP-ribose, SIRT1 activity is tightly coupled to the cell's energy and redox state — making it a molecular sensor that translates nutrient and metabolic signals into changes in gene expression.</p>
<p>SIRT1 sits at the hub of pathways governing metabolism, mitochondrial function, the DNA-damage and oxidative-stress response, inflammation, the circadian clock, and cellular senescence. By deacetylating transcription factors and coactivators such as PGC-1alpha, FOXO proteins, p53, and NF-kB, it shifts cells toward fatty-acid oxidation, mitochondrial biogenesis, stress resistance, and survival under conditions of energy scarcity. These activities have placed SIRT1 at the center of aging biology: it is a principal mediator of the benefits of caloric restriction and the proposed target through which the polyphenol resveratrol exerts its metabolic and pro-longevity effects.</p>
<p>Clinically, SIRT1 is of intense interest in metabolic disease (type 2 diabetes, insulin resistance, fatty liver), cardiovascular and neurodegenerative disease, and healthy aging. Strategies to raise its activity — directly with small-molecule activators or indirectly by elevating cellular NAD+ via precursors such as nicotinamide riboside and NMN — are an active area of translational research, though human outcome data remain preliminary relative to the depth of mechanistic evidence.</p>
<h2>Function Summary</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Enzyme class:</strong> NAD+-dependent class III histone/protein deacetylase (sirtuin).</li>
<li><strong>Reaction:</strong> Acetyl-lysine + NAD+ -&gt; deacetylated lysine + nicotinamide + O-acetyl-ADP-ribose.</li>
<li><strong>Primary role:</strong> Energy/redox sensor that reprograms transcription and metabolism in response to NAD+ availability.</li>
<li><strong>Key outputs:</strong> Mitochondrial biogenesis, fatty-acid oxidation, gluconeogenesis control, antioxidant defense, DNA repair, and pro-survival signaling.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Detailed Description</h2>
<h3>Gene and Protein</h3>
<p>The <em>SIRT1</em> gene is located on the long arm of human chromosome 10 (10q21.3; Entrez Gene 23411, Ensembl ENSG00000096717) and encodes a 747-residue protein (UniProt Q96EB6, calculated mass ~81.7 kDa). The protein is predominantly nuclear — concentrating in PML nuclear bodies — but shuttles between the nucleus and cytoplasm in a regulated, cell-type- and stress-dependent manner, which broadens the range of substrates it can reach. The catalytic core is a conserved sirtuin domain flanked by N- and C-terminal regulatory regions that modulate activity and protein-protein interactions.</p>
<h3>Mechanism of Action</h3>
<p>SIRT1 catalyzes deacetylation through a mechanism that requires NAD+ as a co-substrate rather than as a simple cofactor: the enzyme cleaves NAD+ and transfers the acetyl group from the target lysine onto the ADP-ribose moiety. Because nicotinamide is both a product and a feedback inhibitor, and because the reaction is rate-limited by NAD+ concentration, SIRT1 activity rises when the NAD+/NADH ratio increases — for example during fasting, caloric restriction, or exercise — and falls when NAD+ is depleted (as occurs with aging, overnutrition, or chronic inflammation). This makes the enzyme an integrator of metabolic state and transcriptional output.</p>
<h3>Key Substrates and Downstream Effects</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>PGC-1alpha:</strong> Deacetylation activates this master coactivator of mitochondrial biogenesis and oxidative metabolism, increasing mitochondrial content and fatty-acid oxidation in muscle, liver, and brown fat.</li>
<li><strong>FOXO transcription factors:</strong> SIRT1 modulates FOXO (e.g., FOXO3) activity, biasing the cellular response toward stress resistance and DNA repair rather than apoptosis.</li>
<li><strong>p53:</strong> Deacetylation of p53 dampens p53-dependent transcription, attenuating apoptosis and senescence after stress.</li>
<li><strong>NF-kB (p65/RelA):</strong> Deacetylation reduces NF-kB transcriptional activity, contributing to SIRT1's anti-inflammatory effects.</li>
<li><strong>Histones (H3K9, H4K16, H1K26):</strong> Histone deacetylation promotes heterochromatin formation and gene silencing, with consequences for genome stability and aging.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Clinical Significance</h3>
<p>In metabolic tissues, higher SIRT1 activity improves insulin sensitivity, enhances hepatic and muscle fat oxidation, and protects against diet-induced obesity and type 2 diabetes in preclinical models. In the cardiovascular and nervous systems, SIRT1 limits oxidative stress and inflammation and supports cellular survival, which underlies interest in it as a target in atherosclerosis, heart failure, and neurodegeneration. As a downstream effector of caloric restriction and a putative resveratrol target, SIRT1 is one of the most-studied nodes in the biology of aging and longevity. Translationally, both direct SIRT1-activating compounds and NAD+-boosting precursors (nicotinamide riboside, NMN) are being evaluated to restore declining sirtuin activity with age, although robust clinical efficacy data are still emerging.</p>
<h2>NAD+ Dependence and Boosting Strategies</h2>
<p>SIRT1's strict NAD+ requirement is the basis for the most actively pursued therapeutic approach: raising intracellular NAD+ to drive sirtuin activity. NAD+ levels decline with age and metabolic stress, and supplementation with biosynthetic precursors such as nicotinamide riboside and NMN can elevate NAD+ and, in animal models, enhance SIRT1-dependent mitochondrial and metabolic benefits. NAMPT, the rate-limiting enzyme of the NAD+ salvage pathway, is a key upstream determinant of substrate availability for SIRT1.</p>
