<h1>Bacteroides fragilis</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Common colonic commensal (~1-2% of gut microbiota) with a notable Jekyll-and-Hyde profile. Non-toxigenic strains produce polysaccharide A (PSA) which induces regulatory T cells and is anti-inflammatory. Enterotoxigenic strains (ETBF) produce fragilysin (B. fragilis toxin) and are linked to colorectal cancer and IBD. The most common anaerobe isolated from intra-abdominal infections after gut perforation, where it shows substantial antibiotic resistance.</p>

<h2>Habitat</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Body Sites:</strong> gut (colon)</li>
<li><strong>Oxygen Requirement:</strong> Obligate anaerobe (but unusually aerotolerant for an anaerobe)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Role</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Pathogenicity:</strong> Conditional — beneficial commensal in the gut, opportunistic pathogen when translocated</li>
<li><strong>Key Metabolites Produced:</strong> acetate, propionate, succinate, polysaccharide A (immunomodulatory)</li>
</ul>
<p>Polysaccharide A is one of the few microbial molecules with a clearly defined effect on host immunity: it signals through TLR2 on dendritic cells to induce IL-10-producing Treg cells.</p>
