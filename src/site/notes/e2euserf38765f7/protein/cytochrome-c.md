<h1>Cytochrome C</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Cytochrome c is a small (~12 kDa) soluble heme protein that serves as an essential mobile electron carrier in the mitochondrial <a href="/garden/e2euserf38765f7/pathway/electron-transport-chain/" class="internal-link">Electron Transport Chain</a>. Loosely associated with the outer face of the inner mitochondrial membrane within the intermembrane space, it shuttles single electrons from <a href="/garden/e2euserf38765f7/protein/complex-iii/" class="internal-link">Complex III</a> (cytochrome bc1) to <a href="/garden/e2euserf38765f7/protein/complex-iv/" class="internal-link">Complex IV</a> (cytochrome c oxidase). Beyond its bioenergetic role, cytochrome c is a pivotal signalling molecule: its release from the mitochondrial intermembrane space into the cytosol is a committed step of the intrinsic (mitochondrial) apoptotic pathway. It is one of the most evolutionarily conserved proteins known, reflecting the central importance of <a href="/garden/e2euserf38765f7/pathway/oxidative-phosphorylation/" class="internal-link">Oxidative Phosphorylation</a> across all aerobic life.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>The redox chemistry of cytochrome c is centred on a single covalently bound c-type heme group, whose iron atom cycles between the reduced ferrous (Fe²⁺) and oxidized ferric (Fe³⁺) states. In the respiratory chain, reduced <a href="/garden/e2euserf38765f7/supplement/coenzyme-q10/" class="internal-link">Coenzyme Q10</a> (ubiquinol) delivers electrons to <a href="/garden/e2euserf38765f7/protein/complex-iii/" class="internal-link">Complex III</a>, which in turn reduces the heme iron of cytochrome c (Fe³⁺ → Fe²⁺) at the positive (intermembrane-space) side of the membrane. The reduced cytochrome c then diffuses laterally along the membrane surface and docks onto <a href="/garden/e2euserf38765f7/protein/complex-iv/" class="internal-link">Complex IV</a>, donating its electron and reverting to Fe³⁺. Complex IV ultimately transfers four electrons to molecular <a href="/garden/e2euserf38765f7/metabolite/oxygen/" class="internal-link">Oxygen</a>, reducing it to <a href="/garden/e2euserf38765f7/metabolite/water/" class="internal-link">Water</a>. Because cytochrome c carries only one electron at a time, it functions as a one-electron relay that couples the two-electron chemistry of the quinone pool to the four-electron chemistry of oxygen reduction.</p>
<h2>Biological Role</h2>
<p>By bridging <a href="/garden/e2euserf38765f7/protein/complex-iii/" class="internal-link">Complex III</a> and <a href="/garden/e2euserf38765f7/protein/complex-iv/" class="internal-link">Complex IV</a>, cytochrome c is indispensable for sustaining the electron flux that generates the proton-motive force used by <a href="/garden/e2euserf38765f7/protein/atp-synthase/" class="internal-link">ATP Synthase</a> to phosphorylate ADP to ATP. It is part of the broader <a href="/garden/e2euserf38765f7/pathway/oxidative-phosphorylation/" class="internal-link">Oxidative Phosphorylation</a> machinery housed within the <a href="/garden/e2euserf38765f7/cellularcomponent/mitochondria/" class="internal-link">Mitochondria</a>. Cytochrome c also participates in mitochondrial redox homeostasis and can act as a peroxidase under oxidative stress, contributing to handling of <a href="/garden/e2euserf38765f7/metabolite/reactive-oxygen-species/" class="internal-link">Reactive Oxygen Species</a>.</p>
<p>In its second, apoptotic role, mitochondrial outer-membrane permeabilization releases cytochrome c into the cytosol, where it binds the adaptor protein Apaf-1 to nucleate the apoptosome and activate caspase-9, triggering the intrinsic apoptotic cascade. This dual identity makes cytochrome c a sentinel that links the energetic health of the mitochondrion to the cell's life-or-death decision.</p>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>Because cytochrome c sits at the heart of cellular respiration, mutations in CYCS or defects in cytochrome c oxidase assembly impair ATP production and underlie mitochondrial disease phenotypes (lactic acidosis, myopathy, encephalopathy). Detectable cytosolic or circulating cytochrome c is used as a biomarker of apoptosis and mitochondrial injury in ischemia-reperfusion, sepsis, and certain cancers. Therapeutically, the mitochondrial electron-transport context relevant to cytochrome c is targeted by ubiquinone-based support such as <a href="/garden/e2euserf38765f7/supplement/coenzyme-q10/" class="internal-link">Coenzyme Q10</a>, which helps maintain upstream electron flow into <a href="/garden/e2euserf38765f7/protein/complex-iii/" class="internal-link">Complex III</a>. Cytochrome c is also a classic textbook model for understanding electron-transfer proteins and molecular evolution.</p>
