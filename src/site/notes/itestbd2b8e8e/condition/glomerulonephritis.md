<h1>Glomerulonephritis</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Glomerulonephritis (GN) encompasses a diverse group of diseases that cause inflammation and structural damage to the <strong>glomeruli</strong> -- the tuft of capillaries within each nephron that filters blood to form urine. GN is the <strong>third leading cause of end-stage renal disease (ESRD)</strong> worldwide, after diabetes and hypertension. The clinical spectrum ranges from asymptomatic microscopic hematuria to rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis (RPGN) with acute kidney failure.</p>
<p>GN is classified by:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Pathology:</strong> proliferative (mesangial, endocapillary, extracapillary/crescentic), non-proliferative (membranous, minimal change, FSGS)</li>
<li><strong>Etiology:</strong> primary (idiopathic, intrinsic renal) vs secondary (systemic disease)</li>
<li><strong>Syndrome:</strong> nephritic (hematuria, hypertension, AKI), nephrotic (heavy proteinuria, edema, hypoalbuminemia), or mixed</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Common types:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>IgA nephropathy</strong> (Berger disease) -- most common primary GN worldwide</li>
<li><strong>Post-streptococcal GN</strong> -- classic post-infectious; children; usually self-resolving</li>
<li><strong>Membranous nephropathy</strong> -- most common cause of nephrotic syndrome in Caucasian adults</li>
<li><strong>Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS)</strong> -- increasing incidence; APOL1 risk variants</li>
<li><strong>Minimal change disease</strong> -- most common nephrotic syndrome in children; steroid-responsive</li>
<li><strong>Lupus nephritis</strong> -- SLE complication; classified ISN/RPS I-VI</li>
<li><strong>ANCA-associated GN</strong> -- granulomatosis with polyangiitis, microscopic polyangiitis</li>
<li><strong>Anti-GBM disease</strong> (Goodpasture) -- pulmonary hemorrhage + RPGN</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Kidney biopsy</strong> is the gold standard for definitive diagnosis, guiding prognosis and treatment decisions.</p>
<h2>Clinical Presentation</h2>
<p><strong>Nephritic syndrome:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Hematuria (microscopic or macroscopic; dysmorphic RBCs, RBC casts)</li>
<li>Proteinuria (usually sub-nephrotic, &lt;3.5 g/day)</li>
<li>Hypertension</li>
<li>Edema (periorbital, dependent)</li>
<li>Oliguria, AKI (in RPGN)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Nephrotic syndrome:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Heavy proteinuria (&gt;3.5 g/day/1.73 m²)</li>
<li>Hypoalbuminemia (&lt;3.0 g/dL)</li>
<li>Edema (generalized)</li>
<li>Hyperlipidemia</li>
<li>Thrombotic tendency</li>
</ul>
<h2>Diagnostic Information</h2>
<p><strong>Urinalysis:</strong> dysmorphic RBCs (glomerular bleeding), RBC casts (pathognomonic), proteinuria, WBC casts<br />
<strong>24-hour urine protein</strong> or <strong>UPCR</strong> (&gt;3.5 g or UPCR &gt;2-3 suggests nephrotic range)<br />
<strong>Serology:</strong> ANA, anti-dsDNA (lupus), ANCA (MPA, GPA), anti-GBM (Goodpasture), complement C3/C4 (low in post-strep, lupus, cryoglobulinemia), Hepatitis B/C, HIV<br />
<strong>Renal biopsy:</strong> the gold standard for definitive diagnosis, classification, and treatment guidance<br />
<strong>Immunofluorescence and electron microscopy:</strong> identify immune complex deposition patterns</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>General:</strong> ACE inhibitors or ARBs (reduce proteinuria and intraglomerular pressure), blood pressure control (&lt;130/80), sodium restriction<br />
<strong>Disease-specific immunosuppression:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Minimal change disease:</strong> high-dose corticosteroids (prednisone 1 mg/kg/day)</li>
<li><strong>FSGS:</strong> steroids; calcineurin inhibitors (cyclosporine, tacrolimus) if steroid-resistant</li>
<li><strong>Membranous nephropathy:</strong> steroids + cyclophosphamide or calcineurin inhibitors; rituximab</li>
<li><strong>IgA nephropathy:</strong> ACEi/ARB; steroids for progressive disease; sparsentan (new)</li>
<li><strong>Lupus nephritis (Class III/IV):</strong> mycophenolate + steroids; cyclophosphamide; belimumab, voclosporin</li>
<li><strong>ANCA vasculitis:</strong> cyclophosphamide or rituximab + steroids; plasma exchange for severe</li>
<li><strong>Anti-GBM:</strong> plasma exchange + cyclophosphamide + steroids (emergency)<br />
<strong>ESRD:</strong> dialysis, kidney transplant (recurrence risk varies by type)</li>
</ul>
