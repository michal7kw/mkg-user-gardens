<h1>Pulmonary Hypertension</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Pulmonary hypertension (PH) is defined as mean pulmonary artery pressure (mPAP) &gt;=20 mmHg at rest by right heart catheterization (updated 2019 6th WSPH definition). PH is classified into <strong>5 clinical groups</strong> based on etiology:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Group</th>
<th>Etiology</th>
<th>Hemodynamic Definition</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>1</td>
<td>Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH)</td>
<td>mPAP &gt;20, PVR &gt;=3 WU, PCWP &lt;=15</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>2</td>
<td>PH due to left heart disease</td>
<td>mPAP &gt;20, PCWP &gt;15</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>3</td>
<td>PH due to lung disease/hypoxia</td>
<td>mPAP &gt;20, PVR &gt;=3 WU (COPD, ILD, sleep apnea)</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>4</td>
<td>PH due to pulmonary artery obstruction</td>
<td>Chronic thromboembolic PH (CTEPH)</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>5</td>
<td>Unclear/multifactorial</td>
<td>Sarcoidosis, hematologic, metabolic</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p><strong>Group 1 (PAH)</strong> is the &quot;prototypical&quot; pulmonary vascular disease and has been the focus of most drug development. PAH has an annual incidence of ~2-5 per million. Without treatment, median survival is <strong>2.8 years</strong> from diagnosis. Modern combination therapy has improved 5-year survival to <strong>~60-70%</strong>.</p>
<p>The pathophysiology of PAH involves <strong>pulmonary arteriolar remodeling</strong>: smooth muscle proliferation, endothelial dysfunction, in situ thrombosis, and vasoconstriction. Three major pathways are therapeutically targeted:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Endothelin pathway</strong> (vasoconstrictor; overexpressed in PAH) -&gt; endothelin receptor antagonists</li>
<li><strong>Nitric oxide pathway</strong> (vasodilator; deficient in PAH) -&gt; PDE5 inhibitors, sGC stimulators</li>
<li><strong>Prostacyclin pathway</strong> (vasodilator; deficient in PAH) -&gt; prostacyclin analogues</li>
</ol>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Initial oral combination therapy</strong> (SERAPHIN, AMBITION, GRIPHON evidence; now the standard of care):</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>ERA + PDE5 inhibitor</strong> combination as initial therapy for most PAH patients:
<ul>
<li>Macitentan 10 mg daily + tadalafil 40 mg daily</li>
<li>Ambrisentan 10 mg daily + tadalafil 40 mg daily</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Combination therapy improves outcomes vs monotherapy (AMBITION trial: 50% risk reduction)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Calcium channel blockers:</strong> only for patients with positive <strong>acute vasodilator response</strong> during right heart catheterization (~6.5% of idiopathic PAH); nifedipine, diltiazem, amlodipine</p>
<p><strong>Advanced therapy (high-risk or progressing):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Parenteral prostacyclin:</strong> epoprostenol IV (continuous infusion; gold standard for advanced PAH); treprostinil SC/IV</li>
<li><strong>Inhaled prostacyclin:</strong> iloprost, treprostinil</li>
<li><strong>Oral prostacyclin receptor agonist:</strong> selexipag</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Surgical:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Pulmonary endarterectomy</strong> (for CTEPH; potentially curative)</li>
<li><strong>Balloon pulmonary angioplasty</strong> (for inoperable CTEPH)</li>
<li><strong>Atrial septostomy</strong> (palliative; right-to-left shunt decompresses RV)</li>
<li><strong>Lung or heart-lung transplantation</strong> (for refractory disease)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Supportive:</strong> oxygen for hypoxemia; diuretics for volume overload; anticoagulation (PAH); referral to PH center</p>
