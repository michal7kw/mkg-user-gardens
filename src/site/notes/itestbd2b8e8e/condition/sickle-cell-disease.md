<h1>Sickle Cell Disease</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Sickle cell disease (SCD) is an autosomal recessive hemoglobinopathy caused by a single-nucleotide mutation (Glu6Val; rs334) in the <strong>beta-globin (HBB) gene</strong> that produces <strong>hemoglobin S (HbS)</strong>. Under low-oxygen conditions HbS polymerizes, deforming red blood cells into rigid, sickle shapes that occlude the microvasculature, cause chronic hemolytic anemia, and drive progressive end-organ damage. SCD is the <strong>most common inherited blood disorder worldwide</strong>: ~300,000 infants are born with it each year, predominantly in sub-Saharan Africa, but also in the Mediterranean, Middle East, and parts of India; carrier frequency reaches <strong>10-40% in equatorial African populations</strong> because the HbS trait confers protection against <em>Plasmodium falciparum</em> malaria (balancing selection).</p>
<p>The clinical course is defined by two pathophysiologic processes. <strong>Chronic hemolysis</strong> (from intravascular sickling and splenic clearance of damaged cells) produces anemia, jaundice, and pigment gallstones; it also scavenges nitric oxide, contributing to <strong>pulmonary hypertension</strong> and leg ulcers. <strong>Vaso-occlusion</strong> -- sickled cells adhering to the endothelium and forming aggregates with leukocytes and platelets -- produces the hallmark <strong>pain crisis</strong> (infarction of bone marrow, spleen, lung, brain) and the feared <strong>acute chest syndrome</strong> (the leading cause of death). <strong>Functional asplenia</strong> develops in childhood from autoinfarction, dramatically increasing susceptibility to <strong>encapsulated bacteria</strong> (<em>Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae, Neisseria meningitidis</em>) -- once the leading cause of childhood death, now largely prevented by penicillin prophylaxis and vaccination.</p>
<p>Therapy has been transformed since 2017. <strong>Hydroxyurea</strong>, which induces fetal hemoglobin (HbF) that interferes with HbS polymerization, remains the foundational disease-modifying drug -- reducing crises, acute chest syndrome, transfusions, and mortality. <strong>Crizanlizumab</strong> (anti-P-selectin) and <strong>voxelotor</strong> (HbS polymerization inhibitor) were approved in 2019 for adjunctive use. <strong>Curative options</strong> have expanded dramatically: allogeneic stem-cell transplant cures ~85% of patients with a matched sibling donor, and in 2023 the FDA approved <strong>two gene therapies</strong> (exagamglogene autotemcel, lovotibeglogene autotemcel) -- the first genome-editing cures for a genetic disease.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Disease-modifying therapy:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Hydroxyurea</strong> (20-35 mg/kg/day; titrate to max tolerated dose) -- first-line for all HbSS/HbS-beta0 patients from 9 months of age; induces HbF, reduces crises ~50%, reduces ACS and transfusions</li>
<li><strong>L-glutamine</strong> (0.3 g/kg BID; Endari) -- adjunctive; reduces crisis frequency</li>
<li><strong>Crizanlizumab</strong> (5 mg/kg IV every 4 weeks) -- anti-P-selectin; reduces pain crises</li>
<li><strong>Voxelotor</strong> (1500 mg/day) -- HbS polymerization inhibitor; improves hemoglobin</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Transfusion therapy:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Simple transfusion</strong> for symptomatic severe anemia</li>
<li><strong>Exchange transfusion</strong> for acute stroke, acute chest syndrome, multi-organ failure, severe priapism, and pre-operative (target HbS &lt;30%)</li>
<li><strong>Chronic transfusion</strong> for secondary stroke prevention (children at high risk on transcranial Doppler)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Curative:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplant</strong> -- curative for patients with matched sibling donor (~85% survival with event-free survival ~85%)</li>
<li><strong>Gene therapy</strong> -- exagamglogene autotemcel (CRISPR-Cas9, disrupts BCL11A to induce HbF) and lovotibeglogene autotemcel (lentiviral, anti-sickling beta-globin); FDA-approved 2023-2024</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Preventive care:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Penicillin prophylaxis</strong> from 2 months to age 5 (or life-long if surgical splenectomy)</li>
<li><strong>Vaccinations</strong> -- pneumococcal (PCV20 + PPSV23), Hib, meningococcal ACWY + B, annual influenza, hepatitis B</li>
<li><strong>Annual transcranial Doppler</strong> in children 2-16 (identifies stroke risk; chronic transfusion if abnormal)</li>
<li><strong>Folic acid</strong> 1 mg/day (support erythropoiesis)</li>
<li><strong>Hydration</strong> (avoid dehydration; ~3 L/day adults)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Crisis management:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>IV fluids</strong>, <strong>oxygen</strong>, <strong>analgesia</strong> (opioids; PCA for severe crises; treat pain aggressively within 30-60 min)</li>
<li>Treat triggers (infection, dehydration, hypoxia)</li>
<li><strong>Exchange transfusion</strong> for acute chest syndrome, stroke, severe crises</li>
</ul>
