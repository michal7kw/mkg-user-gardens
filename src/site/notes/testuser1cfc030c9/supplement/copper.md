<h1>Copper</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Copper is an essential trace mineral required for the function of a small but critical set of metalloenzymes. Total body copper is only around 50-120 mg, concentrated in the liver, brain, heart, and kidneys, yet it is indispensable for cellular energy production, iron transport, connective-tissue integrity, and antioxidant defence. Dietary sources include shellfish, organ meats, nuts, seeds, legumes, and dark chocolate. Frank dietary deficiency is uncommon in healthy adults, but acquired deficiency occurs after bariatric surgery, in malabsorption, and notably with chronic high-dose zinc supplementation, which induces intestinal metallothionein that traps copper in enterocytes. Deficiency presents as a microcytic or normocytic anaemia and neutropenia that mimic myelodysplastic syndrome, along with a progressive myeloneuropathy. Copper supplements are therefore used principally for prevention and correction of deficiency rather than for general wellness.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>Copper cycles between Cu(I) and Cu(II) oxidation states, making it a versatile redox cofactor. Cytochrome c oxidase (complex IV) uses copper centres to reduce oxygen at the end of the mitochondrial electron transport chain. Cu/Zn superoxide dismutase neutralises superoxide radicals. Ceruloplasmin and hephaestin are ferroxidases that oxidise Fe(II) to Fe(III) so iron can bind transferrin, which is why copper deficiency causes an iron-refractory anaemia. Lysyl oxidase cross-links collagen and elastin, supporting vascular, bone, and connective-tissue strength, and dopamine beta-hydroxylase converts dopamine to noradrenaline. Because copper and zinc compete for absorption, copper status is tightly coupled to zinc intake.</p>
<h2>Dosage</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>RDA (adults)</strong>: ~0.9 mg/day</li>
<li><strong>Co-supplementation with zinc</strong>: ~1-2 mg copper per 15-30 mg zinc</li>
<li><strong>Maintenance/repletion</strong>: 0.9-2 mg/day of elemental copper</li>
<li><strong>Tolerable upper intake level (adults)</strong>: 10 mg/day</li>
<li><strong>Timing</strong>: With food; separate from high-dose zinc, iron, and antacids</li>
</ul>
