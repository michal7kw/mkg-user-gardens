<h1>Pyelonephritis</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Pyelonephritis is an upper urinary tract infection involving the <strong>renal parenchyma and renal pelvis</strong>. It is most commonly caused by <strong>ascending infection from the bladder</strong> (cystitis), with <em>Escherichia coli</em> responsible for approximately 80% of cases. Other pathogens include <em>Klebsiella, Proteus, Enterococcus</em>, and <em>Pseudomonas</em> (catheter-associated or complicated). Pyelonephritis can be classified as <strong>uncomplicated</strong> (otherwise healthy non-pregnant adult woman) or <strong>complicated</strong> (male, pregnant, diabetic, immunocompromised, obstructed urinary tract, hospital-acquired, or catheter-associated).</p>
<p>The classic presentation is <strong>fever, flank pain, and costovertebral angle (CVA) tenderness</strong>, often accompanied by lower urinary tract symptoms (dysuria, frequency, urgency) and systemic symptoms (nausea, vomiting, malaise). Pyelonephritis is more serious than simple cystitis because the infection involves the kidney parenchyma, with risk of renal scarring, abscess formation, sepsis, and acute kidney injury.</p>
<p><strong>Emphysematous pyelonephritis</strong> is a life-threatening gas-forming infection of the kidney seen almost exclusively in diabetics, requiring aggressive management (nephrectomy, percutaneous drainage, broad-spectrum antibiotics).</p>
<h2>Clinical Presentation</h2>
<p><strong>Classic triad:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Fever</strong> (often &gt;38.5C) with chills</li>
<li><strong>Flank pain</strong> or CVA tenderness (unilateral in most cases)</li>
<li><strong>Lower UTI symptoms</strong> (dysuria, frequency, urgency; may be absent)</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Other symptoms:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Nausea, vomiting (common)</li>
<li>Malaise, anorexia</li>
<li>Abdominal pain</li>
<li>Cloudy or foul-smelling urine</li>
<li>Hematuria (microscopic or gross)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Diagnostic Information</h2>
<p><strong>Urinalysis:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Leukocyte esterase positive</li>
<li>Nitrite positive (if enterobacteriaceae)</li>
<li>Pyuria (&gt;10 WBC/hpf)</li>
<li>Hematuria</li>
<li>WBC casts (pathognomonic for pyelonephritis vs simple cystitis)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Urine culture:</strong> &gt;10^5 CFU/mL (midstream clean-catch); identifies organism and sensitivities</p>
<p><strong>Blood tests:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Leukocytosis with left shift</li>
<li>Elevated CRP and ESR</li>
<li>Elevated creatinine (if AKI develops)</li>
<li>Blood cultures (positive in 10-20%; perform if septic)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Imaging:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>CT abdomen/pelvis</strong> (with contrast) -- gold standard; identifies obstruction, abscess, gas (emphysematous), perfusion defects</li>
<li><strong>Ultrasound</strong> -- first-line in pregnancy and children; screens for obstruction, stones, abscess</li>
<li>Not all patients need imaging; reserve for: complicated cases, diabetics, no improvement in 48-72h, recurrent episodes, male patients, suspected obstruction</li>
</ul>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Uncomplicated pyelonephritis (outpatient):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Fluoroquinolones</strong> (ciprofloxacin 500 mg BID x 7 days; or levofloxacin 750 mg daily x 5 days)</li>
<li><strong>TMP-SMX</strong> DS BID x 14 days (if local resistance &lt;20%)</li>
<li><strong>Oral cephalosporins</strong> (cefixime) -- if fluoroquinolone resistance concern</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Complicated pyelonephritis (inpatient):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>IV ceftriaxone</strong> 1-2 g IV daily</li>
<li><strong>Piperacillin-tazobactam</strong> 3.375 g IV q6h (broad-spectrum, diabetic, healthcare-associated)</li>
<li><strong>Ciprofloxacin</strong> 400 mg IV q12h</li>
<li>Switch to oral when clinically improving and tolerating PO; total duration 7-14 days</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Supportive:</strong> IV fluids, analgesics (acetaminophen; avoid NSAIDs if AKI), antiemetics</p>
<p><strong>Surgical intervention:</strong> percutaneous drainage for abscess; nephrostomy for obstruction; nephrectomy for emphysematous pyelonephritis (severe cases)</p>
