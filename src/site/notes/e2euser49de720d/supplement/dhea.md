<h1>DHEA</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>DHEA (dehydroepiandrosterone) is the most abundant circulating steroid hormone in humans, produced mainly by the adrenal cortex and serving as a precursor to both androgens and estrogens. Its levels peak in the third decade of life and then fall steadily, reaching a small fraction of youthful values by old age, which has driven interest in DHEA as an anti-aging and hormone-restoration supplement. In the United States it is sold over the counter as a dietary supplement, whereas in many other countries it is regulated as a prescription medicine. Evidence supports replacement in documented adrenal insufficiency and a benefit of intravaginal prasterone for vulvovaginal atrophy, while broader claims for healthy aging, body composition, mood, and athletic performance are weakly supported. Because DHEA is converted to active sex steroids, it carries the hormonal risks of those downstream hormones and should be used cautiously and monitored.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>DHEA functions as an inactive prohormone that exerts effects through downstream metabolism rather than by binding a dedicated high-affinity receptor. In circulation most DHEA exists as its sulfate ester DHEA-S, which acts as a long-lived reservoir. Within peripheral tissues, intracrine enzymes convert DHEA to testosterone and dihydrotestosterone and, via aromatase, to estradiol, locally raising sex-steroid signalling without large systemic surges. This tissue-specific conversion underlies its use in vaginal atrophy and its androgenic and estrogenic side-effect profile. DHEA also contributes to the balance between catabolic cortisol and anabolic adrenal androgens, and shifts in the cortisol-to-DHEA ratio are studied as markers of chronic stress and aging.</p>
<h2>Dosage</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Age-related decline</strong>: 25-50 mg once daily, taken in the morning</li>
<li><strong>Adrenal insufficiency replacement</strong>: ~25-50 mg/day, titrated to DHEA-S levels</li>
<li><strong>Vulvovaginal atrophy</strong>: intravaginal prasterone 6.5 mg nightly (approved formulation)</li>
<li><strong>Fertility protocols</strong>: higher doses used under specialist supervision only</li>
<li><strong>Monitoring</strong>: check baseline and follow-up DHEA-S; lower doses generally suffice in women than men</li>
</ul>
