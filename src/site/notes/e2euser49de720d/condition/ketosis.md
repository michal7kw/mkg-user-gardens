<h1>Ketosis</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Ketosis is a metabolic state in which the body shifts from using glucose as its primary fuel to utilizing <strong>ketone bodies</strong> (beta-hydroxybutyrate, acetoacetate, and acetone) derived from <strong>hepatic fatty acid oxidation</strong>. This physiological adaptation occurs during carbohydrate restriction, fasting, prolonged exercise, and in the neonatal period. In ketosis, blood beta-hydroxybutyrate levels typically range from 0.5-3.0 mmol/L (nutritional ketosis) to 3.0-5.0 mmol/L (deep ketosis with prolonged fasting).</p>
<p>The distinction between <strong>nutritional ketosis</strong> (safe, physiological) and <strong>diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)</strong> (dangerous, pathological) is critical. In nutritional ketosis, blood pH remains normal because the body produces modest amounts of ketones that are efficiently buffered and utilized by tissues. In DKA, absolute insulin deficiency causes uncontrolled lipolysis and ketone production (often &gt;10-25 mmol/L), overwhelming the buffering system and causing <strong>metabolic acidosis</strong> (pH &lt;7.3, bicarbonate &lt;18 mmol/L) -- a life-threatening emergency.</p>
<p>The <strong>ketogenic diet</strong> (very low carbohydrate, moderate protein, high fat) induces sustained nutritional ketosis and has demonstrated therapeutic efficacy for <strong>epilepsy</strong> (especially pediatric drug-resistant), and emerging evidence for <strong>weight management, type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome</strong>, and <strong>neurodegenerative conditions</strong> (Alzheimer's, Parkinson's; research-stage). Ketone bodies (especially beta-hydroxybutyrate) have signaling roles beyond energy: they inhibit histone deacetylases (HDACs), reduce NLRP3 inflammasome activation, and improve mitochondrial efficiency.</p>
<h2>Clinical Presentation</h2>
<p><strong>Nutritional ketosis:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Often asymptomatic</li>
<li>Fruity/acetone breath odor</li>
<li>Reduced appetite (ketones suppress ghrelin)</li>
<li>Initial fatigue, headache (&quot;keto flu&quot; during first 1-2 weeks of adaptation)</li>
<li>Improved mental clarity and sustained energy (after adaptation)</li>
<li>Increased thirst and urination (diuretic effect of glycogen depletion)</li>
<li>Constipation (reduced fiber intake)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Deep, rapid breathing (Kussmaul respirations; compensatory for metabolic acidosis)</li>
<li>Fruity breath odor</li>
<li>Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain</li>
<li>Altered mental status, lethargy</li>
<li>Severe dehydration</li>
<li>Hyperglycemia (&gt;250 mg/dL)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Diagnostic Information</h2>
<p><strong>Blood ketone testing:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Beta-hydroxybutyrate</strong> (preferred): nutritional ketosis 0.5-3.0 mmol/L; DKA &gt;3.0 mmol/L</li>
<li><strong>Urine ketones</strong> (dipstick): detects acetoacetate; less accurate than blood</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Blood gases (DKA):</strong> metabolic acidosis (pH &lt;7.30, HCO3 &lt;18 mEq/L, anion gap elevated)<br />
<strong>Glucose:</strong> normal or low in nutritional ketosis (70-100 mg/dL); elevated in DKA (&gt;250 mg/dL)<br />
<strong>Electrolytes:</strong> hypokalemia, hyponatremia (especially in DKA)</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Nutritional ketosis:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>No treatment needed (physiological state)</li>
<li>Maintain hydration and electrolyte balance (sodium, potassium, magnesium)</li>
<li>Monitor for symptoms of &quot;keto flu&quot; and address with electrolyte supplementation</li>
<li>If stopping ketogenic diet: gradual carbohydrate reintroduction to avoid GI distress</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Diabetic ketoacidosis (medical emergency):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>IV fluids</strong> (normal saline 1-1.5 L in first hour; then adjusted)</li>
<li><strong>IV insulin</strong> (0.1 units/kg/hr regular insulin; or 0.1 units/kg bolus then infusion)</li>
<li><strong>Potassium replacement</strong> (before insulin if K &lt;3.3 mEq/L)</li>
<li>Monitor glucose hourly, electrolytes q2-4h</li>
<li>Switch to dextrose-containing fluids when glucose &lt;200 mg/dL</li>
<li>Identify and treat precipitating cause (infection, missed insulin, new-onset T1DM)</li>
</ul>
