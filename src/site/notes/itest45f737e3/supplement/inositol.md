<h1>Inositol</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Inositol is a six-carbon carbocyclic sugar alcohol (a cyclitol) that exists as nine stereoisomers, of which myo-inositol is by far the most abundant in human tissue and D-chiro-inositol is the second most physiologically important. Although historically labelled &quot;vitamin B8,&quot; inositol is not a true vitamin because the body synthesizes it endogenously from glucose-6-phosphate; dietary sources (whole grains, legumes, citrus fruit, nuts) and gut microbial production supplement this pool. Its central biological role is as the structural backbone of the phosphatidylinositol signaling system, which transduces extracellular signals from insulin, follicle-stimulating hormone, thyroid-stimulating hormone, and a range of neurotransmitters into intracellular responses.</p>
<p>Clinically, inositol is most strongly associated with polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) and insulin resistance. Because PCOS involves a tissue-specific defect in inositol metabolism — a relative deficiency of D-chiro-inositol in muscle and an excess in the ovary — supplementation aims to restore the physiological balance, improving insulin sensitivity, ovulation, and androgen profiles. Inositol is generally regarded as a well-tolerated, low-cost alternative or adjunct to metformin in this setting. A secondary, older body of evidence explores high-dose inositol (12-18 g/day) for anxiety-spectrum and mood disorders, based on its interaction with the same phosphoinositide cycle that lithium is thought to deplete.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level:</strong> 4/5</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>Inositol's effects are mediated through the phosphoinositide second-messenger system and its insulin-sensitizing actions:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>IP3/DAG second messenger:</strong> Myo-inositol is phosphorylated and incorporated into membrane phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate (PIP2). Receptor-coupled phospholipase C cleaves PIP2 into inositol-1,4,5-trisphosphate (IP3) and diacylglycerol (DAG); IP3 triggers calcium release from the endoplasmic reticulum, propagating the signal for hormones such as insulin and gonadotropins.</li>
<li><strong>Insulin sensitization (myo- vs D-chiro):</strong> Inositol phosphoglycans act as putative second messengers of insulin. Myo-inositol predominantly facilitates cellular glucose uptake (GLUT4 translocation), whereas D-chiro-inositol promotes glycogen synthesis and modulates insulin-stimulated androgen production in the ovary. The 40:1 myo:D-chiro ratio used therapeutically reflects the physiological plasma ratio.</li>
<li><strong>Ovarian–metabolic axis:</strong> By improving insulin sensitivity, inositol lowers compensatory hyperinsulinemia, which reduces ovarian androgen output, raises sex hormone–binding globulin (SHBG), and helps restore ovulatory cycles.</li>
<li><strong>CNS phosphoinositide cycle:</strong> Inositol is the rate-limiting precursor for neuronal PIP2 resynthesis. The &quot;inositol depletion hypothesis&quot; holds that lithium and valproate exert mood-stabilizing effects partly by depleting inositol; conversely, exogenous inositol can modulate serotonergic (and other Gq-coupled) signaling, the rationale for its trials in panic, OCD, and depression.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Research Summary</h2>
<p>The strongest evidence is in PCOS. An umbrella review of meta-analyses of randomized controlled trials (Duan et al., 2026) concluded that inositol improves insulin resistance (lower HOMA-IR), reduces total and free testosterone, increases SHBG, and improves ovulation rates, with an effect size comparable to metformin and a markedly better gastrointestinal tolerability profile. A GRADE-assessed meta-analysis (Delavar et al., 2025) found favorable but heterogeneous effects on cardiometabolic and anthropometric indices. Direct comparative analyses (Russo et al., 2025) position myo-inositol as a reasonable first-line metabolic agent in PCOS.</p>
<p>Evidence for gestational diabetes prevention is genuinely mixed. While some smaller RCTs in high-risk women suggested benefit (Moini et al., 2025), the large multicenter JAMA randomized trial in PCOS pregnancies (van der Wel et al., 2025) found no significant reduction in composite pregnancy complications, tempering earlier enthusiasm. Psychiatric evidence — chiefly small, older double-blind trials at 12-18 g/day for panic disorder and OCD — is suggestive but underpowered, and inositol is not established as a standalone treatment (Concerto et al., 2023).</p>

<h3>Timing and Form</h3>
<ul>
<li>Take in two divided doses (e.g., morning and evening) to improve tolerability and maintain plasma levels.</li>
<li>The 40:1 myo:D-chiro-inositol combination is the best-studied PCOS formulation; very high D-chiro-inositol ratios may paradoxically worsen oocyte quality and are not recommended.</li>
<li>Powder dissolved in water is the most cost-effective form for higher doses; capsules suit the 2-4 g range.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Safety Information</h2>
<h3>Contraindications</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Bipolar disorder:</strong> High-dose inositol may precipitate hypomania or mania and should be avoided or used only under psychiatric supervision.</li>
<li><strong>Concurrent lithium therapy:</strong> Because lithium is thought to act by depleting inositol, supplementation may theoretically blunt its therapeutic effect.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Drug and Supplement Interactions</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Lithium:</strong> Potential pharmacodynamic antagonism (see above).</li>
<li><strong>Antidiabetic agents (e.g., metformin, insulin):</strong> Additive glucose-lowering effect; monitor for hypoglycemia when combined.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Side Effects</h3>
<p>Inositol is very well tolerated at metabolic doses (2-4 g/day). Gastrointestinal effects — nausea, gas, bloating, and dose-dependent diarrhea — are the main complaints and become common above roughly 12 g/day. Headache and dizziness are uncommon.</p>
<h3>Pregnancy and Lactation</h3>
<p>Myo-inositol has been studied in pregnancy for gestational diabetes prevention and appears safe, though efficacy is uncertain. Use during pregnancy should be guided by an obstetric provider.</p>
<h2>Quality Considerations</h2>
<ul>
<li>Prefer products specifying the <strong>stereoisomer and ratio</strong> (e.g., &quot;40:1 myo:D-chiro-inositol&quot;); unspecified &quot;inositol&quot; may be myo-inositol alone.</li>
<li>Choose third-party-tested supplements (NSF, USP, or ConsumerLab) to confirm label accuracy and purity.</li>
<li>Pharmaceutical-grade myo-inositol powder is inexpensive; be wary of proprietary blends that obscure the actual inositol dose.</li>
</ul>
