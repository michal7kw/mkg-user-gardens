<h1>Partial Thromboplastin Time</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT) is one of the foundational coagulation tests, measuring the time (in seconds) for citrated plasma to clot after the addition of a partial thromboplastin (phospholipid) plus a contact activator (kaolin, silica, celite, or ellagic acid). aPTT assesses the integrity of the <strong>intrinsic and common coagulation pathways</strong> -- specifically the activity of clotting factors XII, XI, IX, VIII (intrinsic), and X, V, II (prothrombin), fibrinogen (common). It is insensitive to factor VII deficiency (which only prolongs PT).</p>
<p>The clinical uses of aPTT fall into three broad categories: (1) <strong>monitoring unfractionated heparin (UFH) therapy</strong> -- the most common inpatient use, targeting a 1.5-2.5x prolongation of the upper limit of normal; (2) <strong>screening for inherited bleeding disorders</strong> -- hemophilia A (factor VIII deficiency), hemophilia B (factor IX deficiency, Christmas disease), von Willebrand disease (severe), and less common factor XI, XII, XI deficiencies; and (3) <strong>detecting inhibitors</strong> -- lupus anticoagulant (which prolongs aPTT in vitro paradoxically despite in vivo thrombotic risk), acquired factor VIII inhibitors, and heparin rebound after cardiac surgery.</p>
<p>aPTT's role has narrowed with the rise of low-molecular-weight heparins (LMWHs -- enoxaparin, dalteparin) that do not require routine aPTT monitoring, and direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs -- dabigatran variably prolongs aPTT, anti-Xa DOACs minimally). For UFH monitoring, anti-Xa assay is replacing aPTT as the preferred monitoring test in many institutions due to better correlation with heparin levels (especially in patients with elevated factor VIII -- e.g., inflammation, pregnancy -- which can spuriously shorten aPTT and lead to underdosing). However, aPTT remains the most widely available and cheapest heparin monitoring assay, and is the standard in most hospitals worldwide.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level: 5/5</strong> (foundational hematology test; universal clinical use; WHO essential laboratory test).</p>

<p><strong>Caveats:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Reference range is lab-specific (depends on aPTT reagent, phospholipid composition, analyzer)</li>
<li>aPTT is influenced by elevated factor VIII (inflammation, pregnancy, obesity) -- can spuriously shorten aPTT and mask therapeutic heparin effect (use anti-Xa in these settings)</li>
<li>Lupus anticoagulants can spuriously prolong aPTT (in vitro artifact) despite in vivo thrombosis risk</li>
<li>aPTT has limited correlation with heparin concentration in patients on high doses or with elevated factor VIII</li>
<li>DOACs (apixaban, rivaroxaban) minimally affect aPTT; dabigatran variably prolongs -- aPTT is not reliable for DOAC monitoring</li>
<li>Under-filled citrate tubes (high citrate-to-plasma ratio) falsely prolong aPTT</li>
<li>Heparin contamination from central line draws falsely prolongs aPTT</li>
</ul>

<h2>Factors Affecting Levels</h2>
<p><strong>Increase aPTT (prolong):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Unfractionated heparin (intended effect)</li>
<li>Hemophilia A (factor VIII deficiency)</li>
<li>Hemophilia B (factor IX deficiency)</li>
<li>Von Willebrand disease (severe)</li>
<li>Factor XI, XII deficiencies</li>
<li>Lupus anticoagulant (in vitro prolongation; in vivo thrombosis)</li>
<li>Acquired factor VIII inhibitors (postpartum, autoimmune, lymphoproliferative)</li>
<li>Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC)</li>
<li>Liver disease (hepatic synthetic dysfunction)</li>
<li>Vitamin K deficiency (also prolongs PT)</li>
<li>Direct thrombin inhibitors (argatroban, bivalirudin -- used in HIT)</li>
<li>Dabigatran (direct thrombin inhibitor DOAC)</li>
<li>Apixaban, rivaroxaban (anti-Xa DOACs; variably prolong)</li>
<li>Hypofibrinogenemia</li>
<li>Massive transfusion / dilutional coagulopathy</li>
<li>Heparin contamination from line draws (false prolongation)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Decrease aPTT (shorten):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Acute-phase reaction (elevated factor VIII) -- can mask therapeutic heparin effect</li>
<li>Thrombophilic state</li>
<li>Pregnancy (factor VIII elevation)</li>
<li>Obesity (chronic inflammation elevates factor VIII)</li>
<li>Early disseminated intravascular coagulation</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p><strong>Prolonged aPTT is associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Therapeutic heparin anticoagulation</strong> (intended): UFH at 1.5-2.5x ULN prevents VTE extension, treats acute MI, supports cardiac surgery, ECMO</li>
<li><strong>Hemophilia A and B:</strong> severe disease (factor &lt;1%) typically prolongs aPTT to &gt;60 s; mild disease may be borderline</li>
<li><strong>Von Willebrand disease (severe):</strong> low factor VIII secondarily prolongs aPTT</li>
<li><strong>Lupus anticoagulant:</strong> in vitro prolongation despite in vivo thrombosis risk (antiphospholipid syndrome)</li>
<li><strong>DIC:</strong> consumption of clotting factors</li>
<li><strong>Acquired factor VIII inhibitors:</strong> postpartum, autoimmune, lymphoproliferative</li>
<li><strong>Massive heparin:</strong> accidental overdose, post-CABG heparin rebound</li>
<li><strong>Hypofibrinogenemia:</strong> congenital or acquired</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Reduced aPTT is associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Thrombophilic states (factor VIII elevation)</li>
<li>Acute-phase reaction</li>
<li>Pregnancy</li>
<li>Obesity</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Monitoring use cases:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>UFH dose titration (acute VTE, acute MI, cardiac surgery, ECMO)</li>
<li>Hemophilia screening (followed by specific factor assays)</li>
<li>Lupus anticoagulant detection (followed by mixing study and LA panel)</li>
<li>DIC monitoring (paired with PT, fibrinogen, D-dimer)</li>
<li>Pre-operative coagulation screening (limited value in isolation)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Strategies to Manage</h2>
<p>aPTT is highly modifiable through heparin dose adjustment and treatment of underlying conditions:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>UFH initiation:</strong> weight-based nomogram (80 units/kg bolus, then 18 units/kg/hour infusion); check aPTT q6h until therapeutic, then q24h</li>
<li><strong>Therapeutic target:</strong> aPTT 1.5-2.5x ULN (institution-specific); alternatively target anti-Xa level 0.3-0.7 IU/mL (more reliable)</li>
<li><strong>Protamine sulfate reversal:</strong> 1 mg per 100 units of UFH (max 50 mg/dose) for bleeding or overdose; check aPTT 5-15 min after reversal</li>
<li><strong>Switch to LMWH or DOAC when stable:</strong> eliminates need for aPTT monitoring</li>
<li><strong>Hemophilia management:</strong> recombinant factor VIII (hemophilia A) or IX (hemophilia B) replacement; monitor with specific factor assays (not aPTT alone)</li>
<li><strong>Lupus anticoagulant:</strong> no treatment for the in vitro prolongation itself; treat antiphospholipid syndrome with lifelong warfarin (DOACs less effective per TRAPS trial)</li>
<li><strong>DIC management:</strong> treat underlying cause (sepsis, trauma, malignancy); supportive blood products (FFP, cryoprecipitate, platelets); do not generally use heparin unless thrombosis-dominant DIC</li>
<li><strong>Acquired factor VIII inhibitors:</strong> immunosuppression (corticosteroids, rituximab, cyclophosphamide); recombinant activated factor VII or FEIBA for acute bleeding</li>
<li><strong>Pre-operative optimization:</strong> correct coagulopathy before elective surgery (target aPTT within normal range)</li>
<li><strong>Heparin contamination workup:</strong> draw from peripheral stick rather than heparin-flushed central line; recheck if unexpectedly prolonged</li>
</ol>
