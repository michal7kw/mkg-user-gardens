<h1>NAD and the Biology of Aging Explained</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Aging isn't one thing — it's a set of cellular processes that drift over time. Several of them converge on a single, central molecule, <strong><a href="/garden/itest6dcac468/metabolite/nad/" class="internal-link">NAD+</a></strong>, and on a handful of nutrient-sensing switches the cell uses to decide between <em>growth</em> and <em>maintenance</em>. Understanding this circuit is the key to making sense of the entire longevity field — fasting, exercise, and the supplements everyone argues about.</p>
<h2>The Story</h2>
<p>You met <a href="/garden/itest6dcac468/metabolite/nad/" class="internal-link">NAD+</a> in the energy story: it shuttles electrons in the <a href="/garden/itest6dcac468/cellularcomponent/mitochondria/" class="internal-link">Mitochondria</a>, cycling between <a href="/garden/itest6dcac468/metabolite/nad/" class="internal-link">NAD+</a> and <a href="/garden/itest6dcac468/metabolite/nadh/" class="internal-link">NADH</a>. But NAD+ does a second job — it is <em>consumed</em> as fuel by repair enzymes, including the <strong>sirtuins</strong> (<a href="/garden/itest6dcac468/pathway/sirtuin-signaling-pathway/" class="internal-link">Sirtuin Signaling Pathway</a>), a family of proteins that tune metabolism, protect DNA, and switch on stress-resistance programs. The catch: NAD+ levels <em>fall with age</em>, and the cell must constantly remake it through the <a href="/garden/itest6dcac468/pathway/nad-salvage-pathway/" class="internal-link">NAD+ Salvage Pathway</a> (recycling) and the <a href="/garden/itest6dcac468/pathway/de-novo-nad-synthesis-pathway/" class="internal-link">De Novo NAD+ Synthesis Pathway</a> (building from scratch). Less NAD+ means less fuel for the very enzymes that keep cells healthy.</p>
<p>Layered on top are two opposing nutrient sensors. <strong><a href="/garden/itest6dcac468/pathway/mtor-pathway/" class="internal-link">mTOR Pathway</a></strong> is the &quot;grow and build&quot; signal — switched on by abundant food, especially protein. <strong><a href="/garden/itest6dcac468/pathway/ampk-pathway/" class="internal-link">AMPK Pathway</a></strong> is the &quot;conserve and repair&quot; signal — switched on by energy scarcity (fasting, exercise). When mTOR is high, cells grow; when AMPK is high, cells turn on <strong><a href="/garden/itest6dcac468/pathway/autophagy/" class="internal-link">Autophagy</a></strong> (via the <a href="/garden/itest6dcac468/pathway/autophagy-lysosomal-pathway/" class="internal-link">Autophagy-Lysosomal Pathway</a>), the recycling program that clears out damaged components. Healthy aging is largely about not leaving the &quot;grow&quot; switch jammed on — giving cells regular periods of repair.</p>
<p>The honest summary of the evidence: the <em>biology</em> is well established — NAD+ decline, sirtuins, mTOR/AMPK balance, and autophagy are genuine hallmarks of aging (PMID: 23746838). The <em>interventions</em> are less certain. Boosting NAD+ raises levels in humans, but proof of slowed aging is still emerging (PMID: 29514064).</p>
<h2>What This Means For You</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>The proven levers are behavioral.</strong> <a href="/garden/itest6dcac468/intervention/intermittent-fasting/" class="internal-link">Intermittent Fasting</a> and <a href="/garden/itest6dcac468/intervention/exercise/" class="internal-link">Exercise</a> both push the AMPK/autophagy side of the balance — these have the strongest evidence and cost nothing. They are the same levers from the metabolic and mitochondrial stories, which is not a coincidence.</li>
<li><strong>The supplements are promising but unproven.</strong> NAD+ precursors like <a href="/garden/itest6dcac468/supplement/nmn/" class="internal-link">NMN</a>, and sirtuin-related compounds like <a href="/garden/itest6dcac468/supplement/resveratrol/" class="internal-link">Resveratrol</a> and the autophagy-inducer <a href="/garden/itest6dcac468/supplement/spermidine/" class="internal-link">Spermidine</a>, have compelling cellular rationales and good safety records, but human longevity data are still thin. Treat them as experiments, not guarantees.</li>
<li><strong>Inflammation is the partner process.</strong> Chronic inflammation accelerates the same aging clock, which is why the inflammation story and this one share so much — calming inflammation and supporting repair pull in the same direction.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Key Takeaways</h2>
<ul>
<li>NAD+ powers both energy production and the sirtuin repair enzymes — and it falls with age.</li>
<li>mTOR (&quot;grow&quot;) and AMPK (&quot;repair&quot;) are opposing nutrient sensors; balance matters.</li>
<li>Autophagy is the cellular recycling program switched on by fasting and exercise.</li>
<li>The behavioral levers (fasting, exercise) are proven; the supplements remain promising-but-unproven.</li>
</ul>
