<h1>Myositis</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>The inflammatory myopathies are a heterogeneous group of acquired autoimmune diseases characterized by <strong>chronic skeletal muscle inflammation and progressive proximal muscle weakness</strong>. The major subtypes are:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Polymyositis (PM):</strong> T-cell mediated muscle destruction; no skin involvement</li>
<li><strong>Dermatomyositis (DM):</strong> humorally mediated microangiopathy with characteristic skin rash (heliotrope, Gottron papules) and increased cancer risk</li>
<li><strong>Immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy (IMNM):</strong> severe necrotizing muscle damage, often anti-SRP or anti-HMGCR positive; associated with statin use</li>
<li><strong>Inclusion body myositis (IBM):</strong> the most common inflammatory myopathy in older adults (&gt;50); slowly progressive; refractory to immunosuppression</li>
<li><strong>Anti-synthetase syndrome:</strong> anti-Jo-1 or other anti-tRNA synthetase antibodies with ILD, arthritis, mechanic's hands, fever, Raynaud's</li>
</ul>
<p>The diagnosis is established by the combination of <strong>proximal muscle weakness</strong>, <strong>elevated muscle enzymes</strong> (CK, aldolase, ALT, AST), <strong>myopathic EMG</strong>, <strong>muscle biopsy</strong> (the gold standard showing inflammatory infiltrates, necrosis, regeneration), and <strong>myositis-specific autoantibodies</strong> (anti-Jo-1, anti-Mi-2, anti-SRP, anti-HMGCR, anti-TIF1-gamma, anti-MDA5, anti-NXP2, anti-SAE).</p>
<p>Dermatomyositis carries an increased risk of <strong>malignancy</strong> (ovarian, breast, lung, gastric, colorectal, nasopharyngeal), warranting age-appropriate cancer screening and additional investigation based on autoantibody profile.</p>
<h2>Clinical Presentation</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Progressive proximal muscle weakness</strong> (weeks to months): difficulty climbing stairs, rising from a seated position, lifting objects overhead, combing hair</li>
<li><strong>Muscle pain</strong> and tenderness (more common in IMNM and infectious myositis)</li>
<li><strong>Dermatomyositis rash:</strong> heliotrope rash (violaceous periorbital), Gottron's papules (violaceous papules over MCP/IP joints), V-sign, shawl-sign, mechanic's hands</li>
<li><strong>Dysphagia</strong> (pharyngeal/cricopharyngeal involvement)</li>
<li><strong>Respiratory symptoms:</strong> interstitial lung disease (anti-synthetase, anti-MDA5), respiratory muscle weakness</li>
<li><strong>Raynaud's phenomenon</strong></li>
<li><strong>Systemic:</strong> fatigue, low-grade fever, weight loss</li>
</ul>
<h2>Diagnostic Information</h2>
<p><strong>Muscle enzymes:</strong> CK (markedly elevated, often &gt;10x ULN), aldolase, ALT, AST, LDH<br />
<strong>Autoantibodies:</strong> ANA, myositis panel (anti-Jo-1, Mi-2, SRP, HMGCR, TIF1-gamma, MDA5, NXP2, SAE, PL-7, PL-12, EJ, OJ)<br />
<strong>EMG:</strong> myopathic changes (short, small, polyphasic motor unit potentials; fibrillations)<br />
<strong>Muscle biopsy:</strong> gold standard; shows inflammatory infiltrates, necrosis, regeneration, fibrosis (patterns vary by subtype)<br />
<strong>MRI muscle:</strong> edema in affected muscles (useful for biopsy site selection)<br />
<strong>Cancer screening:</strong> age-appropriate + additional based on DM autoantibody profile</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>First-line:</strong> High-dose <strong>corticosteroids</strong> (prednisone 1 mg/kg/day; up to 1.5 mg/kg/day); taper slowly over months<br />
<strong>Steroid-sparing:</strong> methotrexate, azathioprine, mycophenolate mofetil (started early to reduce steroid exposure)<br />
<strong>IVIG:</strong> for severe or refractory disease; first-line for dermatomyositis (per EULAR/ACR 2017)<br />
<strong>Rituximab:</strong> for refractory myositis (especially anti-synthetase)<br />
<strong>Calcineurin inhibitors</strong> (tacrolimus, cyclosporine): for ILD-associated myositis<br />
<strong>Physical therapy:</strong> essential for maintaining strength and preventing contractures<br />
<strong>IBM:</strong> generally refractory to immunosuppression; focus on rehabilitation and swallowing therapy<br />
<strong>Cancer search:</strong> mandatory in dermatomyositis; if found, treatment of malignancy often improves myositis</p>
