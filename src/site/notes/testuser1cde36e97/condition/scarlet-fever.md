<h1>Scarlet Fever</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Scarlet fever is a manifestation of <strong>Group A Streptococcus (GAS) pharyngitis</strong> in which the infecting strain produces <strong>erythrogenic exotoxins</strong> (SpeA, SpeB, SpeC). These superantigenic toxins cause the characteristic <strong>diffuse erythematous sandpaper-like rash</strong> (scarlatiniform rash) that distinguishes scarlet fever from simple strep throat. The illness primarily affects children aged <strong>5-15 years</strong> and typically occurs in winter and spring.</p>
<p>The rash begins on the <strong>neck and chest</strong>, then spreads to the trunk and extremities, sparing the face (which appears flushed with <strong>perioral pallor</strong>). In skin folds (axillae, groin, antecubital fossae), the rash may appear as linear petechiae known as <strong>Pastia's lines</strong>. The tongue develops a white coating through which red papillae project (<strong>white strawberry tongue</strong>), which later desquamates to reveal a beefy red appearance (<strong>red strawberry tongue</strong>). After the rash fades (usually within 5-7 days), <strong>desquamation</strong> (peeling) of the hands and feet occurs, especially at the fingertips.</p>
<p>Scarlet fever is clinically diagnosed by the combination of GAS pharyngitis symptoms (sore throat, fever, tonsillar exudate, cervical lymphadenopathy) and the characteristic rash. Throat swab for rapid antigen testing or culture confirms GAS. Treatment with <strong>penicillin</strong> (10 days) resolves the infection and prevents the critical complications: <strong>acute rheumatic fever</strong> (which causes permanent heart valve damage) and <strong>post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis</strong>.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Penicillin V</strong> 250 mg PO QID (children) or 500 mg PO BID-QID (adults) x 10 days</li>
<li><strong>Amoxicillin</strong> 50 mg/kg/day (max 1000 mg) divided BID x 10 days (preferred for children)</li>
<li><strong>Azithromycin</strong> (penicillin-allergic): 12 mg/kg day 1 then 6 mg/kg days 2-5</li>
<li><strong>Clindamycin</strong> 7 mg/kg TID x 10 days (penicillin-allergic)</li>
<li>Single-dose option: <strong>Benzathine penicillin G</strong> 1.2 million units IM (&gt;27 kg) or 600,000 units (&lt;27 kg)</li>
<li><strong>Symptomatic:</strong> acetaminophen or ibuprofen for fever/pain; hydration; soft diet</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Return to school:</strong> after 24 hours of antibiotics and afebrile</p>
