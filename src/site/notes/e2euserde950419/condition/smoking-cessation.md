<h1>Smoking Cessation</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Smoking cessation is the process of discontinuing tobacco smoking. It is widely regarded as the <strong>single most impactful lifestyle intervention</strong> in medicine, providing greater reduction in all-cause mortality than any other behavioral change. Tobacco use remains the <strong>leading preventable cause of death</strong> globally, responsible for ~8 million deaths annually (WHO).</p>
<p><strong>Health benefits of quitting (timeline):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>20 minutes:</strong> heart rate and blood pressure begin to drop</li>
<li><strong>12 hours:</strong> blood carbon monoxide level normalizes</li>
<li><strong>48 hours:</strong> nerve endings recover; smell and taste improve</li>
<li><strong>2-12 weeks:</strong> circulation and lung function improve</li>
<li><strong>1-9 months:</strong> cough and shortness of breath decrease</li>
<li><strong>1 year:</strong> excess risk of coronary heart disease is <strong>half</strong> that of a smoker</li>
<li><strong>5 years:</strong> stroke risk falls to that of a non-smoker</li>
<li><strong>10 years:</strong> lung cancer death rate is <strong>half</strong> that of a smoker</li>
<li><strong>15 years:</strong> risk of coronary heart disease is that of a non-smoker</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Nicotine dependence</strong> is a chronic relapsing disorder driven by neuroadaptation in the brain's reward pathways (mesolimbic dopamine system). The <strong>Fagerström Test for Nicotine Dependence (FTND)</strong> quantifies addiction severity (score 0-10; &gt;=5 = moderate-high dependence). Most smokers require <strong>multiple quit attempts</strong> (average 5-7) before achieving long-term abstinence.</p>
<p><strong>The 5 A's clinical framework:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Ask</strong> about tobacco use at every visit</li>
<li><strong>Advise</strong> all tobacco users to quit</li>
<li><strong>Assess</strong> willingness to make a quit attempt</li>
<li><strong>Assist</strong> with counseling and pharmacotherapy</li>
<li><strong>Arrange</strong> follow-up</li>
</ol>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Pharmacotherapy (all are effective; combination therapy is most effective):</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>
<p><strong>Nicotine Replacement Therapy (NRT):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Nicotine patch</strong> 21 mg/24h (or 15 mg/16h) for heavy smokers; taper every 2-4 weeks (21 mg -&gt; 14 mg -&gt; 7 mg)</li>
<li><strong>Short-acting NRT</strong> (for breakthrough cravings): nicotine gum 2-4 mg, nicotine lozenge 2-4 mg, nicotine nasal spray, nicotine inhaler</li>
<li><strong>Combination NRT</strong> (patch + short-acting) is <strong>most effective</strong>: 1-year abstinence ~25-35% vs ~10-15% for single NRT</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Varenicline (Chantix/Champix):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Partial agonist at alpha-4 beta-2 nicotinic receptors: reduces cravings AND blocks rewarding effects of smoking</li>
<li><strong>Most effective monotherapy</strong> (1-year abstinence ~25-33%)</li>
<li>Dosing: 0.5 mg daily x 3 days -&gt; 0.5 mg BID x 4 days -&gt; 1 mg BID x 12 weeks (can extend to 24 weeks)</li>
<li><strong>Start 1 week before quit date</strong> (can also use &quot;gradual reduction&quot; approach)</li>
<li>Safety: FDA removed black box warning (2016 EAGLES trial showed no significant increase in neuropsychiatric adverse events)</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Bupropion (Zyban/Wellbutrin SR):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Dopamine and norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor; reduces cravings</li>
<li>Dosing: 150 mg daily x 3 days -&gt; 150 mg BID x 7-12 weeks (start 1-2 weeks before quit date)</li>
<li>Avoid: seizure disorder, eating disorders, MAOI use, abrupt alcohol/benzo withdrawal</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Behavioral interventions:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Brief office counseling</strong> (even 3 minutes increases quit rates)</li>
<li><strong>Quitline</strong> (1-800-QUIT-NOW; telephone counseling; free)</li>
<li><strong>Group cessation programs</strong> (American Lung Association, American Cancer Society)</li>
<li><strong>Cognitive behavioral therapy</strong> (identifies triggers; develops coping strategies)</li>
<li><strong>Mobile apps</strong> and <strong>SMS text support</strong> (National Texting Portal)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Combination therapy (most effective):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Varenicline + behavioral counseling</strong> (highest success rate; ~33% 1-year abstinence)</li>
<li><strong>Combination NRT + behavioral counseling</strong></li>
<li><strong>Varenicline + NRT</strong> (emerging evidence; may be superior to varenicline alone)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Managing weight gain:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Average 4-5 kg weight gain in first year after quitting</li>
<li>Bupropion and NRT modestly reduce weight gain</li>
<li>Encourage exercise and healthy diet during quit attempt</li>
<li>Frame weight gain as expected and manageable; the health benefits of quitting far outweigh the risks of modest weight gain</li>
</ul>
