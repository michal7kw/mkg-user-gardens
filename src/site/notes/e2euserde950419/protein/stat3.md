<h1>STAT3</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>STAT3 encodes Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription 3 (UniProt P40763), a member of the STAT family of latent cytoplasmic transcription factors. STAT3 sits at the core of the JAK-STAT signaling pathway and serves as a key relay that converts extracellular cytokine and growth-factor signals into changes in gene expression. It is a multidomain protein containing an SH2 domain that mediates receptor docking and dimerization and a C-terminal transactivation domain.</p>
<p>In the resting state STAT3 resides inactive in the cytoplasm. Upon stimulation — classically by interleukin-6 family cytokines, and also by growth-factor receptors such as EGFR — receptor-associated Janus kinases phosphorylate a conserved tyrosine on STAT3. Phosphorylated STAT3 dimerizes via reciprocal SH2-phosphotyrosine interactions, translocates to the nucleus, and binds specific DNA elements to drive transcription of genes controlling proliferation, survival, angiogenesis, and immune regulation.</p>
<p>STAT3 has a dual role in human disease. It is essential for normal immunity and tissue homeostasis, yet its persistent, constitutive activation is one of the most commonly observed signaling abnormalities across solid and hematologic cancers, where it promotes tumor-cell survival, immune evasion, and an inflammatory microenvironment. Germline STAT3 variants, conversely, cause defined immunodeficiency and autoimmune syndromes.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>STAT3 activation follows the canonical JAK-STAT cascade. Ligand binding (for example, by interleukin-6 engaging the IL-6 receptor/gp130 complex, or by EGFR ligands) clusters receptors and activates associated tyrosine kinases, which phosphorylate STAT3 on its critical tyrosine residue. The resulting phosphotyrosine is bound by the SH2 domain of a second STAT3 molecule, forming an active dimer that enters the nucleus and engages STAT-binding elements in target-gene promoters. The amplitude and duration of this signal are normally restrained by feedback inhibitors and phosphatases; loss of this control underlies pathological, sustained STAT3 activity.</p>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>Constitutive STAT3 activation is a hallmark of many cancers and supports proliferation, anti-apoptotic survival, angiogenesis, and suppression of anti-tumor immunity, making the JAK-STAT3 axis an actively pursued therapeutic target. In dermatology and immunology, dysregulated STAT3-dependent signaling contributes to inflammatory disease such as psoriasis, a basis for JAK-inhibitor and related anti-cytokine therapies. Germline STAT3 mutations have opposing clinical consequences: dominant-negative loss-of-function variants cause autosomal-dominant hyper-IgE (Job) syndrome with recurrent infections and connective-tissue features, whereas gain-of-function variants produce early-onset multi-organ autoimmunity.</p>
