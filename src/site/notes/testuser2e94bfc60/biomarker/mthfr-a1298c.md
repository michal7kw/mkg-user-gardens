<h1>Mthfr A1298C</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>The MTHFR A1298C variant (rs1801131) is a single-nucleotide polymorphism in the methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase (MTHFR) gene on chromosome 1p36.3, resulting in a glutamate-to-alanine substitution at position 429 of the protein. MTHFR catalyzes the irreversible reduction of 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate to 5-methyltetrahydrofolate (5-MTHF) -- the principal circulating form of folate and the methyl donor for the remethylation of homocysteine to methionine via methionine synthase (requires vitamin B12 as cofactor). Methionine is then converted to S-adenosylmethionine (SAM), the universal methyl donor for DNA, RNA, proteins, neurotransmitters, and phospholipids.</p>
<p>The A1298C variant is located in the <strong>regulatory domain</strong> of MTHFR (the SAM binding site), distinguishing it mechanistically from the C677T variant (located in the catalytic domain). Functionally, A1298C primarily affects <strong>tetrahydrobiopterin (BH4) regeneration</strong> and downstream pathways rather than homocysteine remethylation. BH4 is an essential cofactor for: phenylalanine hydroxylase, tyrosine hydroxylase, and tryptophan hydroxylase (enzymes required for dopamine, norepinephrine, serotonin synthesis), and all three nitric oxide synthase (NOS) isoforms. This connection to the BH4-neurotransmitter-NO pathway explains why A1298C has been variably associated with <strong>neuropsychiatric conditions</strong> (depression, anxiety, ADHD, autism) and <strong>vascular dysfunction</strong> (uncoupled NOS produces superoxide instead of nitric oxide, contributing to oxidative stress).</p>
<p>The <strong>CC homozygous variant</strong> causes only modest reduction in MTHFR activity (~60-70% of normal), which is <strong>less severe</strong> than the C677T TT genotype (~30%). The A1298C variant alone rarely causes significant hyperhomocysteinemia. However, <strong>compound heterozygosity</strong> (one C677T allele + one A1298C allele) produces a biochemical phenotype similar to C677T homozygosity, with reduced enzyme activity (~40-50%) and elevated homocysteine potential.</p>
<p>Clinical testing for MTHFR variants is controversial. The American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) and American Congress of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) issued guidance against routine MTHFR testing because: (1) variants are common (40-50% population prevalence for C677T); (2) clinical actionability is limited; (3) homocysteine measurement provides equivalent or superior information. However, MTHFR testing remains popular in functional medicine and nutrigenomic contexts, where the result may guide methylfolate vs. folic acid supplementation choices.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level: 3/5</strong> (well-characterized mechanistically; clinical association evidence mixed; not in mainstream guidelines).</p>

<h2>Factors Affecting Clinical Expression</h2>
<p>The A1298C variant is a <strong>genetic marker</strong> and does not change over time. However, clinical expression is heavily modulated by:</p>
<p><strong>Aggravating factors (worsen biochemical phenotype in CC genotype):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Low folate intake (dietary or supplemental)</li>
<li>Low vitamin B12 status</li>
<li>High methionine / animal protein intake</li>
<li>Alcohol consumption (depletes folate)</li>
<li>Smoking (oxidative stress; depletes B vitamins)</li>
<li>Chronic illness (increases folate utilization)</li>
<li>Pregnancy (increased folate demand)</li>
<li>Certain medications: methotrexate, phenytoin, trimethoprim, sulfasalazine, oral contraceptives</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Mitigating factors (normalize phenotype even in CC):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Adequate folate intake (&gt;400 mcg/day)</li>
<li>Adequate vitamin B12 status</li>
<li>Supplementation with 5-MTHF (methylfolate) instead of synthetic folic acid</li>
<li>Adequate betaine (TMG), choline</li>
<li>Adequate riboflavin (B2; MTHFR cofactor)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p><strong>A1298C CC genotype has been associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Neuropsychiatric symptoms (mixed evidence):</strong> depression, anxiety, ADHD, autism spectrum (effect sizes small; many negative studies)</li>
<li><strong>Vascular dysfunction:</strong> endothelial dysfunction via NOS uncoupling (mechanistic evidence; small clinical effect)</li>
<li><strong>Migraine</strong> (mixed evidence; meta-analyses show small effect)</li>
<li><strong>Pregnancy complications:</strong> recurrent pregnancy loss, pre-eclampsia (mixed evidence)</li>
<li><strong>Elevated homocysteine:</strong> ONLY in compound heterozygotes (C677T + A1298C) or in CC with low folate</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Compound heterozygosity (C677T + A1298C) has been associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Elevated homocysteine (especially with low folate)</li>
<li>Increased neural tube defect risk (in offspring of carrier mothers)</li>
<li>Modestly increased cardiovascular risk</li>
<li>Reduced conversion of folic acid to active 5-MTHF</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Limitations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>ACMG and ACOG recommend against routine MTHFR testing</strong> because: (a) variants are too common to be highly informative; (b) homocysteine level provides equivalent or superior clinical information; (c) clinical actionability is limited (folate supplementation recommended regardless of genotype)</li>
<li><strong>Effect sizes are small</strong> for most associations; meta-analyses show inconsistent results</li>
<li><strong>Folate fortification</strong> (US since 1998) has substantially reduced clinical impact of MTHFR variants</li>
<li><strong>Functional medicine popularity</strong> despite guideline recommendations against</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Monitoring use cases:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Functional medicine / nutrigenomic assessment (controversial)</li>
<li>Research: gene-nutrient interaction studies</li>
<li>Recurrent pregnancy loss workup (some clinicians)</li>
<li>Autism spectrum disorder research (some clinicians)</li>
<li>Compound heterozygosity investigation in patients with elevated homocysteine of unclear cause</li>
</ul>
<h2>Strategies to Manage</h2>
<p>MTHFR genotype cannot be changed, but its biochemical consequences are highly modifiable through nutrition:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Methylfolate (5-MTHF) supplementation</strong> 400-1000 mcg daily -- preferred over synthetic folic acid for variant carriers (especially CC or compound heterozygotes). 5-MTHF bypasses the MTHFR enzyme entirely.</li>
<li><strong>Vitamin B12 (methylcobalamin)</strong> 500-1000 mcg daily -- essential co-substrate for methionine synthase; helps normalize homocysteine</li>
<li><strong>Betaine (trimethylglycine, TMG)</strong> 500-1000 mg daily -- alternative pathway for homocysteine remethylation (via BHMT in liver and kidney)</li>
<li><strong>Vitamin B6 (P5P form)</strong> 50-100 mg daily -- cofactor for cystathionine beta-synthase (transsulfuration pathway that catabolizes homocysteine)</li>
<li><strong>Riboflavin (B2)</strong> 5-25 mg daily -- MTHFR cofactor; may help stabilize variant enzyme</li>
<li><strong>Diet rich in natural folate:</strong> dark leafy greens, legumes, asparagus, avocado, Brussels sprouts</li>
<li><strong>Avoid excessive synthetic folic acid</strong> in variant carriers (&gt;1000 mcg/day) -- unmetabolized folic acid (UMFA) may accumulate with unknown effects</li>
<li><strong>Homocysteine monitoring</strong> in compound heterozygotes or CC with cardiovascular/pregnancy risk -- target &lt;10 umol/L (optimal &lt;8)</li>
<li><strong>Pre-conception supplementation</strong> for women with variant planning pregnancy -- methylfolate 400-800 mcg daily</li>
<li><strong>Alcohol moderation</strong> (depletes folate)</li>
<li><strong>Smoking cessation</strong></li>
<li><strong>Address comorbidities</strong> that raise homocysteine: hypothyroidism, renal insufficiency, certain medications</li>
</ol>
