<h1>Cystatin C</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Cystatin C is a 13 kDa cysteine protease inhibitor produced at a remarkably constant rate by all nucleated cells in the body. It is a member of the cystatin superfamily of protease inhibitors and is encoded by the CST3 gene on chromosome 20p11.21. The protein is freely filtered by the renal glomeruli (due to its small size and low isoelectric point), and then nearly completely reabsorbed and catabolized by the proximal tubular cells. As a result, serum cystatin C concentration is primarily determined by the glomerular filtration rate (GFR), making it an excellent endogenous filtration marker.</p>
<p>Unlike serum creatinine (the traditional GFR marker), cystatin C is <strong>independent of muscle mass, sex, age, race, and diet</strong> -- the principal confounders of creatinine-based GFR estimates. This makes cystatin C particularly valuable for: (1) <strong>elderly patients</strong> (reduced muscle mass causes creatinine to underestimate GFR); (2) <strong>patients with cirrhosis or severe liver disease</strong> (reduced creatinine production); (3) <strong>amputees and patients with neuromuscular disease</strong> (reduced muscle mass); (4) <strong>vegetarians</strong> (low creatinine production from low meat intake); (5) <strong>patients with near-normal GFR</strong> (where creatinine is least sensitive); (6) <strong>detection of early acute kidney injury</strong> (cystatin C rises 1-2 days earlier than creatinine).</p>
<p>The 2021 and 2024 KDIGO (Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes) guidelines recommend cystatin C as the preferred GFR marker in many contexts. The CKD-EPI 2021 cystatin C equation and the CKD-EPI 2021 creatinine-cystatin C combined equation provide more accurate GFR estimates than creatinine alone. A specific advantage is that cystatin C-based eGFRcys avoids the race coefficient that was removed from creatinine equations in 2021 (a major equity consideration).</p>
<p>Beyond its role as a GFR marker, cystatin C is an <strong>independent predictor of cardiovascular risk</strong> -- even after adjustment for traditional risk factors and creatinine-based GFR. Elevated cystatin C predicts incident heart failure, stroke, and all-cause mortality, suggesting it reflects vascular endothelial dysfunction or systemic inflammation beyond its renal role.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level: 5/5</strong> (KDIGO-recommended; universal clinical use; FDA-cleared assays).</p>

<p><strong>Caveats:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Reference range is assay-specific (nephelometry, turbidimetry, PENIA)</li>
<li>Renal function is calculated using CKD-EPI 2021 equations (eGFRcys or eGFRcr-cys)</li>
<li>Cystatin C is an acute-phase reactant (less so than creatinine); inflammation modestly elevates</li>
<li>Corticosteroid therapy and hyperthyroidism can increase production</li>
<li>Pregnancy: cystatin C rises physiologically in third trimester</li>
<li>Children: levels higher in neonates (immature kidney function)</li>
</ul>

<h2>Factors Affecting Levels</h2>
<p><strong>Increase cystatin C (reduced GFR or increased production):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Reduced GFR (any cause): CKD, AKI, glomerulonephritis, diabetic nephropathy, hypertensive nephrosclerosis</li>
<li>Corticosteroid therapy (increases production)</li>
<li>Hyperthyroidism (increases production)</li>
<li>Severe systemic inflammation (acute-phase response)</li>
<li>Malignancy (some tumors increase production)</li>
<li>Smoking (modestly elevates)</li>
<li>Older age (renal function declines; cystatin C rises)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Decrease cystatin C (rare; usually physiologic):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Hyperfiltration in early diabetic nephropathy</li>
<li>Hypothyroidism (decreases production)</li>
<li>Pregnancy (third trimester; physiologic)</li>
<li>Generally not clinically concerning</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p><strong>Elevated cystatin C is associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Chronic kidney disease (CKD):</strong> cystatin C detects CKD earlier than creatinine, especially in early stage 3a (eGFR 45-60) where creatinine may still be normal</li>
<li><strong>Acute kidney injury (AKI):</strong> cystatin C rises 1-2 days earlier than creatinine in AKI (better for early detection in ICU, post-surgery, post-contrast)</li>
<li><strong>Cardiovascular risk:</strong> cystatin C independently predicts incident heart failure, stroke, and all-cause mortality (after adjustment for traditional risk factors and creatinine)</li>
<li><strong>Drug dosing:</strong> accurate GFR is essential for dosing nephrotoxic drugs (chemotherapy, antibiotics, DOACs); cystatin C-based eGFR improves dosing accuracy</li>
<li><strong>Pre-contrast imaging:</strong> accurate GFR before iodinated or gadolinium-based contrast administration</li>
<li><strong>Living kidney donor evaluation:</strong> cystatin C adds confidence to donor clearance</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Limitations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Cost</strong> higher than creatinine (may limit routine use)</li>
<li><strong>Standardization</strong> improved since 2010 (ERMS-DA468 reference material); inter-lab variability now acceptable</li>
<li><strong>Acute-phase reactant</strong> (modestly) -- inflammation can falsely elevate</li>
<li><strong>Corticosteroid therapy</strong> raises production independent of GFR</li>
<li><strong>Hyperthyroidism</strong> raises production</li>
<li>Not yet universal insurance coverage (Medicare covers for specific indications)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Monitoring use cases:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Baseline kidney function assessment (especially in elderly, cirrhosis, amputees, neuromuscular disease)</li>
<li>CKD diagnosis and staging (with CKD-EPI 2021 equations)</li>
<li>AKI early detection (ICU, post-surgery, post-contrast, nephrotoxic drugs)</li>
<li>Drug dosing for renally-cleared medications</li>
<li>Cardiovascular risk stratification (beyond traditional risk factors)</li>
<li>Chemotherapy monitoring (cisplatin, carboplatin, methotrexate)</li>
<li>Pre-transplant evaluation</li>
</ul>
<h2>Strategies to Manage</h2>
<p>Cystatin C reflects kidney function and responds to interventions that improve GFR:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Treat underlying kidney disease:</strong>
<ul>
<li>Diabetic nephropathy: optimize glycemic control (HbA1c &lt;7%), SGLT2 inhibitors, ACE inhibitors/ARBs</li>
<li>Hypertensive nephrosclerosis: tight BP control (&lt;130/80), ACE inhibitors/ARBs preferred</li>
<li>Glomerulonephritis: immunosuppression per disease-specific protocols</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>SGLT2 inhibitors</strong> (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin) -- slow CKD progression regardless of diabetes status (DAPA-CKD, EMPA-KIDNEY trials)</li>
<li><strong>ACE inhibitors or ARBs</strong> -- first-line for proteinuric CKD; reduce intraglomerular pressure</li>
<li><strong>Avoid nephrotoxic drugs</strong> when possible: NSAIDs (chronic), aminoglycosides, iodinated contrast (pre-medicate with IV fluids), lithium, certain chemotherapeutics</li>
<li><strong>Adequate hydration</strong> -- avoid volume depletion</li>
<li><strong>Weight loss</strong> in obese patients -- reduces hyperfiltration injury</li>
<li><strong>Blood pressure control</strong> to &lt;130/80 (KDIGO 2021)</li>
<li><strong>Glycemic control</strong> (HbA1c &lt;7% in diabetics) -- reduces diabetic nephropathy progression</li>
<li><strong>Smoking cessation</strong> -- smoking accelerates CKD progression</li>
<li><strong>Cardiovascular risk factor management</strong> -- CKD patients have elevated CV risk; manage lipids, BP, glucose aggressively</li>
<li><strong>Adjust drug doses</strong> based on cystatin C-based eGFR (more accurate than creatinine-based)</li>
</ol>
