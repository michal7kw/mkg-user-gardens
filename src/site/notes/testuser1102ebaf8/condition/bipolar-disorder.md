<h1>Bipolar Disorder</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Bipolar disorder is a chronic, episodic mood disorder defined by the occurrence of mania or hypomania, usually alternating with episodes of depression. Bipolar I requires at least one full manic episode; bipolar II is defined by hypomania plus recurrent depression. Between episodes many patients have residual mood instability and cognitive symptoms, and the illness carries one of the highest suicide risks in medicine.</p>
<p>Its biology is multifactorial: high polygenic heritability (with substantial genetic overlap with schizophrenia), dysregulation of dopaminergic and other monoamine systems, mitochondrial and bioenergetic abnormalities, circadian-rhythm disruption, and impaired neuroplasticity (reduced <a href="/garden/testuser1102ebaf8/biomarker/bdnf/" class="internal-link">BDNF</a>/<a href="/garden/testuser1102ebaf8/pathway/bdnf-trkb-signaling-pathway/" class="internal-link">BDNF-TrkB Signaling Pathway</a> signaling). <a href="/garden/testuser1102ebaf8/drug/lithium/" class="internal-link">Lithium</a> — the prototypical mood stabilizer — acts in part through GSK-3 inhibition and neurotrophic/neuroprotective effects, and uniquely lowers suicide risk.</p>
<h2>Clinical Presentation</h2>
<h3>Symptoms</h3>
<p>Manic episodes feature elevated/irritable mood, grandiosity, reduced need for sleep, pressured speech, and impulsivity; depressive episodes mirror major depression. Sleep and circadian disruption frequently precede and trigger episodes.</p>
<h2>Diagnostic Information</h2>
<p>Clinical diagnosis (DSM-5/ICD-11) based on episode history; distinguishing bipolar depression from unipolar <a href="/garden/testuser1102ebaf8/condition/depression/" class="internal-link">Depression</a> is critical because antidepressant monotherapy can precipitate mania. No diagnostic biomarker exists.</p>
<h2>Risk Factors</h2>
<p>High heritability and family history dominate; childhood adversity, substance use, and circadian disruption contribute.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p>Lithium is foundational (mood-stabilizing and anti-suicidal); anticonvulsants (valproate, lamotrigine) and atypical antipsychotics (e.g., <a href="/garden/testuser1102ebaf8/drug/quetiapine/" class="internal-link">Quetiapine</a>) are widely used; psychoeducation and chronotherapy support stability.</p>
<h2>Complications</h2>
<p>Suicide, substance use disorders, cognitive and functional impairment, and elevated cardiometabolic risk.</p>
