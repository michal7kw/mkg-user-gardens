<h1>CA 19-9</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>CA 19-9 (Carbohydrate Antigen 19-9, also called sialyl-Lewis-a) is a glycan epitope carried on mucins and shed into the blood by gastrointestinal epithelial cancers. It is measured in serum from a routine venipuncture sample by immunoassay, requiring no fasting. Notably, individuals with a Lewis-negative blood group genotype (roughly 5-10% of people) cannot synthesize the antigen and will show falsely low or undetectable levels regardless of disease.</p>
<p>CA 19-9 is ordered mainly to monitor treatment response and recurrence in pancreatic adenocarcinoma and other gastrointestinal malignancies, and to help assess prognosis. As with all tumor markers, it is a monitoring and adjunctive aid rather than a standalone diagnostic: it is not recommended for screening asymptomatic people because benign conditions frequently elevate it, and results must be interpreted with imaging and the overall clinical context.</p>

<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>Marked CA 19-9 elevations are associated with pancreatic, biliary, gastric, and colorectal cancers, and rising values during follow-up suggest progression or recurrence. Importantly, many benign conditions raise CA 19-9 — including pancreatitis, cholangitis, biliary obstruction, cirrhosis, and diabetes — so elevations are not specific for cancer, and obstructive jaundice in particular can produce very high values that fall after biliary drainage. Lewis-negative patients may have undetectable levels despite active disease, limiting the test's negative predictive value.</p>
