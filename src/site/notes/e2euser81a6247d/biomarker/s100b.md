<h1>S100B</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>S100B is a 21 kDa homodimeric calcium-binding protein of the S100 family (named for being <strong>s</strong>oluble in <strong>100%</strong> ammonium sulfate at neutral pH). It is expressed predominantly by astrocytes, Schwann cells, melanocytes, adipocytes, chondrocytes, and certain dendritic cells. In the central nervous system, astrocytic S100B functions as both an intracellular calcium-sensor (regulating cytoskeletal dynamics, cell-cycle progression, and energy metabolism) and a secreted cytokine-like signal. Blood-brain barrier disruption or astrocyte injury releases S100B into the bloodstream, where it is readily measurable with sensitive immunoassays.</p>
<p>Clinically, S100B has two main uses. First, as a rapid biomarker of brain injury: serum S100B measured within 3-6 hours of head trauma has excellent negative predictive value (96-99%) for clinically important intracranial injury, supporting its use in triaging which patients can avoid CT scanning (per Scandinavian and Italian guidelines). Second, as a tumor marker for malignant melanoma (melanocytes also express S100B), where it correlates with disease stage, treatment response, and prognosis. S100B's lack of brain-specificity is its key limitation -- extracranial trauma (especially multi-system trauma with fat or muscle injury) can yield false-positive elevations.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level: 4/5</strong> (robust validation in TBI triage; well-characterized melanoma marker).</p>

<p><strong>Caveats:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Reference range is assay-dependent (Elecsys, LIAISON, ELISA)</li>
<li>CSF S100B reference range is higher than serum</li>
<li>Children have higher physiological S100B (newborns can be 2-4x adult baseline)</li>
<li>Pregnancy, strenuous exercise, and marathon running can transiently elevate S100B</li>
<li>Hemolysis falsely lowers S100B (erythrocytes contain inhibitors)</li>
</ul>

<h2>Factors Affecting Levels</h2>
<p><strong>Increase S100B:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Traumatic brain injury (mild to severe)</li>
<li>Stroke (ischemic and hemorrhagic)</li>
<li>Subarachnoid hemorrhage</li>
<li>Cardiac arrest with neurological injury (post-CPR encephalopathy)</li>
<li>Malignant melanoma (especially stage III-IV)</li>
<li>Severe hepatic failure (impaired clearance)</li>
<li>Renal failure (impaired clearance)</li>
<li>Strenuous exercise (transient; returns to baseline within hours)</li>
<li>Extracranial trauma (especially fat/muscle injury; key false-positive source)</li>
<li>Children (physiologically higher; newborns highest)</li>
<li>Pregnancy (third trimester)</li>
<li>Marathons / ultra-endurance events</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Decrease S100B:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Treatment response in melanoma (not applicable to brain injury -- baseline is optimal)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p><strong>Elevated levels are associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Mild traumatic brain injury (concussion):</strong> Scandinavian guidelines use serum S100B &lt;0.10 mcg/L measured within 6 h of injury to identify low-risk patients who can avoid head CT (NPV ~99% for clinically important injury)</li>
<li><strong>Severe TBI with intracranial lesion:</strong> levels correlate with injury severity, CT findings, and 6-month Glasgow Outcome Scale</li>
<li><strong>Stroke:</strong> S100B peaks 24-72 h after ischemic stroke; correlates with infarct volume and functional outcome</li>
<li><strong>Cardiac arrest post-ROSC:</strong> elevated S100B at 24-48 h predicts poor neurological outcome</li>
<li><strong>Malignant melanoma:</strong> serum S100B &gt;0.13 mcg/L is a Stage III/IV tumor marker; correlates with disease burden, treatment response, and overall survival</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Limitations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Lack of brain-specificity:</strong> melanocytes, adipocytes, and chondrocytes also release S100B; multi-system trauma can yield false positives</li>
<li>Cannot distinguish between structural brain injury and blood-brain barrier disruption alone</li>
<li>Rapid renal/hepatic clearance means S100B has a narrow diagnostic window (~3-6 h post-TBI)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Monitoring use cases:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>TBI triage (Scandinavian, Italian, French guidelines incorporate S100B)</li>
<li>Post-cardiac arrest prognostication</li>
<li>Melanoma treatment response (every 3 months in stage III/IV)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Strategies to Manage</h2>
<p>S100B reflects tissue injury rather than a modifiable physiologic state. Management focuses on the underlying cause:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>TBI:</strong> immediate resuscitation, neuroimaging (CT if S100B elevated or clinical signs), neurocritical care for severe TBI (target ICP &lt;22 mmHg, CPP 60-70 mmHg)</li>
<li><strong>Stroke:</strong> time-sensitive reperfusion (IV thrombolysis if &lt;4.5 h; mechanical thrombectomy if LVO and &lt;6-24 h)</li>
<li><strong>Melanoma:</strong> oncology referral; stage-appropriate treatment (surgical excision, sentinel node biopsy, immunotherapy for advanced disease -- anti-PD-1 nivolumab/pembrolizumab, BRAF/MEK inhibitors if BRAF V600E mutant)</li>
<li><strong>False-positive workup:</strong> if S100B elevated without neurological signs, evaluate for extracranial trauma, melanoma, or strenuous exercise in past 24 h</li>
</ol>
