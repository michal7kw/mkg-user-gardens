<h1>Urolithin A</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Urolithin A (UA) is a metabolite that the gut microbiome generates from ellagitannins and ellagic acid found in pomegranate, walnuts, and certain berries. Importantly, the capacity to produce it varies between individuals — only a minority of people host the bacterial species required — which is why direct supplementation with synthetic, food-grade urolithin A has become the practical route to reliable exposure.</p>
<p>UA's defining mechanism is the induction of <strong>mitophagy</strong>, the quality-control process that selectively recycles damaged mitochondria. Because mitochondrial quality control declines with age, UA has emerged as a leading &quot;mitochondrial longevity&quot; compound, and — unusually for this category — its benefits on muscle mitochondrial function have been demonstrated in randomized human trials.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>Urolithin A activates mitophagy through the PINK1/Parkin axis and related receptor-mediated pathways, clearing dysfunctional mitochondria while promoting mitochondrial biogenesis. This restores mitochondrial respiratory capacity, improves muscle endurance and strength, and reduces markers of inflammation and cellular senescence. It also engages <a href="/garden/testuser1c7c107b5/pathway/ampk-pathway/" class="internal-link">AMPK Pathway</a> signaling, tying it into the broader energy-sensing and <a href="/garden/testuser1c7c107b5/pathway/autophagy/" class="internal-link">Autophagy</a> network that governs cellular stress resistance. By improving mitochondrial turnover in neurons, UA-driven mitophagy has reversed amyloid and tau pathology in preclinical models.</p>
<h2>Dosage</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Typical dose:</strong> 500-1000 mg/day of food-grade urolithin A.</li>
<li>Clinical trials have used 500 mg and 1000 mg daily; 500 mg improved muscle endurance and mitochondrial gene signatures in older adults.</li>
<li>Well tolerated; take with food.</li>
</ul>
