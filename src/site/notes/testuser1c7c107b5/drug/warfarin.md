<h1>Warfarin</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Warfarin is the prototypical vitamin K antagonist oral anticoagulant. It blocks the enzyme vitamin K epoxide reductase (VKORC1), preventing the recycling of vitamin K to its reduced form. Reduced vitamin K is the essential cofactor for the hepatic gamma-carboxylation that renders coagulation factors II, VII, IX, and X — and the natural anticoagulants protein C and protein S — functional. By depleting functional clotting factors, warfarin produces a dose-dependent anticoagulant effect.</p>
<p>Because the drug acts only on the synthesis of new factors, its onset is delayed by two to three days until circulating pre-formed factors are cleared; bridging with a parenteral anticoagulant is often used when rapid anticoagulation is required. Warfarin has a narrow therapeutic index and large inter-individual variability driven by CYP2C9 and VKORC1 genotype, diet (vitamin K intake), and numerous drug interactions, necessitating regular INR monitoring with a typical target range of 2.0-3.0 (higher for mechanical valves).</p>
<h2>Monitoring and Reversal</h2>
<p>The anticoagulant effect is quantified by the international normalized ratio (INR), a standardized prothrombin time. Supratherapeutic INR or bleeding is managed by withholding doses, oral or intravenous vitamin K, and, for serious bleeding, prothrombin complex concentrate (preferred) or fresh frozen plasma. Dietary consistency in vitamin K-containing foods is more important than avoidance.</p>
