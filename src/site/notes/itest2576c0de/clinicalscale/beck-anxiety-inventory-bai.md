<h1>Beck Anxiety Inventory (BAI)</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>The Beck Anxiety Inventory (BAI) is a 21-item self-report instrument that measures the severity of anxiety symptoms over the preceding week. It was developed by Beck, Epstein, Brown, and Steer (1988) specifically to discriminate anxiety from depression — a long-standing measurement problem, since many anxiety scales are confounded by depressive content. The BAI achieves this discrimination by weighting somatic symptoms of anxiety (e.g., numbness, dizziness, heart pounding, feeling of choking) and panic-related items, which overlap minimally with depression.</p>
<p>It is widely used in both clinical and research settings to quantify anxiety severity and to track change over time. Because it is self-administered and brief, it is practical for routine screening and treatment monitoring.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level:</strong> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (5/5)</p>
<h2>Scoring &amp; Interpretation</h2>
<p>Each of the 21 items is rated on a 0-3 scale describing how much that symptom has bothered the respondent during the past week. Item scores are summed for a total of 0-63 (higher = more severe anxiety).</p>

<p><strong>Recall period:</strong> the past week (including today).</p>
<h2>Psychometrics</h2>
<p>Validated in psychiatric outpatients. The original development study reported high internal consistency (Cronbach's α = 0.92) and good test-retest reliability over one week (r = 0.75). Convergent validity with other anxiety measures is moderate-to-high, while its correlation with depression measures (e.g., the Beck Depression Inventory) is comparatively lower — supporting its discriminant validity. The somatic emphasis means it is most sensitive to panic and physiological arousal and somewhat less so to worry-dominant generalized anxiety, where the GAD-7 may be preferred.</p>
