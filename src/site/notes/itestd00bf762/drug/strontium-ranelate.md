<h1>Strontium ranelate</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Strontium ranelate is an orally administered anti-osteoporotic agent consisting of two atoms of stable (non-radioactive) strontium combined with ranelic acid, which serves as the carrier. It was approved in Europe in 2004 for the treatment of severe osteoporosis and demonstrated reductions in both vertebral and non-vertebral fracture risk in the pivotal SOTI and TROPOS trials. Unlike bisphosphonates, which are purely anti-resorptive, strontium ranelate is described as having a dual effect on bone turnover. Strontium is chemically similar to calcium and is incorporated into the bone crystal lattice, which also produces an artefactual increase in bone mineral density measured by DXA. Following European Medicines Agency reviews of cardiovascular safety signals, its indication was narrowed to severe osteoporosis where alternatives are unsuitable, and several manufacturers have since withdrawn it from a number of markets.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>Strontium ranelate appears to act, at least in part, through the calcium-sensing receptor on bone cells. It stimulates the replication and differentiation of pre-osteoblasts and increases collagen and non-collagenous protein synthesis, promoting bone formation. Simultaneously it reduces osteoclast differentiation and resorbing activity, partly by increasing osteoprotegerin and decreasing RANKL expression. This relative uncoupling of formation from resorption distinguishes it mechanistically from anti-resorptive and purely anabolic agents. Because strontium has a higher atomic number than calcium and substitutes for it in hydroxyapatite, part of the measured DXA bone-density gain reflects the physical presence of strontium rather than new bone matrix alone.</p>
<h2>Indication</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Severe postmenopausal osteoporosis</strong> to reduce the risk of vertebral and hip fractures, when other medicinal products are not appropriate</li>
<li><strong>Osteoporosis in men</strong> at increased risk of fracture</li>
<li>Restricted by the EMA to patients <strong>without</strong> ischaemic heart disease, peripheral arterial disease, cerebrovascular disease, a history of venous thromboembolism, or uncontrolled hypertension, with periodic cardiovascular risk reassessment</li>
</ul>
<h2>Bone Turnover Marker Effects</h2>
<p>Consistent with its dual action, strontium ranelate produces a modest increase in the bone-formation marker P1NP (procollagen type 1 N-terminal propeptide) and a decrease in the bone-resorption marker CTX (C-terminal telopeptide of type 1 collagen), in contrast to the suppression of both markers seen with bisphosphonates.</p>
