<h1>Stomach Neoplasms</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Gastric (stomach) cancer is the <strong>fifth most common cancer</strong> and the <strong>third leading cause of cancer death</strong> worldwide, with the highest incidence in East Asia (Japan, Korea, China), Eastern Europe, and parts of South America. The incidence has been declining globally, largely attributed to <strong>decreasing H. pylori prevalence</strong>, improved food preservation (refrigeration replacing salting/smoking), and screening programs in high-incidence countries.</p>
<p><strong>Histological classification (Lauren):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Intestinal type (~50-60%):</strong> gland-forming; environmental risk factors (H. pylori, diet); decreasing incidence; tends to form well-defined tumors</li>
<li><strong>Diffuse type (~30-40%):</strong> poorly cohesive signet ring cells infiltrating the gastric wall; genetic predisposition (CDH1); stable incidence; poor prognosis; causes linitis plastica (leather-bottle stomach)</li>
<li><strong>Mixed/indeterminate (~10%)</strong></li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Helicobacter pylori</strong> is the strongest environmental risk factor, classified as a <strong>Class I carcinogen by IARC</strong>. Chronic H. pylori infection causes the <strong>Correa cascade</strong>: chronic gastritis -&gt; atrophic gastritis -&gt; intestinal metaplasia -&gt; dysplasia -&gt; gastric adenocarcinoma. Eradication of H. pylori reduces gastric cancer risk by ~50%.</p>
<p>The clinical challenge is that <strong>early gastric cancer is usually asymptomatic</strong>, presenting only with nonspecific dyspepsia or indigestion. By the time symptoms (weight loss, dysphagia, hematemesis) appear, the disease is often advanced. In high-incidence countries (Japan, Korea), <strong>endoscopic screening programs</strong> detect early gastric cancer in ~50-70% of cases, with 5-year survival &gt;90%. In low-incidence countries without screening, most cases are diagnosed at advanced stage with 5-year survival ~20-30%.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Early gastric cancer (limited to mucosa/submucosa):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD):</strong> en bloc resection; for well-differentiated tumors &lt;2 cm, no ulceration, no lymphovascular invasion</li>
<li><strong>Endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR):</strong> for smaller lesions</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Locally advanced (muscularis propria or lymph node positive):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Surgical resection:</strong> total or subtotal gastrectomy with <strong>D2 lymphadenectomy</strong> (standard of care; en bloc removal of perigastric and celiac axis nodes)</li>
<li><strong>Perioperative chemotherapy (FLOT):</strong> 5-fluorouracil + leucovorin + oxaliplatin + docetaxel; 4 cycles pre-op and 4 cycles post-op (FLOT4/AIO trial; improved 5-year survival from 38% to 45%)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Metastatic/advanced:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Chemotherapy:</strong> platinum + fluoropyrimidine (FOLFOX, CAPOX, or FP)</li>
<li><strong>HER2+ (~15-20%):</strong> trastuzumab (first-line with chemotherapy); trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd; second-line)</li>
<li><strong>VEGFR2+ (ramucirumab):</strong> second-line anti-angiogenic</li>
<li><strong>Immunotherapy:</strong> nivolumab (anti-PD-1) for PD-L1+ tumors; pembrolizumab (MSI-H/dMMR)</li>
<li><strong>Palliative:</strong> radiation for bleeding; stenting for obstruction; pain management</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Hereditary diffuse gastric cancer (CDH1 carriers):</strong> prophylactic total gastrectomy recommended (lifetime risk &gt;70%)</p>
