<h1>Vasculitis</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Vasculitis comprises a heterogeneous group of disorders characterized by inflammation of blood vessel walls, leading to vessel narrowing, occlusion, aneurysm formation, and end-organ ischemia. The <strong>2012 Chapel Hill Consensus Conference</strong> classifies vasculitis primarily by the dominant vessel size involved:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Large-vessel vasculitis:</strong> Giant cell arteritis (GCA), Takayasu arteritis</li>
<li><strong>Medium-vessel vasculitis:</strong> Polyarteritis nodosa (PAN), Kawasaki disease</li>
<li><strong>Small-vessel vasculitis (ANCA-associated):</strong> Granulomatosis with polyangiitis (GPA/Wegener), microscopic polyangiitis (MPA), eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (EGPA/Churg-Strauss)</li>
<li><strong>Small-vessel vasculitis (immune complex):</strong> IgA vasculitis (Henoch-Schonlein purpura), cryoglobulinemic vasculitis, anti-GBM disease</li>
<li><strong>Variable-vessel:</strong> Behcet disease, Cogan syndrome</li>
</ul>
<p>The pathogenesis involves immune-mediated vessel wall inflammation -- either through <strong>anti-neutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies (ANCA)</strong> activating neutrophils (ANCA-associated vasculitis), <strong>immune complex deposition</strong> (cryoglobulinemia, IgA vasculitis), or <strong>cell-mediated granulomatous inflammation</strong> (GCA, GPA). Clinical manifestations depend on the size and location of affected vessels.</p>
<p>Untreated vasculitis can cause irreversible organ damage (renal failure, pulmonary hemorrhage, stroke, bowel infarction, blindness). <strong>High-dose corticosteroids</strong> are first-line therapy for most types, with cyclophosphamide or rituximab for severe organ-threatening disease.</p>
<h2>Clinical Presentation</h2>
<p><strong>Constitutional symptoms</strong> (all types): fever, fatigue, weight loss, arthralgia</p>
<p><strong>Large-vessel (GCA, Takayasu):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Headache, scalp tenderness, jaw claudication, visual loss (GCA)</li>
<li>Pulselessness, claudication, bruits, BP discrepancy between arms (Takayasu)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>ANCA-associated (GPA, MPA, EGPA):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Upper respiratory: sinusitis, epistaxis, saddle nose, subglottic stenosis</li>
<li>Pulmonary: hemoptysis, pulmonary hemorrhage, cavitary nodules</li>
<li>Renal: rapidly progressive GN, hematuria, AKI</li>
<li>Neurologic: mononeuritis multiplex</li>
<li>Skin: palpable purpura</li>
</ul>
<h2>Diagnostic Information</h2>
<p><strong>Blood tests:</strong> ESR, CRP (elevated), CBC (anemia, eosinophilia in EGPA), creatinine, LFTs<br />
<strong>ANCA testing:</strong> PR3-ANCA (c-ANCA; GPA), MPO-ANCA (p-ANCA; MPA, EGPA)<br />
<strong>Complement:</strong> C3, C4 (low in cryoglobulinemia, normal in ANCA)<br />
<strong>Viral serology:</strong> HBV, HCV, HIV<br />
<strong>Imaging:</strong> CT angiography (large-vessel wall thickening, aneurysms), MRI/MRA (Takayasu), PET-CT (large-vessel inflammation)<br />
<strong>Biopsy:</strong> gold standard -- temporal artery (GCA), renal (GN), skin (purpura), lung (GPA)</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Induction:</strong> High-dose corticosteroids (methylprednisolone 1 g IV x 3 days then prednisone 1 mg/kg/day)</p>
<ul>
<li>Add <strong>cyclophosphamide</strong> or <strong>rituximab</strong> for severe organ-threatening ANCA-vasculitis</li>
<li><strong>Plasma exchange</strong> for severe RPGN or pulmonary hemorrhage</li>
<li><strong>Tocilizumab</strong> for GCA</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Maintenance:</strong> rituximab (every 6 months), azathioprine, methotrexate, or mycophenolate for 18-24 months after remission</p>
<p><strong>Supportive:</strong> PJP prophylaxis (TMP-SMX), bone protection (calcium, vitamin D, bisphosphonate), vaccination</p>
