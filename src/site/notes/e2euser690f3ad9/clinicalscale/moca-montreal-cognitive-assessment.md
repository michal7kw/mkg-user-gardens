<h1>MoCA (Montreal Cognitive Assessment)</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>The Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) is a one-page, clinician-administered cognitive screening instrument developed by Nasreddine and colleagues in 2005. It was designed specifically to address the poor sensitivity of the MMSE to <strong>mild cognitive impairment (MCI)</strong>, capturing subtler deficits that the MMSE misses. The MoCA samples a broad set of cognitive domains: short-term memory, visuospatial ability (cube copy, clock drawing), executive function (trail-making, verbal fluency, abstraction), attention, concentration, working memory, language, and orientation.</p>
<p>It takes roughly ten minutes to administer and is scored out of 30. The MoCA is freely available in many languages and is widely used to screen for MCI, early dementia, and cognitive impairment in conditions such as Parkinson's disease, stroke, and vascular cognitive impairment.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level:</strong> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (5/5)</p>
<h2>Scoring &amp; Interpretation</h2>
<p>Domain subscores are summed to a total of 0-30 (higher = better cognition). One point is conventionally added for individuals with ≤12 years of formal education to reduce educational bias.</p>

<p>The standard cutoff for abnormal cognition is <strong>&lt; 26</strong>, though some populations and revised norms support adjusted thresholds.</p>
<h2>Psychometrics</h2>
<p>In the original validation, the MoCA detected MCI with ~90% sensitivity versus only ~18% for the MMSE, while maintaining high specificity (~87%) in cognitively normal controls. It is more demanding of executive and visuospatial function than the MMSE, which is why it discriminates MCI more effectively. Subsequent re-examinations of population norms (e.g., the Framingham Heart Study cohort) have refined education- and age-adjusted cutoffs to reduce false positives in healthy older adults.</p>
