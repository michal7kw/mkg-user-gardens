<h1>GHK-Cu</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>GHK-Cu (copper tripeptide-1) is a naturally occurring copper-binding tripeptide — glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine bound to a copper ion — that is present in human plasma and declines with age. It is one of the most established <strong>topical cosmetic peptides</strong>, found in &quot;copper peptide&quot; serums and creams marketed for skin firmness and anti-aging, and it is also studied for wound healing. Crucially, unlike the injectable research peptides, GHK-Cu is applied <strong>to the skin</strong>, which gives it a much more benign safety profile.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>GHK-Cu acts as a <strong>signalling peptide</strong> and a copper carrier. Topically it is reported to stimulate dermal fibroblasts to synthesise collagen and other extracellular-matrix proteins, promote angiogenesis and wound closure, and provide antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity partly through delivery of copper (a cofactor for several skin enzymes). The result, in cosmetic use, is intended to be firmer, better-hydrated skin.</p>
<h2>Clinical Evidence</h2>
<p>GHK-Cu has more supporting data than the injectable research peptides, but it is still modest: small topical cosmetic studies and manufacturer data for skin appearance, and mostly preclinical work for wound healing. Its <code>evidence_level</code> is <strong>2</strong> — better than the level-1 injectables, below the trial-backed collagen peptides.</p>
<h2>Safety</h2>
<p>As a topical cosmetic, systemic risk is low; the main issues are occasional skin irritation or redness in sensitive users. This is the opposite end of the risk spectrum from self-injected gray-market peptides.</p>
