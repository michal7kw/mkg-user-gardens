<h1>Thyroid Function Explained</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>The thyroid is a small butterfly-shaped gland in your neck that acts as the body's metabolic thermostat. The <a href="/garden/itest073ff2bb/hormone/thyroid-hormones/" class="internal-link">Thyroid Hormones</a> it makes set the pace of nearly every cell — how fast you burn energy, how warm you feel, your heart rate, mood, and even how quickly your gut moves. When it runs low (<a href="/garden/itest073ff2bb/condition/hypothyroidism/" class="internal-link">Hypothyroidism</a>) everything slows down; when it runs high (<a href="/garden/itest073ff2bb/condition/hyperthyroidism/" class="internal-link">Hyperthyroidism</a>) everything speeds up.</p>
<h2>The Story</h2>
<p>Thyroid control is a thermostat loop. The brain's <a class="internal-link is-unresolved" href="/404">Hypothalamus</a> and pituitary monitor hormone levels and release <strong><a href="/garden/itest073ff2bb/labtest/tsh/" class="internal-link">Tsh</a></strong> (thyroid-stimulating hormone) to tell the thyroid how hard to work. Here is the counter-intuitive part: a <em>high</em> TSH usually means an <em>underactive</em> thyroid — the pituitary is shouting because it isn't getting enough hormone back. A low TSH usually means the thyroid is overactive.</p>
<p>The thyroid mostly makes T4, a storage form. The active hormone is T3, and most T4 is converted to T3 in the body's tissues — which is why a panel measures both <strong><a href="/garden/itest073ff2bb/biomarker/free-t4/" class="internal-link">Free T4</a></strong> (available T4) and <strong><a href="/garden/itest073ff2bb/biomarker/free-t3/" class="internal-link">Free T3</a></strong> (the active hormone). Under stress, illness, or dieting, the body can divert T4 into an inactive form, <strong><a href="/garden/itest073ff2bb/labtest/reverse-t3/" class="internal-link">Reverse T3</a></strong>, effectively putting metabolism in &quot;energy-saving mode.&quot; This is why someone can feel hypothyroid with a TSH that still looks acceptable.</p>
<p>The most common cause of an underactive thyroid is autoimmune: the immune system attacks the gland, which shows up as <strong><a href="/garden/itest073ff2bb/biomarker/tpo-antibodies/" class="internal-link">Tpo Antibodies</a></strong> — this is <a href="/garden/itest073ff2bb/condition/hashimotos-thyroiditis/" class="internal-link">Hashimotos Thyroiditis</a>. Catching the antibodies early can explain symptoms long before TSH drifts out of range, the stage called <a href="/garden/itest073ff2bb/condition/subclinical-hypothyroidism/" class="internal-link">Subclinical Hypothyroidism</a>. Modern guidelines stress reading the <em>whole</em> picture — symptoms plus the full panel — rather than a single TSH value (PMID: 28336049).</p>
<h2>What This Means For You</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Read the panel, not just TSH.</strong> A complete view is <a href="/garden/itest073ff2bb/labtest/tsh/" class="internal-link">Tsh</a> + <a href="/garden/itest073ff2bb/biomarker/free-t4/" class="internal-link">Free T4</a> + <a href="/garden/itest073ff2bb/biomarker/free-t3/" class="internal-link">Free T3</a>, and <a href="/garden/itest073ff2bb/biomarker/tpo-antibodies/" class="internal-link">Tpo Antibodies</a> if autoimmune disease is suspected. <a href="/garden/itest073ff2bb/labtest/reverse-t3/" class="internal-link">Reverse T3</a> can help explain &quot;normal labs, hypothyroid symptoms.&quot;</li>
<li><strong>The symptoms are metabolic.</strong> Unexplained fatigue, cold intolerance, weight changes, constipation, dry skin, and low mood are classic low-thyroid signs — they overlap heavily with the energy and metabolic stories, so it is worth ruling the thyroid in or out early.</li>
<li><strong>Nutrients the thyroid needs.</strong> Iodine is the raw material for thyroid hormone, and <a href="/garden/itest073ff2bb/supplement/selenium/" class="internal-link">Selenium</a> is required to convert T4 to active T3 and to protect the gland — both are common deficiencies worth checking before reaching for stronger interventions.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Key Takeaways</h2>
<ul>
<li>The thyroid sets the body's metabolic pace via T4 (storage) and T3 (active).</li>
<li>High TSH usually means an <em>underactive</em> thyroid — the pituitary is compensating.</li>
<li>TPO antibodies reveal autoimmune (Hashimoto's) disease, often before TSH moves.</li>
<li>Read the full panel against symptoms, not a single TSH value.</li>
</ul>
