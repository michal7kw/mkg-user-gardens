<h1>ESR1</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>ESR1 is the gene that encodes Estrogen Receptor Alpha (ERα; UniProt P03372), a member of the nuclear hormone receptor superfamily of ligand-activated transcription factors. The protein and its gene are often referred to interchangeably — ESR1 <em>is</em> the gene for ERα. ERα is built from a modular architecture comprising an N-terminal activation function-1 (AF-1) domain, a central zinc-finger DNA-binding domain, and a C-terminal ligand-binding domain that houses the hormone-dependent activation function-2 (AF-2).</p>
<p>In its physiological role, ERα binds estrogens (principally 17β-estradiol), undergoes a conformational change, dimerizes, and binds estrogen response elements in the promoters of target genes to drive transcriptional programs that govern cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival. These programs are essential in the development and maintenance of the breast, uterus, and reproductive tract, as well as in the regulation of bone density, lipid metabolism, and cardiovascular function.</p>
<p>ERα is one of the most important targets in clinical oncology. Roughly 70% of breast cancers are ER-positive and depend on estrogen signaling for growth, which is the rationale for endocrine therapy. Acquired mutations in the ESR1 ligand-binding domain are a major driver of resistance to aromatase inhibitors in advanced disease, motivating newer agents such as oral selective estrogen receptor degraders (SERDs).</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>ERα signals through both classical genomic and non-genomic pathways. In the genomic pathway, estradiol binding triggers receptor dimerization and recruitment of coactivator complexes — including the p160/steroid receptor coactivator family and the histone acetyltransferase EP300/CBP — that remodel chromatin and assemble the basal transcription machinery at target promoters. The tyrosine kinase SRC participates in membrane-proximal, non-genomic signaling, linking ERα to the activation of downstream growth and survival kinases. Ligand binding, coregulator recruitment, and post-translational modification (notably phosphorylation of AF-1) together tune the receptor's transcriptional output.</p>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>ERα status directs the management of breast cancer: ER-positive tumors are treated with endocrine therapies (selective estrogen receptor modulators, aromatase inhibitors, and SERDs), whereas ER-negative tumors are not. Activating ESR1 ligand-binding-domain mutations emerge under the selective pressure of aromatase-inhibitor therapy and confer constitutive, ligand-independent activity, driving endocrine resistance in metastatic disease; these mutations are now a routine biomarker and a target for next-generation degraders. Beyond cancer, estrogen signaling through ERα is central to skeletal health — declining estrogen at menopause accelerates bone loss and predisposes to osteoporosis.</p>
