<h1>Ipamorelin</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Ipamorelin is a selective synthetic <strong>growth-hormone secretagogue</strong> — an agonist at the ghrelin (GH-secretagogue) receptor that prompts the pituitary to release <a href="/garden/itestbfc987d4/hormone/growth-hormone/" class="internal-link">Growth Hormone</a> in a clean pulse, with little of the cortisol or prolactin rise seen with older secretagogues. It is one of the most widely used &quot;research peptides&quot;, typically stacked with the GHRH analogue <a href="/garden/itestbfc987d4/drug/cjc-1295/" class="internal-link">CJC-1295</a>. Like the others in this class, it is <strong>not approved for human use</strong> and is banned in sport.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>Ipamorelin activates the growth-hormone-secretagogue receptor (GHSR, the ghrelin receptor) on pituitary somatotrophs, triggering a selective, physiological-style pulse of growth-hormone release. The elevated growth hormone drives hepatic <a href="/garden/itestbfc987d4/hormone/igf-1/" class="internal-link">IGF-1</a>. Its selectivity (minimal cortisol/prolactin stimulation) is the reason it is preferred over earlier secretagogues in gray-market use. Because a GHRH analogue and a ghrelin-receptor agonist act through complementary pathways, ipamorelin is frequently combined with CJC-1295.</p>
<h2>Clinical Evidence</h2>
<p>As with the other GH secretagogues, human evidence is limited to short pharmacology studies of GH/IGF-1 response; there are <strong>no controlled trials</strong> establishing safe, beneficial effects on body composition or health outcomes. Its <code>evidence_level</code> is <strong>1</strong> — mechanistically real, deliberately not eligible as a recommendation.</p>
<h2>Safety</h2>
<p>Ipamorelin can produce growth-hormone-excess effects (water retention, joint pain, tingling) and, via ghrelin-receptor activity, increased appetite, alongside injection-site reactions and gray-market contamination risk. Long-term human safety is unknown.</p>
