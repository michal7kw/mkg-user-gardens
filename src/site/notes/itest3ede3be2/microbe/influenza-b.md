<h1>Influenza B</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Influenza B virus (IBV) is an enveloped orthomyxovirus with a segmented, negative-sense single-stranded RNA genome of eight gene segments. Unlike influenza A, it has an almost exclusively human host range and does not have HA/NA subtypes; instead it has diverged into two antigenically distinct lineages, B/Victoria and B/Yamagata. Influenza B causes a meaningful share of seasonal influenza disease and tends to affect children and adolescents disproportionately.</p>

<h2>Ecology &amp; Transmission</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Body Sites:</strong> respiratory tract (upper airway and lower respiratory epithelium)</li>
<li><strong>Transmission:</strong> respiratory droplets and aerosols; HA-mediated attachment to sialic-acid receptors</li>
<li><strong>Host range:</strong> essentially restricted to humans (rare detection in seals), limiting the reservoir for reassortment</li>
</ul>
<h2>Pathogenesis &amp; Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>Because influenza B lacks an animal reservoir and does not undergo antigenic shift, it cannot cause pandemics, but it still drifts antigenically and circulates seasonally. The two lineages co-circulate, which motivated the move from trivalent to quadrivalent seasonal vaccines that cover both B lineages. Clinical illness is generally similar to influenza A, with febrile respiratory disease, and influenza B is a notable cause of pediatric influenza morbidity and mortality. Highly conserved epitopes on the influenza B HA stalk are targets for broadly protective antibodies (Dreyfus et al. 2012; Krammer et al. 2018).</p>
