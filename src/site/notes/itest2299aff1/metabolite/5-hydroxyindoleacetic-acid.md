<h1>5-Hydroxyindoleacetic Acid</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>5-Hydroxyindoleacetic acid (5-HIAA) is the principal breakdown product of <a href="/garden/itest2299aff1/neurotransmitter/serotonin/" class="internal-link">Serotonin</a> (5-HT). After serotonin is released and reclaimed, monoamine oxidase (MAO-A) oxidizes it and aldehyde dehydrogenase yields 5-HIAA, which is cleared into cerebrospinal fluid and urine. As with HVA for dopamine, <strong>CSF 5-HIAA is a long-established index of central serotonin turnover</strong>, while 24-hour urinary 5-HIAA reflects peripheral (largely gut enterochromaffin) serotonin production.</p>
<p>5-HIAA is notable for one of the most reproducible findings in biological psychiatry: low CSF 5-HIAA is associated with impulsive aggression and elevated suicide risk, independent of diagnosis.</p>

<h2>Formation</h2>
<p>L-Tryptophan → 5-hydroxytryptophan → <a href="/garden/itest2299aff1/neurotransmitter/serotonin/" class="internal-link">Serotonin</a> (5-HT) → (MAO-A, then aldehyde dehydrogenase) → <strong>5-HIAA</strong>. The pathway derives from dietary <a href="/garden/itest2299aff1/supplement/l-tryptophan/" class="internal-link">L-Tryptophan</a>, which is also the substrate diverted into the kynurenine pathway under inflammation.</p>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Suicide and impulsivity:</strong> low CSF 5-HIAA is one of the most robust biological correlates of impulsive aggression and completed suicide.</li>
<li><strong>Depression:</strong> altered serotonin turnover is reflected in CSF 5-HIAA, though findings are heterogeneous (see <a href="/garden/itest2299aff1/condition/depression/" class="internal-link">Depression</a>).</li>
<li><strong>Carcinoid syndrome:</strong> markedly elevated 24-hour urinary 5-HIAA from serotonin-secreting neuroendocrine tumors is diagnostic.</li>
</ul>
