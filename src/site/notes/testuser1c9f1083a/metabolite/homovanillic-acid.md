<h1>Homovanillic Acid</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Homovanillic acid (HVA) is the major final breakdown product of <a href="/garden/testuser1c9f1083a/neurotransmitter/dopamine/" class="internal-link">Dopamine</a>. After dopamine is released and reclaimed, it is degraded by monoamine oxidase (MAO) and catechol-O-methyltransferase (COMT); the converging product of these reactions is HVA, which is excreted in cerebrospinal fluid and urine. Because HVA accumulates in proportion to how much dopamine has been synthesized, released, and metabolized, <strong>CSF HVA is a long-standing proxy for central dopaminergic activity (&quot;dopamine turnover&quot;)</strong>.</p>
<p>This makes HVA a useful research and clinical biomarker: it is low in conditions of dopaminergic deficiency and elevated where dopamine signaling is excessive or where dopamine-producing tumors are present.</p>

<h2>Formation</h2>
<p>Dopamine → (MAO) → DOPAC and → (COMT) → 3-methoxytyramine (3-MT); both branches converge → <strong>homovanillic acid (HVA)</strong>. In the prefrontal cortex, where dopamine transporter expression is low, COMT-dependent metabolism to HVA is especially important.</p>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Parkinson's disease:</strong> progressive loss of nigrostriatal dopamine neurons lowers CSF HVA, mirroring dopamine depletion (see <a href="/garden/testuser1c9f1083a/condition/parkinsons-disease/" class="internal-link">Parkinson's Disease</a>).</li>
<li><strong>Schizophrenia:</strong> plasma/CSF HVA has been studied as a marker of mesolimbic dopamine activity and antipsychotic response (see <a href="/garden/testuser1c9f1083a/condition/schizophrenia/" class="internal-link">Schizophrenia</a>).</li>
<li><strong>Neuroblastoma/pheochromocytoma:</strong> markedly elevated urinary HVA (with VMA) supports diagnosis of catecholamine-secreting tumors.</li>
</ul>
