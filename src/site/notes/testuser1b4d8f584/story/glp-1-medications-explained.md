<h1>GLP-1 Medications Explained</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>GLP-1 receptor agonists — <a href="/garden/testuser1b4d8f584/drug/semaglutide/" class="internal-link">Semaglutide</a> (Ozempic, Wegovy), <a href="/garden/testuser1b4d8f584/drug/liraglutide/" class="internal-link">Liraglutide</a>, and the dual-agonist <a href="/garden/testuser1b4d8f584/drug/tirzepatide/" class="internal-link">Tirzepatide</a> (Mounjaro, Zepbound) — are the medications behind the recent revolution in diabetes and obesity care. They all imitate a natural hormone your gut releases after you eat. Understanding that one hormone explains everything these drugs do.</p>
<h2>The Story</h2>
<p>When food reaches your intestine, gut cells release <strong><a href="/garden/testuser1b4d8f584/hormone/glp-1/" class="internal-link">GLP-1</a></strong> (glucagon-like peptide-1). It is an &quot;incretin&quot; — a signal that says <em>food has arrived, prepare to handle it.</em> GLP-1 then does three coordinated things across three organs:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>At the <a href="/garden/testuser1b4d8f584/organ/pancreas/" class="internal-link">Pancreas</a>:</strong> it tells the beta cells to release <a href="/garden/testuser1b4d8f584/labtest/insulin/" class="internal-link">Insulin</a> — but <em>only when glucose is high</em>. This glucose-dependence is why GLP-1 drugs rarely cause low blood sugar on their own. It also suppresses <a href="/garden/testuser1b4d8f584/hormone/glucagon/" class="internal-link">Glucagon</a>, the hormone that would otherwise raise blood sugar.</li>
<li><strong>At the stomach:</strong> it slows gastric emptying, so food leaves the stomach gradually and the post-meal glucose spike is blunted. This is also why people feel full longer.</li>
<li><strong>At the <a href="/garden/testuser1b4d8f584/organ/brain/" class="internal-link">Brain</a>:</strong> GLP-1 acts on appetite centers in the <a class="internal-link is-unresolved" href="/404">Hypothalamus</a> and brainstem, reducing hunger and the drive to eat. This <strong>brain-to-pancreas axis</strong> — one hormone tuning both appetite <em>and</em> insulin — is the key insight. The weight loss is not a side effect; it is the same signal working centrally.</li>
</ol>
<p>Natural GLP-1 is destroyed within minutes. The drugs are re-engineered to resist that breakdown, so a once-weekly injection keeps the signal switched on continuously. Tirzepatide goes further, hitting a second incretin receptor (GIP) as well, which is part of why it tends to produce larger effects.</p>
<p>In the STEP 1 trial, once-weekly semaglutide produced roughly 15% average body-weight loss in adults with obesity — far beyond previous medications (PMID: 33567185).</p>
<h2>What This Means For You</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>What improves:</strong> <a href="/garden/testuser1b4d8f584/metabolite/glucose/" class="internal-link">Glucose</a>, <a href="/garden/testuser1b4d8f584/biomarker/hemoglobin-a1c/" class="internal-link">Hemoglobin A1c</a>, body weight, and often <a href="/garden/testuser1b4d8f584/labtest/triglycerides/" class="internal-link">Triglycerides</a> and blood pressure follow. Because the appetite signal is central, these drugs change <em>how much you want to eat</em>, not just how you absorb food.</li>
<li><strong>The biomarkers to track:</strong> <a href="/garden/testuser1b4d8f584/biomarker/hemoglobin-a1c/" class="internal-link">Hemoglobin A1c</a> for glucose control and <a href="/garden/testuser1b4d8f584/metabolite/glucose/" class="internal-link">Glucose</a> day-to-day. Many users also watch weight trend and waist.</li>
<li><strong>The catch:</strong> the same slowed-stomach mechanism causes the common nausea, and appetite returns if the drug stops — because you have removed the artificial signal, not cured the underlying <a href="/garden/testuser1b4d8f584/condition/insulin-resistance/" class="internal-link">Insulin Resistance</a>. They work best alongside the lifestyle foundation, not instead of it. The background hormone <a href="/garden/testuser1b4d8f584/labtest/leptin/" class="internal-link">Leptin</a> (the long-term &quot;fullness&quot; signal from fat tissue) helps explain why appetite regulation is so hard to change permanently.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Key Takeaways</h2>
<ul>
<li>GLP-1 drugs copy a gut hormone that signals the pancreas <em>and</em> the brain at once.</li>
<li>Insulin release is glucose-dependent, so hypoglycemia risk is low by themselves.</li>
<li>The weight loss comes from real appetite reduction in the brain, not from blocking absorption.</li>
<li>They manage, not cure: appetite and weight tend to rebound if stopped without lifestyle change.</li>
</ul>
