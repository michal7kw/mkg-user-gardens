<h1>Biliary Obstruction</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Biliary obstruction is the blockage of bile flow from the liver through the biliary tree (intrahepatic ducts, common hepatic duct, common bile duct) to the duodenum. The most common causes are <strong>gallstones</strong> (choledocholithiasis; ~60% of cases) and <strong>malignancy</strong> (pancreatic head cancer, cholangiocarcinoma; ~30%). Benign strictures and parasites account for the remainder.</p>
<p>The clinical presentation is characterized by <strong>obstructive jaundice</strong>: elevated conjugated (direct) bilirubin with pale stools (absent stercobilin), dark urine (bilirubinuria), and pruritus (from bile acid deposition in skin). The laboratory hallmark is <strong>elevated alkaline phosphatase and GGT</strong>, disproportionate to transaminases.</p>
<p><strong>Charcot's triad</strong> (fever, jaundice, RUQ pain) indicates <strong>acute cholangitis</strong> -- a life-threatening infection of an obstructed biliary tree. <strong>Reynolds' pentad</strong> (Charcot's triad + hypotension + altered mental status) indicates <strong>suppurative cholangitis with septic shock</strong>, requiring urgent biliary decompression.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Diagnostic approach:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Ultrasound</strong> (first-line): identifies gallstones, biliary dilation, level of obstruction</li>
<li><strong>MRCP</strong> (magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography): non-invasive; gold standard for defining anatomy</li>
<li><strong>CT abdomen/pelvis</strong> (with contrast): for masses, staging, vascular involvement</li>
<li><strong>EUS</strong> (endoscopic ultrasound): for small stones, ampullary lesions, tissue diagnosis</li>
<li><strong>ERCP</strong> (endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography): diagnostic AND therapeutic (stone removal, stent)</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Treatment:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Gallstones (choledocholithiasis):</strong> ERCP with sphincterotomy and stone extraction -&gt; then laparoscopic cholecystectomy</li>
<li><strong>Pancreatic head cancer:</strong> surgical resection (Whipple / pancreaticoduodenectomy) if resectable; palliative biliary stenting if not</li>
<li><strong>Cholangiocarcinoma:</strong> surgical resection if resectable; biliary stenting for palliation</li>
<li><strong>Benign strictures:</strong> balloon dilation + stent placement (ERCP); surgical bypass if refractory</li>
<li><strong>Cholangitis:</strong> IV fluids + broad-spectrum antibiotics (pip-tazo) + urgent biliary decompression (ERCP or PTC)</li>
<li><strong>Parasites:</strong> antiparasitic therapy (albendazole/mebendazole) + ERCP extraction</li>
</ul>
