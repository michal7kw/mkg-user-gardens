<h1>SLUMS (Saint Louis University Mental Status)</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>The Saint Louis University Mental Status (SLUMS) examination is a 30-point, clinician-administered cognitive screening tool developed by Tariq and colleagues (2006) at Saint Louis University. It was created as a freely available alternative to the MMSE that is better at detecting <strong>mild neurocognitive disorder</strong> (an early, sub-dementia stage of cognitive decline) in addition to overt dementia.</p>
<p>The SLUMS assesses orientation, memory, attention, numeric/working memory (digit span, arithmetic), executive function (animal fluency, clock drawing, figure recognition), and immediate and delayed recall, with a story-recall task that probes contextual memory. Compared with the MMSE, it places greater weight on executive and memory tasks, which improves detection of early impairment.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level:</strong> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (4/5)</p>
<h2>Scoring &amp; Interpretation</h2>
<p>Items are summed to a total of 0-30 (higher = better cognition). Crucially, the SLUMS uses <strong>education-stratified cutoffs</strong>, applying different thresholds depending on whether the patient completed high school:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Education</th>
<th>Normal</th>
<th>Mild Neurocognitive Disorder</th>
<th>Dementia</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>High-school educated</td>
<td>27–30</td>
<td>21–26</td>
<td>1–20</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Less than high school</td>
<td>25–30</td>
<td>20–24</td>
<td>1–19</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>This education adjustment is intended to reduce false positives in less-educated patients—a known limitation of the MMSE.</p>
<h2>Psychometrics</h2>
<p>The original pilot study compared SLUMS with the MMSE in a Veterans Affairs geriatric outpatient population and found SLUMS comparable to the MMSE for detecting dementia while being more sensitive to mild neurocognitive disorder. As a single-center pilot, the foundational evidence base is smaller than that of the MMSE or MoCA, but subsequent cross-cultural translations and validations (e.g., a Thai-language adaptation) have extended its use internationally.</p>
