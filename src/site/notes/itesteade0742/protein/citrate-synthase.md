<h1>Citrate Synthase</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Citrate synthase (CS) catalyzes the first, committed step of the <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/pathway/citric-acid-cycle/" class="internal-link">Citric Acid Cycle</a> (TCA cycle): the condensation of <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/metabolite/acetyl-coa/" class="internal-link">Acetyl-CoA</a> with <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/metabolite/oxaloacetate/" class="internal-link">Oxaloacetate</a> to form <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/metabolite/citrate/" class="internal-link">Citrate</a>, releasing free <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/metabolite/coenzyme-a/" class="internal-link">Coenzyme A</a>. Located in the <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/cellularcomponent/mitochondria/" class="internal-link">Mitochondria</a>l matrix, it is the entry point through which carbon from <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/pathway/glycolysis/" class="internal-link">Glycolysis</a>, fatty-acid oxidation, and amino-acid catabolism — all converging on <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/metabolite/acetyl-coa/" class="internal-link">Acetyl-CoA</a> — enters terminal oxidation. Because its activity tracks mitochondrial content, citrate synthase is widely used in research and exercise physiology as a quantitative marker of mitochondrial mass and aerobic capacity.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>Citrate synthase catalyzes a Claisen (aldol-type) condensation between the methyl carbon of <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/metabolite/acetyl-coa/" class="internal-link">Acetyl-CoA</a> and the carbonyl carbon of <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/metabolite/oxaloacetate/" class="internal-link">Oxaloacetate</a>, forming citryl-CoA as an enzyme-bound intermediate. This intermediate is then hydrolyzed, cleaving the thioester bond to yield <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/metabolite/citrate/" class="internal-link">Citrate</a> and free <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/metabolite/coenzyme-a/" class="internal-link">Coenzyme A</a>. The hydrolysis of the high-energy thioester makes the overall reaction strongly exergonic (ΔG°' ≈ −31.5 kJ/mol), so it is effectively irreversible and pulls the cycle forward.</p>
<p>The enzyme undergoes a large induced-fit conformational change: binding of <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/metabolite/oxaloacetate/" class="internal-link">Oxaloacetate</a> closes the active site around the substrate, creating the binding pocket for <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/metabolite/acetyl-coa/" class="internal-link">Acetyl-CoA</a> and excluding water until citryl-CoA has formed (preventing wasteful hydrolysis of acetyl-CoA). As the rate-setting, first-committed step, it is feedback-<strong>inhibited</strong> by downstream energy and intermediate signals: <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/metabolite/atp/" class="internal-link">ATP</a>, <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/metabolite/nadh/" class="internal-link">NADH</a>, succinyl-CoA, and <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/metabolite/citrate/" class="internal-link">Citrate</a> itself, so flux into the cycle slows when the cell is energy-replete.</p>
<h2>Role in Metabolism</h2>
<p>By consuming <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/metabolite/oxaloacetate/" class="internal-link">Oxaloacetate</a> and <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/metabolite/acetyl-coa/" class="internal-link">Acetyl-CoA</a>, citrate synthase initiates the cycle that ultimately produces the reduced cofactors <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/metabolite/nadh/" class="internal-link">NADH</a> and <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/metabolite/fadh2/" class="internal-link">FADH2</a> feeding the <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/pathway/electron-transport-chain/" class="internal-link">Electron Transport Chain</a> and <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/pathway/oxidative-phosphorylation/" class="internal-link">Oxidative Phosphorylation</a>. The <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/metabolite/citrate/" class="internal-link">Citrate</a> it makes is not only the cycle's first intermediate but also a key anabolic signal: exported to the cytosol it supplies acetyl-CoA for fatty-acid synthesis and allosterically inhibits <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/protein/phosphofructokinase/" class="internal-link">Phosphofructokinase</a>, linking TCA flux back to the regulation of <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/pathway/glycolysis/" class="internal-link">Glycolysis</a>. Availability of <a href="/garden/itesteade0742/metabolite/oxaloacetate/" class="internal-link">Oxaloacetate</a> (replenished by anaplerotic reactions) is a major determinant of cycle throughput.</p>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>Citrate synthase activity assays are a standard laboratory readout of mitochondrial density in skeletal-muscle biopsies, used to normalize other mitochondrial enzyme activities and to quantify the mitochondrial-biogenesis response to endurance training. Reduced citrate synthase activity is observed in mitochondrial myopathies, sarcopenia, and conditions of physical deconditioning, while exercise training robustly increases it. Although primary inherited deficiency of citrate synthase is exceedingly rare, the enzyme's role as a mitochondrial-mass marker makes it a frequent reference point in metabolic and aging research.</p>
