<h1>Saw Palmetto</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Saw palmetto is a fan palm native to the southeastern United States whose ripe berries yield a fatty-acid-rich (lipidosterolic) extract long used as a traditional remedy for urinary complaints. It is among the most popular over-the-counter botanicals taken by men for benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) and the associated lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS) such as nocturia, weak stream, and incomplete emptying.</p>
<p>The scientific picture is notably at odds with its popularity. Large, well-conducted randomized trials and successive Cochrane systematic reviews have concluded that standardized saw palmetto extract is no more effective than placebo for improving urinary symptoms, urinary flow, or prostate size, and it does not reliably reduce PSA. It is generally well tolerated, so it is often used as a low-risk option, but patients should be counseled that high-quality evidence of efficacy is lacking and that it is not a substitute for evaluation of an elevated PSA.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level:</strong> 2/5</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>The proposed mechanisms are largely mechanistic and preclinical:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>5-alpha-reductase inhibition:</strong> The extract is thought to weakly inhibit both isoforms of 5-alpha-reductase, lowering local conversion of testosterone to the more potent dihydrotestosterone (DHT) that drives prostatic growth.</li>
<li><strong>Androgen receptor antagonism:</strong> Components may competitively block androgen binding in prostate tissue.</li>
<li><strong>Anti-inflammatory and anti-edematous effects:</strong> Inhibition of cyclooxygenase/lipoxygenase pathways and reduced prostatic edema have been described in laboratory models.</li>
</ul>
<p>Crucially, these mechanisms do not produce the symptom reductions seen with pharmaceutical 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors (e.g., finasteride) or alpha-blockers in controlled trials, suggesting the in vivo effect is small.</p>
<h2>Dosage</h2>
<p>The conventional dose is 320 mg/day of a standardized lipidosterolic extract (typically 85-95% fatty acids and sterols), given once daily or split as 160 mg twice daily with food. Clinical trials generally evaluate effects over 6 to 12 months; doubling the dose in trials did not improve outcomes.</p>
