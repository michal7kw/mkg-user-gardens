<h1>Influenza A</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Influenza A virus (IAV) is an enveloped orthomyxovirus with a segmented, negative-sense single-stranded RNA genome of eight gene segments. It is the dominant cause of severe seasonal influenza and the only influenza type to cause pandemics. Strains are classified into subtypes by the antigenic properties of two surface glycoproteins, hemagglutinin (HA, 18 subtypes) and neuraminidase (NA, 11 subtypes); seasonal human disease is currently driven by A(H1N1)pdm09 and A(H3N2).</p>

<h2>Ecology &amp; Transmission</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Body Sites:</strong> respiratory tract (upper airway and bronchial/alveolar epithelium)</li>
<li><strong>Transmission:</strong> respiratory droplets and aerosols; attachment via HA binding to sialic-acid receptors</li>
<li><strong>Host range:</strong> broad — wild aquatic birds are the natural reservoir, with spillover to swine, poultry, and humans</li>
</ul>
<h2>Pathogenesis &amp; Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>The segmented genome permits reassortment: when two influenza A strains co-infect a host, gene segments can be exchanged, producing antigenic shift and the emergence of novel pandemic strains (e.g. 1918 H1N1, 2009 H1N1pdm09). Continuous point mutation of HA and NA (antigenic drift) drives seasonal epidemics and necessitates annual vaccine reformulation. Severe disease features primary viral pneumonia and frequent bacterial co-infection, with the greatest mortality in the very young, elderly, and immunocompromised (Webster et al. 1992; Krammer et al. 2018).</p>
