<h1>Mini-Cog</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>The Mini-Cog is a very brief cognitive screening instrument developed by Borson and colleagues as a &quot;cognitive vital sign&quot; for rapid detection of dementia. It combines two simple tasks: recall of three unrelated words and a clock-drawing test (CDT). The whole assessment takes roughly three minutes, making it well suited to time-constrained primary-care visits and large screening programs.</p>
<p>A key advantage of the Mini-Cog is that it is relatively insensitive to the patient's education level and language/cultural background, because the clock-drawing component is largely nonverbal and the word-recall task is short. This makes it especially useful in multilingual and diverse populations where longer instruments like the MMSE may be biased.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level:</strong> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (5/5)</p>
<h2>Scoring &amp; Interpretation</h2>
<p>The patient is asked to register and later recall three words; between registration and recall they draw a clock showing a specified time. Scoring (0-5):</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Word recall:</strong> 1 point per word recalled (0-3)</li>
<li><strong>Clock-drawing test:</strong> 2 points for a normal clock, 0 for abnormal</li>
</ul>

<p>The Mini-Cog is a <strong>screen, not a diagnosis</strong>; a positive result should prompt fuller cognitive and clinical assessment.</p>
<h2>Psychometrics</h2>
<p>Validated in community-dwelling and multilingual older adults, the Mini-Cog demonstrates sensitivity and specificity for dementia comparable to the longer MMSE while taking a fraction of the time. The original 2000 study reported high discrimination for dementia in a multi-ethnic sample, and a 2003 population-based validation confirmed performance similar to the MMSE. Its brevity and minimal education/language bias are its principal strengths; like all brief screens it is less sensitive to mild cognitive impairment than more detailed instruments such as the MoCA.</p>
