<h1>Nicotinamide Riboside</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Nicotinamide riboside (NR) is a naturally occurring form of vitamin B3 and a direct precursor to <strong>NAD+</strong> (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide), the central redox cofactor of cellular energy metabolism. NAD+ declines with age across tissues, and this decline is implicated in mitochondrial dysfunction, impaired DNA repair, and reduced sirtuin activity. NR supplementation reliably and dose-dependently <strong>raises whole-blood NAD+</strong> in human trials, making it a clean, measurable input to the NAD+ pathway alongside its sibling precursor NMN.</p>
<p>Evidence for NAD+ elevation is strong (Level 3, consistent RCTs); evidence for downstream clinical/longevity outcomes in humans is still emerging. NR is a reasonable lever when a low or declining NAD+ level is measured, and it pairs conceptually with the biological-age and mitochondrial-energy axes of the panel.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>NR is salvaged to NAD+ via nicotinamide riboside kinases (NRK1/NRK2) → NMN → NAD+ (NMNAT). This route bypasses the rate-limiting NAMPT step of the classic salvage pathway, allowing efficient NAD+ repletion. Elevated NAD+ fuels oxidative phosphorylation and serves as substrate for sirtuins (SIRT1/SIRT3), which deacetylate PGC-1α and other targets to drive mitochondrial biogenesis, and for PARPs in DNA-repair signalling. The net effect is improved cellular energetics and stress resilience.</p>
<h2>Dosage</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Typical:</strong> 250-500 mg/day (trials up to 1000 mg/day), once daily.</li>
<li><strong>Onset:</strong> blood NAD+ rises within 1-2 weeks; longevity endpoints are long-term.</li>
<li><strong>Related:</strong> NMN acts on the same pathway one step downstream.</li>
</ul>
