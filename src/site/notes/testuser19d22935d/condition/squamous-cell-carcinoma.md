<h1>Squamous Cell Carcinoma</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) is a malignant tumor arising from <strong>squamous epithelial cells</strong> -- the flat cells that line the skin surface and the mucosal surfaces of the oral cavity, pharynx, larynx, esophagus, cervix, and respiratory tract. SCC is the <strong>second most common skin cancer</strong> (after basal cell carcinoma), with an estimated 1.8 million cases annually in the US alone. However, SCC can arise in any squamous-lined tissue, and the prognosis varies dramatically by location.</p>
<p><strong>Cutaneous SCC:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Second most common skin cancer (~20% of non-melanoma skin cancers)</li>
<li>Primarily caused by <strong>cumulative UV radiation</strong> (unlike melanoma, which is linked to intermittent intense exposure)</li>
<li>Arises from <strong>actinic keratosis</strong> (precursor lesion; rough, scaly patches on sun-exposed skin) in ~60-65% of cases</li>
<li>Most common on sun-exposed areas: face, ears, scalp (bald), backs of hands, forearms</li>
<li><strong>Metastatic rate:</strong> ~2-5% for cutaneous SCC; but much higher for:
<ul>
<li>Immunosuppressed patients (organ transplant: ~10-13% metastatic rate)</li>
<li>Lesions on lips/ears (~10-15%)</li>
<li>Lesions &gt;2 cm diameter or &gt;6 mm depth</li>
<li>Perineural invasion</li>
<li>Recurrent lesions</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Mucosal SCC:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Head and neck SCC (HNSCC):</strong> oral cavity, oropharynx, larynx, hypopharynx; caused by smoking + alcohol (classic) or HPV (oropharyngeal; rising incidence). HPV-positive HNSCC has significantly better prognosis.</li>
<li><strong>Esophageal SCC:</strong> risk factors include smoking, alcohol, hot beverages, nitrosamines, achalasia</li>
<li><strong>Cervical SCC:</strong> almost exclusively caused by HPV (16, 18)</li>
<li><strong>Pulmonary SCC:</strong> centrally located; associated with smoking; cavitation common</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Immunosuppression is the strongest risk factor</strong> for cutaneous SCC: organ transplant recipients have <strong>65-100x increased risk</strong> compared to the general population. SCC in transplant patients is also more aggressive, with higher metastatic rates.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Cutaneous SCC:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Surgical excision</strong> (standard): 4-6 mm margins for low-risk lesions; 6-10 mm for high-risk</li>
<li><strong>Mohs micrographic surgery</strong> (gold standard for high-risk locations: face, ears, lips, genitalia, hands, feet; or for immunosuppressed patients; or recurrent/aggressive histology)</li>
<li><strong>Electrodessication and curettage (ED&amp;C):</strong> for small, low-risk, superficial lesions on trunk/extremities</li>
<li><strong>Radiation therapy:</strong> for patients who cannot undergo surgery (elderly, comorbidities)</li>
<li><strong>Topical imiquimod 5%:</strong> for in situ SCC (Bowen's disease); not for invasive SCC</li>
<li><strong>Systemic therapy:</strong> for metastatic or locally advanced disease:
<ul>
<li><strong>Cemiplimab</strong> (anti-PD-1; FDA-approved for advanced cutaneous SCC)</li>
<li><strong>Pembrolizumab</strong> (anti-PD-1)</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Head and neck SCC:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Early stage (I-II):</strong> single modality -- surgical resection OR definitive radiation therapy</li>
<li><strong>Locally advanced (III-IV):</strong> multimodal -- surgery + adjuvant radiation/chemoradiation; OR definitive chemoradiation</li>
<li><strong>HPV-positive oropharyngeal SCC:</strong> significantly better prognosis; treatment de-escalation trials ongoing</li>
<li><strong>Recurrent/metastatic:</strong> pembrolizumab (anti-PD-1; KEYNOTE-048); platinum + fluorouracil + cetuximab</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Immunosuppressed patients:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>More aggressive treatment (Mohs surgery preferred)</li>
<li>Reduce immunosuppression if possible (mTOR inhibitors like sirolimus may reduce SCC risk)</li>
<li>Close surveillance (every 3-6 months)</li>
<li>Chemoprevention: acitretin (retinoid; reduces new SCC formation in transplant patients)</li>
<li>Sun protection is critical</li>
</ul>
