<h1>Hyperlipidemia</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Hyperlipidemia is the presence of elevated lipids -- cholesterol and/or triglycerides -- in the bloodstream. It is the most common modifiable risk factor for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) and is classified as <strong>primary</strong> (genetic/familial) or <strong>secondary</strong> (lifestyle, diabetes, hypothyroidism, medications). <strong>Familial hypercholesterolemia</strong> (FH, from LDLR/APOB/PCSK9 mutations) causes severely elevated LDL from birth and affects ~1 in 250 people.</p>
<p>The <strong>10-year ASCVD risk calculator</strong> integrates lipid levels with age, sex, blood pressure, diabetes, and smoking to guide statin therapy. Statin therapy reduces cardiovascular events by 25-35% per 1 mmol/L LDL reduction.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Statins</strong> (atorvastatin 40-80 mg, rosuvastatin 20-40 mg) -- first-line; reduce LDL 30-60%</li>
<li><strong>Ezetimibe</strong> 10 mg -- add for 15-20% further LDL reduction</li>
<li><strong>PCSK9 inhibitors</strong> (alirocumab, evolocumab) -- for FH or statin-intolerant</li>
<li><strong>Fibrates</strong> (fenofibrate) -- first-line for triglycerides &gt;500 mg/dL</li>
<li><strong>Icosapent ethyl</strong> 4 g/day -- REDUCE-IT showed 25% CV risk reduction</li>
<li><strong>Lifestyle</strong> -- Mediterranean diet, exercise, weight loss</li>
</ul>
