<h1>Faecalibacterium prausnitzii</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>One of the most abundant butyrate-producing bacteria in the healthy human colon (3-5% of total gut bacteria). Strongly anti-inflammatory through butyrate-mediated histone deacetylase inhibition and a specific microbial anti-inflammatory peptide (MAM) that blocks NF-κB. Consistently depleted in Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis, IBS, and colorectal cancer; recovery of F. prausnitzii correlates with clinical remission.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level:</strong> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (4/5)</p>

<h2>Habitat</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Body Sites:</strong> gut (colon, ileum)</li>
<li><strong>Oxygen Requirement:</strong> Obligate anaerobe (extremely oxygen-sensitive — dies on air contact)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Role</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Pathogenicity:</strong> Beneficial commensal</li>
<li><strong>Key Metabolites Produced:</strong> butyrate, formate, lactate</li>
</ul>
<p>Butyrate is the primary energy source for colonocytes, induces regulatory T cells, and inhibits histone deacetylase. F. prausnitzii MAM peptide directly suppresses NF-κB signaling.</p>
