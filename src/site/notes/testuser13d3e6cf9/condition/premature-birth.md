<h1>Premature Birth</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Premature birth (preterm birth) is defined as delivery before <strong>37 completed weeks of gestation</strong>. It affects approximately <strong>10% of pregnancies worldwide</strong> (~15 million babies annually) and is the <strong>leading cause of death in children under 5 years</strong> globally. The risk of neonatal mortality and long-term disability increases with decreasing gestational age: babies born at 23 weeks have ~20-30% survival, while those at 34+ weeks approach term-infant outcomes.</p>
<p>Preterm birth is classified by gestational age:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Extremely preterm:</strong> &lt;28 weeks (~1% of births)</li>
<li><strong>Very preterm:</strong> 28-31 weeks (~1.5%)</li>
<li><strong>Moderate/late preterm:</strong> 32-36 weeks (~8%)</li>
</ul>
<p>The etiology is <strong>multifactorial</strong> and often idiopathic (~50% of cases have no identifiable cause). Known causes include:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Spontaneous preterm labor</strong> (infection/inflammation, uterine overdistension from multiple gestation, cervical insufficiency, stress)</li>
<li><strong>Preterm premature rupture of membranes (PPROM)</strong> (~30% of preterm births)</li>
<li><strong>Medically indicated preterm delivery</strong> (pre-eclampsia, fetal distress, placental abruption; ~25%)</li>
</ul>
<p>The major complications of prematurity -- collectively termed the &quot;preterm morbidity triad&quot; -- are:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Respiratory distress syndrome (RDS):</strong> surfactant deficiency; managed with antenatal corticosteroids and exogenous surfactant</li>
<li><strong>Intraventricular hemorrhage (IVH):</strong> germinal matrix bleeding; reduced by magnesium sulfate neuroprotection</li>
<li><strong>Necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC):</strong> intestinal injury; reduced by human milk feeding</li>
</ul>
<p>Advances in neonatal care (surfactant replacement, antenatal corticosteroids, gentle ventilation, human milk feeding, probiotics) have dramatically improved survival and reduced disability in premature infants over the past 30 years.</p>
<h2>Clinical Presentation</h2>
<p><strong>Maternal symptoms of preterm labor:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Regular uterine contractions (every 10 minutes or more frequent)</li>
<li>Pelvic pressure or cramping (like menstrual cramps)</li>
<li>Low, dull backache</li>
<li>Change in vaginal discharge (watery, mucus, bloody)</li>
<li>Rupture of membranes (gush or trickle of fluid)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Neonatal signs of prematurity:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Small size and low birth weight (&lt;2500 g for low birth weight; &lt;1500 g for very low; &lt;1000 g for extremely low)</li>
<li>Thin, translucent skin (reduced subcutaneous fat)</li>
<li>Lanugo (fine body hair) and vernix</li>
<li>Weak suck and feeding difficulties</li>
<li>Temperature instability (hypothermia)</li>
<li>Respiratory distress (grunting, retractions, nasal flaring)</li>
<li>Jaundice</li>
</ul>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Prevention of preterm birth (before/at-risk pregnancies):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Vaginal progesterone</strong> (200 mg daily) or 17-OHPC for short cervix</li>
<li><strong>Cervical cerclage</strong> (McDonald or Shirodkar) for cervical insufficiency</li>
<li><strong>Low-dose aspirin</strong> (75-150 mg) for pre-eclampsia prevention</li>
<li>Treat infections (asymptomatic bacteriuria, BV)</li>
<li>Smoking cessation</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Management of threatened preterm labor:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Tocolytics</strong> (delay delivery by 48 hours to allow steroid effect):
<ul>
<li>Nifedipine 20 mg PO then 10-20 mg q6h</li>
<li>Indomethacin 50 mg PR then 25 mg q6h (limited to 48h; &lt;32 weeks)</li>
<li>Atosiban (oxytocin antagonist; available in some countries)</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Antenatal corticosteroids</strong> (betamethasone 12 mg IM q24h x 2 doses; most effective at 24-34 weeks):
<ul>
<li>Accelerate fetal lung maturation</li>
<li>Reduce RDS, IVH, NEC, and neonatal mortality</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Magnesium sulfate</strong> (4 g IV bolus then 1 g/hr for 12-24 hours if &lt;32 weeks):
<ul>
<li>Neuroprotection; reduces cerebral palsy risk</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>GBS prophylaxis</strong> if positive or unknown status</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Neonatal care:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>NICU admission for &lt;34 weeks or complications</li>
<li>Thermoregulation (incubator, skin-to-skin)</li>
<li>Respiratory support (CPAP, mechanical ventilation, surfactant)</li>
<li>Nutrition (human milk, TPN if needed)</li>
<li>Probiotics (reduce NEC risk)</li>
</ul>
