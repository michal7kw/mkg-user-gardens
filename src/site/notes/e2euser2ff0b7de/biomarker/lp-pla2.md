<h1>Lp-PLA2</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Lipoprotein-associated phospholipase A2 (Lp-PLA2), also known as platelet-activating factor acetylhydrolase (PAF-AH), is a 45 kDa calcium-independent phospholipase A2 that circulates bound mainly to LDL (~80%) and HDL (~20%). Unlike hsCRP (a systemic marker of inflammation), Lp-PLA2 is vascular-specific -- it is produced by macrophages and foam cells within atherosclerotic plaques and released into the circulation. The enzyme hydrolyzes oxidized phospholipids on LDL particles to generate two pro-inflammatory mediators: lysophosphatidylcholine (lysoPC) and oxidized non-esterified fatty acids. These mediators are cytotoxic to vascular smooth muscle and endothelial cells, fueling plaque necrotic core expansion, cap thinning, and rupture.</p>
<p>Clinically, Lp-PLA2 is used as a risk-stratification biomarker for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD). The seminal Rotterdam Study (Kardys et al., 2006) and multiple subsequent cohorts have demonstrated that elevated Lp-PLA2 independently predicts coronary events and ischemic stroke, with a ~2x relative risk at the highest vs lowest tertile. The biomarker is particularly useful for stroke risk prediction -- it is one of the few blood biomarkers with predictive value for ischemic stroke specifically (most other biomarkers predict coronary events more than stroke). Lp-PLA2 received FDA 510(k) clearance in 2003 as the PLAC test (diaDexus) for cardiovascular and stroke risk assessment.</p>
<p>The clinical use of Lp-PLA2 is most appropriate as a &quot;tie-breaker&quot; in intermediate-risk patients -- those with 10-year ASCVD risk 7.5-20% where the decision to start statin therapy is uncertain. In this population, an elevated Lp-PLA2 shifts the patient toward statin initiation. Lp-PLA2 is not in primary prevention guidelines as a routine test but is recognized as a &quot;risk-enhancing factor&quot; in the 2018 ACC/AHA cholesterol guideline alongside hsCRP, Lp(a), and family history. Of note, the STABILITY and SOLID-TIMI 52 trials of darapladib (a selective Lp-PLA2 inhibitor) failed to reduce CV events in patients with stable coronary disease, tempering enthusiasm for Lp-PLA2 as a therapeutic target -- though the biomarker retains prognostic value.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level: 4/5</strong> (multiple large cohort studies; FDA-cleared; therapeutic inhibition trials negative, but biomarker value robust).</p>

<p><strong>Caveats:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Reference range is assay-specific (PLAC test ELISA; mass-based; activity-based assays also exist with different cutoffs)</li>
<li>Less affected by acute systemic inflammation than hsCRP (more vascular-specific)</li>
<li>Levels modestly influenced by age, sex, LDL-C, smoking, diabetes</li>
<li>African American populations have higher Lp-PLA2 on average</li>
</ul>

<h2>Factors Affecting Levels</h2>
<p><strong>Increase Lp-PLA2:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Active atherosclerosis (coronary, carotid, peripheral arterial disease)</li>
<li>Hypercholesterolemia (LDL-C is the principal carrier; Lp-PLA2 tracks with LDL)</li>
<li>Smoking (vascular macrophage activation)</li>
<li>Type 2 diabetes / metabolic syndrome</li>
<li>Obesity</li>
<li>Hypertension (vascular inflammation)</li>
<li>Chronic kidney disease</li>
<li>Post-menopausal status</li>
<li>African ancestry (population-level higher)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Decrease Lp-PLA2:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Statin therapy (especially rosuvastatin, atorvastatin; 20-30% reduction)</li>
<li>Ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors</li>
<li>Lifestyle: smoking cessation, weight loss, exercise</li>
<li>Mediterranean diet (modest reduction)</li>
<li>Omega-3 fatty acids (1-4 g/day; 10-20% reduction)</li>
<li>Niacin (modest)</li>
<li>Darapladib (selective Lp-PLA2 inhibitor; investigational only; reduced Lp-PLA2 activity 60-65% but no CV outcome benefit)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p><strong>Elevated Lp-PLA2 is associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Coronary artery disease:</strong> ~2x risk of incident coronary events in the Rotterdam Study; complements hsCRP (Lp-PLA2 is vascular-specific, hsCRP is systemic)</li>
<li><strong>Ischemic stroke:</strong> particularly strong predictor -- ~2x risk; one of the few blood biomarkers with stroke-specific predictive value</li>
<li><strong>Stable atherosclerosis:</strong> elevated Lp-PLA2 predicts future CV events in patients with established CAD</li>
<li><strong>Carotid artery disease:</strong> correlates with carotid intima-media thickness and plaque instability</li>
<li><strong>Heart failure:</strong> modest prognostic value in HF cohorts</li>
<li><strong>Sudden cardiac death:</strong> elevated Lp-PLA2 associated with arrhythmic death</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Limitations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Not in primary prevention guidelines</strong> (ACC/AHA lists as &quot;risk-enhancing factor&quot; but does not recommend universal screening)</li>
<li><strong>Therapeutic inhibition trials negative</strong> (STABILITY, SOLID-TIMI 52 with darapladib did not reduce CV events), tempering enthusiasm for Lp-PLA2 as a drug target</li>
<li><strong>Modest incremental value over traditional risk factors</strong> in some cohorts</li>
<li><strong>Limited insurance reimbursement</strong> in the US</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Monitoring use cases:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Risk stratification in intermediate-risk ASCVD (10-year risk 7.5-20%) to guide statin initiation</li>
<li>Stroke risk assessment (especially in patients with multiple stroke risk factors)</li>
<li>Complementary to hsCRP in multimarker inflammation panels (Lp-PLA2 vascular-specific, hsCRP systemic)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Strategies to Improve</h2>
<p>Lp-PLA2 is modifiable through lifestyle and pharmacologic interventions:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>High-intensity statin therapy</strong> (atorvastatin 40-80 mg, rosuvastatin 20-40 mg) -- reduces Lp-PLA2 by 20-30% via lipid-lowering and pleiotropic anti-inflammatory effects</li>
<li><strong>Ezetimibe and PCSK9 inhibitors</strong> -- additional lipid-lowering further reduces Lp-PLA2</li>
<li><strong>Smoking cessation</strong> -- among the most impactful Lp-PLA2-lowering interventions</li>
<li><strong>Omega-3 fatty acids</strong> 1-4 g/day EPA+DHA -- 10-20% reduction</li>
<li><strong>Niacin</strong> -- modest reduction, limited by side effects</li>
<li><strong>Mediterranean diet</strong> rich in olive oil, fish, vegetables -- observational evidence for modest reduction</li>
<li><strong>Weight loss</strong> in obese patients -- 5-10% body weight reduction lowers Lp-PLA2 substantially</li>
<li><strong>Aerobic exercise</strong> 150 min/week moderate intensity</li>
<li><strong>Curcumin</strong> 500-1000 mg daily -- small studies suggest modest reduction in metabolic-syndrome patients</li>
<li><strong>Treat underlying risk factors</strong> -- optimize blood pressure, glycemic control, lipids</li>
</ol>
