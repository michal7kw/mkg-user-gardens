<h1>Ovaries</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>The ovaries are the paired female gonads located in the pelvis. They serve two coordinated roles: <strong>oogenesis/ovulation</strong> (maturation and release of oocytes from ovarian follicles) and <strong>steroidogenesis</strong> (cyclical production of estrogen — chiefly estradiol — and progesterone). The ovaries are therefore both the female reproductive organ and a primary endocrine gland.</p>
<p>Ovarian function follows the <strong>hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis</strong> and the menstrual cycle: pituitary FSH drives follicular growth and estradiol production in the follicular phase; the mid-cycle LH surge triggers ovulation; and the resulting corpus luteum secretes progesterone in the luteal phase. Estrogen and progesterone feed back on the hypothalamus and pituitary. Across the lifespan, the finite follicular pool declines, culminating in <strong>menopause</strong> — the cessation of ovulation and a marked fall in estrogen.</p>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)</strong> features ovulatory dysfunction, hyperandrogenism, and insulin resistance.</li>
<li><strong>Menopause</strong> and <strong>primary ovarian insufficiency</strong> reflect loss of ovarian estrogen output, driving vasomotor symptoms, bone loss, and metabolic and cardiovascular changes.</li>
<li><strong>Endometriosis</strong> and ovarian cysts/tumors are common ovarian and pelvic pathologies.</li>
<li>Ovarian endocrine status is assessed via estradiol, FSH, LH, progesterone, AMH (ovarian reserve), and SHBG.</li>
</ul>
