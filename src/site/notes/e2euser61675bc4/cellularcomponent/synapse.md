<h1>Synapse</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>The synapse is the fundamental unit of communication in the nervous system — the specialized contact where the axon terminal of one neuron meets a dendrite, soma, or effector cell of another. At a chemical synapse, an arriving action potential opens voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels in the presynaptic terminal, triggering fusion of neurotransmitter-filled vesicles with the membrane (the synaptic vesicle cycle). Released transmitter diffuses across the cleft to bind postsynaptic receptors, converting the chemical signal back into an electrical or biochemical response.</p>
<p>The synapse is not a static wire but a dynamic structure whose strength is continuously adjusted — the physical embodiment of <a href="/garden/e2euser61675bc4/pathway/synaptic-plasticity/" class="internal-link">Synaptic Plasticity</a>. Synapse number and function decline in aging and are lost early in neurodegeneration, while synaptic dysregulation underlies major psychiatric disease.</p>
<h2>Structure and Function</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Presynaptic terminal:</strong> synaptic vesicles, active zone, Ca²⁺ channels, release machinery (SNAREs).</li>
<li><strong>Synaptic cleft:</strong> ~20-40 nm gap across which transmitter diffuses.</li>
<li><strong>Postsynaptic density (PSD):</strong> dense scaffold of receptors (<a href="/garden/e2euser61675bc4/neurotransmitter/glutamate/" class="internal-link">Glutamate</a> NMDA/AMPA, <a href="/garden/e2euser61675bc4/neurotransmitter/gaba/" class="internal-link">GABA</a>, <a href="/garden/e2euser61675bc4/neurotransmitter/dopamine/" class="internal-link">Dopamine</a>, <a href="/garden/e2euser61675bc4/neurotransmitter/serotonin/" class="internal-link">Serotonin</a>, <a href="/garden/e2euser61675bc4/neurotransmitter/acetylcholine/" class="internal-link">Acetylcholine</a> receptors), anchoring proteins, and signaling enzymes.</li>
</ul>
<p>Receptor composition and PSD remodeling — driven by activity and by <a href="/garden/e2euser61675bc4/biomarker/bdnf/" class="internal-link">BDNF</a>/TrkB signaling — determine synaptic strength.</p>
<h2>Clinical Relevance</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Alzheimer's disease:</strong> synapse loss correlates with cognitive decline more strongly than plaque or tangle burden.</li>
<li><strong>Schizophrenia:</strong> excessive developmental synaptic pruning and glutamatergic/dopaminergic dysregulation.</li>
<li><strong>Depression:</strong> chronic stress causes dendritic spine loss in prefrontal and hippocampal circuits; rapid-acting antidepressants restore synapses.</li>
</ul>
