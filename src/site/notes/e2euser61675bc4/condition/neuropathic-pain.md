<h1>Neuropathic Pain</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Neuropathic pain is pain arising as a direct consequence of a lesion or disease affecting the somatosensory nervous system, in contrast to nociceptive pain, which signals actual or threatened tissue damage. It reflects maladaptive changes in pain processing — peripheral and central sensitization, ectopic nerve firing, loss of inhibitory control, and reorganization of nociceptive pathways — so the pain persists in the absence of an ongoing noxious stimulus. Patients typically describe burning, shooting, or electric-shock-like pain, frequently accompanied by sensory loss, allodynia (pain evoked by normally innocuous stimuli such as light touch), and hyperalgesia.</p>
<p>Common causes include diabetic peripheral neuropathy, post-herpetic neuralgia, trigeminal neuralgia, radiculopathy from nerve-root compression, chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy, and central lesions following stroke or spinal cord injury. Vitamin B12 deficiency and other metabolic causes are important and potentially reversible contributors.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p>Neuropathic pain often responds poorly to conventional analgesics such as paracetamol and NSAIDs. First-line pharmacological treatments are agents that modulate neuronal excitability and descending pain control: gabapentinoids (gabapentin, pregabalin), tricyclic antidepressants, and serotonin-noradrenaline reuptake inhibitors (duloxetine). Topical agents (lidocaine, capsaicin) are options for localized pain. Treating the underlying cause and optimizing metabolic factors are essential. Some nutraceuticals studied as adjuncts in diabetic neuropathy include alpha-lipoic acid and acetyl-L-carnitine.</p>
