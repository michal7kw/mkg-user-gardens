<h1>Soluble Fiber</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Soluble fiber refers to the viscous, fermentable fractions of dietary fiber — <strong>psyllium husk</strong>, oat and barley <strong>beta-glucan</strong>, glucomannan, and pectin. Supplemental soluble fiber is a guideline-endorsed lever for elevated LDL cholesterol (psyllium ~7-15 g/day; oat beta-glucan ≥3 g/day) and also blunts postprandial glucose, making it a dual metabolic + lipid recommendation.</p>
<p>Its LDL-lowering mechanism (bile-acid sequestration → increased LDL clearance) is complementary to statins, plant sterols (absorption), and bergamot/berberine (synthesis), so soluble fiber stacks well with them.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>Viscous soluble fiber gels in the small intestine, binding bile acids and reducing their ileal reabsorption. The liver compensates by converting more cholesterol into bile acids, upregulating LDL receptors and lowering LDL-C/ApoB. The gel also slows gastric emptying and glucose absorption (lower postprandial glucose/insulin), and colonic fermentation produces short-chain fatty acids that improve lipid and glucose handling.</p>
<h2>Dosage</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Psyllium:</strong> 7-15 g/day; <strong>oat beta-glucan:</strong> ≥3 g/day.</li>
<li><strong>Timing:</strong> with meals and water; separate from medications by 2-4 h.</li>
<li><strong>Onset:</strong> ~4-8 weeks; titrate to limit bloating/gas.</li>
</ul>
