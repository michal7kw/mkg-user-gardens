<h1>Calcium</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Calcium is the most abundant mineral in the human body, with approximately 99% stored in bones and teeth where it provides structural integrity. The remaining 1% circulates in the blood and soft tissues, playing critical roles in muscle contraction, nerve impulse transmission, blood clotting, and intracellular signaling.</p>
<p>Adequate calcium intake throughout life is essential for achieving peak bone mass during adolescence and preventing osteoporosis in later years. Calcium homeostasis is tightly regulated by parathyroid hormone (PTH), calcitonin, and active vitamin D (1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D), which control intestinal absorption, renal reabsorption, and bone resorption.</p>
<p>Beyond skeletal health, calcium is a universal second messenger in cellular signaling cascades, influencing hormone secretion, enzyme activation, and vascular tone. Calcium balance is influenced by dietary factors such as vitamin D status, magnesium availability, and the presence of oxalates or phytates that can inhibit absorption.</p>
<h2>Key Functions</h2>
<ul>
<li>Provides structural framework for bones and teeth as hydroxyapatite crystite</li>
<li>Enables muscle contraction through calcium-dependent troponin-tropomyosin interactions</li>
<li>Mediates nerve impulse transmission at synaptic junctions</li>
<li>Activates coagulation factors in the blood clotting cascade</li>
<li>Serves as an intracellular second messenger for hormone signaling</li>
<li>Regulates vascular smooth muscle tone and blood pressure</li>
</ul>
<h2>Dietary Sources</h2>
<ul>
<li>Dairy products (milk, yogurt, cheese)</li>
<li>Sardines and canned salmon (with bones)</li>
<li>Fortified plant milks (soy, almond, oat)</li>
<li>Calcium-set tofu</li>
<li>Dark leafy greens (kale, bok choy, broccoli)</li>
<li>Fortified orange juice and cereals</li>
<li>Almonds and sesame seeds</li>
</ul>
<h2>Deficiency and Excess</h2>
<p>Chronic calcium deficiency leads to decreased bone mineral density, increasing the risk of osteoporosis and fractures, particularly in postmenopausal women. Acute hypocalcemia can cause muscle cramps, numbness, tingling in extremities, and cardiac arrhythmias. In children, severe deficiency impairs growth and dental development.</p>
<p>Excess calcium intake, particularly from supplements, may increase the risk of kidney stones, constipation, and impaired absorption of iron and zinc. Very high intakes have been associated with vascular calcification and potential cardiovascular risk, though evidence remains debated.</p>
