<h1>Complex II</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Succinate dehydrogenase (SDH), the only enzyme that participates in both the <a href="/garden/testuser11765219c/pathway/citric-acid-cycle/" class="internal-link">Citric Acid Cycle</a> and the <a href="/garden/testuser11765219c/pathway/electron-transport-chain/" class="internal-link">Electron Transport Chain</a>. It oxidizes <a href="/garden/testuser11765219c/metabolite/succinate/" class="internal-link">Succinate</a> to <a href="/garden/testuser11765219c/metabolite/fumarate/" class="internal-link">Fumarate</a> while reducing its covalently bound FAD cofactor to <a href="/garden/testuser11765219c/metabolite/fadh2/" class="internal-link">FADH2</a>, then transfers electrons to ubiquinone (<a href="/garden/testuser11765219c/supplement/coenzyme-q10/" class="internal-link">Coenzyme Q10</a>). Located in the inner membrane of the <a href="/garden/testuser11765219c/cellularcomponent/mitochondria/" class="internal-link">Mitochondria</a>, Complex II is the smallest respiratory complex and, notably, does <strong>not</strong> pump protons across the membrane.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>Complex II couples a soluble catalytic reaction to membrane electron transfer through four subunits (SDHA–SDHD):</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Succinate oxidation.</strong> In the matrix-facing flavoprotein subunit, <a href="/garden/testuser11765219c/metabolite/succinate/" class="internal-link">Succinate</a> is oxidized to <a href="/garden/testuser11765219c/metabolite/fumarate/" class="internal-link">Fumarate</a>, a step shared directly with the <a href="/garden/testuser11765219c/pathway/citric-acid-cycle/" class="internal-link">Citric Acid Cycle</a>.</li>
<li><strong>FAD reduction.</strong> The two electrons released reduce the covalently bound FAD cofactor to <a href="/garden/testuser11765219c/metabolite/fadh2/" class="internal-link">FADH2</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Electron relay.</strong> Electrons pass through three iron-sulfur clusters (2Fe-2S, 4Fe-4S, 3Fe-4S) toward the membrane domain.</li>
<li><strong>Ubiquinone reduction.</strong> At the membrane-facing site, ubiquinone (<a href="/garden/testuser11765219c/supplement/coenzyme-q10/" class="internal-link">Coenzyme Q10</a>) is reduced to ubiquinol, accepting two electrons and two protons.</li>
</ol>

<p>Because Complex II does not translocate protons, electrons entering the chain via <a href="/garden/testuser11765219c/metabolite/fadh2/" class="internal-link">FADH2</a> generate a smaller proton-motive force — and therefore less <a href="/garden/testuser11765219c/metabolite/atp/" class="internal-link">ATP</a> — than electrons entering at <a href="/garden/testuser11765219c/protein/complex-i/" class="internal-link">Complex I</a> via <a href="/garden/testuser11765219c/metabolite/nadh/" class="internal-link">NADH</a>.</p>
<h2>Role in the Electron Transport Chain</h2>
<p>Complex II provides a second, parallel entry point into the ubiquinone pool. Reducing equivalents captured as <a href="/garden/testuser11765219c/metabolite/fadh2/" class="internal-link">FADH2</a> during succinate oxidation (and, indirectly, during <a href="/garden/testuser11765219c/pathway/beta-oxidation/" class="internal-link">Beta-Oxidation</a>) are funneled to ubiquinone (<a href="/garden/testuser11765219c/supplement/coenzyme-q10/" class="internal-link">Coenzyme Q10</a>) without proton pumping. The reduced <a href="/garden/testuser11765219c/supplement/coenzyme-q10/" class="internal-link">Coenzyme Q10</a> then carries these electrons to <a href="/garden/testuser11765219c/protein/complex-iii/" class="internal-link">Complex III</a>, merging with the electron stream from <a href="/garden/testuser11765219c/protein/complex-i/" class="internal-link">Complex I</a>. The FAD prosthetic group derives from <a href="/garden/testuser11765219c/supplement/riboflavin/" class="internal-link">Riboflavin</a> (vitamin B2).</p>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Tumor suppressor role.</strong> Germline mutations in SDH subunits (SDHB/SDHC/SDHD) cause hereditary paraganglioma and pheochromocytoma; succinate accumulation acts as an oncometabolite that stabilizes HIF-1α.</li>
<li><strong>Mitochondrial disease.</strong> Isolated Complex II deficiency is rare but can present as Leigh syndrome, cardiomyopathy, or optic atrophy.</li>
<li><strong>Riboflavin dependence.</strong> Because the FAD cofactor depends on <a href="/garden/testuser11765219c/supplement/riboflavin/" class="internal-link">Riboflavin</a>, deficiency can impair succinate oxidation; riboflavin supplementation may benefit certain flavoprotein-related disorders.</li>
<li><strong>Pharmacology.</strong> Complex II is the target of agricultural succinate-dehydrogenase-inhibitor (SDHI) fungicides and the experimental cytotoxin malonate.</li>
</ul>
