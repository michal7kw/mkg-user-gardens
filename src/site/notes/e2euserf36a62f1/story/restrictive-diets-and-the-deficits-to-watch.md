<h1>Restrictive Diets and the Deficits to Watch</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Any diet that drops a whole food group buys focus at the cost of coverage. A <a href="/garden/e2euserf36a62f1/intervention/plant-basedvegan-diet/" class="internal-link">Plant-Based/Vegan Diet</a> removes animal foods; a <a href="/garden/e2euserf36a62f1/intervention/keto-diet/" class="internal-link">Keto Diet</a> removes most carbohydrates and, often, fruit and whole grains; a <a href="/garden/e2euserf36a62f1/intervention/carnivore-diet/" class="internal-link">Carnivore Diet</a> removes plants entirely; an <a href="/garden/e2euserf36a62f1/intervention/elimination-diet/" class="internal-link">Elimination Diet</a> strips out suspected triggers for weeks at a time. None of these is automatically unhealthy — but each one predictably narrows the supply of certain nutrients. Knowing <em>which</em> nutrients, and how to watch them, is the difference between a diet that works and one that quietly erodes your blood, bones, or nerves.</p>
<h2>The Story</h2>
<p>Think of your diet as a portfolio. Diversity hedges risk; concentration raises both the upside and the downside. The classic &quot;downside&quot; nutrients cluster into a short, knowable list.</p>
<p><strong>Vitamin B12</strong> is the headline. It is made by microbes and reaches us almost entirely through animal foods, so a plant-based eater has essentially no dietary source. A systematic review found deficiency in a large share of vegetarians and especially vegans — including pregnant women and children, who are most vulnerable (PMID: 23356638). The trap is that B12 deficiency is slow and silent: the body holds years of liver stores, then nerve symptoms (tingling, balance problems, cognitive fog) and macrocytic <a href="/garden/e2euserf36a62f1/condition/anemia/" class="internal-link">Anemia</a> appear, sometimes irreversibly. This makes <a href="/garden/e2euserf36a62f1/supplement/vitamin-b12/" class="internal-link">Vitamin B12</a> non-negotiable to supplement on any animal-free diet.</p>
<p><strong>Iron</strong> is the second. Plant (&quot;non-heme&quot;) iron is absorbed far less efficiently than the heme iron in meat, and plant foods carry phytates and polyphenols that block uptake further. A meta-analysis showed vegetarians have meaningfully lower iron stores (ferritin), with women of reproductive age at highest risk of slipping into <a href="/garden/e2euserf36a62f1/condition/iron-deficiency-anemia/" class="internal-link">Iron Deficiency Anemia</a> (PMID: 27880062). Vitamin C eaten with the same meal can partly rescue absorption.</p>
<p><strong>Vitamin D, calcium, and omega-3s</strong> round out the list — and here the diets diverge. Vegans miss the marine omega-3s <a href="/garden/e2euserf36a62f1/nutrient/omega-3-dha/" class="internal-link">Omega-3 (DHA)</a> and <a href="/garden/e2euserf36a62f1/nutrient/omega-3-epa/" class="internal-link">Omega-3 (EPA)</a> (the plant precursor ALA converts poorly) and, if they avoid fortified foods, <a href="/garden/e2euserf36a62f1/supplement/calcium/" class="internal-link">Calcium</a> and <a href="/garden/e2euserf36a62f1/supplement/vitamin-d/" class="internal-link">Vitamin D</a> for bone. A strict carnivore diet, paradoxically, is also low in calcium (no dairy is rare but plant calcium sources are gone) and provides essentially no <a href="/garden/e2euserf36a62f1/nutrient/dietary-fiber/" class="internal-link">Dietary Fiber</a> or vitamin C. Keto dieters often run low on potassium, magnesium, and fiber simply because the foods richest in them — fruit, legumes, whole grains — are limited. Long elimination diets risk <em>broad</em> shortfalls precisely because the menu is so narrow for so long.</p>
<h2>What This Means For You</h2>
<p>Restriction is fine; flying blind is not. Track the markers that move first.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>B12:</strong> On any vegan or long carnivore-then-broadened pattern, supplement B12 routinely. Watch serum <a href="/garden/e2euserf36a62f1/supplement/vitamin-b12/" class="internal-link">Vitamin B12</a> and, when borderline, methylmalonic acid (MMA), which rises before serum B12 falls.</li>
<li><strong>Iron:</strong> <a href="/garden/e2euserf36a62f1/labtest/ferritin/" class="internal-link">Ferritin</a> is the early-warning gauge — it drops long before <a href="/garden/e2euserf36a62f1/labtest/hemoglobin/" class="internal-link">Hemoglobin</a> does. Menstruating and pregnant people on plant-based diets should check it periodically and pair iron-rich plants with vitamin C.</li>
<li><strong>Bone nutrients:</strong> Monitor <a href="/garden/e2euserf36a62f1/biomarker/vitamin-d-25-oh/" class="internal-link">Vitamin D (25-OH)</a> and ensure a reliable <a href="/garden/e2euserf36a62f1/supplement/calcium/" class="internal-link">Calcium</a> source (fortified plant milks, tofu set with calcium, or a supplement). Chronic shortfalls quietly raise <a href="/garden/e2euserf36a62f1/condition/osteoporosis/" class="internal-link">Osteoporosis</a> risk over years.</li>
<li><strong>Omega-3s:</strong> Vegans benefit from an algae-derived DHA/EPA supplement; ALA from flax and walnuts is a weak substitute.</li>
<li><strong>General rule:</strong> The longer and stricter the diet, the more it pays to either rotate in fortified foods or use a targeted supplement — and to recheck labs rather than assume. <a href="/garden/e2euserf36a62f1/condition/vitamin-d-deficiency/" class="internal-link">Vitamin D Deficiency</a> and <a href="/garden/e2euserf36a62f1/condition/vitamin-b12-deficiency/" class="internal-link">Vitamin B12 Deficiency</a> are common, cheap to detect, and entirely fixable when caught early.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Key Takeaways</h2>
<ul>
<li>Every food-group-removing diet has a predictable shortlist of at-risk nutrients — restriction is fine, blind restriction is not.</li>
<li>B12 is mandatory to supplement on plant-based diets; deficiency is silent for years then can damage nerves permanently.</li>
<li>Iron stores (ferritin) fall long before hemoglobin — check the early gauge, especially in menstruating and pregnant people.</li>
<li>Watch vitamin D, calcium, and marine omega-3s for bone and brain; keto and carnivore have their own fiber and micronutrient gaps.</li>
<li>The fix is cheap: targeted supplements, fortified foods, and periodic labs beat guesswork.</li>
</ul>
