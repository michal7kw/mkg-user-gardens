<h1>Blood Glucose Imbalance</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Blood glucose imbalance refers to deviation of plasma glucose outside the normal physiologic range (~70-100 mg/dL fasting, &lt;140 mg/dL postprandial), encompassing the two opposing extremes of <strong>hyperglycemia</strong> (high) and <strong>hypoglycemia</strong> (low). Normal glucose homeostasis is maintained by a tightly coupled endocrine system: insulin (the only hormone that lowers glucose) counterbalanced by glucagon, epinephrine, cortisol, and growth hormone (the counter-regulatory hormones that raise glucose). Dysregulation at any level -- pancreatic beta cell dysfunction, insulin resistance, counter-regulatory failure, or iatrogenic intervention -- produces dysglycemia.</p>
<p><strong>Hyperglycemia</strong> is by far the more common and chronic problem. The spectrum spans <strong>prediabetes</strong> (fasting glucose 100-125 mg/dL, HbA1c 5.7-6.4%, or 2-hour OGTT 140-199 mg/dL -- affecting ~96 million US adults) and <strong>diabetes</strong> (fasting &gt;=126 mg/dL, HbA1c &gt;=6.5%, 2-hour OGTT &gt;=200, or random &gt;=200 with symptoms). Chronic hyperglycemia drives the devastating <strong>microvascular</strong> (retinopathy, nephropathy, neuropathy) and <strong>macrovascular</strong> (cardiovascular disease, stroke) complications of diabetes via <strong>advanced glycation end products (AGEs)</strong>, <strong>protein kinase C activation</strong>, <strong>polyol pathway flux</strong>, and <strong>hexosamine pathway activation</strong> -- collectively termed &quot;glucose toxicity.&quot; Tight glycemic control (DCCT, UKPDS trials) substantially reduces these complications but does not eliminate them, and aggressive control in older or frail patients increases <strong>hypoglycemia risk</strong>.</p>
<p><strong>Hypoglycemia</strong> is the <strong>limiting factor</strong> of diabetes therapy and is itself dangerous. Clinical hypoglycemia is defined by Whipple's triad: (1) symptoms consistent with hypoglycemia, (2) documented low plasma glucose (&lt;70 mg/dL), (3) symptom resolution with glucose administration. <strong>Symptomatic thresholds</strong> vary: autonomic symptoms (tremor, palpitations, sweating, hunger) appear around 55-65 mg/dL; neuroglycopenic symptoms (confusion, seizures, coma) appear below ~50 mg/dL. <strong>Recurrent hypoglycemia</strong> blunts counter-regulatory responses and symptom awareness (<strong>hypoglycemia unawareness</strong>), creating a dangerous cycle. <strong>Severe hypoglycemia</strong> is associated with <strong>cardiac arrhythmia (prolonged QT)</strong>, <strong>falls and fractures</strong> (especially in older adults), <strong>cognitive decline</strong>, and <strong>death</strong> -- making hypoglycemia arguably more acutely dangerous than hyperglycemia. The ACCORD trial showed that aggressive glucose lowering in high-risk T2DM increased mortality, attributed partly to hypoglycemia.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Identify the underlying cause:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Diabetes</strong> (type 1, type 2, gestational, MODY, secondary to pancreatitis/cystic fibrosis/hemochromatosis)</li>
<li><strong>Prediabetes</strong> (lifestyle modification or metformin)</li>
<li><strong>Iatrogenic</strong> (insulin, sulfonylureas, glucocorticoids, thiazides, atypical antipsychotics)</li>
<li><strong>Endocrine</strong> (Cushing, acromegaly, pheochromocytoma, Addison's)</li>
<li><strong>Insulinoma</strong> (Whipple's triad, supervised 72-hour fast, C-peptide and proinsulin during hypoglycemia)</li>
<li><strong>Reactive (postprandial) hypoglycemia</strong> (post-bariatric surgery, early T2DM)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Hyperglycemia management:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Type 1 diabetes</strong> -- lifelong insulin replacement (basal-bolus regimen; insulin pump + CGM; hybrid closed-loop systems)</li>
<li><strong>Type 2 diabetes</strong> -- per ADA/EASD:
<ul>
<li><strong>Metformin</strong> first-line (if no contraindication)</li>
<li>Add <strong>SGLT2 inhibitor</strong> or <strong>GLP-1 receptor agonist</strong> for ASCVD, heart failure, or CKD (independent of HbA1c)</li>
<li>Add insulin if HbA1c &gt;10% or symptomatic hyperglycemia</li>
<li>Individualized HbA1c target (&lt;7% in most; &lt;8% in elderly/frail to avoid hypoglycemia)</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Lifestyle</strong> -- Mediterranean or low glycemic-index diet, 150 min/week exercise, weight loss 5-10% (GLP-1 agonists and tirzepatide achieve 15-20%)</li>
<li><strong>Continuous glucose monitoring</strong> for T1DM and insulin-treated T2DM; improves HbA1c and reduces hypoglycemia</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Hypoglycemia management (ADA &quot;Rule of 15&quot;):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>If alert and able to swallow: <strong>15 g fast-acting carbohydrate</strong> (4 glucose tablets, 4 oz juice, 1 tbsp sugar); recheck glucose in 15 min; repeat if &lt;70 mg/dL</li>
<li>If unable to swallow or unconscious: <strong>glucagon 1 mg IM/SC</strong> (or intranasal 3 mg, or dasiglucagon 0.6 mg SC); turn on side (vomiting risk); call EMS</li>
<li><strong>IV dextrose</strong> (25 g D50W or D10W) if IV access and glucagon unavailable</li>
<li>After recovery, complex carbohydrate + protein to prevent recurrence</li>
<li><strong>Identify and treat the underlying cause</strong> -- medication error, missed meal, exercise, alcohol, renal failure, insulinoma</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Hypoglycemia prevention:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Patient education on recognition (especially autonomic symptoms) and self-treatment</li>
<li>Carry fast-acting carbohydrate and glucagon (family trained)</li>
<li>Avoid skipping meals; adjust insulin for exercise</li>
<li>Avoid alcohol without food</li>
<li>Continuous glucose monitoring with low-glucose alerts</li>
<li>Address <strong>hypoglycemia unawareness</strong> by relaxing glycemic targets for 2-3 weeks (restores counter-regulation)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Long-term:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Regular follow-up; reassess regimen and targets</li>
<li>Screen for and treat diabetes complications annually</li>
<li>Multidisciplinary care (endocrinology, dietetics, diabetes education, podiatry, ophthalmology)</li>
</ul>
