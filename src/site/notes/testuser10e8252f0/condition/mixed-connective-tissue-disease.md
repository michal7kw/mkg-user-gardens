<h1>Mixed Connective Tissue Disease</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Mixed connective tissue disease (MCTD) is a systemic autoimmune disease defined by <strong>overlapping clinical features of systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), systemic sclerosis (SSc), and polymyositis (PM)</strong>, in the presence of <strong>high-titer antibodies against U1-ribonucleoprotein (U1-RNP)</strong>. The disease affects predominantly women (9:1), with onset usually in the 20s-40s, and is relatively rare (estimated prevalence 1-10 per 100,000). The unifying anti-U1-RNP antibody distinguishes MCTD from other overlap syndromes and undifferentiated connective tissue disease (UCTD).</p>
<p>The clinical picture evolves over years and is characterized by a &quot;mosaic&quot; of overlapping features. <strong>Raynaud's phenomenon</strong> is the most common presenting symptom (occurring in ~90% and often preceding other features by years) and is frequently accompanied by <strong>puffy fingers (&quot;sausage digits&quot;)</strong> -- a hallmark that should prompt ANA and anti-U1-RNP testing. Other characteristic features include <strong>polyarthritis</strong> (often non-erosive, Jaccoud-type deformity), <strong>inflammatory myositis</strong> (proximal weakness, elevated CK), <strong>esophageal dysmotility</strong> (reflux, dysphagia), <strong>sclerodactyly</strong> (skin thickening of fingers), and <strong>pulmonary involvement</strong> (interstitial lung disease, <strong>pulmonary arterial hypertension</strong>). The lupus-like features (malar rash, photosensitivity, pleuritis, pericarditis) are common, but <strong>severe renal disease (membranous nephropathy) and central nervous system involvement are less common than in SLE</strong> -- historically MCTD was considered &quot;benign,&quot; but this has been revised.</p>
<p><strong>Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH)</strong> is now recognized as the <strong>leading cause of death</strong> in MCTD, accounting for a substantial fraction of mortality. PAH may develop insidiously and is often asymptomatic until advanced; this is why <strong>annual screening echocardiography</strong> is recommended in all MCTD patients. Other serious complications include <strong>interstitial lung disease</strong>, <strong>scleroderma renal crisis</strong> (rare but life-threatening; associated with steroid use and anti-RNA polymerase III antibodies), <strong>myocarditis</strong>, <strong>pericarditis</strong>, and <strong>digital gangrene</strong> from severe Raynaud.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p>MCTD management is <strong>symptom- and organ-targeted</strong>, borrowing from the treatment playbooks of SLE, SSc, and PM. There is <strong>no disease-modifying therapy</strong> that addresses the entire condition; rather, treatment is tailored to the dominant clinical features. Corticosteroids are often effective for inflammatory manifestations (myositis, pleuritis, pericarditis, arthritis), while vascular-targeted therapy is essential for Raynaud and PAH.</p>
<p><strong>Raynaud phenomenon and digital ischemia:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Calcium channel blockers</strong> -- <strong>nifedipine ER 30-90 mg</strong>, <strong>amlodipine 5-10 mg</strong> (long-acting dihydropyridines; first-line)</li>
<li><strong>Phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors</strong> -- <strong>sildenafil 25-50 mg TID</strong>, <strong>tadalafil</strong> (second-line; also improves PAH)</li>
<li><strong>Endothelin receptor antagonists</strong> -- <strong>bosentan 62.5 mg BID</strong> (also heals digital ulcers; hepatotoxicity monitoring)</li>
<li><strong>Topical nitrates</strong>, <strong>statins</strong>, <strong>low-dose aspirin</strong>; <strong>IV prostacyclin analogs</strong> (epoprostenol, iloprost) for severe/refractory</li>
<li><strong>Smoking cessation</strong> and cold protection (gloves, hand warmers)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Arthritis and lupus-like features:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Hydroxychloroquine</strong> 5 mg/kg/day -- first-line for cutaneous and articular manifestations</li>
<li><strong>NSAIDs</strong> for mild arthralgia; <strong>methotrexate</strong> 15-25 mg/week for synovitis</li>
<li><strong>Low-dose prednisone</strong> (5-10 mg/day) for flares</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Inflammatory myositis:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>High-dose prednisone</strong> (1 mg/kg/day) -- first-line; taper slowly</li>
<li><strong>Methotrexate</strong>, <strong>azathioprine</strong>, or <strong>mycophenolate mofetil</strong> -- steroid-sparing</li>
<li><strong>Intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG)</strong> for refractory or severe myositis</li>
<li><strong>Rituximab</strong> for refractory disease</li>
<li>Physical therapy to prevent contractures and weakness</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) -- the leading cause of death; treat aggressively:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Endothelin receptor antagonists</strong> -- bosentan, ambrisentan, macitentan</li>
<li><strong>Phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors</strong> -- sildenafil, tadalafil</li>
<li><strong>Soluble guanylate cyclase stimulator</strong> -- riociguat</li>
<li><strong>Prostacyclin analogs</strong> -- inhaled iloprost, treprostinil; IV epoprostenol for severe (class IV)</li>
<li><strong>Calcium channel blockers</strong> -- only for vasoreactive idiopathic PAH; not typically in MCTD-PAH</li>
<li>Combination therapy is now standard for moderate-severe PAH</li>
<li><strong>Annual screening echocardiography</strong> in all MCTD patients (early detection of PAH)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Esophageal involvement:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Proton pump inhibitors</strong> (omeprazole, pantoprazole) for reflux</li>
<li><strong>Prokinetics</strong> (metoclopramide, domperidone) for dysmotility</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Scleroderma renal crisis (rare but life-threatening):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>ACE inhibitor</strong> (<strong>captopril</strong>) -- mandatory, even in acute renal failure (paradoxical; saves kidney)</li>
<li>Avoid high-dose steroids (precipitate renal crisis)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Other organ involvement:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Pleuritis, pericarditis</strong> -- NSAIDs or short-course steroids</li>
<li><strong>Interstitial lung disease</strong> -- mycophenolate mofetil or cyclophosphamide; <strong>nintedanib</strong> for progressive fibrotic ILD</li>
<li><strong>Sicca syndrome</strong> -- artificial tears, pilocarpine</li>
</ul>
