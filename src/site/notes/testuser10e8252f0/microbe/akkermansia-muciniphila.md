<h1>Akkermansia muciniphila</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Mucin-degrading gut bacterium that strengthens intestinal barrier function and is consistently depleted in obesity, type 2 diabetes, IBD, and metabolic syndrome. Considered a next-generation probiotic; pasteurized A. muciniphila has shown improvement of insulin sensitivity in human RCT (Depommier et al., Nature Medicine 2019).</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level:</strong> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (4/5)</p>
<h2>Taxonomy</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>NCBI Taxonomy ID:</strong> <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Taxonomy/Browser/wwwtax.cgi?id=239935">239935</a></li>
<li><strong>Rank:</strong> Species</li>
<li><strong>Parent Taxon:</strong> Akkermansia (genus, family Akkermansiaceae, phylum Verrucomicrobiota)</li>
<li><strong>Gram Stain:</strong> Negative</li>
</ul>
<h2>Habitat</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Body Sites:</strong> gut (lives within the mucus layer of the colon)</li>
<li><strong>Oxygen Requirement:</strong> Obligate anaerobe</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Role</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Pathogenicity:</strong> Beneficial commensal</li>
<li><strong>Key Metabolites Produced:</strong> propionate, acetate</li>
</ul>
<p>Increases mucin turnover (paradoxically thickening the mucus barrier despite consuming it), upregulates tight-junction proteins (occludin, claudin-3), reduces LPS translocation, and improves insulin sensitivity.</p>
