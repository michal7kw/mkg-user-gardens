<h1>L-Glutamine</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>L-Glutamine is the most abundant free amino acid in human blood and muscle, serving as the <strong>primary fuel for enterocytes</strong> and <strong>immune cells</strong>. It is <strong>conditionally essential</strong> during metabolic stress (surgery, sepsis, burns, intense exercise). Supplementation supports gut barrier integrity, immune function, and muscle recovery.</p>
<h2>Clinical Applications</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Critical illness</strong> -- IV glutamine in parenteral nutrition (mixed evidence; REDOX trial)</li>
<li><strong>Inflammatory bowel disease</strong> -- may support mucosal healing (limited evidence)</li>
<li><strong>Exercise recovery</strong> -- reduces muscle soreness (modest evidence)</li>
<li><strong>Leaky gut</strong> -- supports tight junction integrity (preclinical)</li>
<li><strong>Burns and wound healing</strong> -- standard in burn center nutrition protocols</li>
</ul>
<h2>Dosage</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>General</strong>: 5 g, 1-3 times daily</li>
<li><strong>Exercise recovery</strong>: 5 g post-workout</li>
<li><strong>Safety</strong>: generally well tolerated; contraindicated in liver failure</li>
</ul>
