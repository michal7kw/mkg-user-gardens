<h1>Collagen Peptides</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Collagen peptides (hydrolysed collagen) are short chains of amino acids produced by enzymatically breaking down collagen — the main structural protein of skin, tendon, cartilage and bone. Unlike the injectable &quot;research peptides&quot;, collagen peptides are an <strong>ingested dietary protein</strong>, taken as a powder or capsule for skin and connective-tissue support. They are the <strong>best-evidenced peptide product a consumer is likely to buy</strong>, and in Poland they sit in a completely different regulatory box from injectable peptides: they are a <strong>food supplement (suplement diety) overseen by GIS/Sanepid</strong>, freely available without prescription, rather than a medicine regulated by URPL.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>Hydrolysing collagen yields small di- and tripeptides (notably hydroxyproline-containing peptides such as Pro-Hyp) that are absorbed largely intact and can be detected in blood after oral intake. Two mechanisms are proposed: these peptides supply the specific amino-acid building blocks for the body's own collagen synthesis, and some appear to act as <strong>signalling fragments</strong> that stimulate dermal fibroblasts and chondrocytes to produce more extracellular matrix. <a href="/garden/itest542bad04/supplement/vitamin-c/" class="internal-link">Vitamin C</a> is a required cofactor for endogenous collagen synthesis, which is why the two are often paired. Glycine, the most abundant amino acid in collagen, links this note to <a href="/garden/itest542bad04/supplement/glycine/" class="internal-link">Glycine</a>.</p>
<h2>Dosage</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Skin (elasticity/hydration)</strong>: 2.5–10 g/day of hydrolysed collagen for 8–12 weeks in most trials</li>
<li><strong>Joint/connective tissue</strong>: 5–15 g/day</li>
<li><strong>Safety</strong>: excellent — a well-tolerated food protein; mild digestive upset is the main complaint</li>
<li><strong>Sources</strong>: bovine, porcine or marine (fish) collagen; marine tends to be more bioavailable</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Evidence</h2>
<p>Multiple randomised, placebo-controlled trials and reviews report <strong>modest but real improvements in skin elasticity, hydration and wrinkle appearance</strong> with oral collagen peptides, and some benefit for joint comfort. Effects are formulation- and dose-dependent and generally require 8–12 weeks. This moderate, trial-backed evidence is why the <code>evidence_level</code> here is <strong>3</strong> — higher than the level-1 research peptides but below the level-4/5 approved drug peptides.</p>
