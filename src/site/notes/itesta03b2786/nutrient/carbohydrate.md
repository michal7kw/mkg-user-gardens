<h1>Carbohydrate</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Carbohydrates are the body's primary and preferred source of energy, providing 4 kilocalories per gram. They are broken down into glucose, which fuels cellular metabolism, brain function, and muscular activity. The brain alone consumes approximately 120 grams of glucose daily, making carbohydrates especially critical for cognitive performance.</p>
<p>Carbohydrates are classified as simple (monosaccharides and disaccharides) or complex (polysaccharides including starches and fiber). The glycemic index (GI) and glycemic load (GL) measure how rapidly a carbohydrate source raises blood glucose, with lower-GI foods promoting more stable energy and insulin responses. Complex carbohydrates from whole grains, legumes, and vegetables provide sustained energy alongside fiber, vitamins, and minerals.</p>
<p>Carbohydrate metabolism is regulated by insulin and glucagon, maintaining blood glucose within a narrow physiological range. Chronic overconsumption of refined carbohydrates contributes to insulin resistance, metabolic syndrome, and type 2 diabetes, while strategic carbohydrate timing supports athletic performance and recovery.</p>
<h2>Key Functions</h2>
<ul>
<li>Provides glucose as the primary fuel for brain, red blood cells, and muscles</li>
<li>Supplies glycogen for hepatic and muscular energy reserves</li>
<li>Spares protein from being used as an energy source</li>
<li>Supports gut microbiome health through prebiotic fibers</li>
<li>Enables high-intensity exercise performance via anaerobic glycolysis</li>
<li>Modulates serotonin synthesis and mood regulation via tryptophan transport</li>
</ul>
<h2>Dietary Sources</h2>
<ul>
<li>Whole grains (oats, brown rice, quinoa, barley)</li>
<li>Fruits (bananas, berries, apples, oranges)</li>
<li>Starchy vegetables (sweet potatoes, potatoes, corn)</li>
<li>Legumes (lentils, chickpeas, black beans)</li>
<li>Bread and pasta (preferably whole-grain)</li>
<li>Dairy (lactose in milk and yogurt)</li>
<li>Honey and maple syrup (simple sugars)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Deficiency and Excess</h2>
<p>Very low carbohydrate intake forces the body into ketosis, relying on fat-derived ketone bodies for energy. While therapeutic in some contexts (epilepsy, specific metabolic conditions), prolonged carbohydrate restriction can cause fatigue, brain fog, constipation, and impaired high-intensity exercise performance.</p>
<p>Excessive intake of refined carbohydrates drives hyperglycemia, hyperinsulinemia, elevated triglycerides, weight gain, and increased risk of type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease. High sugar intake also promotes dental caries and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.</p>
