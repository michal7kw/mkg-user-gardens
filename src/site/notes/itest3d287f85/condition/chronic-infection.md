<h1>Chronic Infection</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Chronic infection refers to the <strong>persistent presence of a pathogen in the host</strong> for weeks, months, or a lifetime, with ongoing immune activation that produces cumulative tissue damage. Unlike acute infections -- which are typically cleared by the immune system or kill the host within days to weeks -- chronic infections establish a <strong>dynamic equilibrium</strong> between pathogen and host: the organism evades clearance through latency (herpesviruses, TB), antigenic variation (HIV, hepatitis C), biofilm formation (indwelling-device infections), or immunosuppression (HIV), while the host mounts a smoldering inflammatory response that cannot fully eradicate the pathogen.</p>
<p>The major global chronic infections include <strong>tuberculosis</strong> (latency in ~25% of the world's population; reactivation in ~10%), <strong>HIV</strong> (lifelong without antiretroviral therapy), <strong>chronic viral hepatitis B and C</strong> (affecting ~300 million; leading causes of cirrhosis and liver cancer), <strong>endocarditis</strong> (subacute from viridans streptococci or HACEK organisms), <strong>osteomyelitis</strong> (often polymicrobial, requires weeks of antibiotics and surgical debridement), and <strong>latent viral infections</strong> (herpes simplex, varicella-zoster, EBV, CMV). <strong>Healthcare-associated chronic infections</strong> -- catheter-related bacteremia, prosthetic-joint infection, chronic ventilator-associated pneumonia -- are growing problems in modern medicine, driven by biofilm-forming organisms (<em>Staphylococcus epidermidis</em>, <em>Pseudomonas aeruginosa</em>) that are refractory to standard antibiotic courses.</p>
<p>The clinical consequences extend beyond the infected organ. Chronic infections produce a <strong>systemic inflammatory state</strong> (elevated CRP, IL-6, TNF-alpha) that drives <strong>anemia of chronic disease</strong> (hepcidin-mediated iron sequestration), <strong>cachexia and sarcopenia</strong> (cytokine-driven catabolism), <strong>secondary amyloidosis</strong> (AA amyloid from chronic serum amyloid A elevation; causes nephrotic-range proteinuria), and <strong>accelerated atherosclerosis</strong> (inflammation destabilizes plaques). Certain chronic infections are directly <strong>oncogenic</strong>: HBV and HCV cause hepatocellular carcinoma, HPV causes cervical and oropharyngeal cancer, EBV causes Burkitt and Hodgkin lymphoma, and <em>H. pylori</em> causes gastric cancer and MALT lymphoma. Diagnosis integrates the clinical syndrome, <strong>exposure history</strong> (travel, occupation, sexual, healthcare), and <strong>microbiologic testing</strong> (cultures, serology, PCR, antigen detection, biopsy with special stains).</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Establish the diagnosis:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Cultures</strong> -- blood, urine, sputum, tissue, CSF; the foundation for bacterial and fungal diagnosis</li>
<li><strong>Molecular tests</strong> -- PCR (viral load for HIV, HBV, HCV; molecular assays for TB, atypical organisms)</li>
<li><strong>Serology</strong> -- antibody tests (EBV, CMV, Lyme, syphilis, hepatitis); paired acute and convalescent titers</li>
<li><strong>Antigen detection</strong> -- HBsAg, HCV core antigen, cryptococcal antigen, galactomannan, beta-D-glucan</li>
<li><strong>Imaging and biopsy</strong> -- CT/MRI for abscess, osteomyelitis, endocarditis (vegetations on echo); histopathology with special stains (AFB, GMS, Gram)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Targeted antimicrobial therapy (prolonged courses are typical):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Tuberculosis</strong> -- 4-drug RIPE regimen for 2 months, then 2 drugs for 4-7 months (6-9 months total); directly observed therapy improves adherence</li>
<li><strong>HIV</strong> -- lifelong combination antiretroviral therapy (3 drugs; INSTI-based first-line: dolutegravir/tenofovir/emtricitabine)</li>
<li><strong>Hepatitis B</strong> -- nucleos(t)ide analogs with high barrier to resistance (tenofovir, entecavir); lifelong in most</li>
<li><strong>Hepatitis C</strong> -- direct-acting antivirals (sofosbuvir/velpatasvir, glecaprevir/pibrentasvir); 8-12 weeks cure &gt;95%</li>
<li><strong>Endocarditis</strong> -- 4-6 weeks IV antibiotics guided by organism and susceptibilities</li>
<li><strong>Osteomyelitis</strong> -- 4-6 weeks IV then oral; surgical debridement often required</li>
<li><strong>Biofilm/device infections</strong> -- remove the device when possible; prolonged combination therapy</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Source control:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Drain abscesses (percutaneous or surgical)</li>
<li>Remove infected indwelling devices (catheters, prosthetic joints, heart valves)</li>
<li>Debride necrotic tissue (surgical for osteomyelitis, necrotizing fasciitis)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Address host factors:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Reduce or modulate immunosuppression when possible (discontinue biologics, lower steroid dose)</li>
<li>Glycemic control in diabetes</li>
<li>Nutritional support (protein-calorie repletion; treat anemia of chronic disease)</li>
<li>IVIG in primary immunodeficiency; G-CSF in neutropenia</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Monitor and prevent complications:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Drug toxicity monitoring (hepatotoxicity, nephrotoxicity, QT prolongation)</li>
<li>Drug interactions (especially antiretrovirals, rifamycins, azoles)</li>
<li>Screening for secondary malignancy (HCC surveillance with ultrasound + AFP every 6 months in cirrhosis)</li>
<li>Vaccinate against co-infections (HBV in HCV; pneumococcal, influenza in all)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Public health:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Reportable disease notification</li>
<li>Contact tracing (TB, sexually transmitted infections, HBV/HCV, HIV)</li>
<li>Vaccination of contacts and at-risk populations</li>
</ul>
