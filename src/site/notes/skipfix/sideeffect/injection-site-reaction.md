<h1>Injection Site Reaction</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Injection site reactions (ISRs) are localised adverse effects occurring at the site of a subcutaneous or intramuscular injection. They span a spectrum from mild, self-limiting redness (erythema), swelling, itching, bruising and pain, through to firm nodules, sterile abscesses, lipoatrophy, and — when sterility is not maintained — bacterial infection, cellulitis or abscess formation. ISRs are among the most common adverse effects of injectable peptides and biologic drugs.</p>
<p>For approved injectable peptides such as the GLP-1 receptor agonists (<a href="/garden/skipfix/drug/semaglutide/" class="internal-link">Semaglutide</a>, <a href="/garden/skipfix/drug/tirzepatide/" class="internal-link">Tirzepatide</a>), ISRs are usually mild and transient, and the products are supplied sterile with clear injection guidance. The risk profile is very different for unregulated, compounded or gray-market &quot;research peptides&quot; (<a href="/garden/skipfix/drug/bpc-157/" class="internal-link">BPC-157</a>, <a href="/garden/skipfix/drug/tb-500/" class="internal-link">TB-500</a>, <a href="/garden/skipfix/drug/cjc-1295/" class="internal-link">CJC-1295</a>, <a href="/garden/skipfix/drug/ipamorelin/" class="internal-link">Ipamorelin</a>), which are frequently self-reconstituted from lyophilised powder and self-injected without medical supervision. Here ISRs are compounded by unknown purity, potential bacterial or endotoxin contamination, incorrect reconstitution, and non-sterile technique — turning a normally minor reaction into a route for serious infection. This is a core reason regulators including the FDA, and in Poland the URPL and GIF (Główny Inspektorat Farmaceutyczny), warn against self-injection of unauthorised peptides.</p>
<h2>Key Points</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Mild/common</strong>: erythema, swelling, pruritus, bruising, transient pain — usually resolve within hours to days</li>
<li><strong>Moderate</strong>: persistent nodules, induration, sterile inflammation, lipoatrophy at repeated sites</li>
<li><strong>Serious (sterility-dependent)</strong>: bacterial infection, cellulitis, abscess, systemic infection — the dominant risk with non-sterile compounded/gray-market product</li>
<li><strong>Mitigation</strong>: sterile single-use needles, rotation of injection sites, correct reconstitution, and — for approved products — following the manufacturer's instructions; unregulated self-injection removes all of these safeguards</li>
</ul>
