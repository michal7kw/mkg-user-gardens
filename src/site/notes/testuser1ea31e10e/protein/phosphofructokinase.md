<h1>Phosphofructokinase</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Phosphofructokinase-1 (PFK-1) is the rate-limiting and committed-step enzyme of <a href="/garden/testuser1ea31e10e/pathway/glycolysis/" class="internal-link">Glycolysis</a>, catalysing the phosphorylation of <a href="/garden/testuser1ea31e10e/metabolite/fructose-6-phosphate/" class="internal-link">Fructose-6-Phosphate</a> to <a href="/garden/testuser1ea31e10e/metabolite/fructose-16-bisphosphate/" class="internal-link">Fructose-1,6-Bisphosphate</a> using one <a href="/garden/testuser1ea31e10e/metabolite/atp/" class="internal-link">ATP</a>. It is the single most important regulatory point of glycolytic flux, acting as a metabolic sensor that integrates cellular energy charge, pH, and hormonal signals to decide whether glucose carbon is committed to breakdown. Because the reaction it catalyses is essentially irreversible, PFK-1 marks the true &quot;point of no return&quot; of <a href="/garden/testuser1ea31e10e/pathway/glycolysis/" class="internal-link">Glycolysis</a>, downstream of <a href="/garden/testuser1ea31e10e/protein/hexokinase/" class="internal-link">Hexokinase</a>.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>PFK-1 catalyses the transfer of a phosphate group from <a href="/garden/testuser1ea31e10e/metabolite/atp/" class="internal-link">ATP</a> to the C1 hydroxyl of <a href="/garden/testuser1ea31e10e/metabolite/fructose-6-phosphate/" class="internal-link">Fructose-6-Phosphate</a>, producing <a href="/garden/testuser1ea31e10e/metabolite/fructose-16-bisphosphate/" class="internal-link">Fructose-1,6-Bisphosphate</a> and <a href="/garden/testuser1ea31e10e/metabolite/adp/" class="internal-link">ADP</a>. This is the committed, irreversible step of the pathway. The enzyme is a homotetramer subject to extensive <strong>allosteric regulation</strong>: it is inhibited by high <a href="/garden/testuser1ea31e10e/metabolite/atp/" class="internal-link">ATP</a> (signalling abundant energy) at a regulatory site distinct from the catalytic site, and this inhibition is reinforced by <a href="/garden/testuser1ea31e10e/metabolite/citrate/" class="internal-link">Citrate</a>, which signals that the <a href="/garden/testuser1ea31e10e/pathway/citric-acid-cycle/" class="internal-link">Citric Acid Cycle</a> is well supplied. Conversely, the enzyme is activated by <a href="/garden/testuser1ea31e10e/metabolite/adp/" class="internal-link">ADP</a> and AMP, which accumulate when energy is scarce, and by fructose-2,6-bisphosphate, the most potent physiological activator (produced under insulin signalling). This reciprocal control allows PFK-1 to dial glycolytic flux up or down in response to the cell's instantaneous energy demand.</p>
<h2>Biological Role</h2>
<p>By producing <a href="/garden/testuser1ea31e10e/metabolite/fructose-16-bisphosphate/" class="internal-link">Fructose-1,6-Bisphosphate</a>, PFK-1 commits glucose carbon to the energy-yielding &quot;payoff phase&quot; of <a href="/garden/testuser1ea31e10e/pathway/glycolysis/" class="internal-link">Glycolysis</a>, ultimately feeding <a class="internal-link is-unresolved" href="/404">Glyceraldehyde-3-Phosphate</a> and downstream production of <a href="/garden/testuser1ea31e10e/metabolite/pyruvate/" class="internal-link">Pyruvate</a> and <a href="/garden/testuser1ea31e10e/metabolite/atp/" class="internal-link">ATP</a>. The reaction CONSUMES one <a href="/garden/testuser1ea31e10e/metabolite/atp/" class="internal-link">ATP</a> as part of the investment phase. PFK-1 sits at the convergence of catabolic and anabolic signals: its inhibition by <a href="/garden/testuser1ea31e10e/metabolite/atp/" class="internal-link">ATP</a> and <a href="/garden/testuser1ea31e10e/metabolite/citrate/" class="internal-link">Citrate</a> couples glycolysis to the energetic and biosynthetic status reported by the <a href="/garden/testuser1ea31e10e/pathway/citric-acid-cycle/" class="internal-link">Citric Acid Cycle</a>, while its activation by <a href="/garden/testuser1ea31e10e/metabolite/adp/" class="internal-link">ADP</a>/AMP — and indirectly by the energy sensor <a href="/garden/testuser1ea31e10e/protein/ampk/" class="internal-link">AMPK</a> (which raises fructose-2,6-bisphosphate) — accelerates flux during energy stress.</p>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>Inherited deficiency of the muscle PFK isoform (PFKM) causes <strong>Tarui disease (glycogen storage disease type VII)</strong>, presenting with exercise intolerance, myalgia, cramps, and exertional myopathy because muscle cannot adequately sustain glycolytic ATP supply. As the pacemaker of glycolysis, PFK-1 is also a key node in the Warburg effect; up-regulation of PFK activity and its regulator PFKFB3 supports the elevated glycolytic flux of proliferating tumour cells, making this regulatory axis a target of interest in cancer metabolism research. Its sensitivity to pH also links PFK-1 to the inhibition of glycolysis under acidotic conditions such as intense anaerobic exertion.</p>
