<h1>Ovarian Dysfunction</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Ovarian dysfunction encompasses a heterogeneous group of disorders in which the ovaries fail to produce eggs or the appropriate balance of hormones (estrogen, progesterone, and androgens), producing <strong>menstrual irregularity, infertility, and systemic consequences</strong>. The major entities are <strong>polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)</strong>, <strong>premature ovarian insufficiency (POI)</strong>, <strong>hypothalamic amenorrhea</strong>, and <strong>functional ovarian disorders</strong>. Together these affect a substantial fraction of reproductive-age women and have implications far beyond fertility -- they shape <strong>cardiovascular, bone, metabolic, and psychological health</strong> across the lifespan.</p>
<p><strong>Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)</strong> is by far the most common cause, affecting <strong>6-12% of reproductive-age women</strong> (using NIH or Rotterdam criteria) and the leading cause of anovulatory infertility. PCOS is a hyperandrogenic, oligo-ovulatory syndrome with strong <strong>insulin resistance</strong> (~70-80%); it increases risks of <strong>type 2 diabetes</strong> (4x), <strong>metabolic syndrome</strong>, <strong>cardiovascular disease</strong>, <strong>endometrial cancer</strong> (from unopposed estrogen), and <strong>obstructive sleep apnea</strong>. The <strong>Rotterdam criteria</strong> require 2 of 3: oligo/anovulation, clinical or biochemical hyperandrogenism, and polycystic ovarian morphology on ultrasound. Heritability is ~70%, with loci in androgen biosynthesis and insulin signaling.</p>
<p><strong>Premature ovarian insufficiency (POI)</strong> -- loss of ovarian function before age 40 -- affects ~<strong>1% of women</strong> and presents with <strong>amenorrhea, hypoestrogenic symptoms (hot flashes, vaginal dryness), and elevated FSH (&gt;25 mIU/mL on two occasions)</strong>. Causes include <strong>genetic</strong> (Turner syndrome, Fragile X premutation, gonadal dysgenesis), <strong>autoimmune</strong> (autoimmune oophoritis, often with Hashimoto thyroiditis or Addison disease), <strong>iatrogenic</strong> (chemotherapy, radiation, oophorectomy), and <strong>idiopathic</strong> (~90% of cases). POI is distinct from natural menopause -- <strong>~5-10% of women with POI conceive spontaneously</strong> -- and HRT is recommended at least until the average age of natural menopause (51) to protect bone and cardiovascular health.</p>
<p><strong>Hypothalamic amenorrhea</strong> results from suppression of hypothalamic GnRH pulsatility by <strong>stress, excessive exercise, low body weight, or eating disorders</strong>. It is <strong>functional</strong> (reversible with correction of the underlying trigger) and represents a state of <strong>chronic hypoestrogenism</strong> with significant bone and cardiovascular consequences. Diagnosis requires excluding other causes (pregnancy, thyroid, prolactin, POI) and demonstrating low FSH/LH and low/normal estradiol.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Lifestyle modification</strong> -- <strong>weight loss of 5-10%</strong> restores spontaneous ovulation in ~30-60% of overweight women with PCOS; calorie reduction, low glycemic-index diet, regular exercise (150 min/week)</li>
<li><strong>Combined hormonal contraceptives</strong> -- first-line for cycle regulation, hirsutism, acne, and endometrial protection (prevents unopposed-estrogen hyperplasia)</li>
<li><strong>Metformin</strong> 1500-2000 mg/day -- for insulin resistance; improves ovulation, menstrual regularity, and metabolic parameters; not first-line for hirsutism</li>
<li><strong>Ovulation induction</strong> -- <strong>letrozole</strong> (2.5-7.5 mg days 3-7 of cycle) is <strong>first-line</strong> (more effective and safer than clomiphene; PPCOS trial); <strong>clomiphene</strong> as alternative; <strong>gonadotropins</strong> for refractory cases (specialist; OHSS risk)</li>
<li><strong>Hirsutism and acne</strong> -- <strong>spironolactone</strong> 50-200 mg/day (anti-androgen; combine with contraceptive to prevent pregnancy and menorrhagia), <strong>eflornithine</strong> topical, laser hair removal</li>
<li><strong>Inositol</strong> (myo-inositol 2-4 g/day) -- modest benefit for ovulation and insulin sensitivity</li>
<li><strong>Bariatric surgery</strong> for severe obesity with PCOS</li>
<li><strong>IVF</strong> if ovulation induction fails</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Premature ovarian insufficiency (POI):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Hormone replacement therapy (HRT)</strong> -- <strong>physiologic estrogen</strong> (transdermal estradiol 100 mcg/24h or oral estradiol 1-2 mg) + <strong>cyclic progestin</strong> (micronized progesterone 200 mg x12 days/month); continue at least until age 51 (average menopause) to protect bone and cardiovascular health</li>
<li><strong>Address fertility</strong> -- <strong>spontaneous ovulation</strong> may occur (5-10%); <strong>IVF with donor eggs</strong> is the most reliable path to pregnancy; <strong>fertility preservation</strong> should be offered before gonadotoxic therapy</li>
<li><strong>Screen for</strong> -- autoimmune disease (thyroid, adrenal antibodies; morning cortisol), <strong>FMR1 premutation</strong> (genetic counseling; risk of fragile X in offspring), <strong>karyotype</strong> (Turner)</li>
<li><strong>Bone health</strong> -- calcium 1000-1200 mg/day, vitamin D 800-2000 IU/day, weight-bearing exercise, DEXA at baseline</li>
<li><strong>Psychological support</strong> -- diagnosis is devastating; counseling and support groups</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Hypothalamic amenorrhea:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Address the underlying cause</strong> -- reduce exercise intensity, gain weight, treat eating disorder, manage stress</li>
<li><strong>Nutritional rehabilitation</strong> -- restore body weight to threshold (~90% ideal body weight usually restores menses)</li>
<li><strong>Cognitive behavioral therapy</strong> (CBT) has evidence for restoring menses</li>
<li><strong>HRT</strong> if weight restoration fails or during the recovery period (bone protection)</li>
<li><strong>Reassess for</strong> anorexia nervosa complications (cardiac, electrolytes, bone)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Bone health (all hypoestrogenic states):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Calcium</strong> 1000-1200 mg/day (dietary preferred)</li>
<li><strong>Vitamin D</strong> 800-2000 IU/day (target 25-OH vitamin D &gt;30 ng/mL)</li>
<li><strong>Weight-bearing exercise</strong> 30 min x 3-5 days/week</li>
<li><strong>DEXA</strong> at diagnosis; bisphosphonate if osteoporosis and not planning pregnancy</li>
</ul>
