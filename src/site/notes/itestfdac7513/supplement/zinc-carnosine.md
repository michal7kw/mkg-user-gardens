<h1>Zinc Carnosine</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Zinc carnosine (polaprezinc) is a 1:1 chelate of zinc and the dipeptide L-carnosine. Its defining property is that the complex remains intact at gastric pH and adheres preferentially to damaged or inflamed mucosal surfaces, where it acts as a localized delivery vehicle for zinc rather than being absorbed systemically like conventional zinc salts. This mucoadhesive behavior underlies its use as a gastric cytoprotective agent, a role for which it has been a prescription medicine in Japan since 1994.</p>
<h2>Mechanisms</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Mucosal adhesion and repair</strong>: Binds ulcerated tissue and promotes epithelial cell migration and proliferation</li>
<li><strong>Antioxidant and membrane stabilization</strong>: Scavenges reactive oxygen species and stabilizes mast-cell and epithelial membranes</li>
<li><strong>Tight-junction support</strong>: Reduces small-intestinal hyperpermeability (&quot;leaky gut&quot;), shown in NSAID-induced injury models</li>
<li><strong>Heat-shock protein induction</strong>: Enhances cytoprotective stress-response proteins in gastric epithelium</li>
</ul>
<h2>Evidence</h2>
<p>Controlled studies, predominantly from Japan, support a role for polaprezinc as an adjunct to standard triple therapy for Helicobacter pylori eradication and in healing of gastric ulcers. Smaller human studies show reduced gastrointestinal permeability and protection against NSAID-induced injury. Overall evidence quality is moderate and trials are often small.</p>
