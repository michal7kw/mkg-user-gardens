<h1>Plant Sterols</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Plant sterols and stanols (<strong>phytosterols</strong>) are cholesterol-like compounds found naturally in vegetable oils, nuts, seeds, and grains, and added to fortified foods and supplements. At an intake of <strong>~2 g/day</strong> they lower LDL cholesterol by roughly <strong>8-10%</strong> by blocking intestinal cholesterol absorption — one of the best-evidenced non-drug lipid levers, endorsed by ESC/EAS and NLA guidelines as an adjunct to diet and statins.</p>
<p>Because they act on absorption rather than synthesis, their effect is largely <strong>additive to statins</strong> (which act on synthesis) and to soluble fiber. They are a strong evidence-graded recommendation for elevated LDL/ApoB.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>Phytosterols competitively displace cholesterol from intestinal mixed micelles, reducing NPC1L1-mediated cholesterol uptake by enterocytes and increasing fecal cholesterol loss. Lower cholesterol delivery to the liver upregulates hepatic LDL receptors, enhancing clearance of circulating LDL/ApoB particles. The effect is dose-dependent and plateaus around 2-3 g/day.</p>
<h2>Dosage</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Effective dose:</strong> 2 g/day (sterol/stanol esters) with fat-containing meals.</li>
<li><strong>Effect size:</strong> ~8-10% LDL-C reduction; additive to statins and diet.</li>
<li><strong>Onset:</strong> ~3-4 weeks; re-test lipids/ApoB at 8-12 weeks.</li>
</ul>
