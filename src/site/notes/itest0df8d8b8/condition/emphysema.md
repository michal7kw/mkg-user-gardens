<h1>Emphysema</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Emphysema is a chronic progressive lung disease characterized by <strong>permanent enlargement of airspaces distal to the terminal bronchiole, accompanied by destruction of alveolar walls and loss of elastic recoil</strong>. This parenchymal destruction leads to irreversible airflow obstruction, air trapping (hyperinflation), and loss of gas exchange surface area. Together with chronic bronchitis, emphysema is one of the two principal phenotypes of <strong>Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)</strong>, the third leading cause of death worldwide.</p>
<p>The pathogenesis is driven by an <strong>imbalance between proteases (especially neutrophil elastase and matrix metalloproteinases) and antiproteases (especially alpha-1 antitrypsin)</strong>, combined with chronic oxidative stress and inflammation from cigarette smoke. The result is progressive destruction of the extracellular matrix of the alveolar wall, leading to airspace enlargement, bullae formation, and loss of elastic recoil. Airway collapse on expiration causes air trapping and hyperinflation, increasing the work of breathing.</p>
<p>The principal cause is <strong>cigarette smoking</strong> (~80-90% of cases). <strong>Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency (AATD)</strong> is a genetic cause responsible for ~1-3% of COPD cases; it should be suspected in younger patients (&lt;45 years), non-smokers, or those with basal-predominant emphysema and liver disease. Other risk factors include occupational exposures, indoor biomass fuel (developing countries), and air pollution.</p>
<h2>Clinical Presentation</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Progressive exertional dyspnea</strong> (hallmark; typically insidious onset over years)</li>
<li>Chronic cough (often productive)</li>
<li>Wheezing (especially with exertion or exacerbations)</li>
<li><strong>Barrel chest</strong> (increased AP chest diameter from hyperinflation)</li>
<li><strong>Pursed-lip breathing</strong> (auto-PEEP mechanism to prevent airway collapse)</li>
<li>Prolonged expiratory phase</li>
<li>Use of accessory muscles of respiration</li>
<li>Diminished breath sounds, distant heart sounds (hyperinflation)</li>
<li>Cyanosis, clubbing (advanced disease)</li>
<li>Unintentional weight loss (advanced; from increased work of breathing)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Diagnostic Information</h2>
<p><strong>Spirometry (diagnostic):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC &lt; 0.70</strong> confirms persistent airflow limitation (COPD definition)</li>
<li><strong>GOLD staging:</strong> Grade 1 (mild, FEV1 &gt;=80% predicted), Grade 2 (moderate, 50-79%), Grade 3 (severe, 30-49%), Grade 4 (very severe, &lt;30%)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Imaging:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Chest X-ray:</strong> hyperinflation, flattened diaphragms, increased retrosternal air space, bullae</li>
<li><strong>CT chest:</strong> more sensitive; shows distribution and extent of emphysema (centrilobular, panlobular, paraseptal patterns)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Other:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>DLCO</strong> (diffusing capacity) -- reduced proportional to emphysema severity</li>
<li><strong>Arterial blood gas</strong> -- hypoxemia, hypercapnia (advanced disease)</li>
<li><strong>Alpha-1 antitrypsin level</strong> -- screen all COPD patients for AATD</li>
<li><strong>6-minute walk test</strong> -- functional assessment and oxygen need</li>
</ul>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Non-pharmacologic (most important):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Smoking cessation</strong> -- the single most effective intervention to slow disease progression</li>
<li><strong>Pulmonary rehabilitation</strong> -- exercise training, education, nutritional support; improves dyspnea, exercise capacity, and quality of life</li>
<li><strong>Vaccination</strong> -- influenza annually, pneumococcal (PCV13 + PPSV23), COVID-19, RSV</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Pharmacologic:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Bronchodilators</strong> (mainstay):
<ul>
<li>LAMA (tiotropium, umeclidinium) -- long-acting muscarinic antagonist</li>
<li>LABA (salmeterol, formoterol, indacaterol) -- long-acting beta-agonist</li>
<li>Dual therapy (LAMA/LABA) for moderate-severe disease</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Inhaled corticosteroids</strong> (ICS) -- for patients with exacerbations or eosinophilia; combined with LABA (e.g., fluticasone/salmeterol)</li>
<li><strong>Roflumilast</strong> (PDE4 inhibitor) -- for severe COPD with chronic bronchitis and exacerbations</li>
<li><strong>Theophylline</strong> -- rarely used; narrow therapeutic window</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Oxygen therapy:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Long-term oxygen therapy (LTOT)</strong> for severe resting hypoxemia (PaO2 &lt;55 mmHg or SaO2 &lt;88%) -- improves survival</li>
<li><strong>Ambulatory oxygen</strong> for exertional desaturation</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Advanced therapies:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Endobronchial valves</strong> (Zephyr) -- lung volume reduction in selected patients with severe upper-lobe emphysema</li>
<li><strong>Lung volume reduction surgery</strong> -- for selected patients with heterogeneous upper-lobe emphysema (NETT trial)</li>
<li><strong>Lung transplantation</strong> -- end-stage disease</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Exacerbation management:</strong> systemic corticosteroids (prednisone 40 mg x 5 days), antibiotics (if increased sputum purulence), increased bronchodilator therapy, oxygen, NIV if respiratory failure.</p>
