<h1>Scleroderma</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Scleroderma encompasses a spectrum of rare connective tissue diseases unified by <strong>fibrosis of the skin</strong> (scleroderma = hard skin) and, in systemic forms, <strong>internal organ fibrosis</strong> accompanied by <strong>microvascular damage</strong>. The two major categories are:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Localized scleroderma</strong> (morphea, linear scleroderma): confined to the skin; no internal organ involvement; generally more benign</li>
<li><strong>Systemic sclerosis (SSc)</strong>: involves skin and internal organs; further classified as:
<ul>
<li><strong>Limited cutaneous SSc (lcSSc):</strong> skin thickening distal to elbows/knees; formerly &quot;CREST&quot; syndrome (Calcinosis, Raynaud's, Esophageal dysmotility, Sclerodactyly, Telangiectasia); slower progression; higher risk of pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH)</li>
<li><strong>Diffuse cutaneous SSc (dcSSc):</strong> skin thickening proximal to elbows/knees and trunk; rapid progression; higher risk of renal crisis, ILD, and cardiac involvement</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ol>
<p>The pathogenesis involves three interconnected processes:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Vascular damage:</strong> endothelial cell apoptosis and obliteration of small arteries; presents as <strong>Raynaud's phenomenon</strong> (often years before skin changes)</li>
<li><strong>Immune dysregulation:</strong> autoantibody production (anti-centromere in lcSSc; anti-topoisomerase I/Scl-70 and anti-RNA polymerase III in dcSSc); T-cell and macrophage infiltration</li>
<li><strong>Fibrosis:</strong> fibroblast activation and excessive collagen deposition in skin and organs, driven by TGF-beta and PDGF signaling</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH)</strong> is the leading cause of death in lcSSc, while <strong>interstitial lung disease (ILD)</strong> and <strong>scleroderma renal crisis</strong> are more common in dcSSc. There is no disease-modifying therapy; treatment is <strong>organ-specific</strong>.</p>
<h2>Clinical Presentation</h2>
<p><strong>Raynaud's phenomenon</strong> (90-95%; often first symptom; years before other features):</p>
<ul>
<li>Episodic digital pallor (white) -&gt; cyanosis (blue) -&gt; erythema (red)</li>
<li>Triggered by cold or stress</li>
<li>Nailfold capillaroscopy: dilated, distorted capillary loops</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Skin changes:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Puffy fingers (early) -&gt; skin thickening (sclerodactyly)</li>
<li>Digital pitting scars and ulcers</li>
<li>Facial changes (microstomia, loss of wrinkles, beak-like nose)</li>
<li>Hyper- or hypopigmentation</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Organ-specific symptoms:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>GI:</strong> esophageal dysmotility (reflux, dysphagia), gastric emptying delay, small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO)</li>
<li><strong>Pulmonary:</strong> ILD (dyspnea, dry cough), PAH (exertional dyspnea, fatigue)</li>
<li><strong>Renal:</strong> scleroderma renal crisis (new-onset severe hypertension, AKI, microangiopathic hemolysis)</li>
<li><strong>Cardiac:</strong> myocardial fibrosis, conduction abnormalities, pericardial effusion</li>
</ul>
<h2>Diagnostic Information</h2>
<p><strong>Autoantibodies:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Anti-centromere</strong> (ACA; ~60% of lcSSc; associated with PAH)</li>
<li><strong>Anti-topoisomerase I (Scl-70)</strong> (~30%; dcSSc; associated with ILD)</li>
<li><strong>Anti-RNA polymerase III</strong> (~20%; dcSSc; associated with renal crisis and cancer)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Other tests:</strong> ESR, CRP, CBC (microangiopathic hemolysis in renal crisis), BNP/NT-proBNP (PAH screening), PFTs (DLCO for ILD/PAH), echocardiogram (PAH, cardiac involvement), high-resolution CT (ILD), right heart catheterization (PAH diagnosis)</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>No disease-modifying therapy exists. Treatment is organ-specific:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Raynaud's:</strong> calcium channel blockers (nifedipine, amlodipine), PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil), prostacyclin analogues (iloprost); digital sympathectomy for severe ulcers; bosentan for ulcer prevention</li>
<li><strong>Skin:</strong> methotrexate, mycophenolate (limited evidence); physical therapy for contractures</li>
<li><strong>ILD:</strong> mycophenolate mofetil or cyclophosphamide; nintedanib (antifibrotic; approved for SSc-ILD); lung transplant for end-stage</li>
<li><strong>PAH:</strong> endothelin receptor antagonists (bosentan, ambrisentan), PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil, tadalafil), prostacyclins (epoprostenol), riociguat (sGC stimulator)</li>
<li><strong>Renal crisis:</strong> ACE inhibitors (captopril; life-saving; even if AKI), dialysis for renal failure</li>
<li><strong>GI:</strong> PPIs (reflux), prokinetics (domperidone), antibiotics for SIBO</li>
<li><strong>Immunosuppression:</strong> autologous hematopoietic stem cell transplant for severe rapidly progressive dcSSc (selected patients)</li>
</ul>
