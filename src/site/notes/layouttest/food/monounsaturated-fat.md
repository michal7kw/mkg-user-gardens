<h1>Monounsaturated Fat</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Monounsaturated fat (MUFA) refers to dietary fats whose constituent fatty acids carry exactly one carbon-carbon double bond. The predominant dietary MUFA is oleic acid (C18:1, an omega-9 fatty acid), with palmitoleic acid a minor contributor. MUFAs are liquid at room temperature but more oxidatively stable than polyunsaturated fats, and are concentrated in extra virgin olive oil, avocados, and tree nuts. MUFA-rich fats are the defining fat source of the Mediterranean dietary pattern.</p>
<h2>Nutrient Profile</h2>
<p>Like all fats, MUFA provides ~9 kcal/g. Its nutritional distinction is qualitative rather than caloric: replacing saturated fatty acids or refined carbohydrate with monounsaturated fat shifts the blood-lipid profile favorably (lowering LDL cholesterol while largely preserving HDL cholesterol). Whole-food MUFA sources such as olive oil and nuts also deliver accompanying polyphenols and tocopherols.</p>
<h2>Health Effects</h2>
<p>In large dietary trials and meta-analyses, diets emphasizing monounsaturated fat — exemplified by Mediterranean-style eating enriched with extra virgin olive oil — are associated with reduced cardiovascular events and improvements in LDL cholesterol and triglycerides relative to control diets (Estruch et al. 2018). The benefit is most consistent when MUFA displaces saturated fat or refined carbohydrate rather than being added to the diet.</p>
