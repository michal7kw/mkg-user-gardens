<h1>Epa</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA; 20:5n-3) is a 20-carbon omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acid obtained primarily from fatty fish (salmon, mackerel, sardines, anchovies, herring) and marine-derived supplements (fish oil, krill oil, algal oil). EPA is one of the two principal long-chain omega-3 fatty acids measured clinically (alongside DHA / docosahexaenoic acid). Both EPA and DHA are essential for cardiovascular, neurologic, and immune function -- the human body can synthesize only small amounts from the precursor alpha-linolenic acid (ALA; conversion rate ~5-10%), so dietary intake is the principal source.</p>
<p>EPA serves as the <strong>direct precursor to anti-inflammatory and pro-resolving lipid mediators</strong>:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Prostaglandins of the 3-series (PGE3)</strong> -- anti-inflammatory</li>
<li><strong>Leukotrienes of the 5-series (LTB5)</strong> -- less pro-inflammatory than LTB4 from arachidonic acid</li>
<li><strong>Resolvins of the E-series (RvE1, RvE2, RvE3)</strong> -- actively resolve inflammation (pro-resolving mediators)</li>
<li><strong>Protectins, maresins</strong> (mostly DHA-derived; EPA contributes less)</li>
</ul>
<p>EPA also competes with arachidonic acid (AA; omega-6 PUFA) for cyclooxygenase (COX) and lipoxygenase (LOX) enzymes, shifting the eicosanoid balance away from pro-inflammatory 2-series prostaglandins and 4-series leukotrienes. This competition is the basis for the AA:EPA ratio as an inflammatory balance marker.</p>
<p>Clinically, EPA's strongest evidence is in <strong>cardiovascular medicine</strong> and <strong>psychiatry</strong>. The landmark <strong>REDUCE-IT trial</strong> (Bhatt et al., NEJM 2019) demonstrated that high-dose EPA (icosapent ethyl 4 g/day) reduced major cardiovascular events by <strong>25%</strong> in statin-treated patients with elevated triglycerides (135-499 mg/dL) and established ASCVD or diabetes with risk factors -- a transformative result that led to ACC/AHA and ESC guideline endorsement of icosapent ethyl for this population. In mental health, meta-analyses support EPA-predominant omega-3 supplements (≥60% EPA) as effective <strong>adjunctive therapy for depression</strong> (effect size ~0.3-0.5).</p>
<p>EPA is measured as part of an omega-3 fatty acid panel, either as <strong>serum EPA</strong> (reflecting recent dietary intake over 2-4 weeks) or as part of the <strong>Omega-3 Index</strong> (EPA+DHA in RBC membranes, reflecting intake over 3-4 months; the longer-term marker).</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level: 5/5</strong> (extensively studied; REDUCE-IT, JELIS trials; guideline-endorsed for hypertriglyceridemia).</p>

<p><strong>Caveats:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>EPA levels vary significantly with dietary habits</li>
<li>Populations consuming traditional Japanese or Mediterranean diets have substantially higher EPA</li>
<li>Fasting state minimally affects EPA; post-prandial state slightly lower</li>
<li>Reference range is assay-specific (gas chromatography, LC-MS/MS)</li>
</ul>

<h2>Factors Affecting Levels</h2>
<p><strong>Increase EPA:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Dietary fatty fish intake</strong> (salmon 2-3x/week raises EPA 30-50%)</li>
<li><strong>Fish oil or krill oil supplementation</strong> (1-4 g/day EPA+DHA)</li>
<li><strong>Algal oil</strong> (vegan source)</li>
<li><strong>Icosapent ethyl</strong> (Vascepa; purified EPA prescription; 4 g/day) -- raises EPA dramatically (~3-5x baseline)</li>
<li><strong>Mediterranean, Japanese, plant-based whole-food diets</strong></li>
<li><strong>Flaxseed, chia, walnut</strong> (ALA precursor; modest conversion ~5-10%)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Decrease EPA:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Western diet</strong> (low omega-3, high omega-6 from processed seed oils)</li>
<li><strong>Vegetarian / vegan diet without omega-3 supplementation</strong> (low EPA, low DHA)</li>
<li><strong>Alcohol excess</strong></li>
<li><strong>Smoking</strong></li>
<li><strong>Aging</strong> (decreased conversion of ALA to EPA)</li>
<li><strong>FADS1/FADS2 polymorphisms</strong> (reduced conversion of ALA to EPA)</li>
<li><strong>Renal failure, dialysis</strong> (altered lipid metabolism)</li>
<li><strong>Atorvastatin</strong> (modestly lowers EPA via effects on lipid metabolism)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p><strong>Low EPA is associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Cardiovascular disease:</strong> observational studies consistently show low omega-3 (EPA+DHA) is associated with higher CV risk; REDUCE-IT confirms EPA supplementation reduces events</li>
<li><strong>Hypertriglyceridemia:</strong> EPA lowers triglycerides 20-50% (especially high-dose icosapent ethyl 4 g/day)</li>
<li><strong>Depression:</strong> low omega-3 and high AA:EPA ratio associated with depression; EPA-predominant supplements effective adjunctive therapy (meta-analyses)</li>
<li><strong>Inflammatory disease:</strong> rheumatoid arthritis, IBD, psoriasis; EPA modestly reduces symptoms</li>
<li><strong>Cognitive decline, dementia:</strong> low omega-3 associated with faster cognitive decline in elderly</li>
<li><strong>Dry eye syndrome:</strong> omega-3 supplements modestly effective</li>
<li><strong>Pregnancy:</strong> low EPA+DHA associated with preterm birth and suboptimal neurodevelopment</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Elevated EPA is associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Reduced cardiovascular events</strong> (REDUCE-IT, JELIS)</li>
<li><strong>Improved triglyceride profile</strong></li>
<li><strong>Lower inflammatory markers</strong> (hsCRP, IL-6)</li>
<li><strong>Slightly increased bleeding time</strong> (clinically significant bleeding is very rare; stop fish oil 5-7 days before surgery)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Limitations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>EPA alone vs. EPA+DHA:</strong> REDUCE-IT used pure EPA; mixed EPA+DHA trials (ASCEND, VITAL) were negative; debate whether pure EPA is superior</li>
<li><strong>AA:EPA ratio</strong> may better predict inflammatory risk than EPA alone</li>
<li><strong>Omega-3 Index</strong> (EPA+DHA in RBC) is more stable than serum EPA</li>
<li><strong>Cost and insurance coverage</strong> of omega-3 blood testing</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Monitoring use cases:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>CV risk stratification (especially patients with elevated triglycerides on statin)</li>
<li>Guide icosapent ethyl therapy in REDUCE-IT-like population</li>
<li>Adjunctive depression therapy monitoring</li>
<li>Pregnancy omega-3 adequacy assessment</li>
<li>Anti-inflammatory dietary intervention monitoring</li>
</ul>
<h2>Strategies to Improve</h2>
<p>EPA levels respond within 2-4 weeks of dietary or supplemental intervention:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Fatty fish intake</strong> 2-3 servings/week (salmon, mackerel, sardines, anchovies, herring) -- the most natural source</li>
<li><strong>Fish oil or krill oil supplementation</strong> 1-4 g/day EPA+DHA (look for products with EPA &gt;&gt; DHA for CV indications; balanced EPA:DHA for general use)</li>
<li><strong>Algal oil</strong> (vegan source; suitable for vegetarians/vegans)</li>
<li><strong>Icosapent ethyl (Vascepa)</strong> 4 g/day -- FDA-approved for elevated triglycerides in statin-treated patients with ASCVD or diabetes + risk factors (REDUCE-IT population); prescription; highly purified EPA</li>
<li><strong>Mediterranean diet</strong> rich in olive oil, fish, vegetables, whole grains</li>
<li><strong>Plant-based ALA sources</strong> (flaxseed, chia, walnut) -- modest conversion ~5-10% to EPA; suitable for vegetarians</li>
<li><strong>Reduce omega-6 intake</strong> (processed seed oils -- corn, soybean, sunflower, cottonseed) to improve AA:EPA balance</li>
<li><strong>Stop smoking</strong> (improves omega-3 metabolism)</li>
<li><strong>Reduce alcohol</strong> (depletes omega-3)</li>
<li><strong>For depression adjunct:</strong> EPA-predominant supplement (≥60% EPA; 1-2 g EPA/day) for ≥8 weeks</li>
</ol>
