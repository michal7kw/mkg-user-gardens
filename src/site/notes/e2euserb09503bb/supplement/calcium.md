<h1>Calcium</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Calcium is the most abundant mineral in the human body, with about 99% stored in the skeleton and teeth as hydroxyapatite and the remainder serving essential roles in muscle contraction, nerve conduction, blood clotting, and intracellular signalling. Because serum calcium is tightly regulated, dietary or supplemental shortfalls are buffered by withdrawing calcium from bone, so chronically low intake contributes to reduced bone mineral density. Supplemental calcium, most often paired with vitamin D, is widely used in older adults and postmenopausal women to support bone health and reduce fracture and fall risk. The evidence is strongest when habitual dietary intake is low; for people who already meet requirements through diet, additional supplementation offers little benefit and may carry small risks of kidney stones and possibly cardiovascular events, making food-first strategies and moderate dosing preferable.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>Supplemental calcium supplies the Ca2+ needed to maintain serum calcium and to mineralise the collagen scaffold of bone as hydroxyapatite. When intake is adequate, parathyroid hormone secretion falls, lowering the rate of osteoclastic bone resorption that PTH would otherwise stimulate to defend serum calcium. Vitamin D (calcitriol) is required for efficient active intestinal calcium absorption, which is why the two are typically co-administered. Vitamin K2 activates osteocalcin and matrix Gla protein through carboxylation, helping direct calcium into bone and away from vascular tissue. Calcium also competes with iron and zinc for intestinal absorption, so timing matters when these are taken together.</p>
<h2>Dosage</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Total target intake (adults)</strong>: ~1000-1200 mg/day from diet plus supplements combined</li>
<li><strong>Typical supplemental dose</strong>: 500-1000 mg/day of elemental calcium to fill the dietary gap</li>
<li><strong>Per-dose limit</strong>: &lt;=500 mg elemental calcium at a time for best absorption</li>
<li><strong>Forms</strong>: Calcium carbonate (40% elemental, take with food) or calcium citrate (21% elemental, better absorbed, no food needed; preferred with low stomach acid or PPIs)</li>
<li><strong>Co-factors</strong>: Combine with adequate vitamin D; consider vitamin K2; separate from iron/zinc supplements</li>
</ul>
