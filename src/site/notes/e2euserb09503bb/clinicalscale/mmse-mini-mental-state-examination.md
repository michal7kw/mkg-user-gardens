<h1>MMSE (Mini-Mental State Examination)</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>30-point clinician-administered cognitive screening instrument covering orientation (10 points), registration (3 points), attention/calculation (5 points), recall (3 points), and language (9 points). Originally developed by Folstein, Folstein, and McHugh (1975) as a brief bedside cognitive assessment. Long the dominant cognitive screener but partly displaced by MoCA due to (1) ceiling effects in mild cognitive impairment, (2) less sensitivity to executive dysfunction, and (3) copyright restrictions imposed by PAR in 2001 (formal use now requires licensing).</p>
<p>Education and age adjust expected scores. Years of education &lt; 8 lower expected scores; cutoffs of 17-23 may be appropriate depending on education level.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level:</strong> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (5/5)</p>

<h2>Limitations</h2>
<ul>
<li>Ceiling effect for highly educated patients with early MCI</li>
<li>Insensitive to frontal/executive dysfunction (consider Trail Making, Clock Draw)</li>
<li>Less sensitive to vascular cognitive impairment than MoCA</li>
<li>Copyright since 2001 limits free use — MoCA, SLUMS, and Mini-Cog are free alternatives</li>
</ul>
