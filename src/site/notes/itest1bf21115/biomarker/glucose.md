<h1>Glucose</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Blood glucose is the body's primary circulating energy substrate, supplying fuel to the brain (~120 g glucose/day), muscles, and other tissues. Glucose enters the bloodstream from intestinal absorption of dietary carbohydrates and from hepatic gluconeogenesis (de novo glucose synthesis from lactate, alanine, glycerol) and glycogenolysis (breakdown of stored glycogen). Blood glucose is held within a narrow range (~70-100 mg/dL fasting; up to 140 mg/dL postprandial) by the opposing actions of insulin (the only hormone that lowers glucose) and counter-regulatory hormones (glucagon, cortisol, catecholamines, growth hormone -- all of which raise glucose).</p>
<p>The insulin-glucagon axis operates minute-to-minute via pancreatic beta-cell insulin secretion and alpha-cell glucagon secretion. When glucose rises after a meal, beta-cell ATP-sensitive K+ channels close, depolarizing the cell and triggering insulin granule exocytosis. Insulin suppresses hepatic gluconeogenesis and promotes glucose uptake into muscle and adipose tissue via GLUT4 transporter translocation. When glucose falls, glucagon triggers hepatic glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis to restore normal levels. Loss of this tight regulation -- via either insulin deficiency (Type 1 diabetes) or insulin resistance with relative deficiency (Type 2 diabetes) -- causes chronic hyperglycemia.</p>
<p>Fasting plasma glucose (FPG) is one of the three diagnostic criteria for diabetes (along with HbA1c and 2-hour oral glucose tolerance test). Per the American Diabetes Association (ADA), prediabetes is defined as FPG 100-125 mg/dL (&quot;impaired fasting glucose&quot;); diabetes is defined as FPG &gt;=126 mg/dL on two separate occasions. Chronic hyperglycemia drives the microvascular (retinopathy, nephropathy, neuropathy) and macrovascular (coronary artery disease, stroke, peripheral arterial disease) complications of diabetes.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level: 5/5</strong> (foundational metabolic biomarker; ADA, WHO universal diagnostic criteria).</p>

<p><strong>Caveats:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>FPG requires 8-12 hour fast; sample should be drawn in morning</li>
<li>Acute illness, stress, corticosteroids, pregnancy, and recent meal all elevate glucose</li>
<li>A single abnormal FPG is NOT diagnostic for diabetes -- repeat testing on a separate day, OR use HbA1c, OR use 2-hour OGTT</li>
<li>Capillary (fingerstick) glucose values are 10-15% lower than venous plasma</li>
<li>Pregnancy has lower cutoffs: gestational diabetes OGTT 1-h &gt;=180, 2-h &gt;=155, 3-h &gt;=140 mg/dL</li>
</ul>

<h2>Factors Affecting Levels</h2>
<p><strong>Increase glucose (hyperglycemia):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Insulin resistance / Type 2 diabetes:</strong> the principal cause of chronic hyperglycemia</li>
<li><strong>Type 1 diabetes:</strong> absolute insulin deficiency</li>
<li><strong>Recent meal:</strong> postprandial elevation (peaks 30-60 min)</li>
<li><strong>Stress response:</strong> sepsis, trauma, surgery, MI (counter-regulatory hormones)</li>
<li><strong>Corticosteroid therapy:</strong> prednisone, dexamethasone (substantial hyperglycemia)</li>
<li><strong>Atypical antipsychotics:</strong> olanzapine, clozapine, quetiapine (insulin resistance)</li>
<li><strong>Pregnancy:</strong> gestational diabetes (insulin resistance from placental hormones)</li>
<li><strong>Endocrine:</strong> Cushing's syndrome, acromegaly, pheochromocytoma, hyperthyroidism, glucagonoma</li>
<li><strong>Pancreatic disease:</strong> chronic pancreatitis, pancreatectomy, pancreatic cancer</li>
<li><strong>Acromegaly, somatostatinoma</strong></li>
<li><strong>Medications:</strong> thiazide diuretics, beta-blockers, niacin, sympathomimetics, oral contraceptives</li>
<li><strong>Sample handling:</strong> delayed separation, no fluoride tube (glycolysis lowers glucose ~10 mg/dL/h at room temp)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Decrease glucose (hypoglycemia):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Insulin or sulfonylurea therapy</strong> (the most common cause of hypoglycemia)</li>
<li><strong>Insulinoma</strong> (rare pancreatic beta-cell tumor)</li>
<li><strong>Adrenal insufficiency</strong> (Addison's disease)</li>
<li><strong>Severe liver disease</strong> (impaired gluconeogenesis/glycogen stores)</li>
<li><strong>Sepsis</strong> (glucose consumption exceeds production)</li>
<li><strong>Alcohol</strong> (inhibits gluconeogenesis; especially with fasting)</li>
<li><strong>Post-bariatric surgery</strong> (nesidioblastosis)</li>
<li><strong>Anorexia nervosa</strong> (severe malnutrition)</li>
<li><strong>Pregnancy</strong> (physiologic fasting hypoglycemia)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p><strong>Elevated glucose is associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Prediabetes / Type 2 diabetes:</strong> the principal clinical use; ADA/WHO diagnostic criteria</li>
<li><strong>Insulin resistance / metabolic syndrome:</strong> glucose in upper normal range (90-99) correlates with insulin resistance</li>
<li><strong>Acute stress hyperglycemia:</strong> predicts worse outcomes in sepsis, trauma, MI</li>
<li><strong>Gestational diabetes:</strong> increases maternal and fetal complications</li>
<li><strong>Drug-induced hyperglycemia:</strong> corticosteroids, atypical antipsychotics</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Reduced glucose is associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Hypoglycemia from insulin / sulfonylurea therapy:</strong> the most common cause in diabetics</li>
<li><strong>Insulinoma:</strong> rare; Whipple's triad (symptoms + low glucose + relief with glucose)</li>
<li><strong>Adrenal insufficiency, severe liver disease, sepsis, alcohol</strong></li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Limitations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Single measurement reflects only the moment</strong> -- use HbA1c for chronic glycemic assessment</li>
<li><strong>Acute stress, illness, medications confound</strong> -- repeat when stable</li>
<li><strong>Fasting requirement</strong> for diagnostic FPG; missing the fast causes false elevation</li>
<li><strong>Sample handling critical</strong> -- glycolysis in unprocessed serum samples causes false-low</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Monitoring use cases:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Annual diabetes screening (ADA; ages &gt;=35 or overweight with risk factors)</li>
<li>Diagnostic confirmation of diabetes (with HbA1c, OGTT)</li>
<li>Daily self-monitoring for insulin-treated diabetics (4+ times/day)</li>
<li>Less frequent for non-insulin-treated type 2 diabetics</li>
<li>Acute care: monitor stress hyperglycemia in ICU (target 140-180 mg/dL per ADA)</li>
<li>Gestational diabetes screening (24-28 weeks; OGTT)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Strategies to Improve</h2>
<p>Glucose is highly modifiable through lifestyle, diet, and pharmacologic interventions:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Mediterranean diet</strong> or low-glycemic-index dietary pattern -- reduces FPG and HbA1c</li>
<li><strong>Low-carbohydrate / ketogenic diet</strong> (controversial for diabetics but effective for glucose lowering) -- 20-50 g carbs/day</li>
<li><strong>Weight loss</strong> -- 5-10% body weight reduction substantially improves glucose (Diabetes Prevention Program showed 58% reduction in T2DM incidence with lifestyle)</li>
<li><strong>Aerobic exercise</strong> 150 min/week moderate + resistance training 2x/week -- improves insulin sensitivity for 24-72 h</li>
<li><strong>Metformin</strong> (first-line for T2DM) 500-2000 mg/day -- suppresses hepatic gluconeogenesis</li>
<li><strong>GLP-1 agonists</strong> (semaglutide, tirzepatide) -- powerful glucose lowering + weight loss</li>
<li><strong>SGLT2 inhibitors</strong> (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin) -- glucosuria; also reduce CV and renal events</li>
<li><strong>Berberine</strong> 500 mg TID -- meta-analyses show comparable FPG reduction to metformin (~15-25 mg/dL)</li>
<li><strong>Magnesium</strong> 200-400 mg/day (in deficient patients) -- cofactor for insulin receptor function</li>
<li><strong>Cinnamon</strong> 1-6 g/day -- meta-analyses show modest FPG reduction (~10-20 mg/dL)</li>
<li><strong>Chromium picolinate</strong> 200-1000 mcg/day -- small reduction in some studies</li>
<li><strong>Alpha-lipoic acid</strong> 300-600 mg/day -- antioxidant; reduces glucose and improves diabetic neuropathy</li>
<li><strong>Sleep optimization</strong> (7-9 h/night) -- sleep deprivation raises FPG by 10-20 mg/dL</li>
<li><strong>Stress management</strong> -- cortisol elevation raises glucose</li>
<li><strong>For hypoglycemia:</strong> 15-15 rule (15 g carbs; recheck in 15 min); treat underlying cause</li>
</ol>
