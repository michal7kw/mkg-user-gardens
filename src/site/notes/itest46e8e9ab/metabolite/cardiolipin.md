<h1>Cardiolipin</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Cardiolipin is a unique, structurally distinctive phospholipid found almost exclusively in the <strong>inner mitochondrial membrane</strong>, where it can constitute up to ~20% of the lipid content. Unlike other glycerophospholipids it carries four acyl chains and two phosphate groups (a &quot;double&quot; phospholipid), giving it a conical shape ideally suited to the tightly curved cristae membranes. Its evolutionary origin in bacterial membranes is another echo of the mitochondrion's endosymbiotic past.</p>
<p>Cardiolipin is far more than structural filler: it is required for the assembly and stability of the <strong>respiratory chain supercomplexes</strong>, directly enabling efficient electron transfer and ATP synthesis. It also serves as a signaling lipid — when oxidized or externalized to the outer membrane, it triggers cytochrome c release (apoptosis) or tags mitochondria for <a href="/garden/itest46e8e9ab/pathway/mitophagy/" class="internal-link">Mitophagy</a>. Loss or remodeling of cardiolipin (as in Barth syndrome) causes severe mitochondrial disease.</p>
<h2>Biochemical Role</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Supercomplex scaffolding:</strong> binds and stabilizes Complexes I, III, and IV (the &quot;respirasome&quot;), optimizing electron flux in the <a href="/garden/itest46e8e9ab/pathway/electron-transport-chain/" class="internal-link">Electron Transport Chain</a> / <a href="/garden/itest46e8e9ab/pathway/oxidative-phosphorylation/" class="internal-link">Oxidative Phosphorylation</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Cristae architecture:</strong> its conical geometry supports the high membrane curvature of cristae.</li>
<li><strong>Apoptosis:</strong> peroxidized cardiolipin releases bound <a href="/garden/itest46e8e9ab/protein/cytochrome-c/" class="internal-link">Cytochrome C</a>, a committing step of intrinsic apoptosis.</li>
<li><strong>Mitophagy signal:</strong> externalized cardiolipin is recognized by LC3 to target damaged mitochondria for degradation.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>Cardiolipin content and acyl-chain composition decline and remodel with age, ischemia, heart failure, and neurodegeneration, contributing to <a href="/garden/itest46e8e9ab/condition/mitochondrial-dysfunction/" class="internal-link">Mitochondrial Dysfunction</a>. Genetic defects in cardiolipin remodeling (tafazzin/TAZ) cause Barth syndrome (cardiomyopathy, neutropenia). Cardiolipin-targeted compounds (e.g., elamipretide) are in development to restore mitochondrial bioenergetics.</p>
