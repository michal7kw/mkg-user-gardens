<h1>Acute Lung Injury</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Acute lung injury (ALI) and its more severe form, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), constitute a spectrum of acute, diffuse inflammatory lung injury leading to <strong>alveolar-capillary barrier disruption</strong>, <strong>non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema</strong>, and <strong>refractory hypoxemia</strong>. The condition was redefined by the <strong>2012 Berlin Definition</strong> which classifies ARDS severity by PaO2/FiO2 ratio:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Mild ARDS:</strong> PaO2/FiO2 200-300 mmHg</li>
<li><strong>Moderate ARDS:</strong> PaO2/FiO2 100-200 mmHg</li>
<li><strong>Severe ARDS:</strong> PaO2/FiO2 &lt;100 mmHg</li>
</ul>
<p>The key diagnostic criteria (Berlin): acute onset (&lt;1 week), bilateral opacities on imaging not fully explained by effusions or atelectasis, respiratory failure not fully explained by cardiac failure or fluid overload, and PaO2/FiO2 &lt;300 with PEEP &gt;=5 cm H2O.</p>
<p>ARDS is categorized as <strong>pulmonary</strong> (direct lung injury: pneumonia, aspiration, inhalation, contusion) or <strong>extrapulmonary</strong> (indirect: sepsis, pancreatitis, trauma, transfusion). The pathogenesis involves <strong>uncontrolled inflammation</strong> -- neutrophil activation, release of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-alpha), damage to alveolar epithelium and capillary endothelium, and protein-rich alveolar flooding. The <strong>surfactant</strong> layer is disrupted, causing alveolar collapse and refractory hypoxemia.</p>
<p>Mortality remains <strong>25-40%</strong> despite advances in critical care. The landmark ARMA trial (2000) demonstrated that <strong>low tidal volume ventilation</strong> (6 mL/kg ideal body weight) reduces mortality from 40% to 31%.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Lung-protective ventilation (the single most important intervention):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Tidal volume:</strong> 4-6 mL/kg predicted (ideal) body weight</li>
<li><strong>Plateau pressure:</strong> &lt;30 cm H2O</li>
<li><strong>PEEP:</strong> titrate to optimize oxygenation while avoiding barotrauma (FiO2/PEEP table)</li>
<li><strong>Permissive hypercapnia:</strong> accept PaCO2 up to 60 mmHg if pH &gt;7.20 (reduces volutrauma)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Adjunctive therapies:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Prone positioning:</strong> 16+ hours/day for moderate-severe ARDS (PROSEVA trial; 28-day mortality reduction)</li>
<li><strong>Conservative fluid strategy:</strong> target CVP &lt;4 mmHg or negative fluid balance (FACTT trial)</li>
<li><strong>Neuromuscular blockade:</strong> cisatracurium for 48 hours in early severe ARDS (ACURASYS; reduced mortality)</li>
<li><strong>ECMO:</strong> venovenous ECMO for refractory hypoxemia (EOLIA trial; center expertise required)</li>
<li><strong>Inhaled pulmonary vasodilators:</strong> inhaled nitric oxide or epoprostenol (improves oxygenation but no survival benefit)</li>
<li><strong>Corticosteroids:</strong> dexamethasone for COVID-19 ARDS (RECOVERY); methylprednisolone for late-phase ARDS (controversial)</li>
</ul>
