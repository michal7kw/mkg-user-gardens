<h1>NF-kB Pathway</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>The nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) pathway is one of the most important and evolutionarily conserved signaling systems in human physiology, functioning as a master transcriptional switch that links extracellular danger signals to coordinated programs of inflammation, innate and adaptive immunity, and cell survival. The &quot;NF-κB&quot; name refers to a family of five related transcription factor subunits — RelA (p65), RelB, c-Rel, NF-κB1 (p50, from its p105 precursor), and NF-κB2 (p52, from p100) — that assemble into dimers (most commonly the p65/p50 heterodimer) and bind κB DNA elements in the promoters and enhancers of hundreds of target genes. In resting cells these dimers are held inactive in the cytoplasm by inhibitory IκB proteins (chiefly IκBα). The pathway is, in essence, the cell's general-purpose &quot;alarm&quot; circuit, and its activity is a recurring theme across nearly every chronic inflammatory and degenerative disease state.</p>
<p>Activation proceeds through two arms. The <strong>canonical pathway</strong> is triggered rapidly by pro-inflammatory stimuli — TNF-α, IL-1β, bacterial lipopolysaccharide (LPS), Toll-like receptor ligands, oxidative stress, and antigen-receptor engagement — and converges on the IκB kinase (IKK) complex, composed of the catalytic subunits IKKα (CHUK) and IKKβ (IKBKB) plus the regulatory scaffold IKKγ/NEMO. Activated IKKβ phosphorylates IκBα, marking it for ubiquitin-mediated proteasomal degradation; the freed NF-κB dimer translocates to the nucleus and drives transcription. The slower <strong>non-canonical pathway</strong> (NIK→IKKα→p100 processing to p52/RelB) governs lymphoid organogenesis and B-cell maturation. The result is a tightly self-limiting feedback loop, because IκBα is itself an NF-κB target gene that resynthesizes to terminate the response.</p>
<p>Clinically, NF-κB is doubly significant. Appropriately controlled, it is essential for host defense and tissue repair; chronically or aberrantly activated, it sustains the low-grade systemic inflammation (&quot;inflammaging&quot;) implicated in atherosclerosis, type 2 diabetes, autoimmune diseases such as rheumatoid arthritis and inflammatory bowel disease, and tumor promotion and survival. Because so many anti-inflammatory drugs and dietary compounds — glucocorticoids, high-dose salicylates, curcumin, resveratrol, and omega-3-derived mediators — ultimately converge on inhibition of this pathway, NF-κB serves as a unifying mechanistic node connecting pharmacology, nutrition, and chronic disease.</p>
<h2>Mechanism and Regulation</h2>
<p>The canonical cascade is the dominant route in inflammatory and metabolic disease. Ligand binding to TNF receptors, the IL-1 receptor, or Toll-like receptors recruits adaptor proteins (e.g., MyD88, TRAF6) that activate the TAK1 kinase, which in turn phosphorylates and activates the IKK complex. IKKβ-driven phosphorylation of IκBα on serines 32 and 36 triggers its K48-linked polyubiquitination and degradation by the 26S proteasome. The liberated p65/p50 dimer enters the nucleus, where post-translational modifications of p65 (phosphorylation, acetylation) fine-tune its transcriptional output. Negative regulation is layered: newly synthesized IκBα re-sequesters NF-κB, the deubiquitinase A20 dampens upstream signaling, and the NAD⁺-dependent deacetylase SIRT1 deacetylates p65 to suppress its activity — a key reason AMPK and SIRT1 activation are anti-inflammatory.</p>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>Persistent NF-κB activation produces a self-reinforcing inflammatory milieu: it transcribes the very cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6) that activate it, amplifying chronic inflammation. In rheumatoid arthritis and inflammatory bowel disease, constitutive NF-κB activity in synovial and mucosal tissue drives joint and gut destruction. In atherosclerosis it promotes endothelial adhesion-molecule expression and macrophage activation within plaques. In metabolic disease, IKKβ/NF-κB activation in liver and adipose tissue contributes to insulin resistance. In oncology, many tumors hijack NF-κB for survival (via Bcl-2/Bcl-xL), proliferation, angiogenesis, and resistance to chemotherapy, making the pathway a long-standing therapeutic target.</p>
<h2>Biomarkers and Modulation</h2>
<p>Pathway activity is reflected clinically by elevated downstream inflammatory biomarkers — high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hsCRP), IL-6, and TNF-α — rather than by direct measurement of nuclear p65. On the modulation side, endogenous IκBα and glucocorticoids inhibit the pathway, as do high-dose aspirin (which can inhibit IKKβ) and disease-modifying agents like methotrexate. Several well-studied dietary compounds suppress NF-κB signaling: curcumin blocks IKK activation and IκBα degradation, resveratrol and omega-3 fatty acids (via specialized pro-resolving mediators) attenuate signaling, and sulforaphane and N-acetylcysteine act in part by reducing the oxidative stress that primes NF-κB. This convergence makes NF-κB inhibition a shared mechanism behind many anti-inflammatory nutritional and pharmacological strategies.</p>
