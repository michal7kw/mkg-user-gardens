<h1>Sarcoma</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Sarcomas are a diverse group of <strong>malignant tumors of mesenchymal origin</strong> -- arising from bone, cartilage, muscle, fat, fibrous tissue, blood vessels, nerves, and other connective tissues. They comprise over <strong>70 distinct histological subtypes</strong> and are broadly divided into <strong>bone sarcomas</strong> and <strong>soft tissue sarcomas</strong> (STS). Despite this diversity, sarcomas are <strong>rare</strong>: they account for only ~1% of adult cancers but ~15% of pediatric cancers.</p>
<p><strong>Common subtypes:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Bone sarcomas:</strong> Osteosarcoma (most common primary bone malignancy; peak ages 10-20 and &gt;60), Ewing sarcoma (children/adolescents), chondrosarcoma (adults)</li>
<li><strong>Soft tissue sarcomas:</strong> Leiomyosarcoma (smooth muscle), liposarcoma (fat), rhabdomyosarcoma (skeletal muscle; most common STS in children), angiosarcoma (blood vessels), synovial sarcoma, malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumor (MPNST), gastrointestinal stromal tumor (GIST)</li>
</ul>
<p>The most common presentation is a <strong>painless or painful mass</strong>, often growing over weeks to months. Delays in diagnosis are common because the mass may be attributed to trauma or benign causes. Any deep, enlarging soft tissue mass &gt;5 cm should be considered suspicious for sarcoma and evaluated with <strong>MRI</strong> and biopsy.</p>
<p>Multimodal treatment typically involves <strong>surgical resection with wide margins</strong> (the only curative treatment for most), often combined with chemotherapy (osteosarcoma, Ewing, rhabdomyosarcoma) and/or radiation therapy (selected soft tissue sarcomas). <strong>Imatinib</strong> (Gleevec) has revolutionized treatment of GIST by targeting the KIT/PDGFRA mutations. <strong>Pazopanib</strong> and other tyrosine kinase inhibitors have activity in non-GIST STS.</p>
<h2>Clinical Presentation</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Painless mass</strong> (most common; soft tissue sarcoma)</li>
<li><strong>Painful mass</strong> (bone sarcoma; often worse at night; unresponsive to NSAIDs)</li>
<li><strong>Swelling and enlarging mass</strong> (over weeks to months)</li>
<li><strong>Limited range of motion</strong> (if near joint)</li>
<li><strong>Pathologic fracture</strong> (bone sarcoma; minor trauma causes break)</li>
<li><strong>Constitutional symptoms</strong> (weight loss, fatigue; advanced/metastatic disease)</li>
<li><strong>Neurovascular symptoms</strong> (if compressing nerves/vessels)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Diagnostic Information</h2>
<p><strong>Imaging:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>MRI</strong> (preferred for soft tissue masses; defines extent, neurovascular involvement)</li>
<li><strong>CT</strong> (for bony detail; chest CT for staging/metastasis)</li>
<li><strong>Bone scan</strong> or <strong>PET-CT</strong> (for systemic staging)</li>
<li><strong>Plain radiographs</strong> (for bone lesions; can show lytic/sclerotic pattern, periosteal reaction, Codman triangle, sunburst pattern)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Biopsy:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Core needle biopsy</strong> (preferred; less invasive than open)</li>
<li><strong>Incisional biopsy</strong> (open; must be oriented for future resection)</li>
<li><strong>Excisional biopsy</strong> (only for small, superficial lesions &lt;5 cm)</li>
<li>Biopsy tract is excised at definitive surgery to prevent tumor seeding</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Staging:</strong> AJCC TNM 8th edition (tumor size, depth, nodal involvement, grade, metastasis)</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Bone sarcomas:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Osteosarcoma:</strong> neoadjuvant chemotherapy (methotrexate + doxorubicin + cisplatin) -&gt; limb-sparing surgery -&gt; adjuvant chemotherapy</li>
<li><strong>Ewing sarcoma:</strong> neoadjuvant chemotherapy (VAC/IE regimen) -&gt; surgery and/or radiation -&gt; adjuvant chemotherapy</li>
<li><strong>Chondrosarcoma:</strong> surgical resection; generally chemo-resistant</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Soft tissue sarcomas:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Surgical resection</strong> with wide margins (&gt;=1 cm; or fascial barrier); limb-sparing preferred</li>
<li><strong>Radiation therapy</strong> (pre-op or post-op) for intermediate/high-grade, &gt;5 cm</li>
<li><strong>Chemotherapy</strong> (doxorubicin +/- ifosfamide) for advanced/metastatic disease</li>
<li><strong>Pazopanib</strong> (tyrosine kinase inhibitor) for refractory STS</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>GIST:</strong> Imatinib 400 mg daily (adjuvant for high-risk; first-line for advanced); sunitinib, regorafenib for progression</p>
<p><strong>Metastatic disease:</strong> metastasectomy for limited pulmonary metastases; palliative chemotherapy</p>
