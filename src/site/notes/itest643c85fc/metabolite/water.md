<h1>Water</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Water (H2O) is the terminal product of the mitochondrial <a href="/garden/itest643c85fc/pathway/electron-transport-chain/" class="internal-link">Electron Transport Chain</a>. When <a href="/garden/itest643c85fc/metabolite/oxygen/" class="internal-link">Oxygen</a> is reduced at <a href="/garden/itest643c85fc/protein/complex-iv/" class="internal-link">Complex IV</a> (cytochrome c oxidase), four electrons and four protons combine with O2 to yield two molecules of water. This reaction marks the completion of aerobic respiration: the electrons that began their journey on <a href="/garden/itest643c85fc/metabolite/nadh/" class="internal-link">NADH</a> and <a href="/garden/itest643c85fc/metabolite/fadh2/" class="internal-link">FADH2</a> end up in stable, harmless water.</p>
<p>The water produced this way is often called <strong>metabolic water</strong>, and it is a meaningful endogenous water source — particularly during <a href="/garden/itest643c85fc/pathway/beta-oxidation/" class="internal-link">Beta-Oxidation</a> of fat, which generates relatively large amounts of reducing equivalents and therefore of respiratory water.</p>

<h2>Role in Cellular Respiration</h2>
<h3>Formation at Complex IV</h3>
<p><a href="/garden/itest643c85fc/protein/complex-iv/" class="internal-link">Complex IV</a> reduces molecular oxygen to water at its binuclear heme a3–CuB center, using electrons supplied by <a href="/garden/itest643c85fc/protein/cytochrome-c/" class="internal-link">Cytochrome C</a> and protons drawn from the mitochondrial matrix. By consuming matrix protons, water formation contributes (alongside proton pumping) to the proton-motive force that <a href="/garden/itest643c85fc/protein/atp-synthase/" class="internal-link">ATP Synthase</a> converts into ATP during <a href="/garden/itest643c85fc/pathway/oxidative-phosphorylation/" class="internal-link">Oxidative Phosphorylation</a>.</p>
<h3>Metabolic Water</h3>
<p>Across a full turn of substrate oxidation, the reduction of O2 to water is the quantitative endpoint for the hydrogen atoms removed from glucose and fatty acids. This endogenous water can be physiologically important in states of restricted water intake and is the basis for the large water yield of fat oxidation.</p>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Respiratory coupling:</strong> Because water formation is downstream of the entire chain, anything that blocks electron transport (hypoxia, Complex IV inhibition by cyanide or carbon monoxide) stops metabolic water production and oxidative ATP synthesis simultaneously.</li>
<li><strong>Energy metabolism:</strong> The tight link between O2 consumption and water/ATP output underlies indirect calorimetry and measures of metabolic rate.</li>
</ul>
