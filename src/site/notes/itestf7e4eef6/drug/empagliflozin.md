<h1>Empagliflozin</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Empagliflozin is a sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitor originally developed for type 2 diabetes that has become a foundational cardiorenal therapy. By blocking SGLT2 in the proximal tubule of the kidney, it reduces renal glucose reabsorption and increases urinary glucose excretion, lowering plasma glucose in an insulin-independent manner with a low intrinsic risk of hypoglycemia and modest weight and blood pressure reduction.</p>
<p>Its clinical importance was transformed by large outcome trials. EMPA-REG OUTCOME (PMID: 26378978) showed a reduction in cardiovascular death in patients with type 2 diabetes and established cardiovascular disease; the EMPEROR program demonstrated benefit in heart failure with reduced (PMID: 32865377) and preserved (PMID: 34449189) ejection fraction regardless of diabetes status; and EMPA-KIDNEY (PMID: 36331190) showed slowing of chronic kidney disease progression. These benefits are thought to derive largely from natriuretic, hemodynamic, and metabolic effects rather than glucose lowering alone.</p>
<h2>Pharmacological Information</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Class:</strong> SGLT2 inhibitor (gliflozin)</li>
<li><strong>Route:</strong> Oral, once daily (typically 10-25 mg)</li>
<li><strong>Absorption:</strong> Rapidly absorbed; peak ~1.5 h</li>
<li><strong>Metabolism:</strong> Primarily glucuronidation (UGT enzymes); minimal CYP involvement</li>
<li><strong>Elimination:</strong> Roughly split between urine and feces</li>
</ul>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>SGLT2 in the early (S1/S2) segment of the proximal renal tubule mediates the majority of filtered glucose reabsorption. Empagliflozin selectively and reversibly inhibits SGLT2, lowering the renal glucose threshold and producing glucosuria of roughly 60-80 g/day. The accompanying osmotic diuresis and natriuresis reduce plasma volume and preload, while shifts toward ketone body and fatty-acid metabolism and reduced glomerular hyperfiltration are proposed contributors to the observed cardiac and renal protection.</p>
<h2>Clinical Features</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Type 2 diabetes:</strong> Reduces HbA1c by roughly 0.5-0.8%, with weight loss (~2-3 kg) and modest BP reduction</li>
<li><strong>Heart failure:</strong> Reduces HF hospitalization and CV death across the ejection-fraction spectrum (PMID: 32865377; PMID: 34449189)</li>
<li><strong>Chronic kidney disease:</strong> Slows eGFR decline and reduces kidney disease progression (PMID: 36331190)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Safety Information</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Common side effects:</strong> Genital mycotic infections, urinary tract infections, volume depletion/hypotension</li>
<li><strong>Serious effects:</strong> Euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (rare), Fournier gangrene (very rare), acute kidney injury with volume depletion</li>
<li><strong>Interactions:</strong> Additive hypotension/diuresis with antihypertensives and loop diuretics; increased hypoglycemia risk when combined with insulin or sulfonylureas</li>
<li><strong>Contraindications:</strong> Severe renal impairment for glycemic indication (efficacy diminishes); type 1 diabetes (DKA risk)</li>
</ul>
