<h1>Wounds and Injuries</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Wounds and injuries represent physical damage to the body caused by external force. They are the <strong>leading cause of death in people under 45 years</strong> in developed countries and a major public health burden globally. Trauma is classified by <strong>mechanism</strong> (blunt, penetrating, thermal, chemical, blast), <strong>location</strong> (head, chest, abdomen, extremity, polytrauma), and <strong>severity</strong> (minor, moderate, major, critical).</p>
<p><strong>Wound types:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Abrasion:</strong> superficial epidermal loss</li>
<li><strong>Laceration:</strong> tear or cut through skin and subcutaneous tissue</li>
<li><strong>Puncture wound:</strong> deep, narrow penetrating injury (nail, needle, bite)</li>
<li><strong>Avulsion:</strong> tearing away of tissue from its attachment</li>
<li><strong>Contusion:</strong> bruise (intact skin; damage to subcutaneous vessels)</li>
<li><strong>Crush injury:</strong> compression of tissue causing necrosis and rhabdomyolysis</li>
<li><strong>Burn:</strong> thermal, chemical, electrical, radiation injury to skin and deeper tissue</li>
<li><strong>Fracture:</strong> break in bone continuity (closed/simple or open/compound)</li>
</ul>
<p>The systematic approach to trauma resuscitation follows the <strong>Advanced Trauma Life Support (ATLS) primary survey:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li><strong>A</strong>irway with cervical spine protection</li>
<li><strong>B</strong>reathing and ventilation</li>
<li><strong>C</strong>irculation with hemorrhage control</li>
<li><strong>D</strong>isability (neurological status; GCS)</li>
<li><strong>E</strong>xposure/Environmental control</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Hemorrhage is the leading cause of preventable trauma death.</strong> Recent military and civilian data emphasize the importance of early <strong>tourniquet use</strong> for extremity hemorrhage, <strong>hemostatic dressings</strong> (QuikClot, Celox) for junctional wounds, and <strong>massive transfusion protocols</strong> for severe bleeding.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Primary survey (ABCDE):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>A:</strong> secure airway (intubation if GCS &lt;8); maintain C-spine immobilization</li>
<li><strong>B:</strong> assess breathing (tension pneumothorax -&gt; needle decompression; flail chest -&gt; analgesia, possible intubation; hemothorax -&gt; chest tube)</li>
<li><strong>C:</strong> hemorrhage control (direct pressure, tourniquet, pelvic binder); 2 large-bore IVs; blood transfusion; target SBP &gt;90 mmHg</li>
<li><strong>D:</strong> GCS, pupils (asymmetry = mass effect); treat increased ICP</li>
<li><strong>E:</strong> fully expose patient; prevent hypothermia (warmed IV fluids, blankets)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Secondary survey:</strong> head-to-toe examination, AMPLE history, imaging (FAST ultrasound, CT trauma series)</p>
<p><strong>Wound management:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Irrigation:</strong> copious normal saline (pressure irrigation for contaminated wounds)</li>
<li><strong>Debridement:</strong> remove necrotic tissue, foreign bodies</li>
<li><strong>Closure:</strong> primary (clean lacerations within 6-8 hours), delayed (contaminated), or secondary healing (by intention)</li>
<li><strong>Tetanus prophylaxis:</strong> Td/Tdap if &gt;5 years since last dose or unknown status</li>
<li><strong>Antibiotics:</strong> for contaminated/bite wounds; prophylaxis for open fractures</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Fracture management:</strong> reduction (closed or open), immobilization (cast, splint, external fixator, ORIF)</p>
