<h1>Sjogrens Syndrome</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Sjogren's syndrome (SS) is a chronic, systemic autoimmune inflammatory disorder characterized by lymphocytic infiltration of exocrine glands -- most often the <strong>lacrimal and salivary glands</strong> -- producing the hallmark <strong>sicca syndrome</strong>: dry eyes (keratoconjunctivitis sicca) and dry mouth (xerostomia). It is the <strong>second most common rheumatic autoimmune disease</strong> after rheumatoid arthritis, affecting <strong>0.1-0.6% of the population</strong> with a striking <strong>9:1 female predominance</strong> and typical onset between ages 40 and 60. SS is classified as <strong>primary</strong> (occurring alone) or <strong>secondary</strong> (associated with another autoimmune disease such as RA, SLE, or systemic sclerosis).</p>
<p>The pathophysiology involves activation of the <strong>type I interferon pathway</strong> and <strong>B-cell hyperactivity</strong>: CD4+ T cells and B cells infiltrate exocrine tissue, producing pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, IL-17, TNF-alpha, BAFF), autoantibodies (anti-Ro/SSA and anti-La/SSB), and hypergammaglobulinemia. BAFF overexpression drives ectopic germinal-center formation in the glands and predisposes to <strong>B-cell lymphoma</strong>, the most feared complication: patients carry a <strong>16-44x increased risk</strong> of non-Hodgkin lymphoma (predominantly marginal-zone MALT lymphoma) -- roughly 5-10% lifetime risk.</p>
<p>Although sicca symptoms dominate the clinical picture, SS is systemic. Extraglandular manifestations include <strong>fatigue</strong> (often the most disabling symptom), <strong>arthralgia/arthritis</strong>, <strong>interstitial lung disease</strong>, <strong>renal tubular acidosis</strong> (type 1, causing hypokalemia and nephrocalcinosis), <strong>peripheral neuropathy</strong>, <strong>vasculitis</strong>, and <strong>cutaneous vasculitis</strong>. The diagnosis integrates objective glandular hypofunction (Schirmer's test, unstimulated salivary flow), anti-Ro/SSA positivity (the most specific antibody), and minor salivary-gland biopsy showing focal lymphocytic sialadenitis (focus score &gt;=1).</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Sicca symptoms (symptomatic):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Dry eyes</strong> -- preservative-free artificial tears (4-6x/day), lubricating ointment at night, punctal plugs; <strong>topical cyclosporine 0.05%</strong> (Restasis) reduces inflammation</li>
<li><strong>Dry mouth</strong> -- frequent sips of water, sugar-free gum/xylitol, saliva substitutes, fluoride varnish; <strong>pilocarpine 5 mg QID</strong> or <strong>cevimeline 30 mg TID</strong> (muscarinic M3 agonists) stimulate saliva -- avoid in asthma, narrow-angle glaucoma</li>
<li><strong>Vaginal dryness</strong> -- non-hormonal lubricants or topical estrogen</li>
<li>Avoid drying medications (anticholinergics, antihistamines, tricyclics) when possible</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Systemic disease:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Hydroxychloroquine</strong> (5 mg/kg/day) -- first-line for fatigue, arthralgia, and cutaneous involvement</li>
<li><strong>Methotrexate</strong> or <strong>azathioprine</strong> for arthritis, myositis, or interstitial lung disease</li>
<li><strong>Rituximab</strong> (anti-CD20) -- best evidence for severe systemic involvement (neuropathy, cryoglobulinemia, refractory disease); two 1 g IV infusions 2 weeks apart</li>
<li><strong>Glucocorticoids</strong> (short courses) for acute flares or severe organ involvement</li>
<li><strong>Belimumab</strong> (anti-BAFF) emerging evidence in SS</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Lymphoma surveillance:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Persistent major salivary-gland enlargement, new lymphadenopathy, falling rheumatoid factor, or monoclonal gammopathy warrant biopsy</li>
</ul>
