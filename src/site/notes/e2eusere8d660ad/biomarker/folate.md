<h1>Folate</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Folate (vitamin B9) is a water-soluble B-vitamin essential for one-carbon metabolism, DNA synthesis (purine and thymidylate biosynthesis), DNA repair, and methylation reactions throughout the body. The term &quot;folate&quot; refers to the naturally occurring forms found in food (primarily as polyglutamate derivatives in dark leafy greens, legumes, liver, asparagus, avocado, Brussels sprouts), while &quot;folic acid&quot; is the fully oxidized synthetic monoglutamate used in supplements and food fortification (mandatory in US cereal grains since 1998). Active folate circulates in serum as 5-methyltetrahydrofolate (5-MTHF), the methyl donor for the remethylation of homocysteine to methionine via methionine synthase (requires vitamin B12 as cofactor).</p>
<p>Folate status is critically important during periods of rapid cell division and growth, making it essential during <strong>pregnancy</strong> (prevention of neural tube defects such as spina bifida and anencephaly), infancy, and adolescence. Periconceptional folic acid supplementation (400-800 mcg daily starting 3-12 months before conception) reduces neural tube defects by 50-70%. Folate deficiency leads to impaired DNA synthesis with megaloblastic anemia (large, immature RBCs), mucosal breakdown (glossitis, diarrhea), and elevated homocysteine (a cardiovascular risk factor). Folate-B12 interdependency is critical: B12 deficiency traps folate as 5-MTHF (the &quot;methyl-folate trap&quot;), causing functional folate deficiency. Conversely, high folate intake can &quot;mask&quot; the hematological signs of B12 deficiency while allowing irreversible neurologic damage to progress.</p>
<p>Serum folate reflects recent dietary intake (days); RBC folate reflects longer-term folate status (months, since RBCs circulate for ~120 days). For clinical assessment of chronic folate status, RBC folate is preferred; serum folate is used for acute assessment. Both are measured by immunoassay or microbiological assay.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level: 5/5</strong> (universally recognized essential nutrient; USPSTF-recommended for pre-conception supplementation; mandatory fortification in 80+ countries).</p>

<p><strong>Caveats:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Serum folate</strong> reflects recent intake (days); fast 8-12 h for accurate assessment</li>
<li><strong>RBC folate</strong> reflects chronic folate status (months); preferred for assessment of body stores</li>
<li><strong>Hemolysis</strong> falsely elevates serum folate (RBC folate is 30x serum concentration)</li>
<li><strong>Recent meal</strong> rich in folate can falsely normalize serum folate in deficient patients (use RBC folate)</li>
<li><strong>Folate fortification</strong> (US since 1998) has substantially reduced deficiency prevalence in fortified populations</li>
<li><strong>Premature infants</strong> have lower baseline folate; supplement prophylactically</li>
</ul>

<h2>Factors Affecting Levels</h2>
<p><strong>Decrease folate (deficiency):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Inadequate dietary intake:</strong> poor diet, alcoholism, anorexia, elderly</li>
<li><strong>Malabsorption:</strong> celiac disease, IBD (Crohn's), gastric bypass, gastrectomy, tropical sprue, H. pylori</li>
<li><strong>Medications:</strong> methotrexate (folate antagonist), phenytoin, trimethoprim, sulfasalazine, oral contraceptives, metformin (B12 depletion), pyrimethamine, triamterene, barbiturates</li>
<li><strong>Alcoholism</strong> (impaired absorption and metabolism)</li>
<li><strong>Increased demand:</strong> pregnancy, lactation, hemolytic anemia, rapid cell turnover (psoriasis, exfoliative dermatitis), hyperthyroidism, malignancy</li>
<li><strong>Hemodialysis / peritoneal dialysis</strong> (folate loss)</li>
<li><strong>Prematurity</strong> (low stores)</li>
<li><strong>MTHFR variants</strong> (TT or compound heterozygous) reduce folate utilization</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Increase folate:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Excessive supplementation</strong> (&gt;1000 mcg/day synthetic folic acid)</li>
<li><strong>High intake of fortified foods</strong> (cereals, bread, pasta in fortified countries)</li>
<li><strong>Small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO)</strong> (bacterial folate synthesis)</li>
<li><strong>Vitamin B12 deficiency</strong> paradoxically: the methyl-folate trap backs up folate as 5-MTHF; serum folate may be normal or high while functional deficiency exists</li>
<li><strong>Blind-loop syndrome</strong></li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p><strong>Low folate is associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Megaloblastic anemia:</strong> macro-ovalocytes, hypersegmented neutrophils, low hemoglobin; clinically indistinguishable from B12 deficiency anemia</li>
<li><strong>Neural tube defects</strong> (in pregnancy): spina bifida, anencephaly, encephalocele; periconceptional folate reduces risk by 50-70%</li>
<li><strong>Elevated homocysteine:</strong> independent CV risk factor (although homocysteine-lowering trials have been mostly negative for CV outcomes)</li>
<li><strong>Recurrent pregnancy loss</strong> (mixed evidence)</li>
<li><strong>Cognitive decline, dementia</strong> (in elderly; mixed evidence)</li>
<li><strong>Depression</strong> (mixed evidence; some studies suggest adjunctive folate helps)</li>
<li><strong>Cancer risk:</strong> low folate may increase colorectal cancer risk (impaired DNA repair); high folate may accelerate pre-existing adenomas (controversial)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Elevated folate is associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Masking vitamin B12 deficiency:</strong> high folate corrects the anemia of B12 deficiency but allows neurologic damage to progress; this is the principal risk of excessive synthetic folic acid intake</li>
<li><strong>Unmetabolized folic acid (UMFA):</strong> at doses &gt;200 mcg synthetic folic acid, the DHFR enzyme cannot reduce all folic acid to active folate; UMFA circulates and may have immunologic effects (controversial)</li>
<li><strong>Colorectal adenoma acceleration</strong> (in patients with pre-existing adenomas): epidemiologic signal from post-fortification era; not definitively causal</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Limitations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Cannot distinguish synthetic folic acid vs. natural folate</strong> in serum assays</li>
<li><strong>Masking of B12 deficiency</strong> at high folate intake -- always check B12 with folate</li>
<li><strong>Acute phase response</strong> can lower serum folate transiently</li>
<li><strong>Dietary recall is unreliable</strong> -- biochemical measurement is the standard</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Monitoring use cases:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Anemia workup (with CBC, B12, homocysteine, methylmalonic acid)</li>
<li>Pre-pregnancy optimization (target RBC folate &gt;400 ng/mL)</li>
<li>Hyperhomocysteinemia evaluation</li>
<li>Cognitive decline workup in elderly</li>
<li>Methotrexate monitoring (RBC folate guides folinic acid rescue)</li>
<li>Post-bariatric surgery nutritional assessment</li>
</ul>
<h2>Strategies to Improve</h2>
<p>Folate status is highly modifiable through diet and supplementation:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Methylfolate (5-MTHF) supplementation</strong> 400-1000 mcg daily -- preferred over synthetic folic acid, especially for MTHFR variant carriers (C677T TT, compound heterozygotes). 5-MTHF bypasses the MTHFR enzyme and avoids UMFA accumulation</li>
<li><strong>Vitamin B12</strong> 500-1000 mcg daily (methylcobalamin preferred) -- always supplement alongside folate to avoid masking B12 deficiency</li>
<li><strong>Diet rich in natural folate:</strong> dark leafy greens (spinach, kale, arugula), legumes (lentils, chickpeas, black beans), asparagus, avocado, Brussels sprouts, broccoli, liver, fortified cereals</li>
<li><strong>Folic acid fortified foods</strong> (US): cereal grains, bread, pasta, rice (mandatory fortification since 1998)</li>
<li><strong>Pre-conception supplementation:</strong> methylfolate or folic acid 400-800 mcg daily starting 3-12 months before conception; continue through first trimester</li>
<li><strong>High-risk pregnancy (MTHFR variants, prior NTD):</strong> methylfolate 1000-4000 mcg daily</li>
<li><strong>Methotrexate patients:</strong> folic acid or methylfolate 1-5 mg daily to reduce mucosal and hepatic toxicity</li>
<li><strong>Reduce alcohol</strong> (depletes folate)</li>
<li><strong>Treat malabsorption:</strong> celiac disease (gluten-free diet), Crohn's (immunosuppression), H. pylori eradication</li>
<li><strong>Post-bariatric surgery:</strong> lifelong high-potency multivitamin with methylfolate and B12</li>
<li><strong>Avoid excessive synthetic folic acid</strong> (&gt;1000 mcg/day) due to UMFA and masking B12 deficiency risks -- use methylfolate if higher doses needed</li>
</ol>
