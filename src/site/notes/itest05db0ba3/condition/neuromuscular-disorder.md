<h1>Neuromuscular Disorder</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Neuromuscular disorders are a heterogeneous group of diseases that impair the function of the motor unit -- the <strong>anterior horn cell (motor neuron), its axon (peripheral nerve), the neuromuscular junction, and the muscle fibers it innervates</strong>. Dysfunction at any level produces weakness, and the clinical task is to localize the lesion anatomically: <strong>motor neuron disease</strong> (ALS, spinal muscular atrophy), <strong>radiculopathy</strong> (nerve root), <strong>plexopathy</strong> (brachial/lumbosacral plexus), <strong>peripheral neuropathy</strong> (length-dependent axonal vs demyelinating), <strong>neuromuscular junction disorder</strong> (myasthenia gravis, Lambert-Eaton), or <strong>primary muscle disease</strong> (muscular dystrophy, inflammatory myopathy, metabolic myopathy).</p>
<p>Collectively these disorders are an important cause of chronic disability. <strong>Diabetic peripheral neuropathy</strong> affects ~50% of patients with long-standing diabetes; <strong>Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease</strong> (hereditary motor-sensory neuropathy) is the most common inherited neuromuscular disorder (1:2500). <strong>Myasthenia gravis</strong> (autoimmune, anti-AChR) has an incidence of ~20 per million. The hereditary muscular dystrophies range from severe childhood disease (<strong>Duchenne muscular dystrophy</strong>, DMD gene, 1:3500 male births) to adult-onset facioscapulohumeral and myotonic dystrophies.</p>
<p>Diagnosis integrates the <strong>tempo</strong> (acute = GBS, vascular; subacute = inflammatory, neoplastic; chronic = hereditary, metabolic), <strong>distribution</strong> (proximal = myopathy; distal = neuropathy; fluctuating = MG), and <strong>associated features</strong> (sensory loss, bulbar, respiratory). Key investigations are <strong>serum creatine kinase</strong> (elevated in muscle disease), <strong>electrodiagnostic studies (EMG/NCS)</strong> to localize and characterize (denervation, demyelination, neuromuscular junction blockade, myopathy), <strong>nerve/muscle biopsy</strong>, and increasingly <strong>genetic testing</strong> (next-generation panels now first-line for suspected hereditary disease).</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p>Management is cause-specific but shares common principles across neuromuscular disorders: preserve motor function, prevent complications (contractures, respiratory failure, aspiration), and address the underlying mechanism when possible.</p>
<p><strong>Disease-modifying therapies (the new era):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Spinal muscular atrophy (SMA)</strong> -- <strong>nusinersen</strong> (Spinraza; antisense SMN2 splicing modifier), <strong>onasemnogene abeparvovec</strong> (Zolgensma; AAV9 gene therapy, single dose), <strong>risdiplam</strong> (oral SMN2 modifier). These have transformed a uniformly fatal infantile disease into a treatable condition</li>
<li><strong>Duchenne muscular dystrophy</strong> -- <strong>corticosteroids</strong> (prednisone, deflazacort) prolong ambulation by 2-5 years; <strong>exon-skipping antisense</strong> (eteplirsen, golodirsen); <strong>AAV micro-dystrophin gene therapy</strong> (delandistrogene moxeparvovec, Elevidys; FDA-approved 2023)</li>
<li><strong>Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS)</strong> -- <strong>riluzole</strong> (glutamate antagonist; ~3 month survival benefit) and <strong>edaravone</strong> (free-radical scavenger); <strong>tocilizumab</strong> and <strong>masitinib</strong> under study; tofersen for SOD1-mutant ALS</li>
<li><strong>Myasthenia gravis</strong> -- <strong>acetylcholinesterase inhibitors</strong> (pyridostigmine), immunosuppression (prednisone, azathioprine, mycophenolate), <strong>IVIG/plasma exchange</strong> for crisis, <strong>rituximab</strong>, and newer complement inhibitors (<strong>eculizumab, ravulizumab</strong>) and FcRn inhibitors (<strong>efgartigimod</strong>) for refractory disease</li>
<li><strong>Inflammatory neuropathies (GBS, CIDP)</strong> -- <strong>IVIG</strong> and plasma exchange (GBS); chronic immunosuppression and IVIG (CIDP)</li>
<li><strong>Metabolic myopathies</strong> -- enzyme replacement for Pompe disease (alglucosidase alfa)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Symptomatic and supportive care (all patients):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Physical and occupational therapy</strong> -- stretching, strengthening within capability, joint protection, energy conservation; prevents contractures and preserves independence</li>
<li><strong>Orthotics and assistive devices</strong> -- ankle-foot orthoses for foot drop, wheelchairs, communication devices</li>
<li><strong>Respiratory management</strong> -- monitor FVC and maximal inspiratory pressure; <strong>non-invasive ventilation</strong> (BiPAP), mechanical insufflation-exsufflation (cough assist) for weak cough</li>
<li><strong>Nutritional support</strong> -- <strong>gastrostomy</strong> for dysphagia (preserves nutrition and prevents aspiration)</li>
<li><strong>Spasticity and cramps</strong> -- baclofen, tizanidine, botulinum toxin; <strong>carbamazepine, mexiletine</strong> for myotonia</li>
<li><strong>Psychosocial support</strong> and advance care planning (especially in progressive disease)</li>
</ul>
