<h1>Garlic</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Garlic (<em>Allium sativum</em>) has been used for cardiovascular and antimicrobial purposes for millennia and is among the best-studied dietary supplements for heart health. Its benefits are attributed to organosulfur compounds: fresh crushed garlic generates allicin, which is unstable and converts to a range of sulfur species, while aged garlic extract (AGE) is standardized to the stable, water-soluble compound S-allylcysteine.</p>
<p>Randomized controlled trials and meta-analyses support a clinically meaningful, modest reduction in blood pressure—particularly in hypertensive individuals—and reductions in total and LDL cholesterol. A more recent line of research connects garlic to the gut-microbiome-derived metabolite trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO). Dietary precursors (choline, L-carnitine, phosphatidylcholine) are converted by gut bacteria to TMA and then oxidized in the liver to TMAO, which is associated with atherosclerosis and adverse cardiovascular events. Garlic organosulfur compounds can inhibit microbial TMA generation and modulate the microbiome, suggesting a TMAO-lowering route to cardiovascular benefit, though human evidence specific to garlic and TMAO remains preliminary.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level: 4/5</strong> — Consistent RCT and meta-analytic evidence for blood pressure and lipids; the TMAO mechanism is biologically grounded but still emerging.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p><strong>1. Hydrogen sulfide signaling and blood pressure:</strong> Garlic organosulfur compounds serve as H2S donors. H2S relaxes vascular smooth muscle and promotes vasodilation, contributing to reductions in systolic and diastolic blood pressure.</p>
<p><strong>2. Cholesterol synthesis:</strong> Allicin and related compounds inhibit hepatic HMG-CoA reductase and other steps of cholesterol biosynthesis, modestly lowering total and LDL cholesterol.</p>
<p><strong>3. TMAO and the gut microbiome:</strong> Garlic compounds inhibit bacterial enzymes that produce trimethylamine (TMA) from dietary choline and carnitine and shift microbiota composition. Because TMA is the precursor of the pro-atherogenic metabolite TMAO, this can reduce circulating TMAO—a mechanism established in preclinical models with growing but still limited human data.</p>
<p><strong>4. Antiplatelet and antioxidant effects:</strong> Garlic inhibits platelet aggregation and scavenges reactive oxygen species, complementing its blood-pressure and lipid effects but also underlying its bleeding-risk profile.</p>
<h2>Dosage</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Aged garlic extract (AGE):</strong> ~600-1200 mg/day, often divided; best evidence for blood pressure</li>
<li><strong>Fresh garlic:</strong> ~1-2 cloves/day (crush and rest ~10 minutes before use to maximize allicin formation)</li>
<li><strong>Standardized garlic powder:</strong> providing ~3.6-5.4 mg/day allicin potential</li>
<li><strong>Form and timing:</strong> Enteric-coated or aged preparations improve tolerability; take with food</li>
<li><strong>Onset:</strong> Blood-pressure and lipid effects typically develop over 8-12 weeks of consistent use</li>
</ul>
