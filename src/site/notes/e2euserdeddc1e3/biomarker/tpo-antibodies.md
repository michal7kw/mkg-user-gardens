<h1>TPO Antibodies</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Thyroid peroxidase antibodies (TPOAb) are autoantibodies directed against <strong>thyroid peroxidase (TPO)</strong>, the membrane-bound enzyme that catalyzes the iodination of thyroglobulin and the coupling reactions essential for producing T4 and T3. Because the antibody targets the central enzymatic machinery of hormone synthesis, TPOAb both marks and contributes to immune-mediated destruction of thyroid follicular cells. It is the <strong>most sensitive and specific serological marker of autoimmune thyroid disease</strong>.</p>
<p>Elevated TPOAb is the hallmark of <strong>Hashimoto's thyroiditis</strong>, the leading cause of hypothyroidism in iodine-sufficient regions, and is present in <strong>~90-95% of affected patients</strong>. It is also detected in <strong>Graves disease</strong> (~70-80% positive) and <strong>postpartum thyroiditis</strong>. Critically, a positive TPOAb result in a person with <strong>subclinical hypothyroidism</strong> is a strong evidence-graded predictor of progression to overt hypothyroidism -- TPOAb positivity increases the annual risk of progression from ~2% to ~5%. This is why TPOAb is used to <strong>risk-stratify</strong> and to <strong>guide monitoring frequency</strong> (ATA recommends annual TSH monitoring in TPOAb-positive subclinical hypothyroidism).</p>
<p>In pregnancy and the postpartum period, TPOAb has added prognostic value: positive women have higher rates of gestational thyroid dysfunction, postpartum thyroiditis, miscarriage, and preterm birth. The antibody titer does not need to be tracked continuously once autoimmunity is established; instead, <strong>TSH and thyroid hormones are followed</strong> to detect and treat evolving hypothyroidism. TPOAb is typically interpreted together with TgAb, TSH, and free thyroid hormones.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level: 5/5</strong> (ATA/ETA guideline-endorsed for autoimmune thyroid disease workup and pregnancy screening).</p>

<p><strong>Caveats:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Reference range is assay-specific (immunoassay platforms vary)</li>
<li>TPOAb positivity without symptoms is common (~10-15% of population)</li>
<li>Titer magnitude correlates loosely with disease activity but not used for monitoring</li>
<li>TPOAb status is generally stable over time; serial changes are not typically tracked</li>
<li>Pregnancy: TPOAb may rise transiently in postpartum thyroiditis</li>
</ul>

<h2>Factors Affecting Levels</h2>
<p><strong>Increase TPOAb:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Hashimoto's thyroiditis</strong> (chronic autoimmune hypothyroidism; ~90-95% TPOAb-positive)</li>
<li><strong>Graves disease</strong> (~70-80% TPOAb-positive)</li>
<li><strong>Postpartum thyroiditis</strong> (transient autoimmune activation)</li>
<li><strong>Other autoimmune disease:</strong> type 1 diabetes, celiac disease, Addison's disease, vitiligo, pernicious anemia (polyglandular autoimmune syndromes)</li>
<li><strong>Excess iodine intake</strong> (kelp, contrast agents) -- may exacerbate autoimmune thyroiditis</li>
<li><strong>Genetic predisposition:</strong> HLA-DR3, HLA-DR5, CTLA-4 polymorphisms</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Decrease TPOAb:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Selenium supplementation</strong> in deficient patients (meta-analyses show ~30-40% titer reduction)</li>
<li><strong>Treatment of underlying autoimmune disease</strong></li>
<li><strong>Levothyroxine therapy</strong> (some studies show modest titer reduction)</li>
<li><strong>Vitamin D repletion</strong> in deficient patients</li>
<li><strong>Recovery from postpartum thyroiditis</strong> (titers fall over months)</li>
<li><strong>Myo-inositol + selenium</strong> (small RCTs show titer reduction in Hashimoto's)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p><strong>Positive TPOAb is associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Hashimoto's thyroiditis:</strong> the most common cause of hypothyroidism in iodine-sufficient regions</li>
<li><strong>Graves disease:</strong> TPOAb positive alongside TSI/TRAb (the diagnostic Graves marker)</li>
<li><strong>Subclinical hypothyroidism progression:</strong> positive TPOAb increases annual progression risk from ~2% to ~5%</li>
<li><strong>Pregnancy complications:</strong> positive TPOAb associated with miscarriage, preterm birth, postpartum thyroiditis, lower IQ in offspring</li>
<li><strong>Other autoimmune disease:</strong> polyglandular autoimmune syndrome (T1DM, celiac, Addison's, vitiligo, pernicious anemia)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Limitations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>TPOAb positivity without symptoms is common</strong> (~10-15% of population); not all progress to overt disease</li>
<li><strong>Titer magnitude correlates loosely with disease activity</strong>; serial monitoring not typically needed</li>
<li><strong>Assay variability</strong> across labs -- not interchangeable</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Monitoring use cases:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Autoimmune thyroid disease workup (with TgAb, TSH, FT4)</li>
<li>Subclinical hypothyroidism workup (predicts progression)</li>
<li>Pregnancy autoimmune thyroid screening (ATA recommends universal TPOAb screening in early pregnancy)</li>
<li>Risk stratification before pregnancy in women with family history of autoimmune thyroid disease</li>
</ul>
<h2>Strategies to Manage</h2>
<p>TPOAb reflects underlying autoimmunity; management focuses on supporting immune balance and thyroid function:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Selenium</strong> 200 mcg daily in selenium-deficient patients with Hashimoto's -- meta-analyses show 30-40% TPOAb and TgAb reduction; supports thyroid antioxidant defenses (glutathione peroxidase)</li>
<li><strong>Vitamin D</strong> 2000-5000 IU D3 daily (target 25-OH-D 40-60 ng/mL) -- observational evidence for lower thyroid antibody titers with adequate vitamin D</li>
<li><strong>Myo-inositol</strong> 600 mg + selenium 83 mcg daily -- small RCTs show antibody reduction in Hashimoto's</li>
<li><strong>Gluten-free diet</strong> in patients with celiac disease (frequent comorbidity with Hashimoto's)</li>
<li><strong>Anti-inflammatory diet</strong> (Mediterranean; omega-3; antioxidants)</li>
<li><strong>Levothyroxine</strong> when hypothyroidism develops (does not reduce antibody titers but treats the consequence)</li>
<li><strong>Avoid excessive iodine</strong> (kelp, contrast agents) which can exacerbate autoimmune thyroiditis</li>
<li><strong>Address pregnancy-related thyroid autoimmunity:</strong> monthly TSH monitoring during pregnancy in TPOAb-positive women; levothyroxine if TSH &gt;2.5 (1st trimester) or &gt;3.0 (2nd/3rd)</li>
<li><strong>Stress management</strong> -- chronic stress may exacerbate autoimmune activity</li>
<li><strong>Smoking cessation</strong> -- smoking is associated with Graves disease</li>
</ol>
