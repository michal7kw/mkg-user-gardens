<h1>Glutamate-Glutamine Cycle</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>The glutamate-glutamine cycle is the metabolic partnership between neurons and astrocytes that keeps excitatory neurotransmission both efficient and safe. After a neuron releases <a href="/garden/e2euserdeddc1e3/neurotransmitter/glutamate/" class="internal-link">Glutamate</a> — the brain's principal excitatory transmitter — surrounding astrocytes rapidly clear it from the synaptic cleft through the high-affinity excitatory amino acid transporters EAAT1/EAAT2. Inside the astrocyte, glutamine synthetase converts glutamate to electrically inert glutamine, which is exported back to neurons and reconverted to glutamate (or to <a href="/garden/e2euserdeddc1e3/neurotransmitter/gaba/" class="internal-link">GABA</a>) by glutaminase. This shuttle accomplishes two things at once: it terminates the glutamate signal so synapses can fire again, and it prevents glutamate from accumulating to excitotoxic levels.</p>
<p>Failure of astrocytic glutamate uptake is a unifying mechanism of neuronal injury, linking this cycle to the excitotoxicity driven by the kynurenine metabolite <a href="/garden/e2euserdeddc1e3/metabolite/quinolinic-acid/" class="internal-link">Quinolinic Acid</a> and to multiple psychiatric and neurodegenerative diseases.</p>
<h2>Key Steps</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Release:</strong> presynaptic neuron releases glutamate into the cleft.</li>
<li><strong>Uptake:</strong> astrocytic EAAT2 (GLT-1) clears ~90% of synaptic glutamate.</li>
<li><strong>Detoxification:</strong> glutamine synthetase converts glutamate → glutamine (astrocyte-specific).</li>
<li><strong>Return:</strong> glutamine is shuttled to neurons and reconverted to glutamate (glutaminase), replenishing the releasable pool; in interneurons it also feeds GABA synthesis.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Regulation and Dysfunction</h2>
<p>Astrocyte transporter expression, energy supply, and ammonia load regulate cycle throughput. Impaired clearance — from transporter downregulation, astrocyte reactivity, or excess excitatory drive — permits glutamate spillover and NMDA-mediated excitotoxicity. Stress and glucocorticoids acutely enhance glutamate release, stressing the cycle.</p>
<h2>Physiological Role</h2>
<p>The cycle maintains the spatial and temporal precision of <a href="/garden/e2euserdeddc1e3/pathway/glutamatergic-pathways/" class="internal-link">Glutamatergic Pathways</a> and supplies substrate for <a href="/garden/e2euserdeddc1e3/pathway/gabaergic-pathways/" class="internal-link">GABAergic Pathways</a>. Its dysregulation is implicated in excitotoxic neurodegeneration and in the glutamatergic abnormalities of mood and psychotic disorders.</p>
