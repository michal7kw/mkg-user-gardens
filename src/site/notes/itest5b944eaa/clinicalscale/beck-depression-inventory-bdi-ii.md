<h1>Beck Depression Inventory (BDI-II)</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>The Beck Depression Inventory-II (BDI-II) is a 21-item self-report instrument that measures the severity of depressive symptoms over the preceding two weeks. It is one of the most widely used depression-severity measures in both clinical and research settings. The original BDI was introduced by Aaron Beck in 1961; the 1996 BDI-II revision updated the recall period and rewrote items to map onto the DSM-IV diagnostic criteria for major depressive episode, including somatic, affective, and cognitive symptoms.</p>
<p>Each item presents four graded statements describing increasing symptom severity, from which the respondent selects the one best matching their experience. Because it is brief and self-administered, the BDI-II is practical for quantifying severity at baseline and tracking change over the course of treatment.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level:</strong> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (5/5)</p>
<h2>Scoring &amp; Interpretation</h2>
<p>The 21 items are each scored 0-3 and summed for a total of 0-63 (higher = more severe depression).</p>

<h2>Psychometrics</h2>
<p>The BDI-II revision was validated in psychiatric outpatients and a college-student sample. The development study reported high internal consistency (Cronbach's α ≈ 0.92 in outpatients) and strong content alignment with DSM-IV major depression criteria. The BDI-II correlates highly with clinician-rated depression scales and is sensitive to treatment-related change, supporting its use as both a severity measure and an outcome tracker. Because several items are somatic, scores may be elevated by physical illness independent of mood.</p>
