<h1>Triple Therapy</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Triple therapy is the classic first-line regimen for eradicating <em>Helicobacter pylori</em>, the bacterium responsible for most peptic ulcer disease, chronic gastritis, and a major proportion of gastric cancer and MALT lymphoma. It combines a proton pump inhibitor (PPI) with two antibiotics—most commonly clarithromycin and amoxicillin (&quot;clarithromycin triple therapy&quot;), substituting metronidazole for amoxicillin in penicillin-allergic patients. The standard course is 10-14 days, with longer durations generally improving eradication rates.</p>
<p>For decades clarithromycin-based triple therapy was the global standard, but rising clarithromycin resistance has eroded its success rate below the desirable 90% threshold in many regions. Current guidelines (e.g., the Maastricht VI/Florence consensus) recommend reserving clarithromycin triple therapy for areas of low clarithromycin resistance (&lt;15%) and otherwise favoring bismuth quadruple therapy or resistance-guided treatment. Eradication should be confirmed after treatment with a urea breath test or stool antigen test.</p>
<p>Evidence Level: 4/5. Extensively studied across numerous randomized trials and consensus guidelines, with well-characterized and region-dependent efficacy.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>Each component addresses a distinct vulnerability of <em>H. pylori</em>. The PPI suppresses gastric acid secretion, raising intragastric pH; this stabilizes acid-labile antibiotics, increases their mucosal concentrations, and shifts <em>H. pylori</em> into a replicative state in which antibiotics are more effective. Amoxicillin inhibits bacterial cell-wall synthesis, clarithromycin inhibits protein synthesis via the 50S ribosomal subunit, and metronidazole (when used) generates cytotoxic radicals under anaerobic conditions. Using two antibiotics with different targets reduces the probability that resistant subpopulations survive—provided baseline resistance to the chosen agents is low.</p>
<h2>Protocol Details</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Standard regimen</strong>: PPI (standard dose) twice daily + clarithromycin 500 mg twice daily + amoxicillin 1 g twice daily</li>
<li><strong>Penicillin-allergic</strong>: substitute metronidazole 500 mg twice daily for amoxicillin</li>
<li><strong>Duration</strong>: 10-14 days (14 days preferred where resistance is a concern)</li>
<li><strong>Confirmation</strong>: urea breath test or stool antigen test ≥4 weeks after therapy and ≥2 weeks off PPI</li>
</ul>
<h2>Safety Information</h2>
<p>Common adverse effects include diarrhea, nausea, abdominal discomfort, and taste disturbance. Clarithromycin is a potent CYP3A4 inhibitor with numerous drug interactions; metronidazole produces a disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol. Triple therapy is contraindicated in patients allergic to its component antibiotics and is discouraged in regions of high clarithromycin resistance, where eradication rates fall unacceptably low.</p>
