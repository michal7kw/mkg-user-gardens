<h1>Resveratrol</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Resveratrol is a stilbene polyphenol found in the skin of red grapes, red wine, berries, and Japanese knotweed (the main commercial source). It gained attention as a putative &quot;caloric-restriction mimetic&quot;: in preclinical models it activates the NAD+-dependent deacetylase SIRT1 and downstream longevity pathways, and it is studied for cardiovascular, metabolic, and anti-inflammatory benefits. Human evidence is mixed and dose-dependent — bioavailability is low (extensive first-pass glucuronidation/sulfation), so effect sizes in trials are modest and formulation matters. It is best viewed as an adjunct within a broader metabolic/longevity strategy rather than a standalone lever.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>Resveratrol is a direct allosteric activator of SIRT1, the NAD+-dependent deacetylase central to longevity pathways. SIRT1 activation leads to deacetylation of PGC-1α, increasing mitochondrial biogenesis and oxidative metabolism. Resveratrol may also activate AMPK (directly and via SIRT1), promoting cellular energy homeostasis. Additional mechanisms include inhibition of NF-κB inflammatory signaling, antioxidant effects, and modulation of various longevity-associated pathways. Works synergistically with NAD+ precursors (NMN, NR) as SIRT1 requires NAD+ as a cofactor.</p>
<h2>Dosage</h2>
<p>Typical supplemental range is <strong>150-500 mg/day</strong> of trans-resveratrol; longevity/metabolic trials have used up to 1 g/day. Doses above ~1 g/day commonly cause GI upset and diarrhoea. Absorption is poor — take with a fat-containing meal, and micronised or piperine/quercetin co-formulations improve bioavailability. Often paired with an NAD+ precursor (NMN/NR) because SIRT1 activation is NAD+-dependent.</p>
