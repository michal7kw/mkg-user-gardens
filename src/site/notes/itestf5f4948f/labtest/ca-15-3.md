<h1>CA 15-3</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>CA 15-3 (Cancer Antigen 15-3) measures a circulating fragment of the MUC1 transmembrane glycoprotein that is shed into the blood by epithelial tumor cells, most notably in breast carcinoma. The analyte is quantified in serum from a standard venipuncture sample using an automated immunoassay, with no fasting or special preparation required.</p>
<p>Clinically, CA 15-3 is ordered chiefly to monitor disease burden and treatment response in patients with known metastatic breast cancer and to help detect recurrence during follow-up. Like other tumor markers, it is a monitoring and adjunctive aid rather than a standalone diagnostic test: it lacks the sensitivity and specificity for screening, and a normal or abnormal result must always be interpreted alongside imaging, histology, and the clinical picture.</p>

<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>Elevated CA 15-3 levels are most strongly associated with metastatic breast cancer, where rising values can correlate with increasing tumor burden and falling values with treatment response. However, modest elevations occur in benign conditions including benign breast and liver disease, and other adenocarcinomas (lung, ovary, pancreas) may also raise it, so the marker is non-specific. Its principal value is in tracking trends over time within an individual patient rather than as a binary positive/negative test.</p>
