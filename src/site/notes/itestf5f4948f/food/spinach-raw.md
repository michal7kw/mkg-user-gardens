<h1>Spinach (raw)</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Dark leafy green vegetable. Exceptional density of vitamin K1, folate, vitamin A (as carotenoids), magnesium, and dietary nitrate (which converts to nitric oxide, modestly lowering blood pressure). Iron content (2.7 mg/100g) is high on paper but bioavailability is poor (~2-10%) due to oxalate binding — a classic illustration of why raw nutrient-density numbers can mislead. Cooking reduces oxalate but also reduces vitamin C, which would otherwise enhance non-heme iron absorption. Pairing with a vitamin C source (lemon juice, bell pepper) substantially improves iron absorption.</p>
<p>The Popeye-cartoon legend that spinach is an iron powerhouse traces partly to a 19th-century decimal-point error in published values; the actual iron content is respectable but not exceptional, and most of it isn't well-absorbed.</p>
