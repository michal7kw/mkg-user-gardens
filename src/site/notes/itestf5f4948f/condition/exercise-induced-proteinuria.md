<h1>Exercise-Induced Proteinuria</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Exercise-induced proteinuria is a <strong>transient, benign</strong> increase in urinary protein excretion that occurs after strenuous physical activity. It is one of the most common causes of <strong>false-positive proteinuria</strong> detected on routine urinalysis in athletes and active individuals. The proteinuria typically resolves within <strong>24-48 hours of rest</strong>, distinguishing it from pathological proteinuria (persistent proteinuria suggesting glomerular disease).</p>
<p>The mechanism involves exercise-induced <strong>renal hemodynamic changes</strong>: increased sympathetic nervous system activity, increased angiotensin II, transient glomerular hypertension, and altered podocyte permeability. These factors combine to increase filtration of normally retained proteins (albumin, beta-2-microglobulin). The pattern may be <strong>glomerular</strong> (albumin-predominant; from increased permeability) or <strong>tubular</strong> (low-molecular-weight proteins; from reduced reabsorption).</p>
<p><strong>Key distinction from pathological proteinuria:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Transient (resolves with rest)</li>
<li>Absence of hematuria (except in exercise-induced hematuria, which is also benign)</li>
<li>Normal renal function (eGFR)</li>
<li>Normal blood pressure</li>
<li>Absence of red cell casts or dysmorphic RBCs</li>
</ul>
<h2>Management</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Reassurance</strong> that this is a benign physiological response</li>
<li><strong>Repeat urinalysis after 48 hours of rest</strong> to confirm resolution</li>
<li><strong>Further workup</strong> if proteinuria persists or is accompanied by:
<ul>
<li>Hematuria (especially dysmorphic RBCs or casts)</li>
<li>Elevated creatinine or eGFR decline</li>
<li>Hypertension</li>
<li>Edema</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Workup for persistent proteinuria: 24-hour urine protein, renal ultrasound, nephrology referral</li>
</ul>
