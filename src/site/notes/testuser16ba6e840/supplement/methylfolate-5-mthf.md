<h1>Methylfolate (5-MTHF)</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Methylfolate, or 5-methyltetrahydrofolate (5-MTHF), is the predominant naturally circulating form of folate (vitamin B9) and the only folate form that is actively transported across the blood-brain barrier. It is chemically and functionally distinct from synthetic folic acid: folic acid is an oxidized provitamin that must be reduced and methylated by intestinal and hepatic enzymes—including methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase (MTHFR)—before it becomes metabolically usable, whereas 5-MTHF is already the active end-product of that pathway.</p>
<p>This distinction matters most for people carrying common MTHFR variants (such as the C677T polymorphism), which reduce enzyme activity and impair conversion of folic acid to 5-MTHF. Supplemental 5-MTHF restores folate status without depending on that conversion step, and it avoids the accumulation of unmetabolized folic acid seen with high folic-acid intakes.</p>
<p>5-MTHF should be considered a distinct supplemental entity from the dietary nutrient &quot;folate&quot; and from any serum-folate biomarker: it refers specifically to the active, supplemented molecule and its clinical applications.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level: 3/5</strong> — Strong pharmacokinetic and biochemical evidence that 5-MTHF raises folate and lowers homocysteine effectively (including in MTHFR variant carriers); moderate clinical-trial evidence for adjunctive benefit in major depressive disorder, with mixed results across populations.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>5-MTHF is the methyl-group carrier that links the folate cycle to the methionine cycle. Through the vitamin B12-dependent enzyme methionine synthase, it transfers its methyl group to homocysteine, regenerating methionine and yielding tetrahydrofolate (THF) for further one-carbon transfers. Methionine is then converted to S-adenosylmethionine (SAMe), the universal methyl donor for hundreds of methylation reactions, including the synthesis and turnover of dopamine, norepinephrine, and serotonin.</p>
<p>Because 5-MTHF enters this cycle downstream of MTHFR, supplementation bypasses the rate-limiting and genotype-sensitive reduction step required for folic acid. This is the basis for its effectiveness in lowering elevated homocysteine and improving folate status in individuals with reduced MTHFR activity.</p>
<h2>Dosage</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>General repletion:</strong> 400-1000 mcg DFE daily</li>
<li><strong>Adjunctive in major depressive disorder:</strong> 7.5-15 mg/day of L-methylfolate (as studied in clinical trials)</li>
<li><strong>Timing:</strong> With or without food</li>
<li><strong>Co-factors:</strong> Pair with vitamin B12; the remethylation reaction is B12-dependent, and folate alone can mask B12 deficiency</li>
</ul>
