<h1>Bifidobacterium longum</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Common gut commensal and one of the first colonizers of the infant gut. Used as a probiotic for IBS, immune modulation, and (subsp. infantis) infant nutrition. Specific strains (e.g., B. longum 1714) have shown effects on stress reactivity and cognition through the gut-brain axis (Allen et al., 2016).</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level:</strong> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (4/5)</p>
<h2>Taxonomy</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>NCBI Taxonomy ID:</strong> <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Taxonomy/Browser/wwwtax.cgi?id=216816">216816</a></li>
<li><strong>Rank:</strong> Species (with subspecies longum, infantis, suis)</li>
<li><strong>Parent Taxon:</strong> Bifidobacterium (genus, family Bifidobacteriaceae, phylum Actinomycetota)</li>
<li><strong>Gram Stain:</strong> Positive</li>
</ul>
<h2>Habitat</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Body Sites:</strong> gut (especially colon)</li>
<li><strong>Oxygen Requirement:</strong> Obligate anaerobe (some strains aerotolerant)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Role</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Pathogenicity:</strong> Beneficial commensal</li>
<li><strong>Key Metabolites Produced:</strong> acetate, lactate, folate (B9), some strains produce GABA</li>
</ul>
<p>Reduces enteric pathogen colonization, modulates host immunity (induces IL-10 and Treg cells), and metabolizes human milk oligosaccharides (HMOs) in infants.</p>
