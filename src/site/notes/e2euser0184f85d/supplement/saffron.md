<h1>Saffron</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Saffron — the dried stigmas of the <em>Crocus sativus</em> flower — is among the best-evidenced botanicals for mood. Its biologically active constituents are the carotenoids crocin and crocetin (responsible for its color) and the volatile aldehyde safranal (responsible for its aroma). Across multiple randomized controlled trials and meta-analyses, standardized saffron extract has shown antidepressant effects superior to placebo and comparable to standard SSRIs in mild-to-moderate <a href="/garden/e2euser0184f85d/condition/depression/" class="internal-link">Depression</a>, with a favorable tolerability profile.</p>
<p>It is of particular interest as a <a href="/garden/e2euser0184f85d/neurotransmitter/serotonin/" class="internal-link">Serotonin</a>-modulating, BDNF-raising natural agent that bridges the neurotrophic and monoaminergic accounts of depression.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>Saffron's constituents act through convergent pathways: modulation of serotonergic neurotransmission (serotonin-reuptake inhibition), strong antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity (crocin), and upregulation of <a href="/garden/e2euser0184f85d/biomarker/bdnf/" class="internal-link">BDNF</a> supporting neuroplasticity via the <a href="/garden/e2euser0184f85d/pathway/bdnf-trkb-signaling-pathway/" class="internal-link">BDNF-TrkB Signaling Pathway</a>. Dopaminergic and NMDA-modulating effects have also been described. The net effect in trials resembles SSRIs for mild-to-moderate depression.</p>
<h2>Dosage</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Typical dose:</strong> 30 mg/day of standardized extract (often split 15 mg twice daily), as used in most depression trials.</li>
<li>Standardization is usually to crocin/safranal content.</li>
<li>Avoid culinary-scale &quot;therapeutic&quot; megadoses; supplemental doses (~30 mg) are well below the toxic threshold.</li>
</ul>
