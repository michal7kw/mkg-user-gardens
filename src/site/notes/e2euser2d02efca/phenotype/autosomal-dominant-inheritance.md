<h1>Autosomal dominant inheritance</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Autosomal Dominant Inheritance acts as one of the fundamental pillars anchoring classical Mendelian genetics. Under this exceptionally aggressive transmission architecture, the mutated pathogenic gene acts with total biological dominance.</p>
<p>Humans natively possess two copies (alleles) of every single gene resting on their 22 numbered autosomes (one strictly inherited from the mother, one strictly inherited from the father). In a dominant disorder, if even <em>one</em> of those copies carries the catastrophic mutation, it completely overwhelms the perfectly healthy &quot;backup&quot; copy resting on the opposite allele. The disease phenotype will manifest physically inside the patient with total certainty despite carrying one mathematically perfect gene.</p>
<p>This fundamentally dictates two terrifying clinical rules when mapping family pedigree trees:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Vertical Transmission:</strong> The disease almost never skips a generated generation. A sick affected parent holds a staggering 50% geometric probability of passing the disease down directly to their child with every single pregnancy.</li>
<li><strong>Sex Independence:</strong> Because the mutated anchor lies strictly on an autosome (and not the X/Y sex chromosomes), the disease strikes males and females with absolutely perfectly equal prevalence and severity.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Clinical Pathologies</h2>
<p>Diseases mapping to this dominant phenotype generally execute their pathology through &quot;toxic gain-of-function&quot; mechanics (the mutant gene violently creates a hyper-toxic protein that destroys cells independently, rather than just failing to do a job).</p>
<p>Iconic Autosomal Dominant horrors include <strong>Huntington's Disease</strong> (where massive CAG repeats create a massive, incredibly toxic Huntingtin protein that violently shreds the basal ganglia) and <strong>Achondroplasia</strong> (where a permanently &quot;stuck-on&quot; FGFR3 receptor completely halts all longitudinal cartilage bone growth).</p>
