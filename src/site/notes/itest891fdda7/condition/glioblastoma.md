<h1>Glioblastoma</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Glioblastoma (GBM) is a <strong>WHO Grade 4 diffuse astrocytoma</strong> -- the most common and most biologically aggressive malignant primary brain tumor in adults. It accounts for approximately <strong>15% of all primary brain tumors</strong> and <strong>50-60% of all gliomas</strong>. The annual incidence is ~3-4 per 100,000. GBM is characterized by rapid growth, diffuse infiltration of surrounding brain parenchyma, extensive neovascularization (angiogenesis), and areas of <strong>pseudopalisading necrosis</strong> (the histological hallmark).</p>
<p><strong>Molecular classification (2021 WHO):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>GBM, IDH-wildtype</strong> (formerly &quot;primary GBM&quot;; ~90% of cases; arises de novo; median age 65; EGFR amplification, TERT mutation, +7/-10)</li>
<li><strong>GBM, IDH-mutant</strong> (formerly &quot;secondary GBM&quot;; ~10%; arises from lower-grade glioma; younger patients ~45; IDH1 R132H mutation; ATRX loss; better prognosis)</li>
</ul>
<p>The <strong>MGMT promoter methylation status</strong> is the most important molecular predictor of response to temozolomide chemotherapy. MGMT-methylated patients have <strong>significantly longer survival</strong> (median ~23 months) compared to unmethylated (~12 months) when treated with the Stupp protocol.</p>
<p>Despite maximal multimodal therapy (surgical resection + radiation + temozolomide + tumor-treating fields), the prognosis remains <strong>dismal</strong>: median survival ~15-20 months with treatment (Stupp protocol); 5-year survival ~5-7%. Recurrence is universal. Treatment of recurrence includes bevacizumab (anti-VEGF), re-resection, and clinical trials.</p>
<h2>Clinical Presentation</h2>
<p><strong>Common symptoms:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Headache</strong> (often progressive; worse in morning; from increased ICP)</li>
<li><strong>Seizures</strong> (new-onset; generalized or focal; more common in younger patients and cortical tumors)</li>
<li><strong>Focal neurological deficits:</strong> hemiparesis, aphasia, visual field deficits (depend on tumor location)</li>
<li><strong>Cognitive and personality changes</strong> (especially frontal/temporal lobe tumors)</li>
<li><strong>Nausea and vomiting</strong> (increased ICP)</li>
<li><strong>Altered level of consciousness</strong> (advanced disease)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Imaging findings (MRI):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Ring-enhancing lesion</strong> with central necrosis (classic appearance on T1 post-contrast)</li>
<li><strong>Surrounding T2/FLAIR edema</strong> (vasogenic; mass effect)</li>
<li><strong>Butterfly pattern</strong> (crossing corpus callosum)</li>
<li><strong>Diffusion restriction</strong> (high cellularity)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Diagnostic Information</h2>
<p><strong>MRI brain with contrast</strong> (gold standard):</p>
<ul>
<li>T1 post-gadolinium: rim-enhancing mass with necrotic center</li>
<li>T2/FLAIR: extensive surrounding edema</li>
<li>DWI/ADC: restricted diffusion (high cellularity)</li>
<li>Perfusion: elevated rCBV (angiogenesis)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Tissue diagnosis (gold standard):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Stereotactic biopsy or surgical resection</li>
<li>Histopathology: pseudopalisading necrosis, microvascular proliferation, mitotic activity</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Molecular testing (essential for prognosis and treatment):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>IDH1 R132H mutation</strong> (distinguishes primary vs secondary GBM)</li>
<li><strong>MGMT promoter methylation</strong> (predicts temozolomide response)</li>
<li><strong>EGFR amplification/mutation</strong> (potential therapeutic target)</li>
<li><strong>TERT promoter mutation</strong></li>
<li><strong>PTEN, TP53 mutations</strong></li>
<li><strong>1p/19q codeletion</strong> (excludes oligodendroglioma)</li>
<li><strong>Ki-67/MIB-1 proliferation index</strong> (high in GBM)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Standard of care (Stupp protocol):</strong></p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Maximal safe surgical resection</strong> (gross total resection if feasible; preserves eloquent cortex; guided by intraoperative MRI, awake craniotomy, fluorescence-guided surgery with 5-ALA)</li>
<li><strong>Concurrent radiotherapy + temozolomide:</strong>
<ul>
<li>External beam radiation: 60 Gy in 30 fractions (2 Gy/fraction, 5 days/week for 6 weeks)</li>
<li>Temozolomide 75 mg/m²/day daily during radiation</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Adjuvant temozolomide:</strong> 150-200 mg/m²/day x 5 days, every 28 days for 6-12 cycles</li>
<li><strong>Tumor-treating fields (TTF; Optune):</strong> alternating electric fields applied to scalp; added to adjuvant temozolomide (improves median survival from ~15 to ~21 months; Stupp et al., 2017)</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Recurrence:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Bevacizumab</strong> (anti-VEGF; 10 mg/kg q2 weeks; improves PFS and QoL but not OS)</li>
<li><strong>Re-resection</strong> (if accessible)</li>
<li><strong>Re-irradiation</strong> (selected cases)</li>
<li><strong>Lomustine (CCNU)</strong> (nitrosourea; modest activity)</li>
<li><strong>Clinical trial enrollment</strong> (strongly encouraged; CAR-T, oncolytic viruses, peptide vaccines, checkpoint inhibitors under investigation)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Supportive care:</strong> corticosteroids (dexamethasone for vasogenic edema), antiepileptics (if seizures), VTE prophylaxis, palliative care, psychological support</p>
