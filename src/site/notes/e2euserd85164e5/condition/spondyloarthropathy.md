<h1>Spondyloarthropathy</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>The spondyloarthropathies (SpA) are a family of chronic inflammatory rheumatic diseases sharing common clinical features: <strong>inflammatory back pain, sacroiliitis, enthesitis, asymmetric oligoarthritis</strong>, and a strong genetic association with <strong>HLA-B27</strong>. The group includes ankylosing spondylitis (AS), psoriatic arthritis (PsA), reactive arthritis (ReA), enteropathic arthritis (IBD-associated), and undifferentiated SpA.</p>
<p>The unifying pathophysiology involves dysregulation of the <strong>IL-23/IL-17 immune axis</strong> at the enthesis (tendon/ligament insertion site) and synovium. HLA-B27 contributes via several mechanisms (arthritogenic peptide hypothesis, misfolding/unfolded protein response, and homodimer formation activating NK cells). Environmental triggers include gut dysbiosis and bacterial infection (especially <em>Chlamydia</em>, <em>Salmonella</em>, <em>Shigella</em>, <em>Yersinia</em>, <em>Campylobacter</em> for reactive arthritis).</p>
<p><strong>Inflammatory back pain</strong> is the hallmark symptom: insidious onset before age 45, worse with rest, improves with exercise, morning stiffness &gt;30 min, and night pain. The ASAS classification criteria use this plus sacroiliitis on imaging or HLA-B27 for diagnosis. Treatment has been revolutionized by TNF inhibitors and IL-17 inhibitors.</p>
<h2>Clinical Presentation</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Inflammatory back pain</strong> (hallmark): insidious onset, age &lt;45, worse with rest, better with exercise, morning stiffness, night pain</li>
<li><strong>Sacroiliitis:</strong> deep buttock pain, alternating sides</li>
<li><strong>Enthesitis:</strong> Achilles tendinitis, plantar fasciitis, costochondritis</li>
<li><strong>Peripheral arthritis:</strong> asymmetric, predominantly lower limbs</li>
<li><strong>Dactylitis:</strong> &quot;sausage digit&quot; (entire finger/toe swollen)</li>
<li><strong>Uveitis:</strong> acute anterior uveitis (unilateral, painful red eye)</li>
<li><strong>Extra-articular:</strong> psoriasis, IBD symptoms, cardiac (aortic regurgitation, conduction defects)</li>
<li><strong>Fatigue</strong> (often significant)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Diagnostic Information</h2>
<p><strong>ASAS criteria (for axial SpA):</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>Sacroiliitis on imaging (MRI or X-ray) + &gt;=1 SpA feature, OR</li>
<li>HLA-B27 + &gt;=2 SpA features</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>SpA features:</strong> inflammatory back pain, arthritis, enthesitis, uveitis, dactylitis, psoriasis, Crohn's/UC, good response to NSAIDs, family history, HLA-B27, elevated CRP</p>
<p><strong>Imaging:</strong> MRI SI joints (active inflammation = bone marrow edema); X-ray sacroiliac joints (structural changes; grading)<br />
<strong>Labs:</strong> elevated CRP/ESR (~50%); HLA-B27 testing</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>NSAIDs</strong> (first-line): naproxen, indomethacin, celecoxib at full dose; continuous therapy preferred<br />
<strong>TNF inhibitors:</strong> adalimumab, etanercept, infliximab, certolizumab, golimumab (for inadequate NSAID response)<br />
<strong>IL-17 inhibitors:</strong> secukinumab, ixekizumab, bimekizumab<br />
<strong>JAK inhibitors:</strong> upadacitinib, tofacitinib (for peripheral SpA)<br />
<strong>Physical therapy:</strong> essential; daily spinal extension exercises<br />
<strong>Local corticosteroid injections</strong> for symptomatic peripheral joints/entheses</p>
