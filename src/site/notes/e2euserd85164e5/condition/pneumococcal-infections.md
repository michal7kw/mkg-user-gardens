<h1>Pneumococcal Infections</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p><em>Streptococcus pneumoniae</em> (pneumococcus) is a <strong>gram-positive, alpha-hemolytic, lancet-shaped diplococcus</strong> with a polysaccharide capsule that is the basis for serotype classification (&gt;90 serotypes). It is a leading cause of <strong>community-acquired pneumonia (CAP), meningitis, bacteremia, otitis media, and sinusitis</strong> worldwide. Before the conjugate vaccine era, pneumococcus caused an estimated <strong>&gt;1 million deaths annually</strong> in children under 5 globally.</p>
<p><strong>Clinical spectrum:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Non-invasive disease:</strong> otitis media (most common bacterial cause in children), sinusitis, bronchitis</li>
<li><strong>Invasive pneumococcal disease (IPD):</strong> pneumonia with bacteremia, meningitis, sepsis</li>
<li><strong>Pneumococcal pneumonia:</strong> the most common form of CAP (~30-50% of bacterial CAP); classic presentation: abrupt onset, fever, rigors, productive rust-colored sputum, pleuritic chest pain, lobar consolidation on CXR</li>
<li><strong>Pneumococcal meningitis:</strong> one of the top 3 causes of bacterial meningitis in adults; case fatality ~15-20% even with treatment; neurological sequelae (hearing loss, cognitive impairment) in ~30%</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Vaccination impact:</strong> The introduction of PCV7 (2000), PCV13 (2010), and now PCV15/PCV20 has dramatically reduced IPD in children and adults through both <strong>direct protection</strong> and <strong>herd immunity</strong>. PCV13 reduced invasive pneumococcal disease by ~90% for vaccine serotypes in children.</p>
<p><strong>Antibiotic resistance:</strong> Penicillin resistance (~5-30% depending on region; via altered penicillin-binding proteins). Macrolide resistance is high (~25-35%). Vaccine serotypes are being replaced by non-vaccine serotypes (serotype replacement).</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Empiric antibiotic therapy:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Community-acquired pneumonia (outpatient):</strong> amoxicillin 1 g TID x 5-7 days; or doxycycline; or respiratory fluoroquinolone (if comorbidities)</li>
<li><strong>CAP (inpatient, non-severe):</strong> ceftriaxone 1 g IV daily + azithromycin 500 mg IV daily; or respiratory fluoroquinolone</li>
<li><strong>CAP (severe/ICU):</strong> ceftriaxone + azithromycin; or beta-lactam + respiratory fluoroquinolone</li>
<li><strong>Meningitis:</strong> ceftriaxone 2 g IV q12h + vancomycin 15-20 mg/kg IV q8-12h + dexamethasone 10 mg IV q6h x 4 days</li>
<li><strong>Bacteremia/sepsis:</strong> ceftriaxone 2 g IV q12-24h; add vancomycin if resistant strain suspected</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>De-escalate</strong> to penicillin G or amoxicillin once sensitivities confirmed (if susceptible)</p>
