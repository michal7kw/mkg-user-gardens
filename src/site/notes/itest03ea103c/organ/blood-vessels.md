<h1>Blood Vessels</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Blood vessels form the circulatory network comprising <strong>arteries</strong> (carry oxygenated blood from heart), <strong>veins</strong> (return deoxygenated blood), and <strong>capillaries</strong> (site of gas and nutrient exchange). The <strong>vascular endothelium</strong> is the body's largest endocrine organ, producing <strong>nitric oxide</strong> (vasodilation), <strong>prostacyclin</strong> (anti-thrombotic), and <strong>endothelin</strong> (vasoconstriction). <strong>Endothelial dysfunction</strong> is an early event in atherosclerosis.</p>
<h2>Vessel Types</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Elastic arteries</strong> (aorta) -- conduct blood; rich in elastin</li>
<li><strong>Muscular arteries</strong> (coronary, renal) -- distribute blood; rich in smooth muscle</li>
<li><strong>Arterioles</strong> -- primary resistance vessels; regulate blood pressure</li>
<li><strong>Capillaries</strong> -- exchange vessels; single endothelial cell layer</li>
<li><strong>Veins</strong> -- capacitance vessels; hold ~70% of blood volume</li>
</ul>
