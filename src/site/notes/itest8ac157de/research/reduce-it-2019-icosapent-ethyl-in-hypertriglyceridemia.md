<h1>REDUCE-IT 2019 — Icosapent Ethyl in Hypertriglyceridemia</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>RCT (n=8,179) of icosapent ethyl (purified EPA, 4 g/day) vs placebo in statin-treated patients with elevated triglycerides and either established CVD or diabetes plus risk factors. Primary composite CV endpoint reduced by 25% (HR 0.75). Re-established the cardiovascular relevance of EPA-only formulations after the failure of mixed EPA/DHA fish oil RCTs (VITAL, ASCEND, STRENGTH). The trial remains controversial because the mineral oil placebo may have driven LDL and CRP up in the control arm, partly inflating the apparent relative benefit.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level:</strong> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (5/5) with active scientific debate</p>
<h2>Citation</h2>
<p>Bhatt DL, Steg PG, Miller M, et al. <em>Cardiovascular Risk Reduction with Icosapent Ethyl for Hypertriglyceridemia.</em> N Engl J Med. 2019;380(1):11-22. PMID: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415628">30415628</a>.</p>
