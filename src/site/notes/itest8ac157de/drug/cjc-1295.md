<h1>CJC-1295</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>CJC-1295 is a long-acting synthetic analogue of <strong>growth-hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH)</strong>. Rather than supplying growth hormone directly, it prompts the pituitary to secrete the body's own <a href="/garden/itest8ac157de/hormone/growth-hormone/" class="internal-link">Growth Hormone</a>, which then raises <a href="/garden/itest8ac157de/hormone/igf-1/" class="internal-link">IGF-1</a>. It is popular in bodybuilding and anti-aging circles — usually stacked with <a href="/garden/itest8ac157de/drug/ipamorelin/" class="internal-link">Ipamorelin</a> — but is <strong>not approved for human use</strong> and is prohibited in sport.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>CJC-1295 binds GHRH receptors on pituitary somatotroph cells, stimulating pulsatile synthesis and release of growth hormone. Elevated growth hormone drives hepatic production of IGF-1, the main mediator of GH's anabolic effects. The &quot;DAC&quot; (drug-affinity-complex) version binds serum albumin, extending its half-life from the minutes of native GHRH to several days. A small proteomic study in healthy adults confirmed that CJC-1295 raises serum GH and IGF-1 (PMID: 19386527).</p>
<h2>Clinical Evidence</h2>
<p>Human data are limited to small pharmacology studies showing that CJC-1295 does raise GH and IGF-1 — a <em>mechanistic</em> effect. There are <strong>no controlled trials</strong> demonstrating that it safely improves muscle mass, body composition or health outcomes. Its <code>evidence_level</code> is set to <strong>1</strong>: the IGF-1 effect is real and educational, but the peptide is intentionally <strong>not</strong> surfaced as a health recommendation.</p>
<h2>Safety</h2>
<p>Because it amplifies the GH/IGF-1 axis, CJC-1295 can cause the classic effects of growth-hormone excess — water retention, joint pain, carpal-tunnel-type tingling — alongside injection-site reactions and the contamination risks of gray-market product. Long-term safety is unknown, and chronic GH/IGF-1 elevation carries theoretical oncological and metabolic concerns.</p>
