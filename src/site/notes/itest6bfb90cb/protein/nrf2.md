<h1>NRF2</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>NRF2 (Nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2), encoded by the <em>NFE2L2</em> gene on chromosome 2q31.2, is the master transcription-factor regulator of the cell's antioxidant and cytoprotective defense program. A member of the cap'n'collar basic-leucine-zipper (CNC-bZIP) family with an apparent molecular mass near 68 kDa, NRF2 governs the inducible expression of more than 200 genes whose products neutralize reactive oxygen species (ROS), conjugate and excrete electrophilic toxins, and restore redox balance. It is the central node of the so-called &quot;antioxidant response,&quot; and its activity underlies the chemoprotective effects of many dietary phytochemicals.</p>
<p>Under basal conditions NRF2 is kept at low levels: it is bound in the cytoplasm by its repressor Keap1 (Kelch-like ECH-associated protein 1), which presents NRF2 to the Cullin-3 (Cul3) E3 ubiquitin ligase complex for continuous ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation, giving the protein a half-life of only minutes. Oxidative stress, electrophiles, or specific small molecules modify reactive cysteine residues on Keap1, disabling the ligase. Newly synthesized NRF2 then escapes degradation, accumulates, translocates to the nucleus, and heterodimerizes with small Maf proteins to bind antioxidant response element (ARE / EpRE) sequences in target-gene promoters.</p>
<p>Because NRF2 sits at the intersection of redox biology, detoxification, inflammation, and metabolism, it is intensely studied in aging, neurodegeneration, cardiovascular disease, and cancer. Its role is dual-edged: sustained NRF2 activation is broadly cytoprotective and is the goal of many nutraceutical and pharmaceutical strategies, yet constitutive NRF2 activation in established tumors can promote chemoresistance and survival of malignant cells — the &quot;dark side&quot; of NRF2.</p>
<h2>Function</h2>
<p>NRF2 is a transcriptional activator. Once nuclear, the NRF2-small Maf heterodimer binds the ARE consensus motif and recruits coactivators to switch on cytoprotective gene batteries, including:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Glutathione system</strong> — glutamate-cysteine ligase (GCLC/GCLM), glutathione reductase, and glutathione S-transferases, raising intracellular <a href="/garden/itest6bfb90cb/supplement/glutathione/" class="internal-link">Glutathione</a> and conjugation capacity.</li>
<li><strong>Antioxidant enzymes</strong> — NAD(P)H:quinone oxidoreductase 1 (NQO1), heme oxygenase-1 (HMOX1/HO-1), thioredoxin, peroxiredoxins, and sulfiredoxin.</li>
<li><strong>Phase II detoxification and efflux</strong> — UDP-glucuronosyltransferases and multidrug resistance-associated transporters that conjugate and export xenobiotics.</li>
<li><strong>NADPH-generating enzymes</strong> — glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase and malic enzyme, supporting the reducing power that antioxidant enzymes require.</li>
</ul>
<p>By coordinating these programs, NRF2 buffers cells against <a href="/garden/itest6bfb90cb/condition/oxidative-stress/" class="internal-link">Oxidative Stress</a> and excess <a href="/garden/itest6bfb90cb/metabolite/reactive-oxygen-species/" class="internal-link">Reactive Oxygen Species</a>, and supports xenobiotic <a href="/garden/itest6bfb90cb/organ/liver/" class="internal-link">Liver</a> detoxification.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>The Keap1-NRF2-ARE axis is a thiol-based sensor-effector system. Keap1 is cysteine-rich; key sensor cysteines (notably Cys151, Cys273, and Cys288) act as a tripwire. Electrophiles and oxidants covalently modify or oxidize these thiols, altering Keap1 conformation so that it can no longer efficiently hand NRF2 to the Cul3 ligase. Pre-existing NRF2 is spared and newly translated NRF2 accumulates. The &quot;hinge-and-latch&quot; model holds that the NRF2 Neh2 domain docks to Keap1 through high-affinity (ETGE) and low-affinity (DLG) motifs; cysteine modification releases the latch and stalls ubiquitin transfer.</p>
<p>Stabilized NRF2 enters the nucleus, dimerizes with small Maf proteins, and transactivates ARE-driven genes. Phosphorylation by kinases (e.g., PKC, PI3K/AKT pathway signaling) and the competing p62/SQSTM1 autophagy route provide additional, Keap1-independent activation inputs. Termination occurs as redox balance is restored, Keap1 is regenerated, and degradation resumes, making the response transient and self-limiting.</p>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>NRF2 is a therapeutic target across many disease areas. Pharmacological activators (e.g., dimethyl fumarate, approved for multiple sclerosis and psoriasis, and the semisynthetic triterpenoid bardoxolone methyl studied in chronic kidney disease) and dietary activators such as <a href="/garden/itest6bfb90cb/supplement/sulforaphane/" class="internal-link">Sulforaphane</a> from broccoli sprouts exploit the same Keap1-cysteine mechanism. Robust NRF2 signaling is associated with resistance to <a href="/garden/itest6bfb90cb/clinicalpattern/chronic-inflammation/" class="internal-link">Chronic Inflammation</a> and <a href="/garden/itest6bfb90cb/condition/inflammation/" class="internal-link">Inflammation</a>, partly through crosstalk that dampens the <a href="/garden/itest6bfb90cb/pathway/nf-kb-pathway/" class="internal-link">NF-kB Pathway</a>, and with protection against age-related decline in antioxidant capacity.</p>
<p>The relationship with <a href="/garden/itest6bfb90cb/condition/cancer/" class="internal-link">Cancer</a> is bidirectional. NRF2 activation by chemopreventive agents protects normal cells from carcinogen-induced damage, but somatic <em>NFE2L2</em> gain-of-function or <em>KEAP1</em> loss-of-function mutations in tumors create constitutive NRF2 activity that fuels proliferation, metabolic reprogramming, and resistance to chemotherapy and radiotherapy. This duality means NRF2 modulation must be context-specific — activation for prevention and cytoprotection, inhibition in NRF2-addicted tumors.</p>
<h2>Regulation and Interactions</h2>
<p>NRF2 activity is shaped by Keap1 abundance, the autophagy adaptor p62/SQSTM1 (which sequesters Keap1), GSK-3β-mediated phosphorylation (a Keap1-independent degradation route), and the circadian clock. Many supplements and phytochemicals act as indirect NRF2 activators by generating mild electrophilic or oxidative signals that modify Keap1 — a form of hormesis. NRF2 also functionally intersects with <a href="/garden/itest6bfb90cb/protein/sirt1/" class="internal-link">SIRT1</a> in coordinating stress-resistance and metabolic programs, and its induction of antioxidant genes supports <a href="/garden/itest6bfb90cb/cellularcomponent/mitochondria/" class="internal-link">Mitochondria</a> by limiting oxidative damage to mitochondrial components.</p>
