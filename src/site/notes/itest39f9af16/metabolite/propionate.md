<h1>Propionate</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Propionate (propionic acid, C3) is a three-carbon short-chain fatty acid (SCFA) produced in the colon by anaerobic bacterial fermentation of non-digestible carbohydrates — chiefly dietary fiber, resistant starch, and prebiotic oligosaccharides. With acetate and butyrate it forms the major SCFA pool, typically accounting for roughly 20-25% of colonic SCFAs.</p>
<p>Unlike butyrate, which is consumed locally by colonocytes, propionate is largely absorbed and transported via the portal vein to the liver, where it is extensively metabolized. There it acts as a gluconeogenic precursor and, importantly, suppresses hepatic lipogenesis and cholesterol synthesis — a mechanism long proposed to underlie the cholesterol-lowering effect of fermentable fiber. Propionate also signals systemically through the free fatty acid receptors FFAR2 (GPR43) and FFAR3 (GPR41) on enteroendocrine and adipose tissue, stimulating release of the satiety hormones GLP-1 and PYY and modulating glucose homeostasis and inflammation.</p>

<h2>Biochemical Role</h2>
<p>Propionate is generated by gut bacteria primarily through the succinate pathway (Bacteroidetes) and the propanediol/acrylate pathways. Following absorption, it crosses the colonocyte and enters portal blood; the liver extracts the majority. Hepatic propionate is activated to propionyl-CoA, carboxylated to methylmalonyl-CoA, and isomerized to succinyl-CoA, which enters the TCA cycle and gluconeogenesis. By providing carbon for glucose synthesis while inhibiting acetyl-CoA carboxylase and HMG-CoA reductase activity, propionate shifts hepatic metabolism away from de novo lipid and cholesterol synthesis.</p>
<p>As a signaling ligand at FFAR2/FFAR3, propionate triggers enteroendocrine L-cell secretion of GLP-1 and PYY, contributing to appetite suppression and improved insulin secretion.</p>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Lipid and glucose homeostasis:</strong> By promoting GLP-1/PYY secretion and restraining hepatic lipogenesis, propionate is implicated in improved satiety and glycemic control relevant to <a href="/garden/itest39f9af16/condition/insulin-resistance/" class="internal-link">Insulin Resistance</a> and <a href="/garden/itest39f9af16/labtest/triglycerides/" class="internal-link">Triglycerides</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Inflammation:</strong> SCFAs including propionate support regulatory immune tone and barrier integrity; depletion accompanies intestinal <a href="/garden/itest39f9af16/condition/inflammation/" class="internal-link">Inflammation</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Caveat:</strong> Some studies link colonic propionate signaling to hepatic glucose production, so its net metabolic effect is context-dependent.</li>
</ul>
