<h1>Anti-CCP Antibody</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Anti-cyclic citrullinated peptide (anti-CCP) antibody -- also called anti-citrullinated protein antibody (ACPA) -- is the most specific serologic marker for rheumatoid arthritis (RA). Anti-CCP antibodies target citrullinated proteins: proteins in which arginine residues have been enzymatically converted to citrulline by peptidylarginine deiminase (PAD) enzymes. Citrullination is a normal post-translational modification that occurs in inflamed synovium, creating neo-epitopes that break immune tolerance in genetically susceptible individuals (HLA-DRB1 &quot;shared epitope&quot; alleles).</p>
<p>The clinical utility of anti-CCP is its <strong>superb specificity</strong> for RA (95-98% for anti-CCP2 assay), which contrasts with the much lower specificity of rheumatoid factor (RF; ~70-80%). Anti-CCP positivity combined with RF positivity has a positive predictive value (PPV) for RA exceeding 99%. Even in isolation, anti-CCP positivity in a patient with inflammatory arthritis has PPV ~90% for RA. The 2010 ACR/EULAR RA classification criteria assign anti-CCP positivity a weighted score of 3 points (high-positive) or 2 points (low-positive), reflecting its diagnostic weight.</p>
<p>Anti-CCP antibodies can be detected in serum <strong>5-10 years before the onset of clinical arthritis</strong>, indicating a long preclinical autoimmune phase. This enables research into preclinical RA interventions. Higher titers and double positivity (anti-CCP+ and RF+) predict more aggressive erosive disease, faster radiographic progression, and worse functional outcomes. Anti-CCP status is generally fixed throughout life (unlike RF which can fluctuate); once positive, a patient tends to remain positive, with titer changes that are modest.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level: 5/5</strong> (ACR/EULAR classification criteria component; universal clinical use).</p>

<p><strong>Caveats:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Reference range is assay-specific (anti-CCP2 ELISA varies by manufacturer; second-generation assays are most common)</li>
<li>Approximately 70-80% of RA patients are anti-CCP positive (&quot;seropositive RA&quot;); 20-30% are anti-CCP negative (&quot;seronegative RA&quot;)</li>
<li>Anti-CCP status is generally fixed throughout life (unlike RF); serial monitoring not typically useful</li>
<li>Rare false positives in: SLE, Sjögren's syndrome, psoriatic arthritis, tuberculosis, chronic hepatitis C (~5-10% false-positive rate)</li>
</ul>

<h2>Factors Affecting Levels</h2>
<p><strong>Increase anti-CCP:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Rheumatoid arthritis (the principal clinical context)</li>
<li>Preclinical RA (5-10 years before symptom onset)</li>
<li>Rare false positives in: SLE, Sjögren's, psoriatic arthritis, chronic TB, HCV (~5-10% false-positive rate)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Decrease anti-CCP:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (DMARD) therapy (modest titer reduction in some patients)</li>
<li>Disease remission (titers tend to be stable; some patients show modest decline)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Risk factors for being anti-CCP positive in RA:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>HLA-DRB1 shared epitope alleles (genetic susceptibility)</li>
<li>PADI4 gene polymorphisms (especially East Asian populations)</li>
<li>Smoking (potent environmental trigger; interacts with shared epitope)</li>
<li>Silica exposure</li>
<li>Periodontitis (Porphyromonas gingivalis expresses PAD and citrullinates host proteins)</li>
<li>Obesity (chronic inflammation)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p><strong>Positive anti-CCP is associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Rheumatoid arthritis diagnosis:</strong> sensitivity 70-80%, specificity 95-98%; combined with RF, PPV &gt;99%</li>
<li><strong>Preclinical RA:</strong> detectable 5-10 years before symptom onset; enables research into prevention</li>
<li><strong>Erosive / aggressive disease:</strong> higher titers predict faster radiographic progression, more joint destruction</li>
<li><strong>Extra-articular disease:</strong> anti-CCP+ RA has higher rates of nodules, vasculitis, interstitial lung disease, cardiovascular comorbidity</li>
<li><strong>Worse functional outcomes:</strong> long-term disability more common in anti-CCP+ RA</li>
<li><strong>Better response to certain therapies:</strong> anti-CCP+ RA may respond better to rituximab (anti-CD20)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Limitations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Sensitivity 70-80%</strong> -- 20-30% of RA patients are anti-CCP negative (&quot;seronegative RA&quot;); a negative result does NOT exclude RA</li>
<li><strong>Status generally fixed throughout life</strong> -- serial monitoring less useful than for anti-dsDNA in SLE</li>
<li><strong>Not useful for disease activity monitoring</strong> -- titers change modestly with therapy; ESR, CRP, DAS28 are preferred for activity</li>
<li><strong>Rare false positives</strong> in other autoimmune / infectious diseases (~5-10%)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Monitoring use cases:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>RA diagnosis (with RF, ESR, CRP, anti-nuclear antibodies)</li>
<li>Preclinical RA research (in first-degree relatives of RA patients)</li>
<li>Prognostic stratification at diagnosis</li>
<li>Differential diagnosis: distinguish RA from psoriatic arthritis, SLE arthropathy, osteoarthritis</li>
</ul>
<h2>Strategies to Manage</h2>
<p>Anti-CCP status reflects an active autoimmune process and is generally fixed throughout life. Management targets the underlying RA:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>First-line DMARD: methotrexate</strong> 15-25 mg/week PO/SC with folic acid; the &quot;anchor&quot; DMARD for seropositive RA; start within 3 months of symptom onset (treat-to-target per ACR/EULAR 2022)</li>
<li><strong>Combination DMARDs</strong> (triple therapy: methotrexate + sulfasalazine + hydroxychloroquine) -- comparable efficacy to biologic monotherapy in some trials</li>
<li><strong>Biologic DMARDs for inadequate response to methotrexate:</strong>
<ul>
<li>TNF inhibitors (adalimumab, etanercept, infliximab, certolizumab, golimumab)</li>
<li>IL-6 receptor blockers (tocilizumab, sarilumab)</li>
<li>T-cell co-stimulation blocker (abatacept)</li>
<li>Anti-CD20 B-cell depleter (rituximab -- particularly effective in anti-CCP+ RA)</li>
<li>JAK inhibitors (tofacitinib, baricitinib, upadacitinib)</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Treat-to-target strategy:</strong> aim for remission (DAS28 &lt;2.6) or low disease activity; escalate therapy every 3-6 months until target reached</li>
<li><strong>Smoking cessation</strong> -- the most important modifiable risk factor for anti-CCP+ RA development and progression</li>
<li><strong>Periodontal treatment</strong> -- growing evidence that treating periodontitis reduces anti-CCP titers and RA disease activity</li>
<li><strong>Omega-3 fatty acids</strong> (1-4 g/day) -- modest anti-inflammatory effect; observational evidence for reduced RA risk</li>
<li><strong>Mediterranean diet</strong> -- observational evidence for lower RA activity</li>
<li><strong>Physical therapy, occupational therapy</strong> -- joint protection, exercise, splinting</li>
<li><strong>Vaccination before biologics</strong> (pneumococcal, influenza, hepatitis B, zoster)</li>
<li><strong>Cardiovascular risk factor management</strong> -- anti-CCP+ RA has elevated CV risk</li>
</ol>
