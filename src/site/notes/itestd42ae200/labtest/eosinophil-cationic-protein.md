<h1>Eosinophil Cationic Protein (ECP)</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Eosinophil cationic protein (ECP) is a ribonuclease stored in the secondary granules of eosinophils and released when these cells are activated. The test measures ECP in serum by immunoassay following a standardized clotting protocol, since the value reflects in vitro release during clotting and is sensitive to sample handling. ECP is a functional marker of eosinophil activation, complementing the simple eosinophil count which only reports cell numbers.</p>
<p>It is ordered as a specialty test to assess the activity of eosinophilic inflammation, most often in asthma and atopic disease, and occasionally to monitor response to anti-inflammatory therapy. Because of pre-analytical sensitivity, ECP is used less routinely than the blood eosinophil count.</p>

<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>Elevated ECP reflects active eosinophil degranulation and correlates with the intensity of eosinophilic (Type-2) inflammation. Higher levels are seen in poorly controlled asthma, atopic dermatitis, allergic rhinitis, eosinophilic esophagitis, and parasitic infection. ECP can be used to monitor inflammatory activity and treatment response, with falling levels suggesting effective suppression of eosinophilic inflammation (e.g., with corticosteroids). Because results are strongly affected by sample collection and clotting time, ECP is interpreted alongside the absolute eosinophil count and clinical picture rather than in isolation.</p>
