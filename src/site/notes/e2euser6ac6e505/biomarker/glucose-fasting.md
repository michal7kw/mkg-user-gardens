<h1>Glucose Fasting</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Fasting plasma glucose (FPG) measures the concentration of glucose in blood after an 8-12 hour overnight fast, reflecting the balance between hepatic glucose production (via glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis) and peripheral glucose utilization. In the fasting state, the liver is the primary source of circulating glucose, and plasma glucose levels are maintained within a narrow range (70-100 mg/dL) by the opposing actions of insulin and counter-regulatory hormones (glucagon, cortisol, epinephrine, growth hormone).</p>
<p>FPG is the most widely used screening and diagnostic test for diabetes mellitus and prediabetes, recommended by the American Diabetes Association (ADA), World Health Organization (WHO), and International Diabetes Federation (IDF). A value of &gt;=126 mg/dL on two separate occasions is diagnostic for diabetes, while values of 100-125 mg/dL define impaired fasting glucose (IFG), a prediabetic state associated with increased risk of progression to type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease. FPG is routinely measured as part of <a href="/garden/e2euser6ac6e505/labtest/basic-metabolic-panel/" class="internal-link">basic metabolic panel</a> and <a href="/garden/e2euser6ac6e505/labtest/comprehensive-metabolic-panel/" class="internal-link">comprehensive metabolic panel</a> tests.</p>
<p>FPG complements other glycemic markers: while <a href="/garden/e2euser6ac6e505/labtest/homa-ir/" class="internal-link">Homa Ir</a> (HOMA-IR) assesses insulin resistance and hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) reflects average glycemia over 2-3 months, FPG provides a snapshot of current basal glucose homeostasis. Together, these markers offer a comprehensive picture of glucose metabolism and diabetes risk.</p>

<p><strong>Notes:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Diagnostic criteria per ADA 2024 guidelines: diabetes requires confirmation with a second abnormal test (FPG, 2-hr OGTT, or HbA1c) unless classic hyperglycemia symptoms are present</li>
<li>IFG (prediabetes) affects approximately 96 million US adults; without intervention, 15-30% progress to diabetes within 5 years</li>
<li>FPG is less sensitive than 2-hour oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) for detecting early glucose dysregulation; some individuals have normal FPG but abnormal post-challenge glucose</li>
<li>Plasma glucose values are approximately 10-15% higher than whole blood glucose values</li>
<li>Reference ranges may vary slightly between laboratories and assay methods</li>
</ul>

<h2>Factors Affecting Levels</h2>
<h3>Increase Fasting Glucose</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Insulin resistance</strong> (impaired peripheral glucose uptake, elevated hepatic gluconeogenesis)</li>
<li><strong>Dawn phenomenon</strong> (physiological early morning cortisol and growth hormone surge, 4-8 AM)</li>
<li><strong>Stress and illness</strong> (counter-regulatory hormone release — cortisol, catecholamines, glucagon)</li>
<li><strong>Corticosteroid therapy</strong> (direct stimulation of gluconeogenesis, impaired insulin signaling)</li>
<li><strong>Sleep deprivation</strong> (increases cortisol and sympathetic tone, reduces insulin sensitivity)</li>
<li><strong>High glycemic index evening meal</strong> (residual effect on morning glucose)</li>
<li><strong>Sedentary lifestyle</strong> (reduced GLUT4 translocation and muscle glucose uptake)</li>
<li><strong>Hepatic disease</strong> (altered glycogen storage and gluconeogenic capacity)</li>
</ul>
<h3>Decrease Fasting Glucose</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Insulin or sulfonylurea excess</strong> (iatrogenic hypoglycemia)</li>
<li><strong>Intense evening exercise</strong> (enhanced glucose uptake, glycogen depletion)</li>
<li><strong>Alcohol consumption without food</strong> (inhibits hepatic gluconeogenesis)</li>
<li><strong>Adrenal insufficiency</strong> (cortisol deficiency reduces gluconeogenesis)</li>
<li><strong>Severe hepatic failure</strong> (impaired glucose production)</li>
<li><strong>Insulinoma</strong> (autonomous insulin secretion from pancreatic beta-cell tumor)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<h3>Elevated levels are associated with:</h3>
<ul>
<li>
<p><strong>Type 2 Diabetes</strong>: FPG &gt;=126 mg/dL is a primary diagnostic criterion. Chronic hyperglycemia drives microvascular (retinopathy, nephropathy, neuropathy) and macrovascular (coronary artery disease, stroke) complications through advanced glycation end-products (AGEs), oxidative stress, and endothelial dysfunction.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Prediabetes (Impaired Fasting Glucose)</strong>: FPG 100-125 mg/dL indicates impaired glucose regulation. IFG is a strong independent risk factor for progression to type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease. Annual conversion rate is approximately 5-10%, modifiable with lifestyle intervention.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Metabolic Syndrome</strong>: Elevated FPG (&gt;=100 mg/dL) is one of five diagnostic criteria for metabolic syndrome (along with central obesity, elevated triglycerides, reduced HDL, and hypertension). The syndrome reflects underlying <a href="/garden/e2euser6ac6e505/condition/insulin-resistance/" class="internal-link">Insulin Resistance</a> and carries 2-fold increased cardiovascular risk.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Cardiovascular Disease</strong>: Fasting glucose in the upper normal range (85-99 mg/dL) is independently associated with increased cardiovascular mortality in prospective cohort studies, even below the diabetes threshold. This risk is mediated through endothelial dysfunction, dyslipidemia, and pro-inflammatory signaling.</p>
</li>
</ul>
<h3>Reduced levels are associated with:</h3>
<ul>
<li>
<p><strong>Hypoglycemia</strong> (&lt;70 mg/dL): Most commonly caused by diabetes medications (insulin, sulfonylureas). Symptoms include tremor, sweating, confusion, tachycardia, and in severe cases (&lt;54 mg/dL), seizures and loss of consciousness. Recurrent hypoglycemia impairs hypoglycemia awareness and is associated with increased mortality.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Insulinoma</strong>: Rare pancreatic beta-cell tumor causing recurrent fasting hypoglycemia. Diagnosis requires demonstration of Whipple triad: hypoglycemia symptoms, low plasma glucose, and symptom relief with glucose administration.</p>
</li>
</ul>
<h3>Monitoring use cases:</h3>
<ul>
<li>Diabetes screening in adults &gt;=35 years or at-risk individuals (ADA recommendation)</li>
<li>Monitoring glycemic control in diagnosed diabetes (alongside HbA1c)</li>
<li>Assessing prediabetes progression and response to lifestyle intervention</li>
<li>Evaluating dawn phenomenon and fasting hyperglycemia in insulin-treated patients</li>
<li>Diagnosing hypoglycemia (fasting or reactive)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Strategies to Improve</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Maintain healthy body weight</strong> — even 5-7% weight loss in overweight individuals with prediabetes reduces diabetes progression by 58% (DPP trial)</li>
<li><strong>Regular physical activity</strong> — minimum 150 minutes/week of moderate aerobic exercise; improves insulin sensitivity and fasting glucose within 24-72 hours</li>
<li><strong>Low glycemic index diet</strong> — emphasize whole grains, legumes, vegetables; reduce refined carbohydrates and added sugars</li>
<li><strong>Time-restricted eating / intermittent fasting</strong> — 12-14 hour overnight fast may improve insulin sensitivity and morning fasting glucose</li>
<li><strong>Adequate sleep</strong> (7-9 hours) — sleep deprivation directly impairs glucose tolerance and increases fasting glucose</li>
<li><strong>Targeted supplementation</strong> — <a href="/garden/e2euser6ac6e505/supplement/berberine/" class="internal-link">Berberine</a> (strongest evidence for glucose lowering), <a href="/garden/e2euser6ac6e505/supplement/magnesium/" class="internal-link">Magnesium</a> (if deficient), <a href="/garden/e2euser6ac6e505/supplement/chromium/" class="internal-link">Chromium</a>, <a href="/garden/e2euser6ac6e505/supplement/alpha-lipoic-acid/" class="internal-link">Alpha Lipoic Acid</a>, and <a href="/garden/e2euser6ac6e505/supplement/vitamin-d/" class="internal-link">Vitamin D</a> (if deficient) all have RCT evidence for modest fasting glucose reduction</li>
</ol>
