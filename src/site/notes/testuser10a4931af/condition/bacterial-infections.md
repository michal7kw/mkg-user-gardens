<h1>Bacterial Infections</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Bacterial infections represent a major category of human disease caused by pathogenic bacteria invading host tissues and multiplying. Bacteria are classified by <strong>Gram stain</strong> (positive vs negative), <strong>morphology</strong> (cocci, bacilli, spirochetes), <strong>oxygen requirement</strong> (aerobic, anaerobic, facultative), and <strong>growth pattern</strong> (intracellular vs extracellular). This classification guides empiric antibiotic selection before culture results are available.</p>
<p><strong>Common gram-positive pathogens:</strong> <em>Staphylococcus aureus</em> (including MRSA), <em>Streptococcus pyogenes</em> (GAS), <em>Streptococcus pneumoniae</em>, <em>Enterococcus</em>, <em>Clostridium difficile</em>, <em>Bacillus anthracis</em></p>
<p><strong>Common gram-negative pathogens:</strong> <em>Escherichia coli</em>, <em>Klebsiella</em>, <em>Pseudomonas aeruginosa</em>, <em>Neisseria meningitidis</em>, <em>Salmonella</em>, <em>Helicobacter pylori</em>, <em>Chlamydia</em></p>
<p>The clinical spectrum ranges from <strong>localized infections</strong> (cellulitis, abscess, pharyngitis, UTI, pneumonia) to <strong>systemic infection</strong> (bacteremia, sepsis, septic shock). The <strong>host immune response</strong> (innate: complement, neutrophils, macrophages; adaptive: antibodies, T-cells) determines the outcome alongside the virulence of the pathogen.</p>
<p><strong>Antibiotic resistance</strong> (AMR) is a critical global health threat -- MRSA, VRE, CRE, ESBL-producing organisms, and multidrug-resistant <em>Pseudomonas</em> increasingly limit treatment options. <strong>Antimicrobial stewardship</strong> is essential.</p>
<h2>Diagnostic Information</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>CBC:</strong> leukocytosis with left shift, bandemia</li>
<li><strong>CRP, procalcitonin</strong> (procalcitonin more specific for bacterial vs viral)</li>
<li><strong>Blood cultures</strong> (before antibiotics if possible)</li>
<li><strong>Culture of infected site</strong> (wound, sputum, urine, CSF)</li>
<li><strong>Gram stain</strong> (rapid initial categorization)</li>
<li><strong>Molecular diagnostics</strong> (PCR, multiplex panels)</li>
<li><strong>Imaging</strong> (dependent on suspected site: chest X-ray, CT, ultrasound)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Empiric therapy</strong> (before culture results):</p>
<ul>
<li>Know local antibiogram and resistance patterns</li>
<li>Consider MRSA risk factors for vancomycin inclusion</li>
<li>Escalate for sepsis/septic shock (broad-spectrum: piperacillin-tazobactam + vancomycin)</li>
<li>De-escalate once organism and sensitivities identified</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Duration optimization:</strong> shortest effective course (typically 5-7 days for most infections)</p>
<p><strong>Source control:</strong> abscess drainage, infected catheter removal, debridement of necrotic tissue</p>
<p><strong>Sepsis:</strong> hour-1 bundle (lactate, blood cultures, broad-spectrum antibiotics, 30 mL/kg crystalloid, vasopressors for hypotension)</p>
