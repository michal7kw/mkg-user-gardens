<h1>Histamine H1 Receptor</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>The histamine H1 receptor (UniProt: P35367) is a Gq/11-coupled G-protein coupled receptor encoded by the HRH1 gene. Activation by histamine stimulates phospholipase C, generating IP3 and diacylglycerol, raising intracellular calcium, and producing the classic effects of histamine in the periphery: increased vascular permeability (wheal and flare), vasodilation, bronchial and gut smooth-muscle contraction, and sensory nerve stimulation causing itch and pain. In the central nervous system, H1 receptors promote wakefulness and arousal, which is why centrally penetrant antihistamines cause sedation.</p>
<h2>Pharmacology</h2>
<p>H1 antihistamines act as inverse agonists, stabilizing the receptor's inactive conformation. First-generation agents (diphenhydramine, chlorpheniramine) cross the blood-brain barrier and cause sedation and anticholinergic effects, whereas second-generation agents (loratadine, cetirizine, fexofenadine) are designed to be peripherally selective, providing antiallergic effect with little sedation. H1 antagonism is the basis of treatment for allergic rhinitis, urticaria, and allergic conjunctivitis.</p>
