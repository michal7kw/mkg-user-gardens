<h1>Cinnamon</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Cinnamon is one of the most studied culinary spices for metabolic health, valued for its potential to improve glycemic control in people with type 2 diabetes, prediabetes, and metabolic syndrome. The two main commercial types are Cassia cinnamon (<em>Cinnamomum cassia</em>, the most common and inexpensive) and Ceylon or &quot;true&quot; cinnamon (<em>Cinnamomum verum</em>). Cassia is richer in the bioactive polyphenols studied in trials but also contains higher levels of coumarin, a compound that can be hepatotoxic at high cumulative intakes.</p>
<p>The clinical evidence is moderate but heterogeneous. Meta-analyses consistently report a modest lowering of fasting blood glucose, while effects on HbA1c, insulin, and lipids are less consistent across studies, owing to differences in cinnamon type, dose, baseline glycemia, and trial duration. Cinnamon is best regarded as an adjunct to diet, exercise, and standard therapy rather than a replacement.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level: 3/5</strong> — Multiple meta-analyses show a reproducible fasting-glucose effect, but heterogeneity and inconsistent HbA1c results temper the strength of evidence.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>Cinnamon improves glucose handling through several complementary actions:</p>
<p><strong>1. Insulin sensitization:</strong> Cinnamon polyphenols (notably type-A doubly-linked procyanidins) and the aldehyde cinnamaldehyde enhance insulin receptor autophosphorylation and downstream signaling, promoting GLUT4-mediated glucose uptake in skeletal muscle and adipose tissue.</p>
<p><strong>2. Carbohydrate digestion and absorption:</strong> Cinnamon extracts inhibit the brush-border enzymes alpha-glucosidase and alpha-amylase, slowing the breakdown of dietary starch and reducing postprandial glucose spikes. It also delays gastric emptying.</p>
<p><strong>3. Antioxidant activity:</strong> The high polyphenol content reduces oxidative stress, which is implicated in insulin resistance and beta-cell dysfunction.</p>
<h2>Dosage</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Powdered cinnamon:</strong> 1-6 g/day, commonly divided with meals</li>
<li><strong>Standardized aqueous extract:</strong> ~500 mg once or twice daily (concentrates water-soluble polyphenols while minimizing coumarin)</li>
<li><strong>Timing:</strong> With carbohydrate-containing meals to blunt postprandial glucose</li>
<li><strong>Type selection:</strong> Prefer Ceylon (<em>Cinnamomum verum</em>) for higher or long-term dosing to limit coumarin exposure; Cassia is acceptable at culinary amounts</li>
<li><strong>Onset:</strong> Glycemic effects typically appear over 4-12 weeks of consistent use</li>
</ul>
