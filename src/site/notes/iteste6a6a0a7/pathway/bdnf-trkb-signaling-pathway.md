<h1>BDNF-TrkB Signaling Pathway</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>The BDNF-TrkB pathway is the dominant neurotrophin signaling axis in the adult brain. <a href="/garden/iteste6a6a0a7/biomarker/bdnf/" class="internal-link">BDNF</a> (brain-derived neurotrophic factor) is released in an activity-dependent manner and binds its high-affinity receptor tyrosine kinase TrkB (gene NTRK2), triggering receptor dimerization and autophosphorylation. This recruits three downstream cascades — MAPK/ERK (differentiation and growth), PI3K-Akt (survival), and PLC-gamma (synaptic modulation) — converging on CREB-driven transcription.</p>
<p>The pathway is the molecular link between experience, exercise, and brain health: it promotes neuronal survival, <a href="/garden/iteste6a6a0a7/pathway/neurogenesis/" class="internal-link">Neurogenesis</a> in the hippocampus, dendritic and spine growth, and <a href="/garden/iteste6a6a0a7/pathway/synaptic-plasticity/" class="internal-link">Synaptic Plasticity</a>. It is suppressed by chronic stress and is a leading mechanistic candidate for the &quot;neurotrophic hypothesis&quot; of depression and the cognitive benefits of physical activity.</p>
<h2>Key Steps</h2>
<ol>
<li>Activity-dependent BDNF release at the <a href="/garden/iteste6a6a0a7/cellularcomponent/synapse/" class="internal-link">Synapse</a>.</li>
<li>BDNF binds TrkB → receptor dimerization and autophosphorylation.</li>
<li>Activation of MAPK/ERK, PI3K-Akt, and PLC-gamma cascades.</li>
<li>CREB-mediated transcription of plasticity and survival genes; structural remodeling.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Regulation</h2>
<p>Neuronal activity (especially via <a href="/garden/iteste6a6a0a7/neurotransmitter/glutamate/" class="internal-link">Glutamate</a>/NMDA signaling) and exercise upregulate BDNF; the exercise effect is mediated in part by a PGC-1alpha/FNDC5 (irisin) axis driving hippocampal BDNF. Chronic stress and glucocorticoid excess suppress BDNF, while many antidepressants and several supplements increase BDNF/TrkB signaling.</p>
<h2>Physiological Role</h2>
<p>Supports learning and memory, mood regulation, and neuronal resilience. Reduced BDNF-TrkB signaling is implicated in <a href="/garden/iteste6a6a0a7/condition/depression/" class="internal-link">Depression</a>, <a href="/garden/iteste6a6a0a7/condition/bipolar-disorder/" class="internal-link">Bipolar Disorder</a>, and <a href="/garden/iteste6a6a0a7/condition/alzheimers-disease/" class="internal-link">Alzheimer's Disease</a>; restoring it is a shared downstream effect of antidepressants, exercise, and pro-cognitive interventions.</p>
