<h1>LL-37</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>LL-37 (UniProt: P49913, encoded by the <strong>CAMP</strong> gene) is the only <strong>cathelicidin</strong> antimicrobial peptide in humans and the archetypal human <strong>antimicrobial peptide (AMP)</strong>. It is stored as an inactive precursor, hCAP18, and cleaved to release the active ~37-amino-acid peptide — its name comes from the two leucine residues that begin the sequence. AMPs like LL-37 are the concrete molecules behind the &quot;antimicrobial peptides used to heal wounds and build a stronger skin barrier&quot; referenced in consumer skincare; this note represents that class with a real, well-characterised example rather than a vague category.</p>
<h2>Function and Mechanism</h2>
<p>LL-37 is a cationic, amphipathic peptide with three overlapping roles:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Direct antimicrobial action</strong> — its positive charge draws it to the negatively charged membranes of bacteria, fungi and some viruses, which it disrupts, giving broad-spectrum activity that is part of innate immunity's first line of defence.</li>
<li><strong>Immunomodulation</strong> — beyond killing microbes, LL-37 recruits immune cells (chemotaxis), modulates cytokine release, and helps resolve inflammation, which is why AMPs are also called <em>host-defence peptides</em>.</li>
<li><strong>Tissue repair and skin barrier</strong> — LL-37 promotes angiogenesis and re-epithelialisation during wound healing and reinforces the epidermal barrier. Its expression is strongly upregulated by <strong>vitamin D</strong>, linking sunlight/vitamin-D status to cutaneous innate immunity.</li>
</ol>
<p>Dysregulated LL-37 is implicated in inflammatory skin disease (elevated in rosacea and psoriasis), so it is a double-edged molecule rather than a simple &quot;good&quot; peptide.</p>
<h2>Relevance to Peptides</h2>
<p>LL-37 anchors the antimicrobial-peptide branch of the <a href="/garden/e2eusercd5f6e08/story/peptides-explained/" class="internal-link">Peptides Explained</a> overview: unlike the injectable &quot;research peptides&quot; or the topical cosmetic signal peptides, AMPs are endogenous defence molecules the body already makes, and the interest in skincare is in supporting or mimicking their barrier and wound-healing functions.</p>
