<h1>Otitis Media</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Otitis media (OM) is infection or inflammation of the middle ear space, the area behind the tympanic membrane containing the ossicles. It is one of the <strong>most common childhood infections</strong>, with ~80% of children experiencing at least one episode by age 3, and ~40% having &gt;=3 episodes by age 3. Peak incidence occurs between <strong>6 and 24 months</strong> of age.</p>
<p>The principal pathogens are <em>Streptococcus pneumoniae</em> (~25-50%), nontypeable <em>Haemophilus influenzae</em> (~30-40%), and <em>Moraxella catarrhalis</em> (~10-15%). Infection typically follows a <strong>viral upper respiratory infection</strong> that causes Eustachian tube dysfunction, creating negative pressure and fluid accumulation in the middle ear, which then becomes secondarily infected with bacteria.</p>
<p>Management has shifted from universal antibiotic treatment to <strong>watchful waiting</strong> (observation for 48-72 hours with analgesia) in selected patients, especially children &gt;2 years with mild symptoms. This approach reduces unnecessary antibiotic exposure while maintaining outcomes. The introduction of <strong>pneumococcal conjugate vaccines (PCV13)</strong> has reduced OM incidence by ~6-20%.</p>
<h2>Clinical Presentation</h2>
<p><strong>Symptoms (children):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Ear pain</strong> (otalgia; may manifest as ear tugging, irritability)</li>
<li><strong>Fever</strong> (variable; may be absent)</li>
<li><strong>Irritability, fussiness</strong></li>
<li><strong>Sleep disturbance</strong></li>
<li><strong>Fluid drainage</strong> from ear (if TM perforation)</li>
<li><strong>Hearing difficulty</strong> or balance problems</li>
<li>Vomiting, decreased appetite</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Otoscopic findings (diagnostic):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Bulging</strong> tympanic membrane with loss of normal landmarks (most specific finding)</li>
<li><strong>Erythema</strong> of TM (non-specific alone)</li>
<li><strong>Opacification</strong> of TM</li>
<li><strong>Decreased mobility</strong> on pneumatic otoscopy (most sensitive)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Diagnostic Information</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Pneumatic otoscopy</strong> -- the primary diagnostic tool; assesses TM mobility</li>
<li><strong>Tympanometry</strong> -- confirms middle ear effusion; useful in uncertain cases</li>
<li>Bacterial culture of middle ear fluid (via tympanocentesis) -- rarely needed; reserved for treatment failure or immunocompromised</li>
</ul>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Watchful waiting</strong> (observation for 48-72h with analgesia):</p>
<ul>
<li>Appropriate for: children &gt;2 years with mild, unilateral symptoms</li>
<li>Analgesics: acetaminophen, ibuprofen</li>
<li>Follow-up if symptoms persist or worsen</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Antibiotic therapy</strong> (indicated for):</p>
<ul>
<li>Children &lt;2 years with bilateral AOM</li>
<li>Children &lt;2 years with severe symptoms (moderate-severe otalgia, fever &gt;=39C)</li>
<li>TTM perforation with purulent drainage</li>
<li>Symptoms worsening or not improving after 48-72h of observation</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>First-line:</strong> Amoxicillin 80-90 mg/kg/day divided BID x 5-10 days<br />
<strong>Alternative:</strong> Amoxicillin-clavulanate (90 mg/kg/day amoxicillin component) if treatment failure, severe, or concurrent purulent conjunctivitis<br />
<strong>Penicillin-allergic:</strong> Cefdinir, cefuroxime, or azithromycin</p>
<p><strong>Surgical:</strong> Tympanostomy tube placement for:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<blockquote>
<p>=3 episodes in 6 months or &gt;=4 in 12 months (with 1 in past 6 months)</p>
</blockquote>
</li>
<li>Chronic otitis media with effusion (&gt;3 months with hearing loss)</li>
</ul>
