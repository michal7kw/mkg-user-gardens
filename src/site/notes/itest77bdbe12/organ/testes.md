<h1>Testes</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>The testes (singular: testis) are the paired male gonads located within the scrotum. They perform two essential, tightly coordinated functions: <strong>spermatogenesis</strong> (production of sperm within the seminiferous tubules, supported by Sertoli cells) and <strong>steroidogenesis</strong> (synthesis of testosterone and other androgens by the interstitial Leydig cells). Together these make the testes both a reproductive organ and a major endocrine gland.</p>
<p>Testicular function is governed by the <strong>hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis</strong>: hypothalamic GnRH stimulates pituitary release of luteinizing hormone (LH) and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH); LH drives Leydig-cell testosterone production, while FSH supports Sertoli-cell function and spermatogenesis. Circulating testosterone exerts negative feedback on the hypothalamus and pituitary. Most circulating testosterone is bound to sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG) and albumin, with only the free fraction biologically active.</p>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Primary hypogonadism</strong> arises from testicular failure (e.g., Klinefelter syndrome, trauma, orchitis, chemotherapy), producing low testosterone with elevated LH/FSH.</li>
<li><strong>Secondary hypogonadism</strong> reflects hypothalamic-pituitary dysfunction with low testosterone and low/inappropriately normal LH/FSH.</li>
<li>Declining testicular output contributes to age-related testosterone decline, reduced fertility, loss of muscle mass and bone density, and metabolic changes.</li>
<li>Testicular health is assessed via testosterone (total and free), LH, FSH, SHBG, and semen analysis.</li>
</ul>
