<h1>Orexin</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Orexin (also called hypocretin) is an excitatory neuropeptide neurotransmitter discovered independently by two groups in 1998. It is synthesized by a remarkably small cluster of neurons — roughly 70,000 in humans — confined to the lateral <a class="internal-link is-unresolved" href="/404">Hypothalamus</a>, yet these neurons project throughout the brain and exert outsized control over behavioral state. Orexin's defining role is to <strong>stabilize wakefulness</strong>: it provides tonic excitatory drive to the brain's arousal systems, preventing inappropriate transitions into sleep.</p>
<p>There are two peptides (orexin-A/hypocretin-1 and orexin-B/hypocretin-2) acting on two G-protein-coupled receptors (OX1R, OX2R). Beyond arousal, orexin links the sleep-wake system to feeding, reward, and stress, positioning it at the intersection of sleep medicine, metabolism, and addiction.</p>
<h2>Neuropharmacology</h2>
<p>Orexin neurons excite the monoaminergic and cholinergic &quot;wake-promoting&quot; centers — including <a href="/garden/itest77bdbe12/neurotransmitter/histamine/" class="internal-link">Histamine</a> (tuberomammillary nucleus), <a href="/garden/itest77bdbe12/neurotransmitter/norepinephrine/" class="internal-link">Norepinephrine</a> (locus coeruleus), <a href="/garden/itest77bdbe12/neurotransmitter/dopamine/" class="internal-link">Dopamine</a>, and <a href="/garden/itest77bdbe12/neurotransmitter/serotonin/" class="internal-link">Serotonin</a> systems — and are reciprocally inhibited by sleep-active circuits. This places orexin at the top of the sleep-wake &quot;flip-flop&quot; switch (see <a href="/garden/itest77bdbe12/pathway/circadian-rhythm-pathway/" class="internal-link">Circadian Rhythm Pathway</a>). The wake-stabilizing function is mirrored pharmacologically: dual orexin-receptor antagonists (DORAs, e.g., suvorexant) promote sleep in insomnia, while the histaminergic effects of orexin loss explain why H3 modulators help narcolepsy.</p>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Narcolepsy type 1:</strong> selective autoimmune loss of orexin neurons abolishes the wake-stabilizing signal (see <a href="/garden/itest77bdbe12/condition/narcolepsy/" class="internal-link">Narcolepsy</a>); CSF orexin/hypocretin-1 is diagnostically low.</li>
<li><strong>Insomnia:</strong> orexin-receptor antagonists are an approved drug class (see <a href="/garden/itest77bdbe12/condition/insomnia/" class="internal-link">Insomnia</a>, <a href="/garden/itest77bdbe12/condition/sleep-disorder/" class="internal-link">Sleep Disorder</a>).</li>
<li><strong>Metabolism/reward:</strong> orexin signaling modulates feeding, energy expenditure, and drug/food reward.</li>
</ul>
