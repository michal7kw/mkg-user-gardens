<h1>Iodine</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Iodine is an essential trace element required for the synthesis of thyroid hormones thyroxine (T4) and triiodothyronine (T3). The thyroid gland concentrates iodine via the sodium-iodide symporter and incorporates it into thyroglobulin through thyroid peroxidase-mediated organification. Thyroid hormones regulate basal metabolic rate, thermogenesis, growth, and neurodevelopment in every tissue of the body.</p>
<p>Iodine deficiency remains the leading preventable cause of intellectual disability worldwide, primarily affecting populations without access to iodized salt. Even mild-to-moderate deficiency during pregnancy can impair fetal brain development, resulting in reduced IQ and learning difficulties. The WHO estimates that nearly 2 billion individuals have insufficient iodine intake globally.</p>
<p>Iodine requirements increase during pregnancy and lactation. Goitrogenic substances found in cruciferous vegetables (broccoli, cabbage), soy products, and cassava can impair iodine utilization by inhibiting thyroid peroxidase or the sodium-iodide symporter, though this is primarily significant in the context of already marginal iodine status. The RDA is 150 mcg/day for adults, 220 mcg during pregnancy, and 290 mcg during lactation.</p>
<h2>Key Functions</h2>
<ul>
<li>Serves as a structural component of thyroid hormones T3 and T4</li>
<li>Regulates basal metabolic rate and energy expenditure</li>
<li>Essential for normal brain development during fetal and early postnatal life</li>
<li>Supports thermoregulation and body temperature maintenance</li>
<li>Participates in immune defense through thyroid-mediated pathways</li>
<li>Influences reproductive health and fertility</li>
</ul>
<h2>Dietary Sources</h2>
<ul>
<li>Iodized salt</li>
<li>Seaweed (kelp, nori, wakame) -- highly variable content</li>
<li>Cod and other ocean fish</li>
<li>Dairy products (milk, yogurt, cheese)</li>
<li>Shrimp and shellfish</li>
<li>Eggs</li>
<li>Cranberries</li>
<li>Navy beans</li>
</ul>
<h2>Deficiency and Excess</h2>
<p>Iodine deficiency causes goiter (thyroid enlargement), hypothyroidism with fatigue, weight gain, cold intolerance, and cognitive impairment. Severe deficiency during pregnancy leads to cretinism (severe intellectual disability, growth retardation, deaf-mutism). Endemic goiter and iodine deficiency disorders remain public health concerns in many developing regions.</p>
<p>Excess iodine can paradoxically cause both hyperthyroidism (Jod-Basedow phenomenon, especially in those with pre-existing nodular thyroid disease) and hypothyroidism (Wolff-Chaikoff effect). Chronic excess may trigger thyroiditis and autoimmune thyroid disease (Hashimoto's). The tolerable upper limit is 1,100 mcg/day for adults. Seaweed supplements, particularly kelp, can provide dangerously high doses.</p>
