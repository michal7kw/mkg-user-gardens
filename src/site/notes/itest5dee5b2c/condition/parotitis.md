<h1>Parotitis</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Parotitis is inflammation of the parotid gland, the largest of the major salivary glands, located anterior and inferior to the ear. The condition presents with painful swelling over the angle of the jaw, often accompanied by fever, localized tenderness, and sometimes purulent discharge from Stensen's duct (the parotid duct opening into the oral cavity opposite the second upper molar). Parotitis can be unilateral or bilateral, acute or chronic.</p>
<p>The principal causes are: (1) <strong>viral infection</strong> -- mumps (parotitis epidemica) is the classic viral cause, now rare in vaccinated populations; other viruses include Coxsackie, EBV, CMV, influenza, and parainfluenza; (2) <strong>bacterial infection (suppurative parotitis)</strong> -- typically caused by <em>Staphylococcus aureus</em> or oral streptococci, predisposed by dehydration, poor oral hygiene, immunocompromise, or duct obstruction; (3) <strong>non-infectious causes</strong> -- Sjogren's syndrome, sarcoidosis, sialolithiasis (salivary stones), tumors, and radiation-induced.</p>
<p>Suppurative (bacterial) parotitis is a medical urgency -- it predominantly affects <strong>elderly, dehydrated, or postoperative patients</strong> and can progress to parotid abscess, fasciitis, or sepsis if untreated. Viral parotitis is typically self-limiting and managed supportively.</p>
<h2>Clinical Presentation</h2>
<p><strong>Symptoms:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Painful swelling of the parotid gland (unilateral or bilateral)</li>
<li>Fever (higher in bacterial)</li>
<li>Pain worsened by eating (salivary stimulation)</li>
<li>Purulent discharge from Stensen's duct (bacterial)</li>
<li>Dry mouth, difficulty chewing</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Physical findings:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Tender, enlarged parotid gland</li>
<li>Erythema and warmth over the gland</li>
<li>Purulent saliva expressed from Stensen's duct (bacterial)</li>
<li>Facial nerve function usually preserved</li>
</ul>
<h2>Diagnostic Information</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Clinical diagnosis</strong> in most cases</li>
<li><strong>Gram stain and culture</strong> of Stensen's duct discharge (if bacterial suspected)</li>
<li><strong>Ultrasound</strong> to evaluate for abscess or sialolithiasis</li>
<li><strong>CT or MRI</strong> for deep extension or complicated cases</li>
<li><strong>Serology</strong> for mumps (IgM) if suspected</li>
<li><strong>Amylase</strong> may be elevated</li>
</ul>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Viral parotitis:</strong> supportive (hydration, analgesics, warm compresses)<br />
<strong>Bacterial (suppurative) parotitis:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>IV antibiotics (anti-staphylococcal: dicloxacillin, cefazolin, or clindamycin for MRSA)</li>
<li>Hydration and sialagogues (lemon drops to stimulate flow)</li>
<li>Surgical drainage if abscess forms<br />
<strong>Sialolithiasis:</strong> stone removal (manual expression, sialendoscopy, or surgical)<br />
<strong>Recurrent/chronic:</strong> treat underlying cause (Sjogren's, duct stricture)</li>
</ul>
