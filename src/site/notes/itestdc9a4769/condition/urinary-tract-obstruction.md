<h1>Urinary Tract Obstruction</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Urinary tract obstruction is a mechanical or functional blockage anywhere along the urinary outflow tract -- from the renal pelvis, through the ureter, bladder, and urethra -- that impairs urine passage and raises pressure proximal to the blockage. It is one of the <strong>few reversible causes of kidney failure</strong>, making prompt recognition and relief critical. The <strong>site, completeness, duration, and presence of infection</strong> determine clinical severity: <strong>acute complete obstruction</strong> with infection is a urologic emergency, while <strong>chronic partial obstruction</strong> may be asymptomatic and discovered only when irreversible renal damage has occurred.</p>
<p>Obstruction is classified by <strong>site</strong> (upper tract = renal pelvis/ureter; lower tract = bladder/urethra) and by <strong>mechanism</strong> (intrinsic = within the lumen or wall; extrinsic = compression from outside). Common causes vary by age and sex: <strong>congenital anomalies</strong> (UPJ obstruction, posterior urethral valves) in infants, <strong>stones</strong> and <strong>ureteral strictures</strong> in young adults, <strong>pregnancy</strong> (physiologic right-sided hydronephrosis), <strong>BPH</strong> and <strong>prostate cancer</strong> in older men, and <strong>pelvic tumors</strong> (cervical, ovarian, colorectal) and <strong>pelvic organ prolapse</strong> in older women. <strong>Neurogenic bladder</strong> from diabetes, multiple sclerosis, spinal cord injury, or Parkinson disease causes functional obstruction through detrusor-sphincter dyssynergia.</p>
<p>The pathophysiology is hemodynamic and tubular. Obstruction raises <strong>intratubular pressure</strong>, which is transmitted back to the glomerulus and initially reduces glomerular filtration. Renal blood flow transiently increases (prostaglandin-mediated) but then falls as the kidney vasoconstricts (angiotensin II, thromboxane), promoting ischemic injury and interstitial fibrosis. Chronic obstruction produces <strong>tubular atrophy</strong>, <strong>interstitial fibrosis</strong>, and irreversible loss of nephrons. Clinically, obstruction may present with <strong>pain</strong> (flank, abdominal; colicky from ureteral stones, dull from chronic obstruction), <strong>palpable distended bladder</strong> (lower tract), <strong>oliguria/anuria</strong> (bilateral or single kidney), <strong>urinary tract infection</strong> (stasis favors bacterial growth), or <strong>incidental hydronephrosis</strong> on imaging. Diagnosis relies on <strong>renal ultrasound</strong> (first-line; shows hydronephrosis), <strong>CT urography</strong> (defines cause and site), and <strong>post-void residual</strong> measurement (catheter or bladder scan; &gt;100-200 mL suggests retention).</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Immediate priorities:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Relieve the obstruction urgently</strong> if acute, if infected, or if renal function is threatened</li>
<li><strong>Lower tract obstruction</strong> (bladder outlet) -- <strong>urethral catheterization</strong> (Foley); suprapubic cystostomy if catheterization fails (severe urethral stricture, BPH)</li>
<li><strong>Upper tract obstruction</strong> (ureter) -- <strong>percutaneous nephrostomy</strong> (immediate decompression) or <strong>cystoscopic ureteral stent</strong> (preferred for stones &lt;1.5 cm, tumors; stent is internal and more comfortable)</li>
<li><strong>Treat infection aggressively</strong> -- urine culture, broad-spectrum antibiotics (ciprofloxacin, piperacillin-tazobactam); obstructed infected systems can progress to <strong>pyonephrosis</strong> and <strong>urosepsis</strong> within hours</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Treat the underlying cause:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Nephrolithiasis</strong> -- analgesia (NSAIDs, opioids), hydration; <strong>ESWL</strong> for stones &lt;2 cm in upper tract; <strong>ureteroscopy with laser lithotripsy</strong> for lower ureteral stones; <strong>medical expulsive therapy</strong> (tamsulosin) for stones 5-10 mm</li>
<li><strong>BPH</strong> -- <strong>alpha-blockers</strong> (tamsulosin, alfuzosin) +/- <strong>5-alpha-reductase inhibitors</strong> (finasteride, dutasteride) for large prostates; <strong>transurethral resection (TURP)</strong>, <strong>laser enucleation (HoLEP)</strong>, or <strong>UroLift/Rezum</strong> for refractory retention</li>
<li><strong>Prostate cancer</strong> -- androgen deprivation, radical prostatectomy, or radiation</li>
<li><strong>Urethral stricture</strong> -- urethral dilation, internal urethrotomy, or <strong>urethroplasty</strong> (definitive)</li>
<li><strong>Tumor</strong> -- resection, stenting, or nephrostomy depending on site and prognosis</li>
<li><strong>Neurogenic bladder</strong> -- <strong>clean intermittent catheterization</strong>, anticholinergics (oxybutynin), beta-3 agonists (mirabegron); botulinum toxin injection; sacral neuromodulation; urinary diversion in refractory cases</li>
<li><strong>Retroperitoneal fibrosis</strong> -- withdraw causative drugs (ergotamines, dopamine agonists); corticosteroids; tamoxifen</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Manage post-obstructive diuresis (after relief):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>After chronic obstruction is relieved, patients may develop <strong>post-obstructive diuresis</strong> (&gt;200 mL/hour for 2+ hours) due to nephrogenic DI-like state from tubular dysfunction</li>
<li>Monitor urine output, weight, and electrolytes every 2-4 hours for 24-48 hours</li>
<li>Replace urine output with IV fluids (0.45% saline) to prevent hypovolemia and electrolyte derangement</li>
<li>Usually self-limited over 24-72 hours</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Long-term follow-up:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Monitor renal function (creatinine, eGFR) -- partial recovery is typical, but severe or chronic obstruction may cause permanent CKD</li>
<li>Treat hypertension</li>
<li>Address recurrent UTIs (low-dose prophylactic antibiotics, hygienic measures)</li>
<li>Prevent recurrence (stone-prevention diet, BPH management, intermittent catheterization schedule)</li>
</ul>
