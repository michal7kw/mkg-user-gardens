<h1>Regulatory T Cells</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Regulatory T cells (Tregs) are a functionally distinct subpopulation of CD4+ T lymphocytes whose primary role is to maintain immunological self-tolerance and prevent autoimmunity. They are defined phenotypically by surface expression of CD4 and CD25 (the IL-2 receptor alpha chain) together with the lineage-defining transcription factor FOXP3, which programs and stabilizes their suppressive identity.</p>
<p>Tregs arise both in the thymus (thymically derived/natural Tregs) and in the periphery (induced Tregs) from conventional CD4+ T cells under tolerogenic conditions. By dampening the activation and expansion of effector T cells, B cells, and antigen-presenting cells, they hold autoreactive responses in check and resolve inflammation after pathogen clearance, balancing protective immunity against immunopathology.</p>
<h2>Suppressive Mechanisms</h2>
<p>Tregs deploy several non-redundant mechanisms to suppress immune activity: secretion of inhibitory cytokines (IL-10, TGF-beta, and IL-35); cytolysis of effector cells; metabolic disruption, including consumption of IL-2 via high-affinity CD25 and generation of adenosine through CD39/CD73; and modulation of dendritic-cell maturation through CTLA-4 engagement of CD80/CD86. This layered toolkit allows Tregs to tailor suppression to different inflammatory contexts.</p>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>Loss-of-function mutations in FOXP3 cause IPEX syndrome, a severe multi-organ autoimmune disorder, illustrating that Tregs are indispensable for tolerance. Reduced Treg number or function is implicated in many forms of <strong>Autoimmune Disease</strong> and chronic <strong>Inflammation</strong>, while transplantation tolerance and allergy control depend on adequate Treg activity. Conversely, tumors frequently recruit and expand Tregs to evade immune destruction, making Treg depletion or reprogramming an active strategy in cancer immunotherapy. Adoptive Treg cell therapy is being developed for autoimmune and immune-mediated diseases.</p>
