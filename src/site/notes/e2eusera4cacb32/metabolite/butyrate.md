<h1>Butyrate</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Butyrate (butyric acid, C4) is a four-carbon short-chain fatty acid (SCFA) generated almost exclusively in the colon by anaerobic bacterial fermentation of non-digestible carbohydrates — dietary fiber, resistant starch, and prebiotic oligosaccharides that escape digestion in the small intestine. Along with acetate and propionate, it is one of the three principal SCFAs, but it occupies a special place because of its dominant role as the energy source for the cells lining the large intestine.</p>
<p>Butyrate supplies roughly 60-70% of the energy used by colonocytes, which preferentially oxidize it over glucose. This local &quot;fuel&quot; role has systemic consequences: by sustaining colonocyte oxygen consumption, butyrate maintains the hypoxic, anaerobic environment that favors beneficial obligate-anaerobe communities. Beyond energy provision, butyrate is a potent signaling molecule — an endogenous histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitor and agonist of free fatty acid receptors (FFAR2/FFAR3, GPR109A) — through which it reinforces the epithelial barrier, promotes regulatory T-cell differentiation, and dampens intestinal inflammation.</p>

<h2>Biochemical Role</h2>
<p>In the colonic lumen, fiber-derived sugars are fermented by specialized butyrogenic bacteria primarily via the butyryl-CoA:acetate CoA-transferase route, condensing two acetyl-CoA units to butyryl-CoA and ultimately butyrate. Absorbed butyrate is taken up by colonocytes through monocarboxylate transporters (MCT1, SMCT1) and oxidized via <a href="/garden/e2eusera4cacb32/pathway/beta-oxidation/" class="internal-link">Beta-Oxidation</a> to acetyl-CoA, feeding the TCA cycle for ATP production. This high oxidative demand keeps the epithelial surface hypoxic — a key mechanism stabilizing intestinal barrier integrity.</p>
<p>As a signaling metabolite, butyrate inhibits class I/IIa HDACs, increasing histone acetylation and altering transcription of genes governing cell-cycle arrest, differentiation, and immune tolerance. It is the most potent SCFA inducer of colonic regulatory T cells and a ligand for the niacin receptor GPR109A on immune cells.</p>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Gut barrier and inflammation:</strong> Butyrate strengthens tight junctions and stabilizes hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF), augmenting barrier function; deficits in butyrate-producing taxa are characteristic of <a href="/garden/e2eusera4cacb32/condition/inflammation/" class="internal-link">Inflammation</a> and inflammatory bowel disease.</li>
<li><strong>Colorectal health:</strong> Through HDAC inhibition, butyrate promotes apoptosis of transformed colonocytes, contributing to a protective profile against <a href="/garden/e2eusera4cacb32/condition/colorectal-neoplasms/" class="internal-link">Colorectal Neoplasms</a> (the &quot;butyrate paradox&quot;).</li>
<li><strong>Metabolic health:</strong> SCFA-driven secretion of GLP-1 and PYY supports satiety and improved glycemic control, relevant to <a href="/garden/e2eusera4cacb32/condition/insulin-resistance/" class="internal-link">Insulin Resistance</a>.</li>
</ul>
