<h1>LDL Cholesterol</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) is the cholesterol carried by low-density lipoprotein particles -- the principal atherogenic lipoprotein in human circulation. LDL particles (25 nm diameter) transport cholesterol from the liver to peripheral tissues for membrane synthesis, steroid hormone production, and bile acid formation. When circulating LDL-C concentrations are elevated, LDL particles infiltrate the arterial intima, undergo oxidation and modification, and are taken up by macrophage scavenger receptors to form lipid-laden foam cells -- the cellular hallmark of the fatty streak, the earliest lesion of atherosclerosis.</p>
<p>The causal role of LDL-C in atherosclerosis is one of the most thoroughly established relationships in modern medicine, supported by: (1) <strong>Mendelian randomization studies</strong> showing that lifelong low LDL-C (e.g., PCSK9 loss-of-function variants) reduces CV risk proportionally to the cumulative exposure; (2) <strong>randomized trials of statins, ezetimibe, and PCSK9 inhibitors</strong> consistently showing that LDL-C lowering reduces CV events in proportion to the absolute reduction achieved; (3) <strong>the &quot;lower is better&quot; log-linear relationship</strong> with no lower threshold below which benefit ceases. The 2022 ESC/EAS dyslipidemia guideline and 2018 ACC/AHA cholesterol guideline are built on this foundation.</p>
<p>For LDL-C measurement, the <strong>Friedewald equation</strong> (LDL-C = Total Cholesterol - HDL-C - Triglycerides/5) has been used since 1972 but is inaccurate when triglycerides are &gt;200 mg/dL or LDL-C is &lt;70 mg/dL. The <strong>Martin/Hopkins equation</strong> (2013) and <strong>Sampson equation</strong> (2020) provide more accurate estimates in these ranges. <strong>Direct LDL-C assays</strong> avoid calculation errors but add cost. Modern guidelines recommend using validated equations and confirming with direct LDL-C if triglycerides &gt;400 mg/dL or LDL-C nears target.</p>
<p>LDL-C targets depend on <strong>ASCVD risk stratification</strong>:</p>
<ul>
<li>Very high risk (established ASCVD, DM with target-organ damage, severe CKD, FH with ASCVD, SCORE &gt;10%): <strong>LDL-C &lt;55 mg/dL</strong> (ESC) or &lt;70 mg/dL (ACC/AHA)</li>
<li>High risk (DM without target-organ damage, FH without ASCVD, moderate CKD, SCORE 5-10%): <strong>LDL-C &lt;70 mg/dL</strong></li>
<li>Moderate risk (SCORE 1-5%): <strong>LDL-C &lt;100 mg/dL</strong></li>
<li>Low risk: <strong>LDL-C &lt;116 mg/dL</strong> (ESC) or &lt;130 mg/dL (ACC/AHA)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Evidence Level: 5/5</strong> (foundational biomarker; universally accepted as primary lipid target).</p>

<p><strong>Risk-stratified targets (ESC/EAS 2019):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Very high risk: <strong>&lt;55 mg/dL</strong> (&lt;1.4 mmol/L) and &gt;=50% reduction from baseline</li>
<li>High risk: <strong>&lt;70 mg/dL</strong> (&lt;1.8 mmol/L) and &gt;=50% reduction from baseline</li>
<li>Moderate risk: <strong>&lt;100 mg/dL</strong> (&lt;2.6 mmol/L)</li>
<li>Low risk: <strong>&lt;116 mg/dL</strong> (&lt;3.0 mmol/L)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>ACC/AHA 2018 targets</strong> (less aggressive):</p>
<ul>
<li>High intensity statin: aim for &gt;=50% reduction</li>
<li>Very high ASCVD risk: LDL-C &lt;70 mg/dL</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Caveats:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Friedewald equation underestimates LDL-C when triglycerides are &gt;200 mg/dL or LDL-C is &lt;70 mg/dL</li>
<li>Direct LDL-C assay recommended if triglycerides &gt;400 mg/dL or near-target uncertainty</li>
<li>LDL-C rises with age (especially women post-menopause)</li>
<li>LDL-C lower during acute illness, inflammation, infection (false reassurance)</li>
<li>LDL-C is calculated from fasting panel (total cholesterol, HDL-C, triglycerides)</li>
</ul>

<h2>Factors Affecting Levels</h2>
<p><strong>Increase LDL-C (hypercholesterolemia):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Familial hypercholesterolemia (FH):</strong> LDL-C &gt;190 mg/dL (heterozygous) or &gt;500 mg/dL (homozygous); LDL receptor, ApoB, or PCSK9 mutations</li>
<li><strong>Polygenic hypercholesterolemia:</strong> most common cause of mild-moderate elevation</li>
<li><strong>Hypothyroidism:</strong> reduces hepatic LDL receptor expression</li>
<li><strong>Nephrotic syndrome:</strong> increased hepatic lipoprotein synthesis</li>
<li><strong>Cholestasis:</strong> primary biliary cholangitis, primary sclerosing cholangitis</li>
<li><strong>Chronic kidney disease:</strong> altered lipoprotein metabolism</li>
<li><strong>Pregnancy:</strong> rises progressively; LDL-C 50-100% above baseline by third trimester</li>
<li><strong>Drugs:</strong> thiazides, beta-blockers, anabolic steroids, progestins, glucocorticoids, cyclosporine, isotretinoin, amiodarone</li>
<li><strong>Dietary:</strong> high saturated fat, trans fat, dietary cholesterol</li>
<li><strong>Anorexia nervosa</strong> (paradoxically elevated)</li>
<li><strong>Hypopituitarism, growth hormone deficiency</strong></li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Decrease LDL-C:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Statin therapy</strong> (atorvastatin, rosuvastatin, simvastatin, etc.) -- first-line; LDL-C reduction 30-60%</li>
<li><strong>Ezetimibe</strong> -- additional 15-25% reduction</li>
<li><strong>PCSK9 inhibitors</strong> (alirocumab, evolocumab) -- additional 50-60% reduction</li>
<li><strong>Bempedoic acid</strong> -- 15-20% reduction (with ezetimibe 35-40%)</li>
<li><strong>Inclisiran</strong> (siRNA) -- 50% reduction; q6 months dosing</li>
<li><strong>Anabolic steroid abuse</strong> -- lowers LDL-C (but raises CV risk via HDL lowering)</li>
<li><strong>Hyperthyroidism</strong> (increases LDL receptor expression)</li>
<li><strong>Malnutrition, severe liver disease</strong></li>
<li><strong>Tangier disease, abetalipoproteinemia, hypobetalipoproteinemia</strong></li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p><strong>Elevated LDL-C is associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (causal):</strong> each 38.7 mg/dL (1 mmol/L) reduction confers ~22% reduction in major CV events (CTT meta-analysis)</li>
<li><strong>Coronary artery disease:</strong> LDL-C is the primary risk factor; LDL-C &gt;190 mg/dL suggests familial hypercholesterolemia</li>
<li><strong>Stroke (large-artery atherosclerotic):</strong> LDL-C lowering reduces ischemic stroke</li>
<li><strong>Peripheral arterial disease, aortic aneurysm:</strong> LDL-C lowering reduces progression</li>
<li><strong>Aortic valve stenosis (calcific):</strong> LDL-C contributes (PCSK9 inhibitors under investigation)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Reduced LDL-C is associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Reduced CV events</strong> (causally, with statins/ezetimibe/PCSK9i)</li>
<li><strong>Slightly increased hemorrhagic stroke risk</strong> in patients with LDL-C &lt;50 (controversial; not a reason to avoid aggressive lowering in appropriate patients)</li>
<li><strong>Malnutrition, severe liver disease</strong> (low LDL-C from impaired synthesis)</li>
<li><strong>Tangier disease, abetalipoproteinemia</strong> (genetic)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Limitations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>LDL-C concentration does not capture LDL particle function</strong> -- some patients have low LDL-C but dysfunctional LDL (small dense LDL, oxidized LDL)</li>
<li><strong>LDL particle number (LDL-P) may better predict CV risk</strong> in metabolic syndrome ( discordance between LDL-C and LDL-P)</li>
<li><strong>Apolipoprotein B</strong> may be superior to LDL-C as a single marker of all atherogenic lipoproteins</li>
<li><strong>Fasting requirement</strong> for accurate Friedewald calculation</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Monitoring use cases:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>ASCVD risk stratification (pooled cohort equation)</li>
<li>Diagnosis of familial hypercholesterolemia (LDL-C &gt;190 mg/dL or &gt;160 with family history)</li>
<li>Guide statin, ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitor therapy</li>
<li>Monitor therapy response (target &gt;=30-50% reduction)</li>
<li>Pre-pregnancy counseling (especially FH)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Strategies to Improve</h2>
<p>LDL-C is highly modifiable through lifestyle, diet, and pharmacologic interventions:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Statin therapy (first-line)</strong> for elevated LDL-C or ASCVD risk:
<ul>
<li><strong>High-intensity:</strong> atorvastatin 40-80 mg, rosuvastatin 20-40 mg (~50% LDL-C reduction)</li>
<li><strong>Moderate-intensity:</strong> atorvastatin 10-20 mg, rosuvastatin 5-10 mg, simvastatin 20-40 mg (~30-50% reduction)</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Ezetimibe 10 mg daily</strong> -- second-line; additional 15-25% reduction</li>
<li><strong>PCSK9 inhibitor (subcutaneous)</strong> -- alirocumab 75-150 mg q2 weeks or evolocumab 140 mg q2 weeks (or 420 mg monthly); 50-60% additional reduction; for FH, statin-intolerant, or persistent elevation despite statin + ezetimibe</li>
<li><strong>Bempedoic acid</strong> 180 mg daily -- 15-20% reduction; for statin-intolerant patients</li>
<li><strong>Inclisiran</strong> 284 mg SC on day 1, day 90, then q6 months -- siRNA PCSK9 inhibitor; for poor adherence to PCSK9 mAb</li>
<li><strong>Dietary changes:</strong>
<ul>
<li>Reduce saturated fat (&lt;7% calories); replace with monounsaturated (olive oil) and polyunsaturated fats</li>
<li>Eliminate trans fats (banned in US since 2018 but check labels)</li>
<li>Add soluble fiber (oats, psyllium, beans, legumes) -- 10-25 g/day reduces LDL-C 5-15%</li>
<li>Plant sterols/stanols 2 g/day -- reduce LDL-C 8-15%</li>
<li>Nuts (almonds, walnuts) -- modest LDL-C reduction</li>
<li>Soy protein 25 g/day -- modest LDL-C reduction</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Berberine</strong> 500 mg TID -- meta-analyses show ~15-25% LDL-C reduction; comparable to low-dose statin</li>
<li><strong>Omega-3 fatty acids</strong> -- minimal effect on LDL-C (may slightly raise); reduces triglycerides</li>
<li><strong>Red yeast rice</strong> (contains naturally-occurring lovastatin) -- effective but unpredictable dosing; not recommended due to contamination concerns</li>
<li><strong>Weight loss</strong> (5-10% body weight if obese) -- modest LDL-C reduction</li>
<li><strong>Aerobic exercise</strong> 150 min/week -- modest LDL-C reduction (~5-10%); also raises HDL</li>
<li><strong>Smoking cessation</strong> -- does not lower LDL-C but reduces overall CV risk</li>
<li><strong>Treat underlying causes:</strong> hypothyroidism (levothyroxine), nephrotic syndrome (renoprotection), discontinue offending drugs if possible</li>
<li><strong>LDL apheresis</strong> -- for homozygous FH or refractory heterozygous FH with progressive ASCVD</li>
</ol>
