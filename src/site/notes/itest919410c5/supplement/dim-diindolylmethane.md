<h1>DIM (Diindolylmethane)</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>3,3'-Diindolylmethane (DIM) is the principal acid-condensation product of indole-3-carbinol, a compound released when the glucosinolates in cruciferous vegetables (broccoli, cabbage, Brussels sprouts) are chewed or digested. Because reaching meaningful DIM exposure through diet alone is difficult, it is commonly supplemented in enhanced-bioavailability forms to support estrogen metabolism.</p>
<p>The central rationale for DIM is that it nudges estrogen catabolism toward the 2-hydroxyestrone pathway and away from the 16-alpha-hydroxyestrone pathway, increasing the 2:16 ratio that some researchers associate with a more favorable hormonal milieu. It is marketed for estrogen-dominant symptoms, hormonal acne, and prostate health, and explored in oncology prevention. The clinical evidence is mechanistically suggestive (controlled studies do shift urinary estrogen metabolites) but outcome data for disease endpoints remain limited and preliminary.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level:</strong> 2/5</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>DIM acts mainly as a modulator of estrogen metabolism and hormone-responsive signaling:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>CYP-mediated estrogen hydroxylation:</strong> It induces CYP1A1/CYP1A2 activity, promoting 2-hydroxylation of estradiol and estrone and raising the 2-hydroxyestrone:16-alpha-hydroxyestrone ratio. Controlled human studies confirm measurable shifts in the urinary estrogen-metabolite profile.</li>
<li><strong>Aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR) ligand:</strong> DIM binds the AhR, influencing xenobiotic-metabolizing enzyme expression that feeds back into steroid hormone clearance.</li>
<li><strong>Aromatase and anti-proliferative effects:</strong> It weakly modulates aromatase and, in preclinical hormone-responsive (breast and prostate) models, induces cell-cycle arrest and apoptosis, providing the rationale for its study in estrogen- and androgen-related conditions.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Dosage</h2>
<p>Supplemental DIM is usually dosed at 100-200 mg/day of a bioavailability-enhanced (microencapsulated or lipid-formulated) product taken with food, since unformulated DIM is poorly absorbed. Shifts in estrogen-metabolite ratios are typically observed over several weeks of continued use.</p>
