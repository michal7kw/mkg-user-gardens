<h1>Glucagon</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Glucagon is a 29-amino-acid peptide hormone secreted by <strong>alpha cells</strong> of the pancreatic islets in response to <strong>hypoglycemia</strong>, amino acids, and sympathetic activation. It raises blood glucose by stimulating hepatic <strong>glycogenolysis</strong> and <strong>gluconeogenesis</strong>, and promotes <strong>lipolysis</strong> and <strong>ketogenesis</strong>. Glucagon and insulin function as a push-pull system maintaining glucose homeostasis.</p>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Severe hypoglycemia</strong> -- IM/SC/intranasal glucagon is the standard rescue therapy</li>
<li><strong>Glucagonoma</strong> -- rare tumor causing hyperglucagonemia, diabetes, necrolytic migratory erythema</li>
<li><strong>Diabetes</strong> -- excess glucagon contributes to hyperglycemia</li>
<li><strong>GLP-1/glucagon dual agonists</strong> -- in development for obesity</li>
</ul>
