<h1>Eye Infections</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Eye infections encompass a broad spectrum of conditions affecting the eye and its adnexal structures (eyelids, conjunctiva, cornea, orbit, and intraocular contents). The most common are <strong>conjunctivitis</strong> (inflammation of the conjunctiva), <strong>blepharitis</strong> (eyelid margin inflammation), and <strong>keratitis</strong> (corneal infection). Less common but more serious conditions include <strong>endophthalmitis</strong> (intraocular infection following surgery or trauma) and <strong>orbital cellulitis</strong> (infection behind the orbital septum; potentially life-threatening).</p>
<p><strong>Common pathogens by infection type:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Bacterial conjunctivitis:</strong> <em>Staphylococcus aureus</em>, <em>Streptococcus pneumoniae</em>, <em>Haemophilus influenzae</em></li>
<li><strong>Viral conjunctivitis:</strong> Adenovirus (most common; highly contagious), enterovirus, HSV</li>
<li><strong>Keratitis:</strong> <em>Pseudomonas aeruginosa</em> (contact lens users), <em>Staphylococcus aureus</em>, HSV, <em>Acanthamoeba</em> (contact lens)</li>
<li><strong>Endophthalmitis:</strong> Coagulase-negative staphylococci (post-surgical), <em>S. aureus</em>, <em>Streptococcus</em> species, Bacillus (post-traumatic)</li>
<li><strong>Orbital cellulitis:</strong> <em>S. aureus</em>, <em>Streptococcus pyogenes</em>, <em>Haemophilus influenzae</em> (children)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Contact lens use</strong> is the most important modifiable risk factor for serious eye infections (bacterial keratitis, Acanthamoeba keratitis). Proper lens hygiene (daily cleaning, avoiding overnight wear and swimming in lenses) dramatically reduces infection risk.</p>
<h2>Clinical Presentation</h2>
<p><strong>Conjunctivitis:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Redness (conjunctival injection)</li>
<li>Discharge: watery (viral, allergic), mucopurulent (bacterial)</li>
<li>Itching (allergic), burning</li>
<li>Crusting of eyelids on waking</li>
<li>Usually bilateral (viral, allergic); may start unilateral (bacterial)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Keratitis (corneal infection):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Severe eye pain (distinct from conjunctivitis which is usually mild discomfort)</li>
<li>Photophobia</li>
<li>Tearing</li>
<li>Decreased vision</li>
<li>Corneal opacity or infiltrate (seen on slit lamp)</li>
<li>Hypopyon (pus in anterior chamber; severe)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Endophthalmitis:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Severe pain</li>
<li>Marked vision loss</li>
<li>Hypopyon</li>
<li>Conjunctival injection and chemosis</li>
<li>Eyelid swelling</li>
<li>Usually post-surgical or post-trauma</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Orbital cellulitis:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Proptosis (eye pushed forward)</li>
<li>Limited eye movement (ophthalmoplegia)</li>
<li>Pain with eye movement</li>
<li>Vision loss</li>
<li>Systemic signs (fever, malaise)</li>
<li>Often follows sinusitis</li>
</ul>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Bacterial conjunctivitis:</strong> topical antibiotic drops (ofloxacin, ciprofloxacin, polymyxin B/trimethoprim) x 5-7 days; often self-limiting (7-10 days without treatment)<br />
<strong>Viral conjunctivitis:</strong> supportive (cold compresses, artificial tears); highly contagious -- practice hand hygiene; avoid touching eyes; stay home from work/school<br />
<strong>Allergic conjunctivitis:</strong> antihistamine/mast cell stabilizer drops (olopatadine, ketotifen); cool compresses<br />
<strong>HSV keratitis:</strong> topical antiviral (trifluridine) or oral antiviral (acyclovir, valacyclovir); refer to ophthalmology; <strong>steroids contraindicated</strong> without antiviral coverage<br />
<strong>Bacterial keratitis:</strong> empirical topical antibiotics (fluoroquinolone; fortified vancomycin + tobramycin for severe); discontinue contact lenses; ophthalmology referral<br />
<strong>Acanthamoeba keratitis:</strong> topical chlorhexidine or polyhexamethylene biguanide (PHMB); prolonged course (months); often requires corneal transplant<br />
<strong>Endophthalmitis:</strong> emergency vitrectomy; intravitreal antibiotics (vancomycin + ceftazidime); systemic antibiotics<br />
<strong>Orbital cellulitis:</strong> IV antibiotics (ceftriaxone + vancomycin); CT scan for abscess; surgical drainage if needed</p>
