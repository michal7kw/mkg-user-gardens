<h1>Quadruple Therapy</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Quadruple therapy is a <em>Helicobacter pylori</em> eradication regimen that adds bismuth to a proton pump inhibitor (PPI) and two antibiotics—classically tetracycline and metronidazole (&quot;bismuth quadruple therapy&quot;). It was developed to overcome the falling success rates of clarithromycin triple therapy in the face of rising antibiotic resistance. Because bismuth has independent antibacterial activity and helps overcome metronidazole resistance, the regimen retains high efficacy even in regions where clarithromycin and metronidazole resistance are common.</p>
<p>Current consensus guidelines (Maastricht VI/Florence; ACG) recommend bismuth quadruple therapy as a preferred first-line option in areas of high or unknown clarithromycin resistance, and as the standard salvage regimen after a failed clarithromycin-based course. A &quot;concomitant&quot; (non-bismuth) quadruple regimen—PPI plus amoxicillin, clarithromycin, and a nitroimidazole given together—is an alternative where bismuth is unavailable. Standard duration is 10-14 days, and eradication should be confirmed by urea breath test or stool antigen test.</p>
<p>Evidence Level: 4/5. Supported by numerous randomized trials and meta-analyses demonstrating superior eradication versus triple therapy in resistant settings.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>Quadruple therapy combines four complementary actions against <em>H. pylori</em>. The PPI suppresses acid secretion, raising intragastric pH to enhance antibiotic stability and bacterial replication. Bismuth salts disrupt the bacterial cell wall and membrane, inhibit key enzymes, bind to the mucosa to form a protective barrier, and—importantly—partially restore the activity of metronidazole against resistant strains. Tetracycline inhibits bacterial protein synthesis at the 30S ribosomal subunit, and metronidazole generates DNA-damaging radicals under the anaerobic conditions of the gastric niche. The four-agent combination provides redundancy that maintains high eradication rates despite single-drug resistance.</p>
<h2>Protocol Details</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Standard bismuth quadruple regimen</strong>: PPI twice daily + bismuth four times daily + tetracycline 500 mg four times daily + metronidazole 250-500 mg three to four times daily</li>
<li><strong>Duration</strong>: 10-14 days</li>
<li><strong>Use</strong>: first-line in high clarithromycin-resistance regions; salvage after triple-therapy failure</li>
<li><strong>Confirmation</strong>: urea breath test or stool antigen test ≥4 weeks after therapy and ≥2 weeks off PPI</li>
</ul>
<h2>Safety Information</h2>
<p>Bismuth commonly causes harmless darkening of the stool and tongue. Other frequent effects include nausea, diarrhea, and abdominal discomfort; tetracycline can cause photosensitivity and is contraindicated in pregnancy and young children. Tetracycline absorption is impaired by calcium, iron, antacids, and dairy products, which should be separated from dosing. Metronidazole produces a disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol. The four-times-daily dosing schedule can challenge adherence, which is critical to eradication success.</p>
