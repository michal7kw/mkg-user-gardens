<h1>Dietary Fat</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Dietary fat is an essential macronutrient that provides the most energy-dense fuel at 9 kilocalories per gram. Beyond energy, fat is indispensable for the absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K), synthesis of steroid hormones (including testosterone, estrogen, and cortisol), and maintenance of cell membrane integrity through phospholipid bilayers.</p>
<p>Fats are categorized into saturated, monounsaturated (MUFA), and polyunsaturated (PUFA) fatty acids, each with distinct metabolic effects. Monounsaturated fats (olive oil, avocado) and omega-3 polyunsaturated fats are associated with cardiovascular protection, while trans fats and excessive saturated fat intake elevate cardiovascular risk. A minimum of 20-35% of total calories from fat is recommended for optimal hormone health and nutrient absorption.</p>
<p>Fat quality matters more than absolute quantity. The Mediterranean dietary pattern, rich in olive oil, nuts, and fatty fish, demonstrates robust cardiovascular and metabolic benefits. Fat intake triggers cholecystokinin (CCK) release from the duodenum, promoting satiety and slowing gastric emptying, which helps with appetite regulation and meal satisfaction.</p>
<h2>Key Functions</h2>
<ul>
<li>Provides concentrated, sustained energy for low-to-moderate intensity activity</li>
<li>Enables absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K)</li>
<li>Serves as precursor for steroid hormones and eicosanoids</li>
<li>Maintains cell membrane structure and fluidity via phospholipids</li>
<li>Insulates organs and maintains body temperature</li>
<li>Supports brain structure (60% of brain dry weight is fat)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Dietary Sources</h2>
<ul>
<li>Olive oil and avocado oil (monounsaturated)</li>
<li>Avocados</li>
<li>Nuts (almonds, walnuts, macadamias)</li>
<li>Seeds (flax, chia, sunflower)</li>
<li>Fatty fish (salmon, mackerel, sardines)</li>
<li>Butter and ghee</li>
<li>Cheese and full-fat dairy</li>
<li>Coconut oil and coconut cream</li>
</ul>
<h2>Deficiency and Excess</h2>
<p>Fat deficiency leads to essential fatty acid deficiency (dry, scaly skin, dermatitis), impaired absorption of fat-soluble vitamins, hormonal disruption (amenorrhea, low testosterone), poor wound healing, and persistent hunger. Very low-fat diets below 15% of calories can compromise neurological and reproductive health.</p>
<p>Excessive fat intake, particularly from saturated and trans fats, contributes to weight gain, elevated LDL cholesterol, insulin resistance, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, and increased cardiovascular risk. However, the metabolic impact depends heavily on fat type rather than total quantity.</p>
