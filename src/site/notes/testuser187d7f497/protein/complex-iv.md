<h1>Complex IV</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Cytochrome c oxidase (COX), the terminal enzyme of the <a href="/garden/testuser187d7f497/pathway/electron-transport-chain/" class="internal-link">Electron Transport Chain</a>. It catalyzes the four-electron reduction of molecular <a href="/garden/testuser187d7f497/metabolite/oxygen/" class="internal-link">Oxygen</a> to <a href="/garden/testuser187d7f497/metabolite/water/" class="internal-link">Water</a> — the final electron-accepting step of aerobic respiration — while pumping protons across the inner mitochondrial membrane. Located in the inner membrane of the <a href="/garden/testuser187d7f497/cellularcomponent/mitochondria/" class="internal-link">Mitochondria</a>, Complex IV receives electrons from <a href="/garden/testuser187d7f497/protein/cytochrome-c/" class="internal-link">Cytochrome C</a> and is the point at which the entire respiratory chain commits its electrons to oxygen, the property that makes oxygen indispensable to <a href="/garden/testuser187d7f497/pathway/oxidative-phosphorylation/" class="internal-link">Oxidative Phosphorylation</a>.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>Complex IV contains two copper centers (CuA, CuB) and two heme groups (heme a, heme a3) that relay electrons to bound oxygen:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Electron entry.</strong> Reduced <a href="/garden/testuser187d7f497/protein/cytochrome-c/" class="internal-link">Cytochrome C</a> docks on the intermembrane-space side and donates one electron at a time to the binuclear CuA center.</li>
<li><strong>Internal relay.</strong> Electrons pass from CuA to heme a, then to the catalytic binuclear center formed by heme a3 and CuB.</li>
<li><strong>Oxygen reduction.</strong> Molecular <a href="/garden/testuser187d7f497/metabolite/oxygen/" class="internal-link">Oxygen</a> binds at the heme a3–CuB center and is reduced in a controlled four-electron, four-proton reaction, avoiding the release of partially reduced (toxic) intermediates.</li>
<li><strong>Water formation.</strong> Four electrons, four &quot;chemical&quot; protons, and one O2 combine to form two molecules of <a href="/garden/testuser187d7f497/metabolite/water/" class="internal-link">Water</a>.</li>
</ol>

<p>In addition to the protons consumed chemically, Complex IV pumps approximately 4 protons per O2 across the membrane, adding to the proton-motive force harnessed by <a href="/garden/testuser187d7f497/protein/atp-synthase/" class="internal-link">ATP Synthase</a>.</p>
<h2>Role in the Electron Transport Chain</h2>
<p>Complex IV is the chain's terminus and the only complex that consumes <a href="/garden/testuser187d7f497/metabolite/oxygen/" class="internal-link">Oxygen</a>. It accepts electrons delivered by <a href="/garden/testuser187d7f497/protein/cytochrome-c/" class="internal-link">Cytochrome C</a> from <a href="/garden/testuser187d7f497/protein/complex-iii/" class="internal-link">Complex III</a> and disposes of them safely by reducing oxygen to <a href="/garden/testuser187d7f497/metabolite/water/" class="internal-link">Water</a>. By clearing the downstream end of the chain, it keeps <a href="/garden/testuser187d7f497/protein/cytochrome-c/" class="internal-link">Cytochrome C</a>, <a href="/garden/testuser187d7f497/supplement/coenzyme-q10/" class="internal-link">Coenzyme Q10</a>, and the upstream complexes (<a href="/garden/testuser187d7f497/protein/complex-i/" class="internal-link">Complex I</a>, <a href="/garden/testuser187d7f497/protein/complex-ii/" class="internal-link">Complex II</a>, <a href="/garden/testuser187d7f497/protein/complex-iii/" class="internal-link">Complex III</a>) in an oxidized, electron-accepting state, sustaining flux through <a href="/garden/testuser187d7f497/pathway/oxidative-phosphorylation/" class="internal-link">Oxidative Phosphorylation</a>.</p>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Mitochondrial disease.</strong> COX deficiency (e.g., SURF1 mutations) is a frequent cause of Leigh syndrome, presenting with lactic acidosis, hypotonia, and neurodegeneration.</li>
<li><strong>Cyanide and CO toxicity.</strong> Cyanide, carbon monoxide, and azide inhibit Complex IV by binding the heme a3–CuB center, blocking <a href="/garden/testuser187d7f497/metabolite/oxygen/" class="internal-link">Oxygen</a> reduction and causing fatal histotoxic hypoxia.</li>
<li><strong>Copper dependence.</strong> Because CuA and CuB are essential, copper deficiency or transport defects (e.g., Menkes disease) impair Complex IV activity.</li>
<li><strong>Aging marker.</strong> Declining COX activity in tissues is a hallmark of mitochondrial aging and is detectable histochemically as COX-negative fibers.</li>
</ul>
