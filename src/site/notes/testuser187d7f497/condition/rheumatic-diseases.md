<h1>Rheumatic Diseases</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Rheumatic diseases comprise over <strong>200 distinct conditions</strong> affecting joints, muscles, bones, and connective tissues. They are broadly categorized as:</p>
<ol>
<li>
<p><strong>Inflammatory/autoimmune arthritis:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Rheumatoid arthritis (RA):</strong> symmetric polyarthritis; autoantibodies (RF, anti-CCP); affects ~1% of population; 2-3x more common in women; HLA-DRB1 shared epitope; treated with DMARDs and biologics</li>
<li><strong>Spondyloarthritis:</strong> ankylosing spondylitis, psoriatic arthritis, reactive arthritis, IBD-associated; HLA-B27; enthesitis; asymmetric oligoarthritis</li>
<li><strong>Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE):</strong> multi-system autoimmune; ANA, anti-dsDNA; type III hypersensitivity; predominantly young women</li>
<li><strong>Sjogren's syndrome:</strong> sicca syndrome (dry eyes, dry mouth); anti-Ro/La</li>
<li><strong>Systemic sclerosis (scleroderma):</strong> skin fibrosis + organ involvement</li>
<li><strong>Polymyalgia rheumatica:</strong> &gt;50 years; proximal muscle pain; associated with GCA</li>
<li><strong>Juvenile idiopathic arthritis (JIA):</strong> most common rheumatic disease in children</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Degenerative joint disease:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Osteoarthritis (OA):</strong> cartilage degradation; most common joint disease; risk factors: age, obesity, prior injury; no systemic inflammation</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Crystal arthropathies:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Gout:</strong> monosodium urate crystals; podagra (first MTP joint); hyperuricemia</li>
<li><strong>Pseudogout (CPPD):</strong> calcium pyrophosphate crystals; often knee or wrist; older adults</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Infectious arthritis:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Septic arthritis:</strong> bacterial infection of joint (S. aureus most common); medical emergency</li>
<li><strong>Reactive arthritis:</strong> post-infectious (Chlamydia, Salmonella, Shigella)</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Soft tissue rheumatism:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Fibromyalgia:</strong> chronic widespread pain; central sensitization</li>
<li><strong>Tendinitis, bursitis:</strong> localized soft tissue inflammation</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Key clinical distinction:</strong> <strong>Inflammatory</strong> rheumatic diseases (RA, SLE, SpA) feature morning stiffness &gt;30 min, systemic symptoms, symmetrical involvement, and elevated inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP). <strong>Non-inflammatory</strong> diseases (OA, fibromyalgia) feature &lt;30 min morning stiffness, mechanical pain (worse with activity, better with rest), and normal inflammatory markers.</p>
<p><strong>Treat-to-target</strong> strategy for inflammatory arthritis: aim for remission (DAS28 &lt;2.6 for RA) or low disease activity; escalate therapy every 3-6 months until target reached. Modern biologic and targeted synthetic DMARDs have dramatically improved outcomes for inflammatory rheumatic diseases over the past 20 years.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>General principles:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Early diagnosis and treatment (especially for inflammatory arthritis; &quot;window of opportunity&quot; in first 3 months of RA)</li>
<li>Treat-to-target strategy for inflammatory diseases</li>
<li>Multidisciplinary approach (rheumatologist, physical therapist, occupational therapist, primary care)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>NSAIDs:</strong> first-line symptomatic treatment for most rheumatic diseases (ibuprofen, naproxen, celecoxib); GI and renal precautions<br />
<strong>Corticosteroids:</strong> for acute flares (prednisone burst); low-dose chronic in RA (before DMARD onset); intra-articular injections<br />
<strong>DMARDs (RA):</strong> methotrexate 15-25 mg/week (anchor); sulfasalazine, hydroxychloroquine, leflunomide; combination (&quot;triple therapy&quot;) for moderate disease<br />
<strong>Biologics (for inadequate DMARD response):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>TNF inhibitors:</strong> adalimumab, etanercept, infliximab, certolizumab, golimumab</li>
<li><strong>IL-6 receptor:</strong> tocilizumab, sarilumab</li>
<li><strong>Anti-CD20:</strong> rituximab</li>
<li><strong>T-cell co-stimulation:</strong> abatacept</li>
<li><strong>JAK inhibitors:</strong> tofacitinib, baricitinib, upadacitinib<br />
<strong>Osteoarthritis:</strong> acetaminophen, NSAIDs, intra-articular corticosteroids or hyaluronic acid, weight loss, exercise, joint replacement<br />
<strong>Gout:</strong> acute (NSAIDs, colchicine, corticosteroids); chronic (allopurinol, febuxostat; target uric acid &lt;6 mg/dL)<br />
<strong>Physical/occupational therapy:</strong> exercise, joint protection, assistive devices, splinting</li>
</ul>
