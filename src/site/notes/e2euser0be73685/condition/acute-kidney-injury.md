<h1>Acute Kidney Injury</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a syndrome of rapid decline in renal function (hours to days), resulting in the accumulation of nitrogenous waste products (urea, creatinine) and inability to maintain fluid, electrolyte, and acid-base homeostasis. AKI is extremely common in hospitalized patients -- affecting <strong>5-7% of hospital admissions</strong> and <strong>25-50% of ICU patients</strong> -- and is an independent predictor of mortality, with hospital mortality exceeding <strong>50%</strong> in patients requiring dialysis.</p>
<p><strong>KDIGO Diagnostic Criteria (2012):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Increase in serum creatinine by <strong>&gt;=0.3 mg/dL</strong> within 48 hours, OR</li>
<li>Increase in serum creatinine to <strong>&gt;=1.5 times baseline</strong> within the prior 7 days, OR</li>
<li>Urine volume <strong>&lt;0.5 mL/kg/hour for 6 hours</strong></li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Etiological classification:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Pre-renal (most common; ~60%):</strong> reduced renal perfusion from hypovolemia, hypotension, sepsis, heart failure, NSAIDs (afferent arteriole constriction), ACE inhibitors/ARBs (efferent arteriole dilation). Kidney is initially undamaged; rapidly reversible with volume restoration.</li>
<li><strong>Intrinsic (~35%):</strong> direct kidney damage -- Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN; most common intrinsic; from ischemia or nephrotoxins), Acute Interstitial Nephritis (AIN; from drugs or infection), Glomerulonephritis, Vascular (atheroembolic, TTP/HUS).</li>
<li><strong>Post-renal (~5%):</strong> urinary tract obstruction (BPH, stones, tumors, neurogenic bladder). Diagnosed by bladder scan or ultrasound showing hydronephrosis.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>FENa (Fractional Excretion of Sodium):</strong> &lt;1% suggests pre-renal; &gt;2% suggests ATN. However, unreliable with diuretics or CKD. <strong>FEUrea (urea) &lt;35%</strong> suggests pre-renal even with diuretics.</p>
<p>The most feared acute complication is <strong>hyperkalemia</strong> (K+ &gt;6.0 mEq/L), which can cause fatal cardiac arrhythmias. Other complications include metabolic acidosis, fluid overload (pulmonary edema), uremic pericarditis, uremic encephalopathy, and bleeding (uremic platelet dysfunction).</p>
<p>Even after apparent recovery, AKI increases the long-term risk of developing <strong>chronic kidney disease (CKD)</strong> by 2-3x.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>General principles:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Identify and treat the underlying cause</strong></li>
<li><strong>Optimize hemodynamics</strong> (maintain renal perfusion)</li>
<li><strong>Avoid further nephrotoxic injury</strong></li>
<li><strong>Manage complications</strong> (hyperkalemia, acidosis, fluid overload)</li>
<li><strong>Consider renal replacement therapy (RRT)</strong> if indicated</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Pre-renal AKI:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>IV fluid resuscitation</strong> (normal saline or lactated Ringer's; boluses of 500 mL)</li>
<li>Target MAP &gt;=65 mmHg and adequate urine output</li>
<li><strong>Discontinue</strong> NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, diuretics (temporarily)</li>
<li>Monitor response: urine output should increase within 6-12 hours if pre-renal</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Intrinsic AKI (ATN):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Remove the offending agent</strong> (nephrotoxin, drug causing AIN)</li>
<li><strong>Supportive care</strong> -- kidney typically recovers over 1-3 weeks</li>
<li><strong>No role for diuretics</strong> unless volume overloaded (do not &quot;force&quot; urine output)</li>
<li><strong>No role for low-dose dopamine</strong> (multiple RCTs show no benefit)</li>
<li><strong>AIN:</strong> consider corticosteroids (prednisone 1 mg/kg/day) if drug-induced and no improvement after stopping agent</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Post-renal AKI:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Relieve obstruction</strong> urgently: urinary catheter (bladder outlet), nephrostomy or stent (ureteral obstruction)</li>
<li>Post-obstructive diuresis may occur after relief (monitor electrolytes and fluid)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Indications for Renal Replacement Therapy (dialysis) -- &quot;AEIOU&quot;:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>A</strong>cidosis (severe, refractory metabolic acidosis pH &lt;7.1)</li>
<li><strong>E</strong>lectrolyte abnormalities (refractory hyperkalemia K+ &gt;6.5 despite medical therapy)</li>
<li><strong>I</strong>ntoxications (lithium, methanol, ethylene glycol, salicylates)</li>
<li><strong>O</strong>verload (refractory pulmonary edema)</li>
<li><strong>U</strong>remia (pericarditis, encephalopathy, bleeding)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>KDIGO Care Bundle:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Close monitoring of volume status and electrolytes</li>
<li>Avoid hyperglycemia (target glucose 110-149 mg/dL)</li>
<li>Avoid nephrotoxins (review all medications)</li>
<li>Alternative drug dosing for reduced kidney function</li>
<li>Protein intake 0.8-1.0 g/kg/day (non-catabolic); 1.0-1.5 g/kg/day (catabolic with RRT)</li>
</ul>
