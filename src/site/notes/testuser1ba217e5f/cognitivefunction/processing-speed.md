<h1>Processing Speed</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Processing speed is the rate at which the brain performs elementary cognitive operations — perceiving a stimulus, making a simple decision, and producing a response. Although it sounds basic, it is one of the most influential cognitive constructs: faster processing frees capacity for <a href="/garden/testuser1ba217e5f/cognitivefunction/memory-and-learning/" class="internal-link">Memory and Learning</a> and higher-order reasoning, while slowing degrades performance across many domains. The processing-speed theory of cognitive aging holds that age-related slowing is a primary driver of broader cognitive decline, because operations that are too slow cannot complete before their inputs decay.</p>
<p>Unlike functions localized to specific regions, processing speed depends critically on the <strong>integrity of white-matter tracts</strong> that connect distributed networks — making it sensitive to vascular health, demyelination, and aging.</p>
<h2>Neural Basis</h2>
<p>Processing speed reflects efficient, well-myelinated connectivity among the <a class="internal-link is-unresolved" href="/404">Prefrontal Cortex</a>, parietal cortex, and their association tracts. White-matter lesions, small-vessel disease, and demyelination slow processing. <a href="/garden/testuser1ba217e5f/neurotransmitter/dopamine/" class="internal-link">Dopamine</a> tone modulates speeded performance, contributing to slowing in dopaminergic disorders.</p>
<h2>Clinical Relevance</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Cognitive aging:</strong> processing speed declines earliest and most steeply with age.</li>
<li><strong>Mild cognitive impairment / dementia:</strong> slowing is an early, sensitive marker (see <a href="/garden/testuser1ba217e5f/condition/mild-cognitive-impairment/" class="internal-link">Mild Cognitive Impairment</a>, <a href="/garden/testuser1ba217e5f/condition/cognitive-dysfunction/" class="internal-link">Cognitive Dysfunction</a>).</li>
<li><strong>Other:</strong> prominently reduced in multiple sclerosis, traumatic brain injury, depression, and ADHD.</li>
</ul>
