<h1>Bone Fractures</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>A bone fracture is a complete or partial break in the continuity of bone tissue. Fractures are among the most common reasons for ED visits and orthopedic surgery. They are classified by:</p>
<p><strong>Fracture patterns:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Transverse:</strong> straight across the bone</li>
<li><strong>Oblique:</strong> diagonal break</li>
<li><strong>Spiral:</strong> twisting force; fracture spirals around bone</li>
<li><strong>Comminuted:</strong> bone broken into &gt;2 pieces</li>
<li><strong>Greenstick:</strong> incomplete break (one side breaks, other bends; children only)</li>
<li><strong>Segmental:</strong> two separate fracture lines creating a free segment</li>
<li><strong>Compression/impaction:</strong> bone fragments driven into each other (vertebral bodies; distal radius)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Displacement:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Angulated, translated, shortened, or rotated</li>
<li>Determines need for reduction</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Open vs closed:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Closed:</strong> skin intact</li>
<li><strong>Open (compound):</strong> bone communicates with external environment through a wound; <strong>requires antibiotics</strong> and urgent surgical debridement</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Fragility fractures</strong> (from osteoporosis): typically involve the <strong>hip (femoral neck), spine (vertebral compression), distal radius (Colles), and proximal humerus</strong>. These are the hallmark of osteoporosis and indicate significantly elevated risk of future fracture.</p>
<p><strong>Healing phases:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Hematoma/inflammatory</strong> (days 1-7): bleeding, clot formation, inflammatory cell recruitment</li>
<li><strong>Reparative</strong> (weeks 2-6): soft callus (fibrocartilage) forms; chondrocytes and osteoblasts proliferate</li>
<li><strong>Remodeling</strong> (months to years): woven bone replaced by lamellar bone; restoration of original shape</li>
</ol>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>General principles:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Assess for life-threatening injuries first</strong> (ABCDE; ATLS)</li>
<li><strong>Neurovascular assessment</strong> of the affected limb (distal pulses, sensation, motor function)</li>
<li><strong>Imaging:</strong> X-rays (2 orthogonal views; include joint above and below); CT for complex/articular fractures</li>
<li><strong>Reduction</strong> (if displaced): closed (manipulation under sedation) or open (surgical)</li>
<li><strong>Immobilization:</strong> cast, splint, external fixator, or internal fixation</li>
<li><strong>Pain management:</strong> multimodal (acetaminophen, NSAIDs, opioids for severe pain)</li>
<li><strong>VTE prophylaxis:</strong> for lower extremity fractures requiring surgery or prolonged immobilization (LMWH; ~2-6 weeks)</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Specific fracture types:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Hip fracture (femoral neck):</strong> surgical emergency (ideally within 24-48h); arthroplasty (hemiarthroplasty or total hip) for displaced fractures; ORIF for non-displaced</li>
<li><strong>Distal radius (Colles):</strong> closed reduction + cast (most common); ORIF (volar plate) for significantly displaced or intra-articular</li>
<li><strong>Ankle:</strong> ORIF if unstable (bimalleolar, trimalleolar); conservative if stable</li>
<li><strong>Vertebral compression:</strong> conservative (analgesics, early mobilization, bracing); vertebroplasty/kyphoplasty for severe pain</li>
<li><strong>Femoral shaft:</strong> intramedullary nailing (gold standard)</li>
<li><strong>Open fracture:</strong> emergency surgical debridement + irrigation; IV antibiotics (cefazolin + aminoglycoside or piperacillin-tazobactam); tetanus prophylaxis; external or internal fixation depending on Gustilo type</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Bone health optimization (prevent re-fracture):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Calcium 1000-1200 mg/day + vitamin D 800-2000 IU/day</li>
<li>Bisphosphonates (alendronate, zoledronic acid) for confirmed osteoporosis</li>
<li>Denosumab or anabolic agents (teriparatide, romosozumab) for high-risk patients</li>
<li>Fall prevention counseling</li>
</ul>
