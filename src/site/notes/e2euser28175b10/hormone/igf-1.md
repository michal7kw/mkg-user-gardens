<h1>IGF-1</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Insulin-like Growth Factor 1 (IGF-1, formerly Somatomedin C) is a 70-amino-acid peptide hormone structurally similar to insulin. It is produced primarily by the liver in response to growth hormone (GH) stimulation, mediating most of GH's anabolic effects. IGF-1 signals through the IGF-1 receptor to activate the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway, promoting cell growth, proliferation, and survival while inhibiting apoptosis. This places IGF-1 at the center of a fundamental biological trade-off: high IGF-1 promotes growth and tissue repair but also accelerates aging and cancer risk, while low IGF-1 (as seen with caloric restriction) extends lifespan in model organisms.</p>
<h2>Key Functions</h2>
<ul>
<li>Skeletal muscle growth and repair</li>
<li>Bone growth and mineralization</li>
<li>Neuronal survival and synaptic plasticity</li>
<li>Collagen synthesis and wound healing</li>
<li>Hepatic glucose regulation</li>
</ul>
<h2>The Longevity Paradox</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Growth-promoting</strong>: High IGF-1 builds muscle, repairs tissue, supports brain function</li>
<li><strong>Aging-promoting</strong>: High IGF-1 activates mTOR, suppresses autophagy, promotes cell proliferation</li>
<li><strong>Longevity association</strong>: Laron syndrome (GH receptor deficiency → low IGF-1) shows near-zero cancer rates</li>
<li><strong>Optimal strategy</strong>: Likely periodic IGF-1 cycling — high during recovery/exercise, low during fasting</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Relevance</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Acromegaly</strong> — Excess GH/IGF-1 (organ enlargement, diabetes, cardiovascular disease)</li>
<li><strong>GH deficiency</strong> — Low IGF-1 (growth failure, reduced muscle mass, increased adiposity)</li>
<li><strong>Cancer</strong> — Elevated IGF-1 associated with breast, prostate, colorectal cancer risk</li>
<li><strong>Sarcopenia</strong> — Age-related decline in IGF-1 contributes to muscle loss</li>
<li><strong>Neurodegeneration</strong> — Brain IGF-1 resistance linked to Alzheimer's disease</li>
</ul>
