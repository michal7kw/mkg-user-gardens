<h1>Milk Thistle</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Milk thistle (Silybum marianum) is a flowering plant whose seeds yield silymarin, a complex of flavonolignans dominated by silybin (silibinin) together with silydianin and silychristin. Silymarin has been used for centuries for liver complaints and remains one of the most widely used hepatoprotective botanicals. Its oral bioavailability is poor because of low water solubility and extensive first-pass metabolism, which has motivated phospholipid-complexed formulations.</p>
<h2>Mechanisms</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Antioxidant</strong>: Directly scavenges free radicals and increases intracellular glutathione, the liver's principal antioxidant</li>
<li><strong>Membrane stabilization</strong>: Reduces hepatocyte membrane permeability, limiting uptake of hepatotoxins (the basis for its use in Amanita phalloides poisoning)</li>
<li><strong>Antifibrotic</strong>: Suppresses hepatic stellate-cell activation and collagen deposition</li>
<li><strong>Regenerative</strong>: Stimulates RNA polymerase I and ribosomal protein synthesis, supporting hepatocyte regeneration</li>
<li><strong>Anti-inflammatory</strong>: Inhibits NF-kB signaling and pro-inflammatory cytokine production</li>
</ul>
<h2>Evidence</h2>
<p>Clinical trials are heterogeneous in formulation, dose, and population. Meta-analyses suggest modest reductions in serum aminotransferases in chronic liver disease, with the most consistent signals in alcohol-related and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. Evidence for hard outcomes in cirrhosis (mortality, decompensation) is weak and inconsistent. Silymarin is generally well tolerated.</p>
