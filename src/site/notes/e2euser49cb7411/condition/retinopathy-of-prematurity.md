<h1>Retinopathy of Prematurity</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Retinopathy of prematurity (ROP) is a <strong>vasoproliferative retinal disorder</strong> affecting premature infants. Normal retinal vascularization begins at 16 weeks gestation from the optic disc and reaches the peripheral retina (ora serrata) at <strong>36-40 weeks gestational age</strong>. In premature infants, this vascularization is incomplete at birth. Postnatal factors -- especially <strong>fluctuating oxygen levels</strong> -- disrupt normal VEGF and IGF-1 signaling, leading to <strong>abnormal neovascularization</strong> that can contract, bleed, and cause <strong>retinal detachment</strong>.</p>
<p><strong>Pathophysiology -- two-phase model:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Phase 1 (hyperoxic):</strong> the relatively high postnatal oxygen environment (compared to intrauterine hypoxia) suppresses VEGF -&gt; delayed vascularization</li>
<li><strong>Phase 2 (hypoxic):</strong> the increasingly metabolically active but avascular retina becomes hypoxic -&gt; VEGF surge -&gt; disordered neovascularization (fragile, leaky vessels that can bleed and contract -&gt; tractional retinal detachment)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Screening criteria (AAP/AAO/AAPOS):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Birth weight &lt;1500 g OR gestational age &lt;=30 weeks</li>
<li>Selected infants 1500-2000 g with unstable clinical course</li>
<li>First exam: 4 weeks postnatal age OR 31 weeks postmenstrual age (whichever later)</li>
<li>Follow-up: every 1-2 weeks until vascularization complete or treatment indicated</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Classification:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Zone:</strong> location of disease relative to optic disc (Zone I = worst, closest to disc; Zone III = best, peripheral)</li>
<li><strong>Stage:</strong> 1 (demarcation line) -&gt; 2 (ridge) -&gt; 3 (extraretinal fibrovascular proliferation) -&gt; 4 (partial retinal detachment) -&gt; 5 (total retinal detachment)</li>
<li><strong>Plus disease:</strong> venous dilation and arterial tortuosity in posterior pole (indicates aggressive disease)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Treatment-ROP (ETROP) criteria</strong> (requires treatment within 72 hours):</p>
<ul>
<li>Type 1 ROP: Zone I any stage with plus disease; Zone I stage 3 without plus; Zone II stage 2-3 with plus</li>
</ul>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Laser photocoagulation:</strong> ablation of peripheral avascular retina (reduces VEGF production); traditional gold standard; permanent peripheral visual field loss</p>
<p><strong>Anti-VEGF injections</strong> (bevacizumab 0.625 mg, ranibizumab 0.2 mg): for Zone I and posterior Zone II disease; rapidly regress neovascularization; may allow normal vascularization; concerns about systemic VEGF suppression (neurodevelopmental effects debated)</p>
<p><strong>Surgical:</strong> scleral buckle or vitrectomy for stage 4-5 (retinal detachment); visual outcomes poor in advanced disease</p>
<p><strong>Long-term follow-up:</strong> all ROP survivors need lifelong ophthalmologic care (high risk of myopia, strabismus, amblyopia, glaucoma, late retinal detachment)</p>
