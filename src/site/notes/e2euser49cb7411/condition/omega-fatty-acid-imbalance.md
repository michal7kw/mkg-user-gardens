<h1>Omega Fatty Acid Imbalance</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Omega fatty acid imbalance refers to a distorted ratio of omega-6 to omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) in the diet and consequently in cell membranes. Omega-6 and omega-3 are essential fatty acids -- the body cannot synthesize them -- but they compete for the same desaturase and elongase enzymes (notably delta-6-desaturase encoded by <em>FADS2</em>) and produce functionally opposing eicosanoids. <strong>Omega-6-derived arachidonic acid</strong> is the precursor of series-2 prostaglandins and series-4 leukotrienes, which are largely <strong>pro-inflammatory</strong>; <strong>omega-3-derived EPA/DHA</strong> generate series-3 prostaglandins and series-5 leukotrienes along with the <strong>pro-resolving mediators</strong> resolvins, protectins, and maresins.</p>
<p>Historical human diets are estimated to have had an <strong>omega-6:omega-3 ratio near 1:1 to 4:1</strong>, whereas the modern Western diet sits at <strong>15:1 to 20:1</strong>. This shift displaces omega-3 from membrane phospholipids, reduces the production of resolution mediators, and is mechanistically linked to the low-grade chronic inflammation that underlies cardiovascular disease, metabolic syndrome, autoimmune disease, and mood disorders. The omega-3 index (EPA+DHA as a percentage of red blood cell fatty acids) is the most validated biomarker; values <strong>&lt;4%</strong> indicate high cardiovascular risk, while <strong>&gt;8%</strong> is cardioprotective.</p>
<p>Clinically the imbalance manifests subtly: dry/scaly skin, brittle hair and nails, joint stiffness, fatigue, mood lability, and poor sleep -- reflecting omega-3's structural role in cell membranes, neuronal myelination, and inflammation regulation. Correction is primarily dietary (fatty fish 2-3x/week, reduced seed oils), with supplementation of EPA/DHA (1-4 g/day) as needed. Plant ALA (flax, chia, walnut) is a poor substitute because conversion to EPA/DHA is inefficient (&lt;5-10% for EPA, &lt;0.5-5% for DHA), especially in carriers of unfavorable <em>FADS</em> variants.</p>
