<h1>Inflammatory Bowel Disease</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) refers to a group of chronic, relapsing-remitting inflammatory disorders of the gastrointestinal tract. Its two principal forms are Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis. Crohn's disease can involve any segment of the gut from mouth to anus, characteristically in a discontinuous (&quot;skip lesion&quot;) pattern with transmural (full-thickness) inflammation that predisposes to strictures and fistulae. Ulcerative colitis is limited to the colon, producing continuous, superficial mucosal inflammation extending proximally from the rectum.</p>
<p>The prevailing model holds that IBD arises from a dysregulated mucosal immune response to the commensal gut microbiota in genetically susceptible individuals, with environmental triggers such as smoking, diet, and the microbiome shaping risk and course. The chronic inflammation involves the same NF-kB-driven cytokine pathways (TNF-alpha, IL-12/23, IL-6) that are the targets of modern biologic therapies.</p>
<h2>Diagnosis and Monitoring</h2>
<p>Diagnosis integrates clinical features, endoscopy with biopsy, imaging, and laboratory markers. Faecal calprotectin is a sensitive non-invasive marker of intestinal inflammation used to distinguish IBD from functional bowel disease and to monitor activity, while serum C-reactive protein (hs-CRP) reflects systemic inflammation. Colonoscopy assesses disease extent, monitors mucosal healing, and provides cancer surveillance, since long-standing colonic inflammation increases colorectal cancer risk.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p>Treatment aims to induce and maintain remission and achieve mucosal healing using aminosalicylates (mainly ulcerative colitis), corticosteroids for flares, immunomodulators, and biologic/small-molecule therapies (anti-TNF, anti-integrin, anti-IL-12/23, JAK inhibitors). Nutritional support, correction of deficiencies (iron, B12, vitamin D), and, when medical therapy fails, surgery are important components of care.</p>
