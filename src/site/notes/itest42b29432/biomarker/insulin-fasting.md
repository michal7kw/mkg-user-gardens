<h1>Insulin Fasting</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Fasting insulin is the amount of insulin circulating in the blood after an overnight fast (typically 8-12 hours), measured in serum or plasma. Insulin is a peptide hormone secreted by the beta cells of the pancreatic islets in response to rising blood glucose and certain amino acids. It promotes glucose uptake in muscle and adipose tissue, suppresses hepatic glucose production, and drives glycogen, lipid, and protein synthesis. The fasting value reflects basal secretory tone of the beta cells in the absence of a recent meal, and is therefore a window onto how hard the pancreas must work to maintain normal glucose.</p>
<p>In the earliest stages of insulin resistance, peripheral tissues respond poorly to insulin, and the beta cells compensate by secreting more of it. This compensatory hyperinsulinemia can keep fasting and post-prandial glucose within the normal range for years, which means fasting insulin frequently becomes abnormal long before fasting glucose or HbA1c. Because of this, an elevated fasting insulin in the setting of normal glucose is one of the most useful early signals of evolving metabolic dysfunction. Conversely, a falling insulin level alongside rising glucose can signal beta-cell exhaustion in advanced type 2 diabetes, or absolute deficiency in type 1 diabetes.</p>
<p>Clinically, fasting insulin is most powerful when interpreted together with fasting glucose. The two values are combined in the Homeostasis Model Assessment of Insulin Resistance (HOMA-IR), calculated as (fasting insulin in mIU/L x fasting glucose in mg/dL) / 405, which provides a simple surrogate estimate of insulin sensitivity validated against the gold-standard euglycemic clamp. Fasting insulin alone is not used to diagnose diabetes, but is widely used to characterize insulin resistance, stratify cardiometabolic risk, evaluate PCOS, and monitor the metabolic response to diet, exercise, weight loss, and pharmacologic or supplement interventions.</p>

<p><strong>Notes:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Reference intervals are assay-dependent. Insulin assays are not fully standardized across platforms, so absolute values can differ between laboratories; serial measurements should ideally use the same lab and method.</li>
<li>&quot;Optimal&quot; thresholds (&lt;5 mIU/L) come from metabolic-health and longevity literature and are tighter than conventional laboratory upper limits.</li>
<li>The clinical meaning of a value depends heavily on the simultaneous glucose: high insulin with normal glucose indicates compensated resistance, whereas low insulin with high glucose indicates inadequate secretion.</li>
<li>Hemolysis degrades insulin and can falsely lower results; samples should be processed promptly.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Factors Affecting Levels</h2>
<p><strong>Increase Fasting Insulin:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Insulin resistance from excess visceral adiposity (compensatory hypersecretion)</li>
<li>High-carbohydrate or high-glycemic-load diet and frequent eating</li>
<li>Physical inactivity and loss of muscle mass</li>
<li>Chronic stress and elevated cortisol (counter-regulatory effect)</li>
<li>Sleep deprivation and circadian disruption</li>
<li>Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)</li>
<li>Certain medications (e.g., glucocorticoids, some antipsychotics)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Decrease Fasting Insulin:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Weight loss and reduction of visceral fat</li>
<li>Aerobic and resistance exercise (improved insulin sensitivity)</li>
<li>Carbohydrate restriction and caloric restriction or fasting</li>
<li>Improved sleep</li>
<li>Insulin-sensitizing agents and supplements</li>
<li>Advanced beta-cell failure (a pathologic, not favorable, cause of low insulin)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p><strong>Elevated levels are associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Insulin Resistance</strong> -- the central abnormality; elevated fasting insulin is the most direct circulating marker of compensatory hyperinsulinemia.</li>
<li><strong>Metabolic Syndrome</strong> -- hyperinsulinemia clusters with central obesity, dyslipidemia, and hypertension.</li>
<li><strong>Type 2 Diabetes</strong> -- elevated insulin precedes overt hyperglycemia, often by years, as the pancreas struggles to compensate.</li>
<li><strong>Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS)</strong> -- hyperinsulinemia drives ovarian androgen production and is a therapeutic target.</li>
<li><strong>Cardiovascular Disease</strong> -- prospective data link insulin resistance and hyperinsulinemia to increased ischemic heart disease risk.</li>
<li><strong>Non-alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease</strong> -- hepatic insulin resistance promotes lipogenesis and steatosis.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Reduced levels are associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Type 1 diabetes / beta-cell destruction</strong> -- low insulin with hyperglycemia indicates absolute deficiency.</li>
<li><strong>Late-stage type 2 diabetes</strong> -- beta-cell exhaustion after prolonged hypersecretion.</li>
<li><strong>Excellent insulin sensitivity</strong> -- low fasting insulin with normal glucose is favorable.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Monitoring use cases:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Tracking response to weight loss, low-carbohydrate diets, and exercise programs.</li>
<li>Computing HOMA-IR to quantify insulin sensitivity over time.</li>
<li>Assessing and managing PCOS-related hyperinsulinemia.</li>
<li>Detecting early metabolic dysfunction when glucose and HbA1c remain normal.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Strategies to Improve</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Reduce refined carbohydrate and added sugar intake</strong> -- lowering glycemic load decreases the insulin secretory demand and is among the most effective dietary levers.</li>
<li><strong>Lose visceral fat</strong> -- even 5-10% body-weight reduction substantially lowers fasting insulin and improves HOMA-IR.</li>
<li><strong>Exercise regularly</strong> -- combine aerobic training with resistance training to increase glucose disposal and insulin sensitivity, partly via non-insulin-dependent (GLUT4) pathways.</li>
<li><strong>Berberine</strong> -- ~500 mg three times daily has demonstrated glucose- and insulin-lowering effects comparable to metformin in some trials (PMID:18442638).</li>
<li><strong>Alpha Lipoic Acid</strong> -- oral supplementation can modestly improve insulin sensitivity in type 2 diabetes (PMID:10468203).</li>
<li><strong>Magnesium</strong> -- repletion improves insulin resistance, particularly in hypomagnesemic and insulin-resistant individuals (PMID:27329332).</li>
<li><strong>Inositol (myo-inositol)</strong> -- improves insulin sensitivity in PCOS and insulin-resistant states (PMID:35236761).</li>
<li><strong>Prioritize sleep and stress management</strong> -- both directly modulate insulin sensitivity and counter-regulatory hormones.</li>
</ol>
