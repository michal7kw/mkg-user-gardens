<h1>Synaptic Plasticity</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Synaptic plasticity is the capacity of synapses to change their strength in response to activity, and it is the leading cellular model for how the brain learns and stores memory. Its two canonical forms are <strong>long-term potentiation (LTP)</strong>, a persistent strengthening of synaptic transmission following high-frequency activity, and <strong>long-term depression (LTD)</strong>, a corresponding weakening. Both depend on the precise coincidence detection performed by the NMDA subtype of <a href="/garden/itest959d0821/neurotransmitter/glutamate/" class="internal-link">Glutamate</a> receptor, whose calcium influx triggers downstream kinase cascades (notably CaMKII) that traffic AMPA receptors into or out of the postsynaptic membrane.</p>
<p>Beyond glutamatergic mechanics, plasticity is gated by neurotrophic support — <a href="/garden/itest959d0821/biomarker/bdnf/" class="internal-link">BDNF</a> acting through TrkB (see <a href="/garden/itest959d0821/pathway/bdnf-trkb-signaling-pathway/" class="internal-link">BDNF-TrkB Signaling Pathway</a>) — and is tuned by inhibitory <a href="/garden/itest959d0821/neurotransmitter/gaba/" class="internal-link">GABA</a> tone. Plasticity occurs at the <a href="/garden/itest959d0821/cellularcomponent/synapse/" class="internal-link">Synapse</a> and is the substrate that links molecular neurochemistry to <a href="/garden/itest959d0821/cognitivefunction/memory-and-learning/" class="internal-link">Memory and Learning</a>.</p>
<h2>Key Steps (LTP)</h2>
<ol>
<li>Glutamate release activates postsynaptic AMPA receptors, depolarizing the membrane.</li>
<li>Depolarization relieves the Mg²⁺ block of NMDA receptors; coincident glutamate binding opens them, admitting Ca²⁺.</li>
<li>Ca²⁺ activates CaMKII and other kinases, driving AMPA-receptor phosphorylation and insertion.</li>
<li>Sustained potentiation engages CREB-dependent transcription and BDNF/TrkB signaling for structural consolidation (spine growth).</li>
</ol>
<h2>Regulation</h2>
<p>LTP/LTD balance is set by the magnitude and timing of postsynaptic Ca²⁺. Endogenous NMDA modulators tune the threshold — for example, the kynurenine metabolite <a href="/garden/itest959d0821/metabolite/kynurenic-acid/" class="internal-link">Kynurenic Acid</a> is an NMDA glycine-site antagonist that dampens plasticity, providing a direct link between tryptophan metabolism and learning. Sleep and exercise enhance plasticity; chronic stress and neuroinflammation impair it.</p>
<h2>Physiological Role</h2>
<p>Synaptic plasticity underlies hippocampal and cortical learning, memory consolidation, and adaptive behavior. Its impairment is central to cognitive decline in <a href="/garden/itest959d0821/condition/alzheimers-disease/" class="internal-link">Alzheimer's Disease</a>, the anhedonia and cognitive symptoms of <a href="/garden/itest959d0821/condition/depression/" class="internal-link">Depression</a>, and developmental/psychiatric disorders.</p>
