<h1>Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) is a <strong>chronic, often disabling psychiatric disorder</strong> that develops after exposure to a <strong>traumatic event</strong> involving actual or threatened death, serious injury, or sexual violence. It is defined by DSM-5 criteria requiring <strong>&gt;=1 month</strong> of symptoms in four clusters: <strong>(1) intrusion</strong> (intrusive memories, flashbacks, nightmares), <strong>(2) avoidance</strong> (of trauma-related thoughts or external reminders), <strong>(3) negative alterations in cognition and mood</strong> (persistent negative beliefs, anhedonia, detachment), and <strong>(4) alterations in arousal and reactivity</strong> (hypervigilance, irritability, sleep disturbance, exaggerated startle). Lifetime prevalence is <strong>~6-9% in the general population</strong>, rising to <strong>10-30% in combat veterans, sexual assault survivors, and refugees</strong>.</p>
<p>The neurobiology is increasingly understood. The core finding is <strong>amygdala hyperactivity</strong> with <strong>medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC) hypoactivity</strong> -- mPFC normally inhibits the amygdala, but in PTSD this inhibition fails, producing exaggerated fear responses to trauma reminders that fail to extinguish. The <strong>hippocampus is reduced in volume</strong> (~5-10%), reflecting both a risk factor (smaller hippocampus predicts PTSD after trauma) and a consequence (chronic cortisol and glutamate neurotoxicity). <strong>HPA axis dysregulation</strong> is paradoxical -- PTSD patients often have <strong>low baseline cortisol</strong> with <strong>enhanced negative feedback</strong> (dexamethasone suppression test), the opposite of major depression. <strong>Norepinephrine</strong> is elevated, driving hyperarousal. <strong>Epigenetic changes</strong> from childhood trauma (methylation of NR3C1 glucocorticoid receptor gene) sensitize the stress response and predict PTSD in adulthood.</p>
<p>PTSD is <strong>highly comorbid</strong> -- <strong>major depression</strong> (~50%), <strong>substance use disorders</strong> (~30-50%, especially alcohol), <strong>other anxiety disorders</strong>, <strong>suicidality</strong> (2-3x increased risk), and <strong>chronic pain</strong>. <strong>Complex PTSD</strong> -- resulting from chronic, repeated trauma (childhood abuse, captivity, trafficking) -- includes the core PTSD symptoms plus <strong>disturbances in self-organization</strong> (emotion dysregulation, negative self-concept, interpersonal difficulties); recognized in ICD-11 but not DSM-5. Untreated PTSD is chronic in ~one-third of patients, but <strong>trauma-focused therapy is highly effective</strong>, with ~50-70% achieving remission.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Trauma-focused psychotherapy (first-line; most evidence for efficacy):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Prolonged Exposure (PE)</strong> -- 8-15 sessions; imaginal exposure (recounting trauma memory) and in vivo exposure (avoided situations). Gold-standard; ~50-70% response</li>
<li><strong>Cognitive Processing Therapy (CPT)</strong> -- 12 sessions; identifies and modifies maladaptive beliefs (&quot;the world is entirely dangerous,&quot; &quot;I am to blame&quot;) that maintain symptoms</li>
<li><strong>Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing (EMDR)</strong> -- 8-session protocol combining imaginal exposure with bilateral eye movements; equivalent efficacy to PE and CPT</li>
<li><strong>Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for PTSD (CBT-TF)</strong> -- broader category</li>
<li><strong>Group therapy</strong> for veterans, survivors of shared trauma</li>
<li><strong>Trauma-focused therapy is more effective than medication for PTSD and should be offered to all patients</strong></li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Pharmacotherapy (second-line or adjunct to psychotherapy):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>SSRIs</strong> -- <strong>sertraline</strong> 50-200 mg and <strong>paroxetine</strong> 20-60 mg are <strong>FDA-approved for PTSD</strong>. First-line pharmacotherapy. Effective for all symptom clusters. Onset 4-8 weeks. Continue for at least 12 months after response</li>
<li><strong>SNRIs</strong> -- <strong>venlafaxine</strong> 75-225 mg; alternative first-line</li>
<li><strong>Prazosin</strong> 2-15 mg at night -- alpha-1 antagonist; reduces trauma-related nightmares and sleep disturbance (mixed evidence; some trials negative, including the PACT trial in veterans)</li>
<li><strong>Topiramate, quetiapine, risperidone</strong> -- off-label adjuncts; limited evidence</li>
<li><strong>Avoid benzodiazepines</strong> -- no evidence of efficacy, risk of dependence and disinhibition, may impair fear extinction</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Other treatments:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Stellate ganglion block</strong> -- emerging interventional; mixed evidence</li>
<li><strong>MDMA-assisted psychotherapy</strong> -- phase 3 trials (MAPP1, MAPP2) showed strong benefit; under FDA review (delayed by 2024 negative advisory committee vote but research ongoing)</li>
<li><strong>Ketamine/esketamine</strong> -- limited evidence; rapid but transient benefit</li>
<li><strong>Repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS)</strong> -- emerging evidence, especially over right dorsolateral prefrontal cortex</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Address comorbidities:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Treat <strong>depression</strong> (SSRIs cover both)</li>
<li>Treat <strong>substance use</strong> (integrated treatment; avoid untreated addiction undermining PTSD therapy)</li>
<li>Address <strong>sleep disturbance</strong> (CBT-I, prazosin, trazodone)</li>
<li>Treat <strong>chronic pain</strong> and TBI if present</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Veterans Affairs (VA) and military populations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>VA/DoD Clinical Practice Guideline</strong> recommends trauma-focused psychotherapy over medication as first-line</li>
<li>Group-based programs (e.g., Warrior Combat Stress Reset Program)</li>
<li>Psychiatric service dogs and equine therapy (adjunctive)</li>
<li>Peer support and family therapy</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Special populations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Children and adolescents</strong> -- Trauma-Focused CBT (TF-CBT) is first-line</li>
<li><strong>Refugees</strong> -- culturally adapted CBT, narrative exposure therapy</li>
<li><strong>Complex PTSD</strong> (ICD-11) -- phase-based treatment: stabilization, trauma processing, reintegration</li>
</ul>
