<h1>Infections</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Infections represent one of the most fundamental categories of human disease, caused by invasion and multiplication of pathogenic microorganisms -- <strong>bacteria, viruses, fungi, parasites, and prions</strong>. Despite dramatic advances in vaccination, sanitation, and antimicrobial therapy, infectious diseases remain the <strong>leading cause of death worldwide</strong> in low-income countries and a major cause of morbidity in all populations.</p>
<p>The outcome of an infection depends on the dynamic interplay between <strong>pathogen virulence</strong> (toxins, adhesion factors, immune evasion mechanisms, capsule formation), <strong>inoculum size</strong>, <strong>route of entry</strong>, and <strong>host immune defenses</strong> (innate immunity: complement, phagocytes, NK cells; adaptive immunity: T-cells, B-cells/antibodies). The clinical spectrum ranges from asymptomatic colonization, through localized infection (cellulitis, pneumonia, cystitis), to systemic infection (sepsis, bacteremia) and life-threatening multi-organ failure.</p>
<p>The emergence of <strong>antimicrobial resistance (AMR)</strong> is one of the greatest threats to global health -- multidrug-resistant organisms (MRSA, VRE, CRE, MDR-TB, MDR gram-negatives) increasingly limit treatment options. <strong>Antimicrobial stewardship</strong> (judicious use of antibiotics, de-escalation, duration optimization) is now a critical priority in all healthcare settings.</p>
<h2>Clinical Presentation</h2>
<p>Infection presentation varies enormously by pathogen and site, but <strong>systemic signs of infection</strong> include:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Fever</strong> (most common; absent in elderly, immunocompromised, or septic shock)</li>
<li><strong>Chills, rigors</strong></li>
<li><strong>Tachycardia</strong> and <strong>tachypnea</strong></li>
<li><strong>Altered mental status</strong> (especially elderly; septic encephalopathy)</li>
<li><strong>Leukocytosis</strong> or leukopenia</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>qSOFA</strong> criteria for sepsis risk: respiratory rate &gt;=22, altered mentation, systolic BP &lt;=100</p>
<h2>Diagnostic Information</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>CBC with differential</strong> (leukocytosis, left shift, bandemia)</li>
<li><strong>CRP, procalcitonin</strong> (procalcitonin more specific for bacterial infection)</li>
<li><strong>Blood cultures</strong> (before antibiotic initiation if possible)</li>
<li><strong>Imaging</strong> (chest X-ray, CT, ultrasound depending on suspected site)</li>
<li><strong>Molecular diagnostics</strong> (PCR, multiplex panels, next-generation sequencing)</li>
<li><strong>Serology</strong> (antibody detection for viral, atypical bacterial, parasitic infections)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Antimicrobial therapy:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Empiric</strong> (broad-spectrum) for severe infections before culture results</li>
<li><strong>Directed</strong> (pathogen-specific) once organism and sensitivities identified</li>
<li><strong>De-escalation</strong> to narrowest effective agent</li>
<li><strong>Duration optimization</strong> (shortest effective course; typically 5-7 days for most infections)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Supportive:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Fluid resuscitation (sepsis; 30 mL/kg crystalloid)</li>
<li>Vasopressors (norepinephrine first-line for septic shock)</li>
<li>Oxygen therapy, mechanical ventilation if respiratory failure</li>
<li>Source control (abscess drainage, infected hardware removal)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Sepsis bundle (hour-1):</strong> lactate, blood cultures, broad-spectrum antibiotics, crystalloid 30 mL/kg, vasopressors if hypotensive</p>
