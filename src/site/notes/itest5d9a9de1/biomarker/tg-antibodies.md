<h1>Tg Antibodies</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Thyroglobulin antibodies (TgAb) are autoantibodies that target <strong>thyroglobulin</strong>, the large glycoprotein that thyroid follicular cells use as a scaffold for synthesizing and storing thyroid hormones (T3 and T4). The detection of TgAb in serum is a marker of autoimmune thyroid disease -- the immune system has mounted a response against thyroid tissue. TgAb frequently coexist with thyroid peroxidase antibodies (TPOAb), and the two together define the autoimmune basis of the most common thyroid disorders.</p>
<p>Elevated TgAb is strongly associated with <strong>Hashimoto's thyroiditis</strong> (chronic autoimmune hypothyroidism; ~60-80% of patients TgAb-positive) and is also seen in <strong>Graves' disease</strong> (~50-70% TgAb-positive) and <strong>postpartum thyroiditis</strong>. TgAb alone is generally considered a <strong>less sensitive and less specific marker</strong> of autoimmune thyroiditis than TPOAb (TgAb is positive in fewer patients), but it adds diagnostic value, particularly in patients who are TPOAb-negative yet clinically suspected of autoimmune disease.</p>
<p>TgAb carries special importance in <strong>differentiated thyroid cancer management</strong>. After thyroidectomy for papillary or follicular thyroid cancer, serum thyroglobulin is used as a tumor marker to detect residual or recurrent disease, but circulating TgAb can interfere with the thyroglobulin assay and produce falsely low or unreliable results. For this reason TgAb is measured alongside thyroglobulin in cancer surveillance, and a <strong>rising TgAb trend itself can serve as a surrogate marker of recurrent thyroid tissue</strong>. ATA and ETA guidelines recommend TgAb measurement with every serum thyroglobulin measurement in thyroid cancer follow-up.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level: 4/5</strong> (well-validated; ATA/ETA thyroid cancer follow-up guidelines).</p>

<p><strong>Caveats:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Reference range is assay-specific (immunoassay platforms vary)</li>
<li>TgAb positivity without TPOAb positivity is less common but does occur</li>
<li>TgAb status is generally stable over time (unlike thyroid hormone levels); serial changes are most informative in thyroid cancer follow-up</li>
<li>Pregnancy: TgAb may rise transiently in postpartum thyroiditis</li>
</ul>

<h2>Factors Affecting Levels</h2>
<p><strong>Increase TgAb:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Hashimoto's thyroiditis</strong> (chronic autoimmune hypothyroidism; ~60-80% TgAb-positive)</li>
<li><strong>Graves' disease</strong> (~50-70% TgAb-positive)</li>
<li><strong>Postpartum thyroiditis</strong> (transient autoimmune activation)</li>
<li><strong>Other autoimmune disease:</strong> type 1 diabetes, celiac disease, Addison's disease, vitiligo (polyglandular autoimmune syndromes)</li>
<li><strong>Residual thyroid tissue in thyroid cancer</strong> (TgAb can rise with recurrence)</li>
<li><strong>Excess iodine intake</strong> (kelp, contrast agents) -- may exacerbate autoimmune thyroiditis</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Decrease TgAb:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Selenium supplementation</strong> in deficient patients (some evidence for antibody reduction)</li>
<li><strong>Treatment of underlying autoimmune disease</strong></li>
<li><strong>Successful ablation/removal of thyroid tissue</strong> over time (thyroid cancer follow-up)</li>
<li><strong>Recovery from postpartum thyroiditis</strong></li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p><strong>Positive TgAb is associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Hashimoto's thyroiditis:</strong> the most common cause of hypothyroidism in iodine-sufficient regions</li>
<li><strong>Graves' disease:</strong> TgAb positivity common alongside TSI/TRAb (the diagnostic Graves marker)</li>
<li><strong>Subclinical hypothyroidism progression:</strong> positive TgAb (or TPOAb) predicts progression from subclinical to overt hypothyroidism</li>
<li><strong>Thyroid cancer follow-up interference:</strong> TgAb produces falsely low thyroglobulin results (tumor marker); a rising TgAb trend alone can signal recurrence</li>
<li><strong>Pregnancy complications:</strong> positive TgAb associated with increased risk of miscarriage, preterm birth, postpartum thyroiditis</li>
<li><strong>Other autoimmune disease:</strong> polyglandular autoimmune syndrome (T1DM, celiac, Addison's, vitiligo)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Limitations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Less sensitive than TPOAb</strong> for detecting autoimmune thyroiditis</li>
<li><strong>Assay variability</strong> across labs -- not interchangeable</li>
<li><strong>Stable over time</strong> -- single measurement often sufficient (except in thyroid cancer follow-up)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Monitoring use cases:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Autoimmune thyroid disease workup (with TPOAb, TSH, FT4)</li>
<li>Thyroid cancer follow-up (mandatory with every serum thyroglobulin; q6-12 months)</li>
<li>Subclinical hypothyroidism monitoring (predicts progression)</li>
<li>Pregnancy autoimmune thyroid screening</li>
</ul>
<h2>Strategies to Manage</h2>
<p>TgAb reflects underlying autoimmunity; management focuses on supporting immune balance and thyroid function:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Selenium</strong> 200 mcg daily in selenium-deficient patients with Hashimoto's -- meta-analyses show modest TgAb and TPOAb reduction; supports thyroid antioxidant defenses (glutathione peroxidase)</li>
<li><strong>Vitamin D</strong> 2000-5000 IU D3 daily (target 25-OH-D 40-60 ng/mL) -- observational evidence for lower thyroid antibody titers with adequate vitamin D</li>
<li><strong>Myo-inositol</strong> 600 mg + selenium 83 mcg daily -- small RCTs show antibody reduction in Hashimoto's</li>
<li><strong>Gluten-free diet</strong> in patients with celiac disease (frequent comorbidity with Hashimoto's)</li>
<li><strong>Anti-inflammatory diet</strong> (Mediterranean; omega-3; antioxidants)</li>
<li><strong>Levothyroxine</strong> when hypothyroidism develops (does not reduce antibody titers but treats the consequence)</li>
<li><strong>Avoid excessive iodine</strong> (kelp, contrast agents) which can exacerbate autoimmune thyroiditis</li>
<li><strong>Address pregnancy-related thyroid autoimmunity:</strong> monthly TSH monitoring during pregnancy in TgAb-positive women; levothyroxine if TSH &gt;2.5 (1st trimester) or &gt;3.0 (2nd/3rd)</li>
<li><strong>Thyroid cancer follow-up:</strong> monitor TgAb trend q6-12 months alongside serum thyroglobulin</li>
<li><strong>Stress management</strong> -- chronic stress may exacerbate autoimmune activity</li>
</ol>
