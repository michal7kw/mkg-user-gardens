<h1>Thymus</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Bilobed lymphoid organ situated in the upper anterior mediastinum, posterior to the sternum, where T-lymphocyte precursors from bone marrow undergo positive and negative selection to produce a self-tolerant, pathogen-reactive T-cell repertoire. Most active in childhood and adolescence; undergoes age-related involution after puberty, with most thymic tissue replaced by adipose by middle age. Critical for adaptive immunity and central tolerance.</p>
<h2>Function</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>T-cell maturation</strong> — thymocytes (immature T-cells) migrate from bone marrow to the thymic cortex, undergo TCR rearrangement, then positive selection (must recognize self-MHC) and negative selection (must not react too strongly to self-antigens). Survivors emigrate as naïve CD4+ or CD8+ T-cells.</li>
<li><strong>Central tolerance</strong> — medullary thymic epithelial cells (mTECs) express the autoimmune regulator (AIRE) to present a wide array of tissue-specific antigens, eliminating autoreactive clones</li>
<li><strong>Endocrine secretion</strong> — thymulin, thymopoietin, thymosin — small peptides influencing T-cell development and broader immune signaling</li>
<li><strong>Zinc-dependent activity</strong> — thymulin requires zinc as a cofactor; thymic function is impaired in zinc deficiency</li>
</ul>
<h2>Pathology</h2>
<p>DiGeorge syndrome (thymic hypoplasia/aplasia), thymoma, thymic carcinoma, myasthenia gravis (paraneoplastic thymic involvement), severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID).</p>
