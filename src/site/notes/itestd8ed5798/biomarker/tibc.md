<h1>TIBC</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Total iron-binding capacity (TIBC) is an indirect measurement of serum transferrin, the principal iron transport protein synthesized by the liver. Transferrin is a beta-globulin that reversibly binds two atoms of ferric iron (Fe3+) per molecule, transporting iron from the gut (absorption site) and reticuloendothelial stores (recycling) to the bone marrow (erythropoiesis) and other tissues. Each transferrin molecule has two iron-binding sites; TIBC measures the total iron-binding capacity of all transferrin in serum.</p>
<p>The clinical utility of TIBC derives from its <strong>inverse relationship with iron stores</strong>: when body iron stores are depleted, the liver up-regulates transferrin synthesis to maximize absorption from the diet; TIBC rises. Conversely, in iron overload (hemochromatosis) or when iron is already saturated, the liver down-regulates transferrin synthesis; TIBC falls. In <strong>chronic inflammation</strong>, interleukin-6 (IL-6) drives hepatic hepcidin synthesis which sequesters iron in macrophages and reduces transferrin production; TIBC falls (a key distinguishing feature from true iron deficiency, where TIBC rises).</p>
<p>The classic iron panel comprises <strong>serum iron</strong> (transient measure of current iron bound to transferrin), <strong>TIBC</strong> (transferrin concentration surrogate), <strong>transferrin saturation</strong> (serum iron / TIBC × 100; the most clinically useful single marker), and <strong>ferritin</strong> (storage iron). Together these differentiate:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Iron deficiency anemia:</strong> low iron, HIGH TIBC, low transferrin saturation (&lt;20%), low ferritin</li>
<li><strong>Anemia of chronic disease:</strong> low iron, LOW/normal TIBC, low-normal transferrin saturation, NORMAL/high ferritin</li>
<li><strong>Hemochromatosis / iron overload:</strong> HIGH iron, LOW TIBC, very HIGH transferrin saturation (&gt;45% women, &gt;50% men at fasting), HIGH ferritin</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Evidence Level: 5/5</strong> (foundational hematology test; universal clinical use).</p>

<p><strong>Caveats:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Fasting morning sample preferred (iron has diurnal variation; TIBC more stable but transferrin mildly affected by recent meal)</li>
<li>Hemolysis in vitro falsely lowers TIBC</li>
<li>Oral contraceptives and estrogen therapy raise TIBC (transferrin up-regulated by estrogen)</li>
<li>Pregnancy raises TIBC (estrogen + increased iron demand)</li>
<li>Inter-assay variability across labs; use lab-specific reference range</li>
</ul>

<h2>Factors Affecting Levels</h2>
<p><strong>Increase TIBC (iron deficiency state):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Iron deficiency anemia</strong> (most common cause; hepatic transferrin up-regulation)</li>
<li>Acute or chronic blood loss</li>
<li><strong>Pregnancy</strong> (estrogen-driven; physiologic)</li>
<li><strong>Oral contraceptives / estrogen therapy</strong> (estrogen raises transferrin)</li>
<li>Late-stage liver disease (occasionally; impaired synthesis regulation)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Decrease TIBC:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Anemia of chronic disease / chronic inflammation</strong> (IL-6 → hepcidin → reduced transferrin synthesis)</li>
<li><strong>Iron overload / hereditary hemochromatosis</strong> (down-regulated transferrin synthesis)</li>
<li><strong>Malnutrition, protein deficiency</strong> (transferrin is a negative acute-phase reactant and a visceral protein)</li>
<li><strong>Nephrotic syndrome</strong> (urinary loss of transferrin)</li>
<li><strong>Chronic liver disease / cirrhosis</strong> (impaired hepatic synthesis)</li>
<li><strong>Malignancy</strong> (chronic inflammation)</li>
<li><strong>Hemodialysis</strong> patients</li>
<li><strong>Corticosteroid therapy</strong> (reduces transferrin)</li>
<li><strong>Sickle cell disease</strong> (chronic inflammation)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p><strong>Elevated TIBC is associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Iron deficiency anemia</strong> (the principal clinical use): low serum iron + HIGH TIBC + low transferrin saturation + low ferritin is the classic tetrad</li>
<li><strong>Pregnancy:</strong> physiologic elevation; ensure adequate iron supplementation</li>
<li><strong>Estrogen therapy / OCPs:</strong> physiologic; no pathology</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Low TIBC is associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Anemia of chronic disease:</strong> low serum iron + LOW/normal TIBC + low transferrin saturation + NORMAL/high ferritin (distinguishes from iron deficiency where TIBC is high and ferritin is low)</li>
<li><strong>Iron overload (hemochromatosis):</strong> HIGH serum iron + LOW TIBC + HIGH transferrin saturation + HIGH ferritin</li>
<li><strong>Protein-losing states:</strong> nephrotic syndrome, severe liver disease, malnutrition</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Limitations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Non-specific alone</strong> -- always interpret with serum iron, transferrin saturation, ferritin, and CBC</li>
<li><strong>Transferrin saturation</strong> (serum iron / TIBC × 100) is more clinically useful than TIBC alone for distinguishing iron deficiency from iron overload</li>
<li><strong>Acute inflammation can normalize or lower TIBC</strong> despite concurrent iron deficiency (mixed picture; soluble transferrin receptor may help)</li>
<li><strong>Diurnal variation</strong> modest; standardize morning fasting collection</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Monitoring use cases:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Anemia workup (with serum iron, transferrin saturation, ferritin, CBC)</li>
<li>Hemochromatosis screening (with serum iron, transferrin saturation, ferritin, genetic testing)</li>
<li>Iron supplementation monitoring (target: TIBC normalizes as stores replete)</li>
<li>Pregnancy iron status assessment</li>
<li>Chronic kidney disease anemia workup</li>
</ul>
<h2>Strategies to Manage</h2>
<p>TIBC reflects underlying iron metabolism; management targets the cause:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>For elevated TIBC (iron deficiency):</strong>
<ul>
<li>Oral iron supplementation: ferrous sulfate 325 mg TID (65 mg elemental iron TID) on empty stomach; alternate-day dosing improves absorption (hepcidin-sparing)</li>
<li>Vitamin C 200-500 mg with iron enhances absorption</li>
<li>Heme-iron dietary emphasis: red meat, organ meats, shellfish (oysters, clams)</li>
<li>Cast-iron cookware (acidic foods absorb iron)</li>
<li>Avoid calcium, coffee, tea, bran at the same meal as iron</li>
<li>Investigate and treat underlying cause: celiac disease, H. pylori, menorrhagia, GI bleeding</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>For low TIBC (chronic inflammation / iron overload):</strong>
<ul>
<li>Treat underlying inflammation: RA (DMARDs), SLE (immunomodulation), chronic infection</li>
<li>For hemochromatosis: phlebotomy (target ferritin &lt;50 ng/mL); iron chelation (deferoxamine, deferasirox) for non-phlebotomy candidates</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Curcumin</strong> 500-1000 mg daily -- modest anti-inflammatory effect; may help normalize TIBC in chronic inflammation</li>
<li><strong>For nephrotic syndrome:</strong> treat underlying glomerular disease; ACE inhibitors/ARBs reduce proteinuria</li>
<li><strong>Treat malnutrition / protein deficiency:</strong> nutritional rehabilitation with high biological-value protein</li>
<li><strong>Avoid unnecessary iron supplementation</strong> when TIBC is low (iron overload risk)</li>
</ol>
