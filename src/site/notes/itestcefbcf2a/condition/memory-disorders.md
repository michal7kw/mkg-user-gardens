<h1>Memory Disorders</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Memory disorders span a spectrum from <strong>normal age-related memory change</strong> (&quot;benign senescent forgetfulness&quot;) through <strong>mild cognitive impairment (MCI)</strong> to <strong>dementia</strong>. The clinical distinction rests on functional impact: MCI produces objective cognitive decline (typically 1-1.5 SD below age-matched norms on testing) <strong>without</strong> loss of independence in daily activities, while dementia impairs <strong>both cognition and function</strong>. <strong>Subjective cognitive decline</strong> -- self-perceived worsening without objective deficit -- is an emerging concept identifying individuals at higher risk of progression.</p>
<p>The dementia syndromes have distinct signatures. <strong>Alzheimer disease (AD)</strong> -- the most common (~60-70% of cases) -- begins with <strong>episodic memory impairment</strong> (hippocampal involvement), then language, visuospatial, and executive dysfunction. Pathologically, AD is defined by <strong>extracellular amyloid-beta plaques</strong> and <strong>intraneuronal tau neurofibrillary tangles</strong>, with the tau burden correlating best with cognitive decline. <strong>Vascular dementia</strong> (second most common) follows a stepwise course tied to stroke or small-vessel disease; executive dysfunction dominates. <strong>Dementia with Lewy bodies</strong> features fluctuating cognition, visual hallucinations, parkinsonism, and REM sleep behavior disorder. <strong>Frontotemporal dementia</strong> presents with personality change, behavioral disinhibition, or language deficits (primary progressive aphasia) with relative sparing of memory early on.</p>
<p>Importantly, <strong>not all memory disorders are irreversible</strong>. The dementia workup must exclude the <strong>treatable causes</strong> -- <strong>vitamin B12 deficiency, hypothyroidism, neurosyphilis, depression (pseudodementia), medication effects (anticholinergics, benzodiazepines), normal-pressure hydrocephalus, subdural hematoma, sleep apnea, and infection</strong>. These account for up to 10-20% of dementia referrals. The <strong>Lancet Commission</strong> (2020, updated 2024) identified <strong>12 modifiable risk factors</strong> accounting for ~40% of dementia risk worldwide: low education, hypertension, hearing loss, smoking, obesity, depression, physical inactivity, diabetes, excessive alcohol, traumatic brain injury, air pollution, and social isolation -- underscoring that dementia is substantially preventable through midlife vascular health and lifelong cognitive engagement.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Diagnostic workup:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>History and collateral</strong> from family/caregiver (subtle decline often missed on self-report)</li>
<li><strong>Cognitive testing</strong> -- MoCA (Montreal Cognitive Assessment; sensitivity ~90% for MCI; cutoff &lt;26), MMSE, or neuropsychological testing for ambiguous cases</li>
<li><strong>Laboratory</strong> -- CBC, TSH, B12 with methylmalonic acid, comprehensive metabolic panel, syphilis (RPR/VDRL), HIV if indicated</li>
<li><strong>Structural imaging</strong> -- MRI brain (rule out mass, hydrocephalus, infarcts; hippocampal atrophy supports AD)</li>
<li><strong>Biomarkers</strong> -- CSF amyloid-beta/tau or amyloid PET in selected cases (required before anti-amyloid therapy)</li>
<li><strong>Blood-based biomarkers</strong> -- emerging (p-tau217); increasingly available 2024+</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Treat reversible causes first:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>B12 deficiency</strong> -- replacement (1000 mcg IM daily x1 week, then weekly x4, then monthly; or high-dose oral 1000-2000 mcg/day)</li>
<li><strong>Hypothyroidism</strong> -- levothyroxine</li>
<li><strong>Depression</strong> -- SSRI/SNRI; depression often mimics dementia (&quot;pseudodementia&quot;)</li>
<li><strong>Medications</strong> -- deprescribe anticholinergics, benzodiazepines, opioids</li>
<li><strong>Sleep apnea</strong> -- CPAP</li>
<li><strong>Normal-pressure hydrocephalus</strong> -- ventriculoperitoneal shunt in selected cases</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Disease-modifying therapy for Alzheimer disease (new era):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Lecanemab</strong> (Leqembi; anti-amyloid monoclonal) -- FDA-approved 2023; slowed decline by 27% over 18 months in CLARITY-AD. Requires amyloid positivity, APOE testing (ARIA risk in epsilon4 carriers), baseline and periodic MRI monitoring</li>
<li><strong>Donanemab</strong> (Kisunla; anti-amyloid) -- FDA-approved 2024; cleared amyloid plaques and slowed decline ~35%. Same ARIA monitoring</li>
<li><strong>Aducanumab</strong> (Aduhelm) -- withdrawn from market 2024; superseded</li>
<li>Candidate selection: early AD (MCI or mild dementia), amyloid-positive, no contraindicating anticoagulation</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Symptomatic therapy:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Cholinesterase inhibitors</strong> (donepezil 5-10 mg, rivastigmine patch, galantamine) -- first-line for mild-moderate AD, Lewy body dementia, Parkinson dementia; modest benefit (~6 months delay in decline)</li>
<li><strong>Memantine</strong> (5-20 mg; NMDA antagonist) -- for moderate-severe AD; can combine with cholinesterase inhibitor</li>
<li><strong>Anti-amyloid therapy + cholinesterase inhibitor</strong> is emerging as the combined approach</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Lifestyle and preventive interventions:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Physical exercise</strong> -- 150 min/week moderate aerobic; strongest single modifiable factor for cognitive preservation; increases hippocampal volume and BDNF</li>
<li><strong>Mediterranean or MIND diet</strong> -- associated with slower cognitive decline and lower AD risk</li>
<li><strong>Cognitive stimulation and social engagement</strong> -- mental activity, lifelong learning</li>
<li><strong>Vascular risk factor control</strong> -- BP &lt;130/80 (SPRINT-MIND), glycemic control, statin, smoking cessation</li>
<li><strong>Hearing aids</strong> in hearing loss (ACHIEVE trial showed reduced cognitive decline)</li>
<li><strong>Sleep optimization</strong> -- 7-8 hours; treat sleep apnea (clearance of amyloid during slow-wave sleep)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Behavioral and psychological symptoms of dementia (BPSD):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Non-pharmacologic first</strong> -- structured routine, environmental modification, address pain and unmet needs</li>
<li><strong>Depression</strong> -- SSRIs (citalopram, sertraline); avoid TCAs (anticholinergic)</li>
<li><strong>Agitation</strong> -- consider <strong>brexpiprazole</strong> (FDA-approved 2023 for agitation in AD), citalopram (CitAD trial); antipsychotics have black-box warning (increased mortality) and are last resort</li>
<li><strong>Sleep disturbance</strong> -- treat underlying causes; trazodone, melatonin</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Caregiver support and planning:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Education, support groups, respite care (caregiver burnout predicts institutionalization)</li>
<li>Advance care planning early (power of attorney, advance directives, goals of care)</li>
<li>Safety evaluation (driving, medication management, fall risk, wandering)</li>
</ul>
