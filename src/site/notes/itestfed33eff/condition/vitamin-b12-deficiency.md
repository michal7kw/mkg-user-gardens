<h1>Vitamin B12 Deficiency</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Vitamin B12 (cobalamin) deficiency is a common and treatable cause of anemia and neurological disease. B12 is an essential cofactor for two reactions: methionine synthase, which converts homocysteine to methionine and is required for DNA synthesis and methylation, and methylmalonyl-CoA mutase, which converts methylmalonyl-CoA to succinyl-CoA. Impairment of the first reaction produces megaloblastic, macrocytic anemia (shared with folate deficiency), while accumulation of substrates underlies the characteristic neurological injury.</p>
<p>Because the body stores several years' worth of B12 in the liver, deficiency usually develops slowly. Absorption is complex, requiring gastric acid, intrinsic factor produced by gastric parietal cells, and an intact terminal ileum — so the most common causes are malabsorptive: pernicious anemia (autoimmune destruction of parietal cells), atrophic gastritis, ileal disease, and drugs that reduce gastric acid or absorption (proton-pump inhibitors, long-term metformin). Dietary deficiency occurs in strict vegans, as B12 is found almost exclusively in animal foods.</p>
<h2>Diagnosis</h2>
<p>Low serum B12 supports the diagnosis but is imperfect; elevated methylmalonic acid and homocysteine are more sensitive functional markers and rise before overt anemia. The blood film may show macro-ovalocytes and hypersegmented neutrophils. Critically, the neurological manifestations — peripheral neuropathy and subacute combined degeneration of the dorsal columns and corticospinal tracts — can occur without anemia and may become irreversible if untreated, so a high index of suspicion is warranted.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p>Treatment is replacement with cobalamin, parenterally (intramuscular) when malabsorption is the cause or neurological features are present, or with high-dose oral supplementation in dietary or mild cases. Underlying causes (e.g., pernicious anemia) require ongoing maintenance therapy. Folate should not be given alone in suspected B12 deficiency, as it can correct the anemia while allowing neurological damage to progress.</p>
