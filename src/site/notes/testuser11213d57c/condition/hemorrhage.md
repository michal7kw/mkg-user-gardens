<h1>Hemorrhage</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Hemorrhage is the escape of blood from a ruptured blood vessel. It is classified by location (external vs internal), source (arterial, venous, capillary), and severity (Class I-IV based on blood volume lost). Hemorrhage is the leading cause of <strong>preventable death</strong> after trauma and a major complication of anticoagulant therapy, surgery, and bleeding disorders.</p>
<p><strong>Classification by severity (ATLS):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Class I:</strong> &lt;15% blood volume loss (&lt;750 mL); minimal physiological change</li>
<li><strong>Class II:</strong> 15-30% loss (750-1500 mL); tachycardia, narrowed pulse pressure, anxiety</li>
<li><strong>Class III:</strong> 30-40% loss (1500-2000 mL); hypotension, tachycardia, altered mental status</li>
<li><strong>Class IV:</strong> &gt;40% loss (&gt;2000 mL); lethargy, severe hypotension, rapid thready pulse; life-threatening</li>
</ul>
<p>Major hemorrhage activates the <strong>trauma-induced coagulopathy (TIC)</strong> cascade: tissue injury + shock + acidosis + hypothermia create a vicious cycle of worsening bleeding (the &quot;lethal triad&quot;). Early recognition and <strong>damage control resuscitation</strong> -- permissive hypotension, hemostatic resuscitation (high ratio of plasma and platelets to RBCs), and rapid surgical hemostasis -- has transformed trauma mortality.</p>
<p>Non-traumatic hemorrhage includes gastrointestinal bleeding (upper: variceal, peptic ulcer; lower: diverticular, angiodysplasia), intracranial hemorrhage (subdural, epidural, subarachnoid, intraparenchymal), and spontaneous bleeding from anticoagulant therapy.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Immediate:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>ABC</strong>: secure Airway, ensure Breathing, maintain Circulation</li>
<li><strong>Direct pressure</strong> on external bleeding sites</li>
<li><strong>Two large-bore IVs</strong> (14-16 gauge)</li>
<li><strong>Crystalloid</strong> (1-2 L warmed normal saline or lactated Ringer's)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Massive transfusion protocol:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Activate when &gt;10 units PRBC expected</li>
<li><strong>1:1:1 ratio</strong> (PRBC : FFP : platelets) per PROPPR trial</li>
<li><strong>Tranexamic acid</strong> 1 g IV bolus then 1 g infusion over 8 h (within 3 h of injury; CRASH-2)</li>
<li><strong>Calcium gluconate</strong> (citrate toxicity from transfusion causes hypocalcemia)</li>
<li>Keep <strong>INR &lt;1.5</strong>, <strong>platelets &gt;50K</strong>, <strong>fibrinogen &gt;1.5-2.0 g/L</strong></li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Anticoagulant reversal:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Warfarin:</strong> Vitamin K 10 mg IV + 4-factor PCC (Kcentra 25-50 U/kg)</li>
<li><strong>Dabigatran:</strong> Idarucizumab 5 g IV</li>
<li><strong>Apixaban/rivaroxaban:</strong> Andexanet alfa (low or high dose based on drug/dose/timing)</li>
<li><strong>Heparin:</strong> Protamine sulfate (1 mg per 100 units heparin)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Definitive hemostasis:</strong> surgical ligation, angiographic embolization, endoscopic therapy (GI bleed)</p>
