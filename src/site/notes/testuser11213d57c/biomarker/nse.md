<h1>NSE</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Neuron-specific enolase (NSE; also called enolase 2, ENO2, or gamma-enolase) is the neuron- and neuroendocrine-cell isoform of the glycolytic enzyme enolase, which catalyzes the conversion of 2-phosphoglycerate to phosphoenolpyruvate. The active dimer is a gamma-gamma homodimer encoded by the ENO2 gene on chromosome 12p13. NSE is highly expressed in neurons, neuroendocrine cells (pineal, pituitary, parathyroid, pancreatic islet, thyroid C cells), and their derived tumors. It is the most widely used serum and CSF biomarker for both neuronal injury and neuroendocrine tumors.</p>
<p>Clinically, NSE has two principal indications. As a tumor marker, NSE is the gold-standard serum marker for small cell lung cancer (SCLC), used for diagnosis, prognostic stratification, and treatment monitoring -- elevated in ~70-80% of SCLC patients at diagnosis (higher with extensive disease). It is also elevated in neuroblastoma (alongside urinary HMMA/VMA), Merkel cell carcinoma, and other neuroendocrine tumors. As a neuronal injury biomarker, NSE in serum and CSF correlates with injury severity and prognosis after stroke, traumatic brain injury, and cardiac arrest (post-ROSC neurological prognosis). However, NSE suffers from a major pre-analytical limitation: erythrocytes also contain NSE, so even mild hemolysis produces false-positive elevations.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level: 4/5</strong> (well-validated NCCN-endorsed SCLC marker; established neuro-prognostic use).</p>

<p><strong>Caveats:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Hemolysis falsely elevates NSE (erythrocyte NSE is 100x serum); reject hemolyzed samples</li>
<li>Reference range is assay-dependent (Roche Elecsys, Abbott Architect, Diasorin LIAISON)</li>
<li>Renal failure impairs clearance and elevates baseline</li>
<li>Sampling timing in SCLC: trend over time more useful than single value</li>
</ul>

<h2>Factors Affecting Levels</h2>
<p><strong>Increase NSE:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Small cell lung cancer (SCLC) -- the canonical tumor marker; &gt;70% sensitivity in extensive-stage SCLC</li>
<li>Neuroblastoma (especially high-risk)</li>
<li>Other neuroendocrine tumors: Merkel cell carcinoma, pancreatic NET (insulinoma, gastrinoma), pheochromocytoma, carcinoid, medullary thyroid carcinoma</li>
<li>Neuronal injury: ischemic stroke, severe TBI, post-cardiac arrest encephalopathy, status epilepticus</li>
<li>Hemolysis (false positive -- critical pre-analytical issue)</li>
<li>Renal failure (impaired clearance)</li>
<li>Prostatic tissue (NSE-positive); rare false positives in prostatic disease</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Decrease NSE:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Treatment response in SCLC (kinetics over weeks of chemotherapy)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p><strong>Elevated levels are associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Small cell lung cancer (SCLC):</strong> sensitivity ~70-80% in extensive-stage, ~40% in limited-stage; NCCN-recommended for diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment monitoring. Combination with ProGRP improves sensitivity</li>
<li><strong>Neuroblastoma:</strong> elevated in high-risk disease; used alongside urinary HMMA/VMA in pediatric abdominal mass workup</li>
<li><strong>Merkel cell carcinoma:</strong> elevated in advanced disease; useful prognostic marker</li>
<li><strong>Other neuroendocrine tumors:</strong> variable elevation; chromogranin A is often more sensitive for carcinoid/pNET</li>
<li><strong>Ischemic stroke:</strong> NSE peaks at 24-72 h; correlates with infarct volume and functional outcome</li>
<li><strong>Severe TBI:</strong> serum NSE correlates with GCS, CT findings, and 6-month outcome</li>
<li><strong>Post-cardiac arrest:</strong> NSE at 48-72 h is one of the strongest predictors of poor neurological outcome (alongside SSEP N20 and brain MRI); current ERC/ESICM guidelines include NSE &gt;60 mcg/L as predictor of poor outcome</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Limitations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Hemolysis false positives:</strong> critical pre-analytical issue; reject any visible hemolysis</li>
<li><strong>Lower brain-specificity than GFAP or UCH-L1</strong> (NSE is in erythrocytes and platelets)</li>
<li><strong>Cannot distinguish SCLC from other neuroendocrine tumors</strong> in isolation</li>
<li><strong>Not a screening marker</strong> -- used for diagnosis/monitoring in patients with known/suspected NET</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Monitoring use cases:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>SCLC diagnosis, staging, and treatment response (every 3 months in advanced disease)</li>
<li>Neuroblastoma workup</li>
<li>Post-cardiac arrest prognostication (single sample at 48-72 h)</li>
<li>Stroke outcome prediction (research)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Strategies to Manage</h2>
<p>NSE reflects underlying tissue state rather than a modifiable physiologic level. Management focuses on the underlying cause:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>SCLC:</strong> oncology referral for staging (PET-CT, brain MRI), treatment (platinum-etoposide + immunotherapy -- atezolizumab/durvalumab), prophylactic cranial irradiation in responders. Recheck NSE q6-9 weeks during treatment</li>
<li><strong>Neuroblastoma:</strong> pediatric oncology referral for risk-stratified therapy (low/intermediate/high risk protocols)</li>
<li><strong>Merkel cell carcinoma:</strong> surgical excision + sentinel node biopsy; consider avelumab (anti-PD-L1) for advanced disease</li>
<li><strong>Stroke / TBI:</strong> standard neurocritical care (revascularization for stroke; ICP/CPP management for severe TBI)</li>
<li><strong>Hemolysis false-positive workup:</strong> check free hemoglobin, reticulocyte count, haptoglobin; if confirmed, redraw carefully and retest</li>
<li><strong>Renal failure adjustment:</strong> interpret NSE in context of eGFR; cutoffs may need upward adjustment</li>
</ol>
