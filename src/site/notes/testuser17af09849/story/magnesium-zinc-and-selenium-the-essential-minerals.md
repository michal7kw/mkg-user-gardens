<h1>Magnesium, Zinc and Selenium — the Essential Minerals</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Three trace minerals do an outsized amount of quiet work: <strong>magnesium</strong> is a cofactor for over 300 enzymes (including every reaction that uses ATP), <strong>zinc</strong> is structural and catalytic in hundreds more (immunity, testosterone, taste, wound healing), and <strong>selenium</strong> sits at the active site of the antioxidant and thyroid-hormone-activating enzymes. All three are common, easy-to-miss deficiencies — and each has a matching biomarker: <a href="/garden/testuser17af09849/biomarker/rbc-magnesium/" class="internal-link">RBC Magnesium</a>, <a href="/garden/testuser17af09849/supplement/zinc/" class="internal-link">Zinc</a>, and <a href="/garden/testuser17af09849/supplement/selenium/" class="internal-link">Selenium</a>.</p>
<h2>The Story</h2>
<p><strong>Magnesium</strong> is the classic &quot;silent&quot; deficiency. Serum magnesium is tightly defended and often looks normal even when tissues are depleted, because the body pulls magnesium from bone and cells to keep blood levels steady. That is why <a href="/garden/testuser17af09849/biomarker/rbc-magnesium/" class="internal-link">RBC Magnesium</a> — measured inside red cells — is a better window on true status. Low magnesium tracks with insulin resistance, higher blood pressure, poor sleep, and higher <a href="/garden/testuser17af09849/biomarker/hs-crp/" class="internal-link">hs-CRP</a>, and repletion is one of the better-tolerated micronutrient interventions (PMID: 29387426).</p>
<p><strong>Zinc</strong> sits at the crossroads of immunity and male hormones. It is required for normal immune cell function, for testosterone production, and even for the sense of taste and smell — early clues to deficiency. Because the body has no large zinc store, intake matters continuously, and deficiency is surprisingly common worldwide, especially on plant-heavy diets where phytates bind zinc (PMID: 23209782). Zinc status is read with serum <a href="/garden/testuser17af09849/supplement/zinc/" class="internal-link">Zinc</a>, interpreted alongside intake and inflammation.</p>
<p><strong>Selenium</strong> is the one most tied to the thyroid. Selenium-dependent enzymes (glutathione peroxidases and the deiodinases) both defend against oxidative stress and <strong>activate thyroid hormone</strong> by converting T4 to the active T3. Adequate selenium supports thyroid function and may reduce thyroid autoantibodies, which is why it appears in <a href="/garden/testuser17af09849/story/thyroid-function-explained/" class="internal-link">Thyroid Function Explained</a> as well (PMID: 22381456). But selenium has a narrow window — too much is toxic — so it is a &quot;replete-a-deficiency, don't megadose&quot; nutrient.</p>
<h2>What This Means For You</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Test the right marker.</strong> For magnesium, prefer <a href="/garden/testuser17af09849/biomarker/rbc-magnesium/" class="internal-link">RBC Magnesium</a> over serum — serum can look normal while cells are depleted. For zinc and selenium, serum levels read against intake and inflammation.</li>
<li><strong>What lowers them:</strong> restrictive or highly processed diets, high phytate intake (zinc), GI malabsorption, heavy alcohol use (magnesium), and diuretics or PPIs (magnesium).</li>
<li><strong>Repletion:</strong> <a href="/garden/testuser17af09849/supplement/magnesium/" class="internal-link">Magnesium</a> (glycinate/citrate are well absorbed; add <a href="/garden/testuser17af09849/supplement/vitamin-b6/" class="internal-link">Vitamin B6</a> for cellular uptake); <a href="/garden/testuser17af09849/supplement/zinc/" class="internal-link">Zinc</a> (short courses, balanced with copper on long-term use); <a href="/garden/testuser17af09849/supplement/selenium/" class="internal-link">Selenium</a> to correct deficiency only — its therapeutic window is narrow. Antioxidant partners <a href="/garden/testuser17af09849/supplement/vitamin-e/" class="internal-link">Vitamin E</a> and <a href="/garden/testuser17af09849/supplement/nac/" class="internal-link">NAC</a> work alongside selenium.</li>
<li><strong>Watch the ceilings.</strong> Zinc and selenium are toxic in excess — replete documented deficiencies rather than megadosing.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Key Takeaways</h2>
<ul>
<li>Magnesium, zinc, and selenium run hundreds of enzymes across energy, immunity, hormones, and thyroid.</li>
<li>RBC magnesium beats serum magnesium — blood levels stay normal while cells deplete.</li>
<li>Zinc underpins immunity and testosterone; deficiency is common on plant-heavy, high-phytate diets.</li>
<li>Selenium activates thyroid hormone and supports antioxidant defense but has a narrow safe window.</li>
<li>Replete documented deficiencies; zinc and selenium are toxic in excess.</li>
</ul>
