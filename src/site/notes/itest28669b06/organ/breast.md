<h1>Breast</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>The breast is a paired organ on the anterior chest wall, overlying the pectoralis major muscle and extending from roughly the second to the sixth rib. It is a modified apocrine sweat (mammary) gland whose defining biological role is the production and delivery of milk during lactation. Each breast is organized into 15–20 lobes, each containing lobules of milk-secreting alveoli that drain through a branching ductal tree to the nipple, all embedded in a supporting matrix of fibrous (Cooper's ligaments) and adipose tissue.</p>
<p>Breast tissue is exquisitely hormone-responsive: estrogen drives ductal growth while progesterone promotes lobuloalveolar development, and prolactin and oxytocin govern milk synthesis and ejection. The gland undergoes dramatic remodeling across puberty, the menstrual cycle, pregnancy, lactation, and involution after menopause. This lifelong proliferative activity, under hormonal control, underlies both its normal function and its susceptibility to neoplasia.</p>
<h2>Key Functions</h2>
<ul>
<li>Synthesis and secretion of milk (lactation) to nourish the infant</li>
<li>Hormone-responsive growth and remodeling under estrogen, progesterone, prolactin, and oxytocin</li>
<li>Milk ejection via oxytocin-driven contraction of myoepithelial cells</li>
<li>Secondary sexual characteristic and role in thermoregulation and signaling</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>The breast is the most common site of cancer in women worldwide. <strong>Breast Neoplasms</strong> range from benign fibroadenomas and cysts to invasive ductal and lobular carcinomas, with risk modulated by age, reproductive and hormonal history, family history, and genetic factors (e.g., BRCA1/BRCA2 mutations). Population screening with <strong>Mammography</strong> reduces breast-cancer mortality through early detection, and supplemental ultrasound or MRI is used in women with dense breasts or elevated risk. Benign conditions such as mastitis, fibrocystic change, and gynecomastia (in males) are also common.</p>
