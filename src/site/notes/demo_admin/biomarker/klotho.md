<h1>Klotho</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Klotho (specifically alpha-Klotho) is a protein first identified as an &quot;aging-suppressor&quot;: loss-of-function in mice produces a syndrome resembling accelerated human aging, while overexpression extends lifespan. Beyond its membrane-bound role as an obligate co-receptor for FGF23 in phosphate homeostasis, a <strong>soluble form is shed into the circulation</strong> and can be measured as a biomarker. This circulating Klotho is the focus of aging research because higher levels track with longevity, cognitive resilience, and cardiovascular and renal health.</p>
<p>Klotho declines with age and is further reduced in chronic kidney disease, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease — positioning low Klotho both as a marker and a potential mediator of age-related decline. It also modulates insulin/IGF-1 signaling, linking it to metabolic aging.</p>

<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>Low circulating Klotho is associated with <a href="/garden/demo_admin/condition/mild-cognitive-impairment/" class="internal-link">Mild Cognitive Impairment</a> and <a href="/garden/demo_admin/condition/cognitive-dysfunction/" class="internal-link">Cognitive Dysfunction</a>, reduced kidney function, vascular calcification, and <a href="/garden/demo_admin/condition/insulin-resistance/" class="internal-link">Insulin Resistance</a>. Higher levels are linked to better executive function and longevity. As an aging biomarker, Klotho is frequently considered alongside <a href="/garden/demo_admin/biomarker/gdf15/" class="internal-link">GDF15</a> (which rises with stress/aging) to characterize biological age.</p>
