<h1>Bergamot</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Bergamot (<em>Citrus bergamia</em>) is a citrus fruit grown almost exclusively in the Calabria region of southern Italy. Its juice and albedo are unusually rich in polyphenols, and standardized <strong>Bergamot Polyphenolic Fraction (BPF)</strong> extracts are used as a natural lipid-lowering supplement. The defining feature is the presence of <strong>brutieridin</strong> and <strong>melitidin</strong>, two flavanone glycosides bearing an HMG side chain that confers partial, statin-like inhibition of HMG-CoA reductase — a mechanism no other common dietary citrus shares.</p>
<p>Randomized and observational studies report reductions in total cholesterol, LDL-C, ApoB, and triglycerides with concurrent increases in HDL-C, plus improvements in fasting glucose and markers of oxidative stress, at doses of 500-1000 mg BPF/day over 4-12 weeks. Bergamot is a reasonable evidence-graded lever for elevated LDL/ApoB in users who are not yet candidates for (or prefer to defer) statin therapy, and it is sometimes combined with statins for additive effect. Very high ApoB/LDL remains a statin conversation.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>Bergamot's flavonoids brutieridin and melitidin contain a 3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl (HMG) moiety structurally analogous to statins, producing partial competitive inhibition of HMG-CoA reductase — the rate-limiting step of hepatic cholesterol synthesis. The broader polyphenolic fraction (neoeriocitrin, neohesperidin, naringin) additionally reduces intestinal cholesterol absorption, inhibits cholesterol esterification (ACAT), activates AMPK to improve insulin sensitivity and reduce hepatic lipogenesis, and exerts antioxidant effects that lower oxidized-LDL burden. The net effect is lower ApoB-containing atherogenic particle number, lower triglycerides, and modestly higher HDL-C.</p>
<h2>Dosage</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Standardized BPF:</strong> 500-1000 mg/day (≈38% polyphenols) with the evening meal.</li>
<li><strong>Onset:</strong> measurable lipid change over 4-12 weeks; re-test lipids and ApoB at ~8-12 weeks.</li>
<li><strong>Synergy:</strong> may be combined with plant sterols, soluble fiber, or low-dose statins for additive LDL reduction.</li>
</ul>
