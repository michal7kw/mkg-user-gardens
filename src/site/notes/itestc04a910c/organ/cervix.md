<h1>Cervix</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>The cervix is the cylindrical, lower third of the uterus, projecting into the upper vagina. It surrounds the cervical canal, which connects the uterine cavity (via the internal os) to the vagina (via the external os). The vaginal portion (ectocervix) is lined by stratified squamous epithelium, while the canal (endocervix) is lined by mucus-secreting columnar epithelium. The boundary between these two epithelia — the squamocolumnar junction or transformation zone — is dynamic and is the critical region for cervical pathology.</p>
<p>Functionally, the cervix acts as a regulated gateway: it produces cervical mucus whose viscosity changes across the menstrual cycle (thin and receptive to sperm around ovulation, thick at other times), it remains closed to retain a pregnancy, and it dilates dramatically during labor to allow delivery. The transformation zone undergoes continuous metaplastic change, which makes it especially vulnerable to persistent human papillomavirus (HPV) infection.</p>
<h2>Key Functions</h2>
<ul>
<li>Forms the gateway between the uterine cavity and the vagina</li>
<li>Produces cyclically varying cervical mucus that modulates sperm passage</li>
<li>Maintains a closed barrier during pregnancy and dilates during labor</li>
<li>Hosts the transformation zone where squamous metaplasia occurs</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>Persistent infection of the transformation zone with high-risk HPV types causes cervical intraepithelial neoplasia and <strong>Cervical Cancer</strong>, one of the most preventable cancers. Screening with the <strong>Pap Test</strong> (cytology) and the <strong>HPV-DNA Test</strong> detects precancerous change early, and primary prevention is achieved through HPV vaccination. Combined vaccination and screening programs are driving global cervical-cancer elimination efforts. Other cervical conditions include cervicitis, polyps, and cervical insufficiency contributing to preterm birth.</p>
