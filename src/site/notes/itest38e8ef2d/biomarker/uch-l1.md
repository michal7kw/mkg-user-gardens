<h1>UCH-L1</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Ubiquitin C-terminal hydrolase L1 (UCH-L1; also known as PGP 9.5, protein gene product 9.5) is a 25 kDa deubiquitinating enzyme of the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway. It is among the most abundant proteins in the brain (~1-5% of soluble neuronal protein), highly expressed in neurons, neuroendocrine cells, and the diffuse neuroendocrine system. UCH-L1's physiological role is to cleave ubiquitin from small C-terminal adducts (e.g., ubiquitin-CEtNH2), recycling ubiquitin for re-use in proteasomal protein degradation. After neuronal injury, UCH-L1 leaks from damaged neurons into the extracellular space and bloodstream, where it can be measured as a sensitive serum biomarker of acute neuronal injury.</p>
<p>On February 14, 2018, the U.S. FDA cleared the Abbott i-STAT Alinity UCH-L1 + GFAP combination rapid blood test (results in ~15 minutes) for use in adults with suspected mild traumatic brain injury (Glasgow Coma Scale 13-15). The test was cleared based on the landmark ALERT-TBI study (Welch et al., <em>Lancet Neurol</em> 2016; n=1959), which demonstrated that the combined biomarker panel had a negative predictive value of &gt;99% for traumatic CT findings -- supporting safe deferral of head CT in low-risk patients and reducing unnecessary radiation and costs. This was the first FDA-cleared blood biomarker for TBI evaluation.</p>
<p>Beyond TBI, UCH-L1 has been investigated as a prognostic marker after cardiac arrest (post-ROSC neurological outcome), severe ischemic stroke, and anoxic encephalopathy. It is also a marker of certain neuroendocrine tumors (UCH-L1 expression is characteristic of small cell lung cancer, neuroblastoma, and pancreatic endocrine tumors). Plasma UCH-L1 rises within 1-6 hours of neuronal injury and has a half-life of ~7-10 hours, making it useful in the acute setting.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level: 4/5</strong> (FDA-cleared with strong validation for TBI triage; less robust for non-TBI indications).</p>

<p><strong>Caveats:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Reference range is assay-dependent and clinical-context-dependent</li>
<li>FDA-cleared use is as a binary CT-triage rule (with GFAP), not a quantitative marker</li>
<li>Plasma preferred over serum (less inter-assay variability)</li>
<li>Hemolysis may interfere (erythrocytes contain proteases that degrade UCH-L1)</li>
</ul>

<h2>Factors Affecting Levels</h2>
<p><strong>Increase UCH-L1:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Traumatic brain injury (peak 1-6 h; half-life ~7-10 h)</li>
<li>Severe ischemic or hemorrhagic stroke (within hours)</li>
<li>Post-cardiac arrest anoxic encephalopathy (peak at 24-48 h)</li>
<li>Status epilepticus</li>
<li>Some neuroendocrine tumors (small cell lung cancer, neuroblastoma)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Decrease UCH-L1:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Successful neurocritical care with tissue preservation</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p><strong>Elevated levels are associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Mild TBI:</strong> FDA-cleared to predict traumatic CT findings; NPV &gt;99% for the combination GFAP+UCH-L1 (ALERT-TBI, Welch 2016)</li>
<li><strong>Severe TBI:</strong> correlates with GCS, CT findings, and 6-month Glasgow Outcome Scale</li>
<li><strong>Cardiac arrest (post-ROSC):</strong> elevated UCH-L1 at 24-72 h is one of the strongest predictors of poor neurological outcome, alongside NSE and SSEP N20. ERC/ESICM 2020 prognostication guidelines include elevated UCH-L1 among multimodal prognostication markers</li>
<li><strong>Severe stroke:</strong> rising UCH-L1 in the acute phase correlates with infarct volume and functional outcome</li>
<li><strong>Neuroendocrine tumors:</strong> UCH-L1 expression by IHC is characteristic of small cell lung cancer; less used as a serum marker in this context</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Limitations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Acute marker only (half-life ~7-10 h); not useful for chronic neurodegeneration</li>
<li>FDA clearance is specifically for adults with mTBI and GCS 13-15 within 12 h of injury</li>
<li>Not for use in patients with isolated orthopedic injuries (extracranial UCH-L1 contribution negligible, but data limited)</li>
<li>Not for pediatric use (&lt;18 years)</li>
<li>Should not be the sole basis for clinical decisions -- integrate with imaging, GCS, and clinical judgment</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Monitoring use cases:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>TBI triage (FDA-cleared)</li>
<li>Multimodal prognostication after cardiac arrest (with NSE, SSEP, neuroimaging)</li>
<li>Research: stroke outcome prediction</li>
</ul>
<h2>Strategies to Manage</h2>
<p>UCH-L1 reflects acute tissue injury and is not directly modifiable. Management targets the underlying cause:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Mild TBI (FDA-cleared use):</strong> if GFAP+UCH-L1 below CT-triage threshold AND clinical features low-risk, can defer head CT; otherwise standard trauma imaging and observation</li>
<li><strong>Severe TBI:</strong> neurocritical care -- ICP monitoring (target &lt;22 mmHg), CPP 60-70 mmHg, surgical evacuation if mass lesion</li>
<li><strong>Post-cardiac arrest:</strong> targeted temperature management (32-36C x 24 h); multimodal prognostication at 72+ h</li>
<li><strong>Stroke:</strong> time-sensitive reperfusion (IV thrombolysis if &lt;4.5 h; mechanical thrombectomy for LVO)</li>
<li><strong>Neuroendocrine tumors:</strong> oncology referral; tissue-based diagnosis</li>
</ol>
