<h1>Sleep and Circadian Health Explained</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Sleep can feel like wasted time, but biologically it is some of the most active and important work your body does. During sleep your brain clears metabolic waste, consolidates memory, and your hormones reset for the day ahead. Running underneath it all is a roughly 24-hour internal timer — the circadian clock — that coordinates nearly every system. When sleep and that clock are disrupted, the effects ripple straight into metabolism, mood, and long-term health.</p>
<h2>The Story</h2>
<p>Your circadian clock lives in the <a class="internal-link is-unresolved" href="/404">Hypothalamus</a> and is set primarily by light. In the evening, as light fades, the brain releases <strong><a href="/garden/itest1af554c1/hormone/melatonin/" class="internal-link">Melatonin</a></strong>, the &quot;it's night&quot; signal that prepares the body for sleep. In the morning, <a href="/garden/itest1af554c1/hormone/cortisol/" class="internal-link">Cortisol</a> rises to wake you up and mobilize energy — so melatonin and cortisol run on deliberately <em>opposite</em> schedules. A healthy day has high morning cortisol and high evening melatonin; modern life (bright screens at night, dim days indoors) blurs both, which is why light exposure is the most powerful lever for fixing sleep.</p>
<p>Sleep itself is not uniform. Deep slow-wave sleep is when the body does physical repair and releases growth hormone; REM sleep handles emotional and memory processing. The <a href="/garden/itest1af554c1/organ/brain/" class="internal-link">Brain</a> even has a clearance system that flushes waste products most actively during deep sleep — a process relevant to long-term brain health.</p>
<p>The metabolic cost of poor sleep is striking and fast. In a classic experiment, healthy young men restricted to ~4 hours of sleep developed measurably worse glucose handling within days — looking metabolically older and more <a href="/garden/itest1af554c1/condition/insulin-resistance/" class="internal-link">Insulin Resistance</a>-like (PMID: 10543671). Short sleep raises evening <a href="/garden/itest1af554c1/hormone/cortisol/" class="internal-link">Cortisol</a>, increases hunger hormones, and pushes <a href="/garden/itest1af554c1/metabolite/glucose/" class="internal-link">Glucose</a> up. This is why sleep is, quite literally, a metabolic intervention.</p>
<h2>What This Means For You</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Anchor your clock with light.</strong> Bright light in the morning and dim, warm light in the evening do more for sleep than almost anything else; <a href="/garden/itest1af554c1/intervention/red-light-therapy/" class="internal-link">Red Light Therapy</a> in the evening is one gentle tool, and morning sunlight is free.</li>
<li><strong>Protect the basics.</strong> Consistent timing, a cool dark room, and limiting late caffeine and alcohol are the core of <a href="/garden/itest1af554c1/intervention/sleep-hygiene/" class="internal-link">Sleep Hygiene</a> and <a href="/garden/itest1af554c1/intervention/sleep-optimization/" class="internal-link">Sleep Optimization</a>. A morning <a href="/garden/itest1af554c1/biomarker/cortisol-am/" class="internal-link">Cortisol (AM)</a> that is appropriately high (and falls by night) reflects a well-set clock.</li>
<li><strong>It connects to everything.</strong> Because poor sleep worsens <a href="/garden/itest1af554c1/condition/insulin-resistance/" class="internal-link">Insulin Resistance</a>, raises cortisol, and impairs the <a href="/garden/itest1af554c1/organ/brain/" class="internal-link">Brain</a>'s overnight clear-out, improving sleep quietly improves the metabolic, stress, and cognitive stories all at once.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Key Takeaways</h2>
<ul>
<li>A light-set circadian clock runs melatonin (night) and cortisol (morning) on opposite schedules.</li>
<li>Deep sleep drives physical repair and the brain's overnight waste clearance.</li>
<li>Even a few nights of short sleep measurably worsen glucose handling.</li>
<li>Morning light and consistent timing are the most powerful sleep levers.</li>
</ul>
