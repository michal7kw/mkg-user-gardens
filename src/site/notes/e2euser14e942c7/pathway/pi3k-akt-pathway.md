<h1>PI3K-Akt Pathway</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>The PI3K-Akt pathway is a central signaling cascade activated by receptor tyrosine kinases (including the <strong>insulin receptor</strong>) and GPCRs. PI3K converts PIP2 to PIP3, recruiting Akt to the membrane where it is activated. Akt phosphorylates targets (<strong>mTORC1</strong>, <strong>GSK3</strong>, <strong>FOXO</strong>) to promote cell growth, protein synthesis, glycolysis, and survival while inhibiting apoptosis. <strong>PTEN</strong> is the key negative regulator. Mutated in <strong>over 50% of cancers</strong>.</p>
<h2>Key Components</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>PI3K</strong> (class IA) -- p85 regulatory + p110 catalytic subunits</li>
<li><strong>AKT</strong> (AKT1/2/3) -- serine/threonine kinase; central node</li>
<li><strong>mTORC1</strong> -- master regulator of protein synthesis and growth</li>
<li><strong>PTEN</strong> -- tumor suppressor; negative regulator (PIP3 phosphatase)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Therapeutic Targeting</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>mTOR inhibitors</strong> (rapamycin, everolimus) -- cancer and transplant</li>
<li><strong>PI3K inhibitors</strong> (alpelisib, idelalisib) -- cancer</li>
<li><strong>AKT inhibitors</strong> (capivasertib) -- clinical trials</li>
<li><strong>Metformin</strong> -- indirectly inhibits mTORC1 via AMPK</li>
</ul>
