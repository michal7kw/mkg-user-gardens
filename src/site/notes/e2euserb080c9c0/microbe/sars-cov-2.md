<h1>SARS-CoV-2</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>SARS-CoV-2 is an enveloped, positive-sense single-stranded RNA virus of the genus <em>Betacoronavirus</em> (subgenus <em>Sarbecovirus</em>) and the etiologic agent of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19). First identified in late 2019, it caused a global pandemic with diverse clinical outcomes ranging from asymptomatic infection to severe pneumonia, acute respiratory distress, and death. A subset of infected individuals develop persistent post-acute symptoms (Long COVID).</p>

<h2>Ecology &amp; Transmission</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Body Sites:</strong> respiratory tract (nasopharynx, bronchi, alveolar epithelium); systemic spread via ACE2-expressing tissues</li>
<li><strong>Transmission:</strong> respiratory droplets and aerosols; primary attachment via the spike glycoprotein</li>
<li><strong>Genome:</strong> ~30 kb positive-sense ssRNA encoding the replicase (ORF1ab), structural proteins (S, E, M, N), and accessory factors</li>
</ul>
<h2>Pathogenesis &amp; Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>Cell entry depends on the spike protein engaging the host ACE2 receptor, with the serine protease TMPRSS2 priming S for membrane fusion (Hoffmann et al. 2020). Severe disease is driven by alveolar damage, dysregulated innate immunity, and a hyperinflammatory response. The virus undergoes ongoing antigenic evolution, generating variants of concern with altered transmissibility and immune escape.</p>
