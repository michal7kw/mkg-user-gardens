<h1>Fidaxomicin</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Fidaxomicin is a narrow-spectrum macrocyclic antibiotic and the first member of a new antibacterial class approved (2011) specifically for Clostridioides difficile infection (CDI). Because it is minimally absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract, it delivers high luminal concentrations directly to the colon where C. difficile resides, while its narrow spectrum largely spares Bacteroides and other commensal anaerobes that constitute colonization resistance.</p>
<p>In pivotal phase 3 trials, fidaxomicin (200 mg twice daily for 10 days) was non-inferior to oral vancomycin for clinical cure but produced significantly fewer recurrences, the principal cause of CDI morbidity. On the strength of these data, the 2021 IDSA/SHEA guideline update recommends fidaxomicin as a preferred first-line agent over vancomycin for both initial and recurrent episodes.</p>
<h2>Pharmacological Information</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Class:</strong> Macrocyclic antibiotic (18-membered macrolactone)</li>
<li><strong>Route:</strong> Oral only (tablet and pediatric oral suspension)</li>
<li><strong>Absorption:</strong> Negligible systemic absorption; acts locally in the colon</li>
<li><strong>Active metabolite:</strong> OP-1118 (formed by hydrolysis), also microbiologically active</li>
<li><strong>Spectrum:</strong> Narrow — potent against C. difficile, sparing of normal gut flora</li>
</ul>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>Fidaxomicin inhibits bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase by binding the switch region of the enzyme's sigma subunit, a site distinct from that targeted by rifamycins. This prevents the open-complex formation required for transcription initiation, halting RNA synthesis. The drug is bactericidal against C. difficile and also suppresses sporulation and toxin production, which contributes to its low recurrence profile.</p>
<h2>Clinical Features</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Standard dosing (adults):</strong> 200 mg orally twice daily for 10 days; an extended-pulsed regimen is an alternative for recurrence</li>
<li><strong>Efficacy:</strong> Non-inferior to vancomycin for sustained clinical cure with markedly lower recurrence (Louie OPT-80, PMID: 21288078)</li>
<li><strong>Guideline status:</strong> Preferred first-line for initial and recurrent CDI (IDSA/SHEA 2021, PMID: 34492699)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Safety Information</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Common side effects:</strong> Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, gastrointestinal hemorrhage (uncommon)</li>
<li><strong>Contraindications:</strong> Hypersensitivity to fidaxomicin</li>
<li><strong>Interactions:</strong> Minimal due to negligible systemic exposure; caution with strong P-glycoprotein inhibitors (e.g., cyclosporine) which may raise plasma levels</li>
<li><strong>Cost:</strong> Substantially more expensive than vancomycin, the main barrier to use</li>
</ul>
