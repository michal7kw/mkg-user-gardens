<h1>Candidiasis</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Candidiasis is a fungal infection caused by yeasts of the genus <em>Candida</em>, with <strong>Candida albicans</strong> responsible for the majority of cases. Other species -- <em>C. glabrata, C. parapsilosis, C. tropicalis, C. krusei</em> -- are increasingly important, particularly in healthcare-associated infections and in immunocompromised patients where they tend to be more resistant to antifungal therapy.</p>
<p>Candida species are normal commensal organisms of the gastrointestinal, genitourinary, and skin microbiota in healthy individuals. Disease occurs when the balance between the host immune system and the fungal population is disrupted -- by <strong>broad-spectrum antibiotics</strong> (eliminate competing bacteria), <strong>immunocompromise</strong> (HIV, chemotherapy, transplant), <strong>diabetes mellitus</strong> (glycosuria promotes fungal growth), or <strong>breakdown of mucocutaneous barriers</strong> (catheters, burns, surgery).</p>
<p>The clinical spectrum ranges from <strong>superficial mucocutaneous infections</strong> (oral thrush, vulvovaginal candidiasis, cutaneous intertrigo) -- common, easily treated with topical or oral antifungals -- to <strong>invasive candidiasis</strong> (candidemia, deep organ involvement) -- a life-threatening condition requiring IV antifungal therapy, seen in ICU patients, neutropenic patients, and those with central venous catheters. <strong>Invasive candidiasis</strong> carries a mortality of 30-40%.</p>
<h2>Clinical Presentation</h2>
<p><strong>Oral candidiasis (thrush):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>White, curd-like patches on tongue, buccal mucosa, palate</li>
<li>Can be scraped off, revealing erythematous base</li>
<li>Burning sensation, altered taste</li>
<li>Angular cheilitis (cracking at corners of mouth)</li>
<li>Common in infants, denture wearers, immunocompromised, inhaled corticosteroid users</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Vulvovaginal candidiasis (VVC):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Intense vulvar itching and burning</li>
<li>Thick, white, &quot;cottage cheese&quot; vaginal discharge</li>
<li>Dyspareunia, dysuria</li>
<li>Vulvar erythema and edema</li>
<li>Very common: ~75% of women will have at least one episode</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Cutaneous candidiasis (intertrigo):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Red, macerated, sharply demarcated rash in skin folds (groin, axillae, inframammary, interdigital)</li>
<li>Satellite pustules</li>
<li>Pruritus, burning</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Esophageal candidiasis:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Odynophagia, dysphagia</li>
<li>Usually in HIV/AIDS (CD4 &lt;100), cancer, transplant</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Invasive candidiasis / candidemia:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Fever unresponsive to broad-spectrum antibiotics</li>
<li>Sepsis, septic shock</li>
<li>Often in ICU patients with central venous catheters</li>
<li>Endophthalmitis (hematogenous spread to retina)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Diagnostic Information</h2>
<p><strong>Mucocutaneous:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Clinical appearance is often diagnostic</li>
<li><strong>KOH preparation</strong> of scraping -- pseudohyphae and budding yeast</li>
<li><strong>Fungal culture</strong> -- confirms species and sensitivities</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Invasive:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Blood cultures</strong> (positive in 50-80% of candidemia; may be negative in deep-tissue candidiasis)</li>
<li><strong>Beta-D-glucan</strong> (fungal cell wall marker; non-specific but sensitive)</li>
<li><strong>Tissue biopsy</strong> with culture and histopathology (gold standard for deep infection)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Oral thrush:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Nystatin</strong> oral suspension (swish and swallow) x 7-14 days</li>
<li><strong>Clotrimazole</strong> troches x 7-14 days</li>
<li><strong>Fluconazole</strong> 100-200 mg PO daily x 7-14 days (moderate-severe or immunocompromised)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Vulvovaginal candidiasis:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Topical azoles</strong> (clotrimazole, miconazole) intravaginally x 1-7 days</li>
<li><strong>Fluconazole</strong> 150 mg PO single dose (uncomplicated)</li>
<li>Recurrent VVC: fluconazole weekly x 6 months (suppressive)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Invasive candidiasis:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Remove central venous catheter</strong> (if source)</li>
<li><strong>Echinocandin</strong> (caspofungin, micafungin, anidulafungin) -- first-line for most patients</li>
<li><strong>Fluconazole</strong> 400-800 mg daily -- for stable patients with C. albicans (if susceptible)</li>
<li><strong>Amphotericin B</strong> -- for resistant species or if echinocandin not available</li>
<li>Duration: 2 weeks after first negative blood culture (candidemia); longer for deep-tissue</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Address underlying causes:</strong> glucose control, discontinue unnecessary antibiotics, treat immunodeficiency</p>
