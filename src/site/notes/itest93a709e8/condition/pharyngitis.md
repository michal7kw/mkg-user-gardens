<h1>Pharyngitis</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Pharyngitis is inflammation of the pharynx, most commonly caused by viral or bacterial infection. It presents with sore throat (the hallmark symptom), pain with swallowing (odynophagia), pharyngeal erythema, and frequently fever and cervical lymphadenopathy. Pharyngitis is one of the most common reasons for outpatient medical visits, accounting for an estimated 15 million healthcare visits annually in the United States.</p>
<p>The clinical challenge in pharyngitis is <strong>distinguishing Group A Streptococcus (GAS) pharyngitis</strong> -- the only common form requiring antibiotic treatment -- from the multitude of viral causes (rhinovirus, adenovirus, Epstein-Barr virus, coxsackievirus, respiratory syncytial virus). The <strong>Centor criteria</strong> (fever, tonsillar exudate, tender anterior cervical adenopathy, absence of cough) help estimate the probability of GAS pharyngitis. A modified Centor score with rapid antigen detection testing (RADT) guides treatment decisions.</p>
<p>Untreated GAS pharyngitis can lead to <strong>suppurative complications</strong> (peritonsillar abscess, retropharyngeal abscess, otitis media, sinusitis) and <strong>non-suppurative complications</strong> (acute rheumatic fever, post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis). Prompt antibiotic therapy (penicillin V or amoxicillin for 10 days) prevents rheumatic fever but does not reliably prevent glomerulonephritis.</p>
<h2>Clinical Presentation</h2>
<p><strong>Symptoms:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Sore throat (hallmark)</li>
<li>Odynophagia (pain with swallowing)</li>
<li>Fever (usually &lt;39C in viral; may be higher in GAS)</li>
<li>Cervical lymphadenopathy (tender anterior chain in GAS)</li>
<li>Headache, malaise</li>
<li>Cough, rhinorrhea (favor viral)</li>
<li>Absence of cough (favors GAS)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Physical findings:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Pharyngeal erythema</li>
<li>Tonsillar exudate (white/yellow)</li>
<li>Tender anterior cervical lymphadenopathy</li>
<li>Palatal petechiae (GAS)</li>
<li>Scarlet fever rash (scarlatiniform; GAS)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Diagnostic Information</h2>
<p><strong>Centor / McIsaac criteria</strong> (1 point each):</p>
<ol>
<li>Fever &gt;38C</li>
<li>Tonsillar exudate</li>
<li>Tender anterior cervical adenopathy</li>
<li>Absence of cough</li>
<li>Age 3-14 (1 point); 15-44 (0); 45+ (-1)</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Score interpretation:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>0-1: No testing; no antibiotic</li>
<li>2-3: RADT; treat if positive</li>
<li>4-5: Empiric antibiotic (or RADT confirmation)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Laboratory:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Rapid antigen detection test (RADT) for GAS -- sensitivity ~70-90%; specificity ~95%</li>
<li>Throat culture -- gold standard; sensitivity 90-99%; results in 24-48 h</li>
</ul>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Viral pharyngitis (majority of cases):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Supportive care: rest, hydration, saltwater gargles</li>
<li>Analgesics: acetaminophen, ibuprofen</li>
<li>Topical anesthetics (benzocaine, lidocaine lozenges)</li>
<li>Antibiotics NOT recommended</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>GAS pharyngitis (confirmed):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>First-line: Penicillin V 500 mg PO BID x 10 days (or amoxicillin 500 mg BID x 10 days)</li>
<li>Penicillin-allergic: Cephalexin 500 mg BID x 10 days (if non-anaphylactic) or azithromycin 500 mg day 1 then 250 mg days 2-5</li>
<li>Single-dose option: Benzathine penicillin G 1.2 million units IM</li>
</ul>
<h2>Complications</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Peritonsillar abscess</strong> -- most common suppurative complication</li>
<li><strong>Retropharyngeal abscess</strong> -- requires surgical drainage</li>
<li><strong>Acute rheumatic fever</strong> -- prevented by prompt antibiotic therapy</li>
<li><strong>Post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis</strong> -- not reliably prevented by antibiotics</li>
<li><strong>Otitis media, sinusitis</strong> -- direct extension</li>
</ul>
