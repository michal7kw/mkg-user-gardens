<h1>Klotho</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Alpha-Klotho (commonly called Klotho) is a ~130 kDa transmembrane protein identified in 1997 by Makoto Kuro-o and colleagues at the National Institute of Neuroscience in Tokyo. The gene was named after the Greek goddess Klotho, one of the three Fates who spun the thread of life. The seminal experiment showed that mice with a disrupted Klotho gene developed a syndrome resembling accelerated human aging -- including atherosclerosis, osteoporosis, emphysema, infertility, cognitive decline, and drastically shortened lifespan (~2 months vs ~2 years for wild-type mice). Conversely, mice overexpressing Klotho lived ~20-30% longer than wild-type mice. This established Klotho as a paradigmatic &quot;aging-suppressor&quot; protein.</p>
<p>Klotho exists in two principal forms:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Membrane-bound Klotho</strong> (~130 kDa) is expressed primarily in the kidney (distal convoluted tubule), parathyroid gland, and choroid plexus. It serves as the obligate co-receptor for FGF23 (fibroblast growth factor 23), enabling FGF23 signaling that regulates phosphate homeostasis and vitamin D metabolism.</li>
<li><strong>Soluble Klotho</strong> (~65-70 kDa) is generated by extracellular cleavage of membrane-bound Klotho by ADAM10 and ADAM17 proteases (&quot;shedding&quot;). This soluble form circulates in blood, CSF, and urine and exerts endocrine effects independent of FGF23 -- including Wnt signaling modulation, oxidative stress suppression, insulin/IGF-1 pathway modulation, and anti-inflammatory effects.</li>
</ol>
<p>Circulating soluble Klotho is the form measured as a biomarker. <strong>Levels decline progressively with age</strong> -- roughly halving from age 40 to age 80 -- and are further reduced in chronic kidney disease (the principal site of Klotho production), diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and cognitive decline. Higher circulating Klotho is associated with longevity, preserved executive function, reduced vascular calcification, and greater resilience to aging insults. Animal studies show that Klotho supplementation or genetic overexpression reverses age-related phenotypes, making it an attractive target for longevity therapeutics.</p>
<p>Mechanistically, Klotho modulates multiple aging-relevant pathways: (1) <strong>insulin/IGF-1 signaling</strong> (Klotho inhibits insulin/IGF-1, extending lifespan in model organisms); (2) <strong>Wnt signaling</strong> (Klotho suppresses Wnt, which accumulates with age and drives cellular senescence); (3) <strong>oxidative stress</strong> (Klotho upregulates SOD2 and other antioxidants); (4) <strong>inflammation</strong> (Klotho suppresses NF-kB and NLRP3 inflammasome); (5) <strong>cellular senescence</strong> (Klotho reduces SASP, the senescence-associated secretory phenotype).</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level: 3/5</strong> (strong mechanistic foundation; observational human studies; no clinical-grade assays; not in clinical guidelines).</p>

<h2>Factors Affecting Levels</h2>
<p><strong>Increase Klotho:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Regular aerobic exercise</strong> (the most consistent lifestyle intervention; raises Klotho in animal and human studies)</li>
<li><strong>Ketogenic diet</strong> (animal studies; emerging human evidence)</li>
<li><strong>Caloric restriction</strong> (animal studies; mechanism: reduced insulin/IGF-1 signaling)</li>
<li><strong>Vitamin D optimization</strong> (VDR activation upregulates Klotho)</li>
<li><strong>Quercetin, fisetin</strong> (senolytic compounds; restore Klotho in animal models)</li>
<li><strong>Statins</strong> (observational evidence for modest Klotho increase)</li>
<li><strong>NMN/NR</strong> (NAD+ precursors; animal studies)</li>
<li><strong>Metformin</strong> (animal studies; AMPK-mediated)</li>
<li><strong>Intermittent fasting</strong> (animal and emerging human studies)</li>
<li><strong>Younger age</strong> (the strongest non-modifiable determinant)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Decrease Klotho:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Aging</strong> (the principal cause of Klotho decline; halving from 40 to 80 years)</li>
<li><strong>Chronic kidney disease</strong> (the kidney is the principal Klotho source; CKD markedly lowers Klotho)</li>
<li><strong>Type 1 and type 2 diabetes</strong> (acute and chronic hyperglycemia suppress Klotho)</li>
<li><strong>Cardiovascular disease</strong> (heart failure, coronary disease)</li>
<li><strong>Hypertension</strong></li>
<li><strong>Cognitive impairment, dementia, Alzheimer's disease</strong></li>
<li><strong>Depression, chronic psychological stress</strong></li>
<li><strong>Obesity, metabolic syndrome</strong></li>
<li><strong>Smoking</strong></li>
<li><strong>Sedentary lifestyle</strong></li>
<li><strong>Chronic inflammation</strong> (NF-kB activation suppresses Klotho)</li>
<li><strong>Acute illness, sepsis</strong> (transient suppression)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p><strong>Lower circulating Klotho is associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Accelerated biological aging:</strong> Klotho decline tracks with frailty, sarcopenia, multimorbidity</li>
<li><strong>Chronic kidney disease progression:</strong> Klotho falls as eGFR falls; predicts CKD progression and mortality</li>
<li><strong>Cardiovascular disease:</strong> low Klotho predicts vascular calcification, arterial stiffness, CV events</li>
<li><strong>Cognitive decline and Alzheimer's disease:</strong> low Klotho correlates with executive dysfunction and progression from MCI to AD</li>
<li><strong>Type 2 diabetes:</strong> low Klotho with insulin resistance and metabolic syndrome</li>
<li><strong>Osteoporosis:</strong> FGF23-Klotho axis regulates phosphate; dysregulation causes bone loss</li>
<li><strong>Depression, chronic stress:</strong> bidirectional relationship</li>
<li><strong>All-cause mortality:</strong> low Klotho predicts mortality in elderly cohorts</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Higher circulating Klotho is associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Longevity:</strong> centenarians and their offspring have higher Klotho</li>
<li><strong>Cognitive resilience:</strong> better executive function, slower cognitive decline</li>
<li><strong>Reduced vascular stiffness</strong></li>
<li><strong>Greater kidney function preservation</strong></li>
<li><strong>Greater resilience to stress, infection, and inflammation</strong></li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Limitations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>No universally standardized clinical assay</strong> -- values differ substantially across platforms</li>
<li><strong>Not in clinical guidelines</strong> -- research and functional-medicine biomarker only</li>
<li><strong>Causation vs correlation unclear</strong> -- Klotho declines may be a consequence of aging rather than a driver</li>
<li><strong>No interventional trials</strong> showing that raising Klotho improves hard clinical outcomes in humans (only observational data)</li>
<li><strong>Multiple forms of Klotho</strong> (membrane-bound, soluble, secreted) measured differently</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Monitoring use cases:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Research: aging biomarker in longevity-cohort studies</li>
<li>Functional medicine: longevity panel (alongside GDF15, p16INK4a, DNA methylation clocks)</li>
<li>Research: chronic kidney disease progression marker</li>
<li>Research: cognitive aging biomarker</li>
</ul>
<h2>Strategies to Improve</h2>
<p>Klotho is modifiable through lifestyle interventions:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Aerobic exercise</strong> (150 min/week moderate or 75 min/week vigorous) -- the most consistent intervention raising Klotho in animal and human studies</li>
<li><strong>Resistance training</strong> 2-3x/week -- complements aerobic exercise</li>
<li><strong>Ketogenic diet</strong> -- animal studies show substantial Klotho elevation; emerging human evidence</li>
<li><strong>Caloric restriction</strong> (12-16 h daily fasting; or 5:2 intermittent fasting) -- animal studies</li>
<li><strong>Intermittent fasting</strong> (16:8, 5:2, alternate-day fasting) -- emerging human evidence</li>
<li><strong>Vitamin D optimization</strong> (2000-5000 IU D3 daily; target 25-OH-D 40-60 ng/mL) -- VDR activation upregulates Klotho</li>
<li><strong>Quercetin</strong> 500-1000 mg daily (senolytic; restores Klotho in animal models)</li>
<li><strong>Fisetin</strong> (senolytic; clinical trials ongoing)</li>
<li><strong>NAD+ precursors</strong> (NMN, NR) -- animal studies show Klotho upregulation</li>
<li><strong>Metformin</strong> (in diabetics; observational evidence for modest Klotho increase)</li>
<li><strong>Statin therapy</strong> (observational evidence for modest Klotho increase)</li>
<li><strong>Treat underlying CKD, diabetes, hypertension, obesity</strong></li>
<li><strong>Smoking cessation</strong></li>
<li><strong>Stress management</strong> -- mindfulness, meditation, sleep optimization</li>
</ol>
