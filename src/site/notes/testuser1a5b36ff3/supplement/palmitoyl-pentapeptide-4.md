<h1>Palmitoyl Pentapeptide-4</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Palmitoyl Pentapeptide-4 — best known by the trade name <strong>Matrixyl</strong> — is a synthetic <strong>signal peptide</strong> used widely in anti-aging skincare. It joins a five-amino-acid sequence (KTTKS, a fragment of type I procollagen) to a palmitic-acid (fatty) chain that helps it cross the skin barrier. Once in the dermis it acts as a &quot;matrikine&quot;, signalling skin cells to behave as if collagen has been damaged and needs rebuilding. Like GHK-Cu, it is a <strong>topical cosmetic</strong> applied to the skin, not an injectable.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>The KTTKS fragment is a piece of type I procollagen. When collagen breaks down, such fragments normally signal fibroblasts to synthesise new collagen and elastin. Palmitoyl Pentapeptide-4 exploits this feedback loop: applied topically, it mimics that &quot;repair&quot; signal, encouraging the dermis to produce more extracellular-matrix proteins — the intended result being plumper skin with reduced fine lines. The palmitoyl tail is what makes an otherwise poorly-absorbed peptide able to penetrate the skin.</p>
<h2>Clinical Evidence</h2>
<p>Evidence is limited to small cosmetic studies and manufacturer data showing modest improvements in wrinkle depth and skin firmness. There are no large independent trials, so its <code>evidence_level</code> is <strong>2</strong>. It is popular precisely because it is low-risk and topical rather than because the effect is large.</p>
<h2>Safety</h2>
<p>As a topical cosmetic, systemic risk is minimal; occasional mild irritation is the main concern. This places it, with GHK-Cu, at the safe end of the peptide spectrum.</p>
