<h1>Chronic Hypoxia</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Chronic hypoxia is a sustained state of inadequate oxygen delivery to tissues, most commonly arising from <strong>chronic lung disease</strong> (COPD, pulmonary fibrosis), <strong>heart failure</strong>, <strong>sleep-disordered breathing</strong> (obstructive sleep apnea), or residence at <strong>high altitude</strong>. The body mounts several compensatory responses to chronic hypoxia, each with clinical consequences:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Increased erythropoietin (EPO) secretion</strong> -&gt; <strong>secondary polycythemia</strong> (elevated hematocrit and hemoglobin). While adaptive for oxygen-carrying capacity, excessive polycythemia (Hct &gt;55-60%) increases blood viscosity and risk of thrombosis, requiring phlebotomy.</li>
<li><strong>Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction</strong> -&gt; <strong>pulmonary hypertension</strong> -&gt; right ventricular hypertrophy and <strong>cor pulmonale</strong> (right heart failure).</li>
<li><strong>Increased 2,3-BPG</strong> -&gt; rightward shift of hemoglobin-oxygen dissociation curve -&gt; enhanced tissue oxygen unloading.</li>
<li><strong>Mitochondrial adaptation</strong> -&gt; increased mitochondrial density and capillary angiogenesis.</li>
</ol>
<p>The clinical picture is dominated by the <strong>underlying disease</strong> (COPD symptoms, heart failure symptoms) combined with hypoxia-specific signs: cyanosis, digital clubbing (in long-standing cases), cognitive impairment, and exercise intolerance. Morning headache suggests nocturnal hypoventilation with CO2 retention.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Supplemental oxygen (long-term oxygen therapy, LTOT):</strong> indicated for severe resting hypoxemia (PaO2 &lt;55 mmHg or SpO2 &lt;88%); improves survival in COPD (NOTT, MRC trials)</li>
<li><strong>Treat underlying cause:</strong> bronchodilators for COPD; CPAP for OSA; diuretics for CHF; immunosuppression for pulmonary fibrosis</li>
<li><strong>Phlebotomy:</strong> for symptomatic polycythemia (Hct &gt;56% with hyperviscosity symptoms)</li>
<li><strong>Pulmonary rehabilitation:</strong> exercise training, education, nutritional support</li>
</ul>
