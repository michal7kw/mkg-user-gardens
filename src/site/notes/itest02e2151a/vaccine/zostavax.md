<h1>Zostavax</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Zostavax is a <strong>live-attenuated</strong> zoster vaccine that uses a high-potency formulation of the Oka/Merck varicella-zoster virus (VZV) strain — the same strain as the childhood varicella vaccine but at a substantially higher viral titer. Administered as a <strong>single subcutaneous dose</strong>, it was the first vaccine approved to prevent <strong>herpes zoster (shingles)</strong> and its most feared complication, <strong>post-herpetic neuralgia</strong>, in older adults.</p>
<p>In the pivotal Shingles Prevention Study, Zostavax reduced the incidence of herpes zoster by roughly <strong>51%</strong> and post-herpetic neuralgia by about <strong>67%</strong> in adults aged 60 and older. However, its efficacy was lower in the oldest recipients (&gt;= 70 years) and <strong>waned substantially over several years</strong>. As a live vaccine, it was also <strong>contraindicated in immunocompromised individuals</strong>, the very population at highest risk for severe zoster.</p>
<p>Because of its moderate, waning efficacy and live-vaccine limitations, Zostavax was superseded by the recombinant adjuvanted subunit vaccine <strong>Shingrix</strong> (&gt;90% efficacy, usable in immunocompromised adults), which became the ACIP-preferred option in 2017. Zostavax was <strong>discontinued in the United States in 2020</strong>.</p>
<h2>Mechanism and Use</h2>
<p>The live attenuated VZV boosts waning VZV-specific cell-mediated immunity in adults who were previously infected with wild-type virus (latent in dorsal root ganglia), raising the immune threshold that normally keeps the virus from reactivating. A single subcutaneous injection was the complete schedule, with no booster recommended.</p>
<h2>Comparison to Recombinant Zoster Vaccine</h2>
<p>Relative to Shingrix, Zostavax offered lower and less durable protection and could not be given to immunocompromised patients. Shingrix's recombinant glycoprotein E subunit plus AS01B adjuvant platform avoids the live-virus safety concern and achieves markedly higher, more durable efficacy, which is why current guidelines favor the recombinant vaccine even for people previously vaccinated with Zostavax.</p>
