<h1>Narcolepsy</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Narcolepsy is the prototypical disorder of sleep-wake state instability — the brain cannot maintain stable boundaries between wakefulness, non-REM, and REM sleep, so elements of one state intrude into another. Its hallmark is excessive daytime sleepiness, and in <strong>type 1 narcolepsy</strong> this is accompanied by cataplexy (sudden emotion-triggered loss of muscle tone), which reflects intrusion of REM-sleep atonia into wakefulness.</p>
<p>The disorder provided one of neuroscience's clearest gene-to-behavior stories: type 1 narcolepsy is caused by the selective loss of the ~70,000 neurons in the lateral <a class="internal-link is-unresolved" href="/404">Hypothalamus</a> that produce <a href="/garden/itestb7bd9e0c/neurotransmitter/orexin/" class="internal-link">Orexin</a> (hypocretin). Without orexin's stabilizing &quot;wake&quot; signal, the sleep-wake switch flips erratically. The tight association with HLA-DQB1*06:02 and post-H1N1 cases point to an autoimmune destruction of orexin neurons.</p>
<h2>Clinical Presentation</h2>
<h3>Symptoms</h3>
<p>Excessive daytime sleepiness is universal; cataplexy, sleep paralysis, and hypnagogic hallucinations form the classic tetrad (with disrupted night sleep). Cataplexy specifically marks type 1 (orexin-deficient) narcolepsy.</p>
<h2>Diagnostic Information</h2>
<p>Diagnosis combines clinical features with the multiple sleep latency test (short sleep latency, sleep-onset REM periods) and, for type 1, low CSF orexin/hypocretin-1. HLA typing is supportive.</p>
<h2>Risk Factors</h2>
<p>HLA-DQB1*06:02 carriage, autoimmune predisposition, and the epidemiologic link to H1N1 infection/Pandemrix vaccination implicate immune-mediated orexin-neuron loss.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p>Wake-promoting agents (modafinil, the H3-inverse-agonist pitolisant acting on <a href="/garden/itestb7bd9e0c/neurotransmitter/histamine/" class="internal-link">Histamine</a>, solriamfetol) treat sleepiness; sodium oxybate addresses cataplexy and fragmented sleep; scheduled naps help.</p>
