<h1>Quinolinic Acid</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Quinolinic acid (QUIN) is a downstream metabolite of the <a href="/garden/itestb7bd9e0c/pathway/kynurenine-pathway/" class="internal-link">Kynurenine Pathway</a> with a striking dual identity. On one hand it is the obligate precursor of de novo <a href="/garden/itestb7bd9e0c/metabolite/nad/" class="internal-link">NAD+</a> synthesis: quinolinate phosphoribosyltransferase (QPRT) converts it to nicotinic acid mononucleotide en route to NAD+. On the other hand, when it accumulates it is a potent neurotoxin — an endogenous agonist of the N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA) glutamate receptor that drives excitotoxicity.</p>
<p>QUIN is produced predominantly by activated microglia and infiltrating macrophages, which express the KMO branch enzymes. Neurons and astrocytes lack QPRT capacity to clear it efficiently, so under sustained neuroinflammation QUIN accumulates to pathological levels in the brain.</p>

<h2>Formation and Fate</h2>
<p>Kynurenine → 3-hydroxykynurenine (KMO) → 3-hydroxyanthranilic acid (KYNU) → quinolinic acid (3-HAO). QUIN is then either (a) salvaged to <a href="/garden/itestb7bd9e0c/metabolite/nad/" class="internal-link">NAD+</a> by QPRT, or (b) released into the extracellular space where it engages NMDA receptors.</p>
<h2>Neurotoxic Mechanism</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>NMDA-receptor agonism:</strong> QUIN directly activates NMDA receptors (preferentially NR2A/NR2B subtypes), causing excessive Ca²⁺ influx and excitotoxic neuronal death — a chronic, low-grade analogue of <a href="/garden/itestb7bd9e0c/neurotransmitter/glutamate/" class="internal-link">Glutamate</a> excitotoxicity.</li>
<li><strong>Oxidative stress &amp; lipid peroxidation:</strong> QUIN forms complexes with iron, generating reactive oxygen species.</li>
<li><strong>Astrocyte and BBB dysfunction:</strong> promotes gliotoxicity and disrupts glutamate reuptake, amplifying excitotoxicity.</li>
</ul>
<p>These mechanisms link QUIN accumulation to <a href="/garden/itestb7bd9e0c/pathway/neuroinflammation/" class="internal-link">Neuroinflammation</a>-associated neuronal injury.</p>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>Elevated CSF and brain QUIN is documented in <a href="/garden/itestb7bd9e0c/condition/depression/" class="internal-link">Depression</a> (especially inflammation-associated and treatment-resistant forms), <a href="/garden/itestb7bd9e0c/condition/alzheimers-disease/" class="internal-link">Alzheimer's Disease</a>, <a href="/garden/itestb7bd9e0c/condition/parkinsons-disease/" class="internal-link">Parkinson's Disease</a>, HIV-associated neurocognitive disorder, and other neuroinflammatory conditions. The QUIN/kynurenic-acid ratio is used as an index of the pathway's neurotoxic versus neuroprotective balance.</p>
