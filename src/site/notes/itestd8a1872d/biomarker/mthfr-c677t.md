<h1>Mthfr C677T</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>The MTHFR C677T variant (rs1801133) is the most clinically studied single-nucleotide polymorphism in the methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase (MTHFR) gene on chromosome 1p36.3. The variant (cytosine → thymine at nucleotide 677) results in an alanine-to-valine substitution at position 222 of the protein, producing a <strong>thermolabile enzyme with substantially reduced catalytic activity</strong>. MTHFR catalyzes the irreversible reduction of 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate to 5-methyltetrahydrofolate (5-MTHF) -- the principal circulating form of folate and the methyl donor for the remethylation of homocysteine to methionine via methionine synthase (requires vitamin B12).</p>
<p>Enzyme activity reductions are gene-dose-dependent: <strong>heterozygous carriers (CT)</strong> retain ~65% of normal activity; <strong>homozygous TT</strong> retain only ~30%. The TT genotype is associated with elevated plasma homocysteine, particularly in the setting of low folate status -- a gene-nutrient interaction of major public health importance. Since the United States and ~80 other countries mandated folic acid fortification of cereal grains (US 1998), the clinical impact of MTHFR variants has substantially diminished, as adequate folate intake normalizes homocysteine even in TT carriers.</p>
<p>Population frequencies vary significantly by ethnicity: <strong>TT genotype is most common in Mediterranean (Italy, Spain ~18-25%) and Hispanic (Mexico ~15-32%) populations</strong>, intermediate in European and South Asian (~10-15%), and least common in African populations (~1-2%). This ethnic distribution likely reflects evolutionary selection related to historical folate availability and the protective effect against malaria (similar to G6PD deficiency).</p>
<p>The clinical significance of C677T remains debated. Meta-analyses consistently show <strong>TT genotype is associated with</strong>: (1) <strong>neural tube defects</strong> in offspring of TT mothers (relative risk ~1.5-2x); (2) <strong>modestly elevated cardiovascular risk</strong> (OR ~1.15-1.20 in large meta-analyses); (3) <strong>venous thromboembolism</strong> in some but not all studies; (4) <strong>recurrent pregnancy loss</strong> in some studies. However, the American College of Medical Genetics (ACMG) and American Congress of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) recommend <strong>against routine MTHFR testing</strong> because: (a) the variants are too common to be highly informative; (b) homocysteine measurement provides equivalent or superior information; (c) folate supplementation is recommended regardless of genotype. Despite these recommendations, MTHFR testing remains popular in functional medicine, nutrigenomic, and integrative medicine contexts.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level: 4/5</strong> (well-characterized mechanistically; clinical associations confirmed in meta-analyses but with small effect sizes; not in mainstream clinical guidelines).</p>

<h2>Factors Affecting Clinical Expression</h2>
<p>The C677T variant is a <strong>genetic marker</strong> and does not change over time. However, clinical expression (especially homocysteine elevation) is heavily modulated by:</p>
<p><strong>Aggravating factors (worsen biochemical phenotype in TT genotype):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Low folate intake (dietary or supplemental)</li>
<li>Low vitamin B12 status</li>
<li>High methionine / animal protein intake</li>
<li>Alcohol consumption (depletes folate)</li>
<li>Smoking (oxidative stress; depletes B vitamins)</li>
<li>Chronic illness</li>
<li>Renal insufficiency (impaired homocysteine excretion)</li>
<li>Hypothyroidism</li>
<li>Certain medications: methotrexate, phenytoin, trimethoprim, sulfasalazine, oral contraceptives, metformin (B12 depletion)</li>
<li>Male sex (higher homocysteine than premenopausal women)</li>
<li>Older age</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Mitigating factors (normalize phenotype even in TT):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Adequate folate intake</strong> (&gt;400 mcg/day) -- the dominant modifier</li>
<li>Adequate vitamin B12 status</li>
<li>Riboflavin (B2) -- MTHFR cofactor</li>
<li>Betaine (TMG) -- alternative homocysteine remethylation pathway</li>
<li>Vitamin B6 (P5P) -- transsulfuration cofactor</li>
<li>Post-fortification era (since 1998 in US): fortified cereals/grains provide ~100-200 mcg folic acid daily</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p><strong>C677T TT genotype has been associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Elevated homocysteine:</strong> the principal biochemical effect; magnitude depends heavily on folate status. In the pre-fortification era, TT homozygotes had homocysteine 2-5 umol/L higher than CC; post-fortification, the difference is much smaller</li>
<li><strong>Neural tube defects (NTDs):</strong> TT mothers have ~1.5-2x risk of NTD-affected pregnancies; periconceptional folate supplementation reduces risk regardless of genotype</li>
<li><strong>Cardiovascular disease:</strong> large meta-analyses show OR ~1.15-1.20 for CHD in TT; effect mediated by homocysteine</li>
<li><strong>Venous thromboembolism:</strong> modest association in some studies; not in others</li>
<li><strong>Recurrent pregnancy loss:</strong> mixed evidence; some studies show increased risk</li>
<li><strong>Stroke:</strong> meta-analyses show OR ~1.20 for ischemic stroke in TT</li>
<li><strong>Migraine (with aura):</strong> TT modestly associated with migraine</li>
<li><strong>Psychiatric disorders (schizophrenia, depression):</strong> mixed evidence; effect sizes small</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Compound heterozygosity (C677T + A1298C) has similar risk profile to TT genotype</strong></p>
<p><strong>Limitations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>ACMG and ACOG recommend against routine MTHFR testing</strong> -- variants are common, homocysteine provides equivalent information, folate supplementation is recommended regardless</li>
<li><strong>Effect sizes are small</strong> for most associations; meta-analyses show inconsistent results across populations</li>
<li><strong>Folate fortification</strong> (US since 1998) has substantially reduced clinical impact</li>
<li><strong>Functional medicine popularity</strong> despite guideline recommendations against</li>
<li><strong>Causal vs association:</strong> elevated homocysteine may be a marker rather than a cause of CV disease (homocysteine-lowering trials have been mostly negative for CV outcomes)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Monitoring use cases:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Functional medicine / nutrigenomic assessment (controversial)</li>
<li>Research: gene-nutrient interaction studies</li>
<li>Recurrent pregnancy loss workup (some clinicians)</li>
<li>Premature cardiovascular disease workup (some clinicians)</li>
<li>Persistent hyperhomocysteinemia of unclear cause</li>
<li>Should NOT be used as a screening test in asymptomatic individuals</li>
</ul>
<h2>Strategies to Manage</h2>
<p>MTHFR genotype cannot be changed, but its biochemical consequences (especially homocysteine elevation) are highly modifiable:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Methylfolate (5-MTHF) supplementation</strong> 400-1000 mcg daily -- preferred over synthetic folic acid for TT carriers; 5-MTHF bypasses the MTHFR enzyme entirely</li>
<li><strong>Vitamin B12 (methylcobalamin)</strong> 500-1000 mcg daily -- essential co-substrate for methionine synthase</li>
<li><strong>Betaine (TMG)</strong> 500-1000 mg daily -- alternative pathway for homocysteine remethylation (BHMT in liver and kidney)</li>
<li><strong>Vitamin B6 (P5P form)</strong> 50-100 mg daily -- cofactor for cystathionine beta-synthase (transsulfuration)</li>
<li><strong>Riboflavin (B2)</strong> 5-25 mg daily -- MTHFR cofactor; may stabilize variant enzyme</li>
<li><strong>Diet rich in natural folate:</strong> dark leafy greens, legumes, asparagus, avocado, Brussels sprouts</li>
<li><strong>Avoid excessive synthetic folic acid</strong> in variant carriers (&gt;1000 mcg/day) -- unmetabolized folic acid (UMFA) may accumulate</li>
<li><strong>Homocysteine monitoring</strong> in TT carriers with CV/pregnancy risk -- target &lt;10 umol/L (optimal &lt;8)</li>
<li><strong>Pre-conception supplementation</strong> for women with variant planning pregnancy -- methylfolate 400-800 mcg daily; ideally start 3-12 months before conception</li>
<li><strong>Alcohol moderation</strong> (depletes folate)</li>
<li><strong>Smoking cessation</strong></li>
<li><strong>Address comorbidities</strong> that raise homocysteine: hypothyroidism, renal insufficiency, certain medications</li>
</ol>
