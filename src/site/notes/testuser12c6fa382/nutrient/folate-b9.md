<h1>Folate (B9)</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Folate (vitamin B9) is a water-soluble B-vitamin essential for one-carbon metabolism, which underpins DNA synthesis, repair, and methylation reactions throughout the body. It serves as a cofactor for enzymes involved in nucleotide biosynthesis and is especially critical during periods of rapid cell division, including embryonic development, making it indispensable during pregnancy.</p>
<p>Folate plays a central role in the remethylation of homocysteine to methionine via methionine synthase (a vitamin B12-dependent enzyme). Elevated homocysteine, caused by folate or B12 deficiency, is an independent risk factor for cardiovascular disease and cognitive decline. The MTHFR C677T polymorphism, carried by approximately 10-15% of the population, reduces the enzyme methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase activity, impairing conversion of folic acid to the active 5-methyltetrahydrofolate (5-MTHF) form.</p>
<p>The bioactive form, 5-MTHF (methylfolate), bypasses the MTHFR bottleneck and is increasingly preferred in supplementation over synthetic folic acid. Adequate folate status supports mood regulation (via SAMe synthesis), red blood cell production, and immune function. Folate works synergistically with vitamins B12 and B6 in the methionine and transsulfuration cycles.</p>
<h2>Key Functions</h2>
<ul>
<li>Provides one-carbon units for DNA synthesis and repair</li>
<li>Remethylates homocysteine to methionine, lowering cardiovascular risk</li>
<li>Essential for neural tube development in early pregnancy</li>
<li>Supports red blood cell maturation and prevents megaloblastic anemia</li>
<li>Participates in epigenetic regulation through DNA methylation</li>
<li>Contributes to neurotransmitter synthesis (serotonin, dopamine) via SAMe</li>
</ul>

<h2>Deficiency and Excess</h2>
<p>Folate deficiency causes megaloblastic anemia (large, immature red blood cells), elevated homocysteine, fatigue, irritability, and mouth ulcers. In pregnancy, deficiency dramatically increases the risk of neural tube defects (spina bifida, anencephaly). Deficiency may also contribute to depression and cognitive decline.</p>
<p>Excessive folic acid (synthetic form) from supplements may mask vitamin B12 deficiency by correcting anemia while allowing neurological damage to progress undetected. Very high doses have been associated with potential immune dysfunction and, controversially, promotion of pre-existing colorectal neoplasia. The tolerable upper limit for synthetic folic acid is 1,000 mcg/day, though methylfolate does not carry the same masking risk.</p>
