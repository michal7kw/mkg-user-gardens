<h1>Melanoma</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Melanoma is a malignant tumor arising from <strong>melanocytes</strong> -- the neural crest-derived cells responsible for producing the pigment melanin. While melanoma accounts for only ~1% of skin cancers, it causes the <strong>vast majority of skin cancer deaths</strong> (~7,200 deaths/year in the US). The global incidence has risen rapidly over the past 50 years, especially in light-skinned populations with high UV exposure (Australia, New Zealand, North America, Northern Europe).</p>
<p>The primary cause is <strong>ultraviolet (UV) radiation</strong> exposure (both UVA and UVB), which causes DNA damage (C→T and CC→TT mutations) in melanocytes. The <strong>BRAF V600E mutation</strong> is present in ~50% of cutaneous melanomas and is a target for BRAF inhibitor therapy. Other driver mutations include NRAS (~15%), NF1 (~15%), and KIT (mucosal and acral melanomas).</p>
<p>Early detection is critical: <strong>5-year survival for localized melanoma is &gt;99%</strong>, but drops to ~35% for regional metastasis and ~10-15% for distant metastasis. The <strong>ABCDE criteria</strong> (Asymmetry, Border irregularity, Color variation, Diameter &gt;6mm, Evolving) guide clinical suspicion. <strong>Dermoscopy</strong> improves diagnostic accuracy.</p>
<p>The treatment of advanced melanoma has been revolutionized by <strong>immune checkpoint inhibitors</strong> (anti-PD-1: pembrolizumab, nivolumab; anti-CTLA-4: ipilimumab) and <strong>BRAF/MEK targeted therapy</strong> (dabrafenib + trametinib). These have transformed melanoma from a disease with near-uniform fatality to one with 5-year survival rates of 40-55% in advanced disease.</p>
<h2>Clinical Presentation</h2>
<p><strong>ABCDE criteria for suspicious pigmented lesions:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>A</strong>symmetry (one half unlike the other)</li>
<li><strong>B</strong>order irregularity (notched, scalloped, poorly defined)</li>
<li><strong>C</strong>olor variation (multiple shades of brown, black, blue, red, white)</li>
<li><strong>D</strong>iameter &gt;6 mm (though smaller melanomas occur)</li>
<li><strong>E</strong>volving (changing in size, shape, color, elevation, or new symptoms like itching/bleeding)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>The &quot;ugly duckling sign&quot;</strong> (a mole that looks different from the patient's other moles) is also valuable.</p>
<p><strong>Subtypes:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Superficial spreading melanoma</strong> (most common, ~70%)</li>
<li><strong>Nodular melanoma</strong> (~15-30%; aggressive; vertical growth)</li>
<li><strong>Lentigo maligna melanoma</strong> (~10-15%; older adults; sun-exposed face)</li>
<li><strong>Acral lentiginous melanoma</strong> (~5%; palms, soles, subungual; not UV-related)</li>
<li><strong>Mucosal melanoma</strong> (&lt;2%; oral, genital, rectal)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Diagnostic Information</h2>
<p><strong>Dermoscopy</strong> -- magnified, polarized light examination of skin lesions; improves sensitivity for melanoma detection<br />
<strong>Biopsy</strong> -- full-thickness excisional biopsy (preferred) or punch biopsy; <strong>Breslow thickness</strong> (depth of invasion in mm) is the most important prognostic factor<br />
<strong>Sentinel lymph node biopsy (SLNB)</strong> -- for melanomas &gt;0.8 mm Breslow depth (or &lt;0.8 mm with ulceration); provides staging information<br />
<strong>Molecular testing</strong> -- BRAF V600 mutation status (guides targeted therapy)<br />
<strong>Staging imaging</strong> -- CT, PET-CT, or MRI brain for stage III/IV</p>
<p><strong>AJCC TNM staging (8th edition):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Stage 0 (in situ): confined to epidermis</li>
<li>Stage I: &lt;=2.0 mm thickness, no ulceration, node-negative</li>
<li>Stage II: &gt;2.0 mm or any thickness with ulceration, node-negative</li>
<li>Stage III: regional lymph node or in-transit metastasis</li>
<li>Stage IV: distant metastasis</li>
</ul>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Surgical excision (primary treatment for all stages):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>In situ: 0.5-1.0 cm margin</li>
<li>&lt;=1.0 mm: 1.0 cm margin</li>
<li>1.01-2.0 mm: 1-2 cm margin</li>
<li>2.01-4.0 mm: 2.0 cm margin</li>
<li>
<blockquote>
<p>4.0 mm: 2.0 cm margin</p>
</blockquote>
</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Adjuvant therapy (Stage IIB-IV, after complete resection):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Anti-PD-1: nivolumab or pembrolizumab x 1 year</li>
<li>BRAF/MEK inhibitors (if BRAF V600 mutated): dabrafenib + trametinib</li>
<li>Combination: nivolumab + ipilimumab (high-risk Stage III/IV)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Advanced/metastatic melanoma (Stage IV):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>First-line: anti-PD-1 (nivolumab or pembrolizumab) ± anti-CTLA-4 (ipilimumab)</li>
<li>BRAF-mutated: BRAF/MEK inhibitors (dabrafenib + trametinib, encorafenib + binimetinib)</li>
<li>Intralesional: T-VEC (talimogene laherparepvec) for injectable cutaneous metastases</li>
<li>Radiation therapy: palliative or for brain metastases</li>
</ul>
