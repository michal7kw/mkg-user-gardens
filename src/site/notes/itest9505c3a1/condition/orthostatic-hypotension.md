<h1>Orthostatic Hypotension</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Orthostatic hypotension (OH) is defined as a <strong>sustained reduction in systolic blood pressure of &gt;=20 mmHg or diastolic &gt;=10 mmHg within 3 minutes of standing</strong> (or head-up tilt to 60 degrees). It is one of the most common causes of <strong>syncope and falls</strong> in older adults, with a prevalence of ~5-30% in those &gt;65 and &gt;50% in nursing home residents.</p>
<p>The normal physiological response to standing involves <strong>baroreceptor-mediated sympathetic activation</strong>: immediate vasoconstriction (splanchnic and peripheral), increased heart rate, and maintained cardiac output. In OH, this compensatory mechanism fails -- either because of <strong>impaired vasoconstriction</strong> (autonomic neuropathy, neurodegenerative disease, medications) or <strong>insufficient intravascular volume</strong> (dehydration, hemorrhage).</p>
<p><strong>Classification:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Neurogenic OH:</strong> from autonomic nervous system failure (central: Parkinson's, MSA, dementia with Lewy bodies; peripheral: diabetic autonomic neuropathy, amyloidosis, autoimmune autonomic ganglionopathy, paraneoplastic)</li>
<li><strong>Non-neurogenic OH:</strong> volume depletion, medications, cardiac (aortic stenosis, arrhythmia), venous pooling (prolonged standing, pregnancy, varicose veins)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Classic vs. delayed OH:</strong> Classic OH occurs within 3 minutes of standing. <strong>Delayed OH</strong> (or &quot;initial&quot; or &quot;progressive&quot; OH) occurs after 3 minutes -- sometimes 10-30 minutes of standing -- and is often missed by standard 3-minute BP measurement. A <strong>standing test for 10+ minutes</strong> or tilt-table testing may be needed.</p>
<p>The <strong>&quot;coat-hanger ache&quot;</strong> (suboccipital/paracervical pain) is a distinctive symptom caused by ischemia of the paracervical muscles during standing -- it resolves with sitting or lying down and is highly specific for neurogenic OH.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Non-pharmacologic (first-line, always):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Hydration:</strong> 2-3 L water/day; rapidly drink 500 mL water on waking</li>
<li><strong>Salt supplementation:</strong> 6-10 g/day (NaCl tablets or dietary); monitor for edema</li>
<li><strong>Slow positional changes:</strong> sit on edge of bed 1-2 min before standing; stand slowly; march in place before walking</li>
<li><strong>Avoid triggers:</strong> hot baths/showers, large carbohydrate meals (splanchnic pooling), alcohol, prolonged standing, straining (Valsalva)</li>
<li><strong>Elevate head of bed</strong> 10-20 degrees (reduces nocturnal diuresis and supine hypertension)</li>
<li><strong>Compression garments:</strong> abdominal binder &gt; thigh-high stockings &gt; knee-high; apply before standing</li>
<li><strong>Exercise:</strong> leg-strengthening, swimming/recumbent cycling (avoid upright exercise)</li>
<li><strong>Review and adjust medications:</strong> reduce antihypertensives, diuretics, alpha-blockers; consider switching PPIs (may cause Mg deficiency)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Pharmacologic:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Fludrocortisone</strong> 0.1-0.2 mg/day (mineralocorticoid; expands plasma volume; watch for edema, hypokalemia, supine hypertension)</li>
<li><strong>Midodrine</strong> 5-10 mg TID (alpha-1 agonist; vasoconstricts arterioles and venules; FDA-approved; avoids CNS effects; last dose before 6 PM to avoid supine hypertension)</li>
<li><strong>Droxidopa</strong> (Northera) 100-600 mg TID (synthetic norepinephrine precursor; FDA-approved for neurogenic OH; for Parkinson's, MSA, PAF)</li>
<li><strong>Pyridostigmine</strong> 30-60 mg TID (cholinesterase inhibitor; improves ganglionic transmission; modest effect; less supine hypertension)</li>
<li><strong>Atomoxetine</strong> 18 mg (norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor; emerging evidence)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Goal:</strong> improve standing time and reduce falls, NOT normalize standing BP</p>
