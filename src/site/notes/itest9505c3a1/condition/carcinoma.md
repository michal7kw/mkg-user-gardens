<h1>Carcinoma</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Carcinoma is a malignant neoplasm derived from <strong>epithelial cells</strong> -- the cells that line internal organs, glands, and the skin surface. It is the <strong>most common histological type of cancer</strong>, accounting for approximately 80-90% of all malignancies. Carcinomas are further classified by cell type: <strong>adenocarcinoma</strong> (glandular epithelium; lung, breast, prostate, colon, pancreas), <strong>squamous cell carcinoma</strong> (squamous epithelium; skin, esophagus, cervix, lung), <strong>ductal carcinoma</strong> (breast ducts), <strong>transitional cell carcinoma</strong> (urinary tract), and <strong>basal cell carcinoma</strong> (skin).</p>
<p>The molecular pathogenesis involves accumulation of <strong>genetic and epigenetic alterations</strong> that confer the hallmarks of cancer: sustained proliferative signaling, evasion of growth suppressors, resistance to apoptosis, replicative immortality, angiogenesis, invasion and metastasis, reprogrammed energy metabolism, and immune evasion. Key driver mutations vary by carcinoma type (e.g., EGFR/KRAS in lung adenocarcinoma; HER2/ER/PR in breast; KRAS/APC in colorectal; BRAF in melanoma).</p>
<p>Cancer is the <strong>second leading cause of death globally</strong> (after cardiovascular disease). Tobacco use is the single most important preventable cause, responsible for ~22% of cancer deaths. Early detection through organized screening programs (mammography, colonoscopy, Pap smear, PSA, CT lung screening) significantly reduces mortality for several carcinoma types.</p>
<h2>Clinical Presentation</h2>
<p>Carcinoma presentation varies widely by organ of origin:</p>
<p><strong>General cancer symptoms (late or advanced):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Unexplained weight loss</li>
<li>Fatigue, weakness</li>
<li>Night sweats</li>
<li>Anorexia</li>
<li>Recurrent fevers</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Organ-specific symptoms:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Lung:</strong> persistent cough, hemoptysis, dyspnea, chest pain</li>
<li><strong>Breast:</strong> palpable mass, nipple discharge, skin dimpling</li>
<li><strong>Colorectal:</strong> change in bowel habits, rectal bleeding, abdominal pain</li>
<li><strong>Prostate:</strong> urinary symptoms, bone pain (metastatic)</li>
<li><strong>Pancreas:</strong> jaundice, weight loss, epigastric pain</li>
<li><strong>Skin:</strong> non-healing lesion, changing mole (ABCDE criteria)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Diagnostic Information</h2>
<p><strong>Tissue diagnosis (gold standard):</strong> biopsy with histopathology and immunohistochemistry<br />
<strong>Molecular testing:</strong> next-generation sequencing for actionable mutations (EGFR, KRAS, BRAF, HER2, etc.)<br />
<strong>Staging:</strong> TNM system (Tumor size, Node involvement, Metastasis)<br />
<strong>Tumor markers:</strong> organ-specific (CEA, CA 125, CA 19-9, AFP, PSA)<br />
<strong>Imaging:</strong> CT, MRI, PET-CT, ultrasound</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p>Multidisciplinary approach tailored to type, stage, and patient factors:</p>
<p><strong>Surgery:</strong> primary curative treatment for localized disease<br />
<strong>Radiation therapy:</strong> definitive (some head/neck, cervix), adjuvant, or palliative<br />
<strong>Systemic therapy:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Chemotherapy:</strong> cytotoxic (platinum, taxane, antimetabolite)</li>
<li><strong>Targeted therapy:</strong> EGFR TKIs, HER2 antibodies, BRAF/MEK inhibitors, PARP inhibitors</li>
<li><strong>Immunotherapy:</strong> anti-PD-1/PD-L1 (pembrolizumab, nivolumab), anti-CTLA-4 (ipilimumab)</li>
<li><strong>Hormone therapy:</strong> endocrine manipulation (breast, prostate)</li>
</ul>
