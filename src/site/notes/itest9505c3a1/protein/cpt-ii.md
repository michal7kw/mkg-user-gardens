<h1>CPT-II (Carnitine Palmitoyltransferase II)</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>CPT-II completes the carnitine shuttle system on the matrix side of the inner mitochondrial membrane. It regenerates <a href="/garden/itest9505c3a1/metabolite/fatty-acyl-coa/" class="internal-link">Fatty Acyl-CoA</a> from <a href="/garden/itest9505c3a1/metabolite/acylcarnitine/" class="internal-link">Acylcarnitine</a>, making the fatty acid chain available for <a href="/garden/itest9505c3a1/pathway/beta-oxidation/" class="internal-link">Beta-Oxidation</a> in the mitochondrial matrix.</p>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Reaction</strong>: Acylcarnitine + CoA → fatty acyl-CoA + L-carnitine</li>
<li><strong>Carnitine recycling</strong>: Free carnitine is returned to the intermembrane space via carnitine-acylcarnitine translocase</li>
<li><strong>Not rate-limiting</strong>: Unlike <a href="/garden/itest9505c3a1/protein/cpt-i/" class="internal-link">CPT-I</a>, CPT-II is not subject to malonyl-CoA inhibition</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>CPT-II deficiency</strong> is the most common inherited disorder of fatty acid oxidation</li>
<li>Three forms: lethal neonatal, severe infantile hepatocardiomuscular, and adult myopathic (exercise-induced rhabdomyolysis)</li>
<li>Adult form presents as recurrent rhabdomyolysis triggered by prolonged exercise, fasting, cold, or infection</li>
</ul>
