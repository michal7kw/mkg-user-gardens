<h1>MAPK Pathway</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>The MAPK pathway transmits extracellular signals from growth factors to the nucleus via the canonical <strong>Ras-Raf-MEK-ERK</strong> cascade. It regulates cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival. Mutations in <strong>BRAF</strong> (V600E in melanoma) and <strong>RAS</strong> (KRAS, NRAS) drive many cancers.</p>
<h2>Key Components</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>RAS</strong> (KRAS, NRAS, HRAS) -- small GTPase</li>
<li><strong>RAF</strong> (BRAF, RAF1) -- serine/threonine kinase</li>
<li><strong>MEK</strong> (MAP2K1/2) -- dual-specificity kinase</li>
<li><strong>ERK</strong> (MAPK1/3) -- terminal kinase; enters nucleus</li>
</ul>
<h2>Therapeutic Targeting</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>BRAF inhibitors</strong> (vemurafenib, dabrafenib) -- BRAF V600E melanoma</li>
<li><strong>MEK inhibitors</strong> (trametinib, cobimetinib) -- combined with BRAF inhibitors</li>
<li><strong>KRAS G12C inhibitors</strong> (sotorasib, adagrasib) -- KRAS-mutant NSCLC</li>
</ul>
