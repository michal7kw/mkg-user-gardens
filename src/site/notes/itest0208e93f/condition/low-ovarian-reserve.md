<h1>Low Ovarian Reserve</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Low ovarian reserve (LOR), also called diminished ovarian reserve (DOR), is defined as a <strong>reduced quantity of remaining oocytes</strong> compared to age-matched peers, resulting in <strong>reduced fertility</strong> and <strong>diminished response to ovarian stimulation</strong> during assisted reproductive technology (ART). It is distinct from <strong>premature ovarian insufficiency (POI)</strong>, which additionally involves menstrual irregularity and hypoestrogenism.</p>
<p>A female fetus has approximately <strong>6-7 million oocytes at 20 weeks gestation</strong>, declining to <strong>1-2 million at birth</strong>, <strong>300,000-500,000 at puberty</strong>, and fewer than <strong>1,000 at menopause</strong> (average age 51). The rate of oocyte loss accelerates after approximately <strong>age 32</strong> and steeply after <strong>age 37</strong>. Women with LOR have oocyte counts below the expected range for their age, though the absolute number is not directly measurable in life.</p>
<p><strong>Diagnostic criteria (Bologna criteria for poor ovarian response; modified):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>AMH &lt;1.1 ng/mL</strong> (most sensitive single marker; reflects growing follicle pool)</li>
<li><strong>Day-3 FSH &gt;10 IU/L</strong> (less reliable; fluctuates cycle-to-cycle; must repeat)</li>
<li><strong>AFC (antral follicle count) &lt;5-7</strong> on transvaginal ultrasound (day 2-5 of cycle)</li>
<li><strong>Age &gt;40</strong> or prior poor response to ovarian stimulation</li>
</ul>
<p>AMH is the <strong>best single test</strong> because it is cycle-day independent, relatively stable between cycles, and correlates well with oocyte yield during IVF. Levels &lt;0.5 ng/mL indicate severely diminished reserve.</p>
<p>Management focuses on <strong>fertility preservation</strong> (if future childbearing desired) and <strong>optimized IVF protocols</strong>. The prognosis for live birth depends primarily on <strong>age</strong> and <strong>AMH level</strong>, with oocyte quality declining in parallel with quantity.</p>
