<h1>Bladder Disease</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Bladder disease encompasses a broad spectrum of disorders affecting the urinary bladder, including <strong>infections</strong> (cystitis; the most common), <strong>functional disorders</strong> (overactive bladder, neurogenic bladder, interstitial cystitis), <strong>structural abnormalities</strong> (bladder diverticula, vesicoureteral reflux), <strong>stones</strong> (bladder calculi), and <strong>neoplasms</strong> (bladder cancer). The bladder's dual function -- storage and controlled voiding of urine -- means that any bladder disorder can significantly impact quality of life.</p>
<p><strong>Common categories:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Urinary tract infection (cystitis):</strong> bacterial infection of the bladder mucosa; most common in women due to short urethra; typically caused by <em>E. coli</em></li>
<li><strong>Overactive bladder (OAB):</strong> urgency, frequency, and urge incontinence without identifiable cause; affects ~12% of adults; treated with anticholinergics or beta-3 agonists</li>
<li><strong>Neurogenic bladder:</strong> bladder dysfunction from neurological disease (spinal cord injury, MS, Parkinson's); may cause retention or incontinence</li>
<li><strong>Interstitial cystitis/bladder pain syndrome (IC/BPS):</strong> chronic bladder pain, urgency, frequency without infection; challenging to treat</li>
<li><strong>Bladder cancer:</strong> urothelial (transitional cell) carcinoma; strongly associated with smoking</li>
<li><strong>Bladder stones:</strong> typically secondary to bladder outlet obstruction (BPH) or foreign bodies (catheters)</li>
</ul>
