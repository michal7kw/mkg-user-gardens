<h1>Icosapent Ethyl</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Icosapent ethyl is a prescription omega-3 fatty acid consisting of a highly purified ethyl ester of eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA). It is distinct from mixed fish-oil supplements and from combination EPA/DHA prescription products because it contains essentially no docosahexaenoic acid (DHA), an important distinction because DHA can raise LDL cholesterol. It is indicated as an adjunct to diet for severe hypertriglyceridemia and, on the basis of outcome data, for cardiovascular risk reduction.</p>
<p>In the REDUCE-IT trial (PMID: 30415628), icosapent ethyl 4 g/day reduced the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events by about 25% in statin-treated patients with elevated triglycerides and either established cardiovascular disease or diabetes plus risk factors. This built on earlier signals from JELIS (PMID: 17398308), which showed EPA reduced major coronary events in hypercholesterolemic Japanese patients. The magnitude of cardiovascular benefit exceeded what triglyceride lowering alone would predict, suggesting pleiotropic mechanisms.</p>
<h2>Pharmacological Information</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Class:</strong> Omega-3 fatty acid, purified EPA ethyl ester</li>
<li><strong>Route:</strong> Oral capsule, typically 2 g twice daily (4 g/day total) with food</li>
<li><strong>Composition:</strong> EPA only (no DHA), differentiating it from mixed omega-3 products</li>
<li><strong>Metabolism:</strong> EPA is hydrolyzed and oxidized via beta-oxidation; minimal hepatic CYP involvement</li>
</ul>
<h2>Mechanism of Action</h2>
<p>EPA reduces hepatic synthesis and secretion of very-low-density lipoprotein (VLDL) triglycerides and increases triglyceride clearance, lowering serum triglycerides. Beyond lipid effects, EPA incorporates into cell membranes and is proposed to exert anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, plaque-stabilizing, and antithrombotic actions, and to improve endothelial function — mechanisms thought to underlie the cardiovascular event reduction seen in REDUCE-IT that is disproportionate to its triglyceride-lowering effect.</p>
<h2>Clinical Features</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Hypertriglyceridemia:</strong> Lowers triglycerides by roughly 20-30% without raising LDL cholesterol</li>
<li><strong>Cardiovascular risk reduction:</strong> ~25% relative reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events in REDUCE-IT (PMID: 30415628)</li>
<li><strong>Population:</strong> Statin-treated patients with triglycerides ~135-500 mg/dL and high cardiovascular risk</li>
</ul>
<h2>Safety Information</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Common side effects:</strong> Musculoskeletal pain, peripheral edema, constipation, gout</li>
<li><strong>Serious effects:</strong> Increased risk of atrial fibrillation/flutter; increased bleeding risk, especially with antiplatelet/anticoagulant therapy</li>
<li><strong>Interactions:</strong> Additive bleeding risk with anticoagulants and antiplatelet agents</li>
<li><strong>Contraindications:</strong> Known hypersensitivity to icosapent ethyl; caution in patients with fish/shellfish allergy</li>
</ul>
