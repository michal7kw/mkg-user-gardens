<h1>Ocular Hypertension</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Ocular hypertension (OHT) is defined as <strong>intraocular pressure (IOP) &gt;21 mmHg</strong> in one or both eyes, <strong>without evidence of glaucomatous optic nerve damage or visual field loss</strong>. It is distinguished from glaucoma by the absence of optic nerve cupping, visual field defects, or retinal nerve fiber layer thinning. OHT is a significant <strong>risk factor</strong> for developing primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG): approximately <strong>4.4% of OHT patients per year</strong> convert to glaucoma without treatment, declining to ~2% per year with IOP-lowering therapy.</p>
<p>The <strong>Ocular Hypertension Treatment Study (OHTS)</strong>, a landmark trial (Gordon et al., 2002), established the key predictors of glaucoma conversion:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Higher baseline IOP</strong> (each mmHg increases risk)</li>
<li><strong>Older age</strong></li>
<li><strong>Larger cup-to-disc ratio</strong></li>
<li><strong>Higher pattern standard deviation</strong> (early visual field change)</li>
<li><strong>Thinner central cornea</strong> (&lt;555 micrometers is a major risk factor; artifactual underestimation of true IBP is not the sole mechanism -- thin corneas may be associated with weaker optic nerve structural support)</li>
</ol>
<p>The decision to treat OHT is individualized. The OHTS showed that IOP-lowering reduces conversion to glaucoma by ~50% over 5 years, but many OHT patients (especially with low risk) can be monitored without treatment. <strong>Risk calculators</strong> (e.g., STAR, OHTS-EGPS) help guide treatment decisions.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Risk stratification (OHTS predictors):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Age, IOP level, cup-to-disc ratio, pattern standard deviation, central corneal thickness, family history</li>
<li>Calculate 5-year risk using STAR or OHTS-EGPS calculator</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Low-risk (&lt;5% 5-year risk):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Observation with annual monitoring (IOP, optic nerve, visual field, OCT)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Moderate-to-high risk (&gt;=5% 5-year risk):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Topical IOP-lowering therapy</strong> (target ~20-30% reduction from baseline IOP):
<ul>
<li>Prostaglandin analogs: latanoprost, travoprost, bimatoprost (first-line; most effective; once daily)</li>
<li>Beta-blockers: timolol (second-line; avoid in asthma/bradycardia)</li>
<li>Alpha-2 agonists: brimonidine</li>
<li>Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors: dorzolamide, brinzolamide</li>
<li>Rho kinase inhibitors: netarsudil (newer class)</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Selective laser trabeculoplasty (SLT)</strong> -- increasingly used as first-line alternative to drops</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Monitoring:</strong> annual comprehensive eye exam with:</p>
<ul>
<li>Tonometry (IOP)</li>
<li>Optic nerve evaluation (stereoscopic photography, OCT RNFL)</li>
<li>Visual field testing (automated perimetry)</li>
<li>Gonioscopy (angle assessment; periodic)</li>
</ul>
