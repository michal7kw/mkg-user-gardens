<h1>Sirtuin Signaling Pathway</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>The sirtuins are a family of seven mammalian enzymes (SIRT1-7) that catalyze NAD+-dependent removal of acetyl and other acyl groups from lysine residues on histones and metabolic proteins. Because every catalytic cycle consumes <a href="/garden/testuser184884fdf/metabolite/nad/" class="internal-link">NAD+</a> and releases nicotinamide, sirtuin activity is directly tied to the cell's energy and redox state: when NAD+ is abundant (fasting, exercise, caloric restriction), sirtuins are active; when NAD+ falls (overnutrition, aging), they are suppressed. This makes the pathway a master sensor that translates nutrient availability into adaptive changes in metabolism, chromatin state, and stress resistance.</p>
<p>Sirtuins are among the most studied longevity effectors. Their activation reproduces many of the protective effects of caloric restriction, and their decline with age — secondary to falling NAD+ — is implicated in metabolic and neurodegenerative disease.</p>
<h2>Key Components</h2>
<p>The seven sirtuins differ by subcellular location and substrate:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>SIRT1</strong> (nucleus/cytoplasm): deacetylates PGC-1alpha, FOXO3a, p53, and NF-kB; the principal metabolic and longevity sirtuin.</li>
<li><strong>SIRT2</strong> (cytoplasm): tubulin and cell-cycle regulation.</li>
<li><strong>SIRT3, SIRT4, SIRT5</strong> (mitochondrial): control fuel oxidation, the urea cycle, and antioxidant defense (SIRT3 deacetylates SOD2).</li>
<li><strong>SIRT6, SIRT7</strong> (nucleus): genome stability, DNA repair, and ribosomal regulation; SIRT6 is required for normal lifespan in mice.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Regulation</h2>
<p>Activity is governed primarily by <strong>NAD+ availability</strong>. Upstream inputs that raise NAD+ or the NAD+/NADH ratio — <a href="/garden/testuser184884fdf/intervention/caloric-restriction/" class="internal-link">Caloric Restriction</a>, <a href="/garden/testuser184884fdf/intervention/intermittent-fasting/" class="internal-link">Intermittent Fasting</a>, <a href="/garden/testuser184884fdf/intervention/exercise/" class="internal-link">Exercise</a>, and NAD+ precursors — activate the pathway. Polyphenols such as <a href="/garden/testuser184884fdf/supplement/resveratrol/" class="internal-link">Resveratrol</a> and <a href="/garden/testuser184884fdf/supplement/pterostilbene/" class="internal-link">Pterostilbene</a> are reported allosteric SIRT1 activators. Nicotinamide, the reaction product, is a feedback inhibitor.</p>
<h2>Physiological Role</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Mitochondrial biogenesis:</strong> SIRT1 → PGC-1alpha deacetylation drives new mitochondria and oxidative metabolism.</li>
<li><strong>Stress resistance &amp; autophagy:</strong> FOXO3a deacetylation upregulates antioxidant genes and promotes <a href="/garden/testuser184884fdf/pathway/autophagy/" class="internal-link">Autophagy</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Genome stability:</strong> SIRT1/SIRT6 support DNA repair and silence repetitive elements.</li>
<li><strong>Metabolic crosstalk:</strong> reciprocal regulation with <a href="/garden/testuser184884fdf/pathway/ampk-pathway/" class="internal-link">AMPK Pathway</a> (energy sensing) and antagonism of growth signaling through <a href="/garden/testuser184884fdf/pathway/mtor-pathway/" class="internal-link">mTOR Pathway</a>.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Relevance</h2>
<p>Reduced sirtuin signaling is linked to insulin resistance, metabolic syndrome, and neurodegeneration; pharmacological or nutritional activation (CR mimetics, NAD+ precursors, STACs) is under investigation for healthspan extension and metabolic disease.</p>
