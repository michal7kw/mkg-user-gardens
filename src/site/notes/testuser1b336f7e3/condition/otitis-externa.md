<h1>Otitis Externa</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Otitis externa (OE) is an infection of the external auditory canal, affecting the skin from the tympanic membrane to the pinna. The most common pathogens are <strong>Pseudomonas aeruginosa</strong> (especially in swimmers and hot climates) and <strong>Staphylococcus aureus</strong>. Fungal causes (Aspergillus, Candida) account for ~10% of cases, typically after prolonged antibiotic drop use. The warm, dark, moist environment of the ear canal predisposes to microbial overgrowth, especially after swimming (&quot;swimmer's ear&quot;).</p>
<p>Most cases are uncomplicated and resolve within 7-10 days with <strong>topical antibiotic drops</strong> (often combined with a corticosteroid). <strong>Malignant (necrotizing) otitis externa</strong> is a rare but life-threatening complication seen predominantly in <strong>elderly diabetics and immunocompromised patients</strong>, where <em>Pseudomonas</em> infection spreads to the skull base causing osteomyelitis, cranial nerve palsies (especially CN VII), and intracranial extension.</p>
<h2>Clinical Presentation</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Otalgia</strong> (ear pain), worse with tragal manipulation or auricular traction (distinguishes from otitis media)</li>
<li><strong>Pruritus</strong> (itching)</li>
<li><strong>Otorrhea</strong> (discharge: purulent, serous, or serosanguineous)</li>
<li><strong>Erythema and edema</strong> of the ear canal</li>
<li><strong>Hearing loss</strong> (conductive; from canal occlusion by edema or debris)</li>
<li><strong>Feeling of fullness</strong> or pressure</li>
<li><strong>Malignant OE:</strong> severe unrelenting pain out of proportion to exam, granulation tissue at osseocartilaginous junction, cranial nerve palsy</li>
</ul>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Uncomplicated OE:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Topical antibiotic drops</strong> (ofloxacin 0.3%, ciprofloxacin 0.3%) x 7 days</li>
<li><strong>Combination drops</strong> (antibiotic + corticosteroid: ciprofloxacin/dexamethasone, neomycin/polymyxin/hydrocortisone)</li>
<li><strong>Analgesics</strong> (acetaminophen, ibuprofen)</li>
<li><strong>Ear wick</strong> (Pope wick) if canal too swollen for drops to penetrate</li>
<li><strong>Aural toilet</strong> (gentle cleaning/debridement)</li>
<li><strong>Keep ear dry</strong> during treatment (no swimming)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Malignant (necrotizing) OE:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>IV antibiotics</strong> (anti-pseudomonal: ceftazidime, cefepime, piperacillin-tazobactam)</li>
<li><strong>Surgical debridement</strong> of necrotic tissue</li>
<li><strong>Hyperbaric oxygen</strong> (adjunct)</li>
<li><strong>Diabetes control</strong></li>
<li>Duration: 6+ weeks</li>
</ul>
