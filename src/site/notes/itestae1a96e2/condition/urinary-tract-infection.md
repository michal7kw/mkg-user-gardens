<h1>Urinary Tract Infection</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Urinary tract infection (UTI) is one of the most common bacterial infections, affecting approximately <strong>150 million people annually</strong> worldwide. It involves any part of the urinary system: urethra (urethritis), bladder (cystitis), or kidneys (pyelonephritis). The vast majority of UTIs are <strong>uncomplicated cystitis</strong> in otherwise healthy non-pregnant adult women. UTIs are classified as <strong>uncomplicated</strong> (healthy adult woman, lower tract) or <strong>complicated</strong> (male, pregnant, diabetic, immunocompromised, obstructed urinary tract, catheter-associated, or involving the kidney).</p>
<p><strong>Etiology:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Escherichia coli</strong> (~75-80% of uncomplicated UTIs)</li>
<li><strong>Klebsiella pneumoniae</strong> (~5-10%)</li>
<li><strong>Proteus mirabilis</strong> (~5%; associated with stones)</li>
<li><strong>Staphylococcus saprophyticus</strong> (~5-10%; young sexually active women)</li>
<li><strong>Enterococcus, Enterobacter, Pseudomonas</strong> (complicated/healthcare-associated)</li>
</ul>
<p>The pathogenesis involves <strong>ascending infection</strong>: bacteria from the perineal/perianal area enter the urethra, ascend to the bladder, and adhere to uroepithelial cells via <strong>fimbriae</strong> (P-fimbriae in pyelonephritis). Women are at 30x higher risk than men due to shorter urethra and proximity to the anus.</p>
<p><strong>Antibiotic resistance</strong> is an increasing problem: E. coli fluoroquinolone resistance exceeds 10-20% in many regions, prompting recommendations to use <strong>nitrofurantoin or TMP-SMX</strong> as first-line agents for uncomplicated cystitis.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Uncomplicated cystitis (non-pregnant adult women):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Nitrofurantoin</strong> 100 mg BID x 5 days (first-line; minimal resistance)</li>
<li><strong>TMP-SMX</strong> DS BID x 3 days (if local E. coli resistance &lt;20%)</li>
<li><strong>Fosfomycin</strong> 3 g single dose (convenient but slightly less effective)</li>
<li><strong>Cephalexin</strong> 500 mg QID x 5-7 days (pregnancy-safe)</li>
<li><strong>Pivmecillinam</strong> 400 mg BID x 5-7 days (available in some countries)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Pyelonephritis (outpatient):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Ciprofloxacin</strong> 500 mg BID x 7 days</li>
<li><strong>Levofloxacin</strong> 750 mg daily x 5 days</li>
<li><strong>Ceftriaxone</strong> 1 g IV/IM daily x 10-14 days</li>
<li>Add <strong>initial IV dose</strong> if fluoroquinolone used (ensures rapid therapeutic levels)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Pregnancy:</strong> cephalexin, nitrofurantoin (avoid at term), or fosfomycin; avoid fluoroquinolones and TMP-SMX</p>
<p><strong>Catheter-associated UTI (CAUTI):</strong> remove or change catheter; treat only if symptomatic; 7 days if rapid response, 10-14 if delayed</p>
<p><strong>Recurrent UTI prevention (&gt;=3 per year):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Post-coital prophylaxis:</strong> single-dose TMP-SMX or nitrofurantoin</li>
<li><strong>Continuous prophylaxis:</strong> TMP-SMX daily or nitrofurantoin 50-100 mg nightly x 6 months</li>
<li><strong>Topical vaginal estrogen</strong> (postmenopausal women; reduces recurrence ~60%)</li>
<li><strong>Cranberry</strong> products (modest evidence; may reduce adherence)</li>
<li><strong>D-mannose</strong> (2 g daily; evidence growing)</li>
<li><strong>Methenamine hippurate</strong> (urinary antiseptic)</li>
</ul>
