<h1>EGR1</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>EGR1 (Early Growth Response Protein 1; UniProt <a href="https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P18146">P18146</a>, 543 amino acids, ~57.5 kDa) is a Cys2-His2 zinc-finger transcription factor and a prototypical <strong>immediate early gene (IEG)</strong>. It is transcribed within minutes of cell stimulation — by growth factors, mitogens, cellular stress, ischemia, and, in neurons, by activity-dependent calcium influx — without requiring prior protein synthesis. Once expressed, EGR1 binds GC-rich EGR response elements in target promoters and switches on downstream &quot;late&quot; genes, linking a transient extracellular signal to a durable change in gene expression.</p>
<h2>Function and Mechanism</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Signal-activated transcription factor.</strong> EGR1 recognises a GC-rich consensus element through three tandem zinc fingers and regulates programmes governing cell growth, differentiation, survival, and apoptosis.</li>
<li><strong>Neuronal plasticity and memory.</strong> In the hippocampus and cortex, NMDA-receptor-dependent calcium entry during high-frequency activity rapidly induces EGR1. The factor is required for the transcription that consolidates <strong>long-term potentiation (LTP)</strong> into stable synaptic change — a core step in converting short-term activity into long-term memory.</li>
<li><strong>Vascular and inflammatory signalling.</strong> In endothelium, shear stress and hypoxia induce EGR1, which then drives transcription of pro-coagulant and pro-inflammatory mediators.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Relevance</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Alzheimer's disease / memory impairment.</strong> Disrupted activity-dependent EGR1 induction impairs hippocampal memory consolidation, a mechanism implicated in the learning and memory deficits seen in Alzheimer's disease.</li>
<li><strong>Cardiovascular disease.</strong> Endothelial EGR1 activation by haemodynamic stress promotes the inflammatory and thrombotic gene expression that contributes to atherosclerosis.</li>
<li><strong>Cancer.</strong> EGR1 is context-dependent — acting as a tumour suppressor in some tissues (e.g. via p53/PTEN) and as a growth-promoting factor in others.</li>
</ul>
