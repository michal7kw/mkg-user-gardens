<h1>MTHFR Gene Variants</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>The MTHFR (methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase) gene encodes the enzyme that converts 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate to 5-methyltetrahydrofolate (5-MTHF) -- the principal circulating form of folate and the methyl donor for homocysteine remethylation to methionine. Two common polymorphisms reduce enzyme activity:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>C677T (rs1801133):</strong> alanine-to-valine at position 222; produces a thermolabile enzyme with reduced activity. <strong>CT genotype: ~65% activity; TT genotype: ~30% activity</strong>. TT frequency: ~10-15% of Europeans, ~15-32% of Hispanics.</li>
<li><strong>A1298C (rs1801131):</strong> glutamate-to-alanine at position 429. <strong>CC genotype: ~60-70% activity</strong>. Less clinically significant alone but <strong>compound heterozygosity</strong> (C677T + A1298C) produces ~40-50% activity.</li>
</ul>
<p>Clinical significance remains <strong>debated</strong>:</p>
<ul>
<li>The American College of Medical Genetics (ACMG) recommends <strong>against routine MTHFR testing</strong> -- variants are too common, clinical actionability is limited, and folate supplementation is recommended regardless of genotype</li>
<li>Meta-analyses show TT genotype is associated with a <strong>modestly increased cardiovascular risk</strong> (OR ~1.15-1.20) -- largely mediated by homocysteine</li>
<li>Post-folic acid fortification (US 1998+), the clinical impact has substantially diminished because most people get adequate folate from fortified foods</li>
<li>The TT genotype remains a risk factor for <strong>neural tube defects</strong> in offspring of TT mothers (pre-conception folate supplementation is recommended regardless of genotype)</li>
</ul>
<p>Management focuses on <strong>folate and B-vitamin sufficiency</strong>, not genotype-specific therapy. <strong>Methylfolate (5-MTHF)</strong> is preferred in functional/nutrigenomic practice because it bypasses the MTHFR enzyme entirely.</p>
