<h1>Pulmonary Sarcoidosis</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Sarcoidosis is a multisystem granulomatous disease of unknown etiology characterized by the formation of <strong>non-caseating granulomas</strong> in affected organs. The lungs and intrathoracic lymph nodes are involved in &gt;90% of cases. The disease affects approximately <strong>10-40 per 100,000</strong> in the US, with a markedly higher incidence in <strong>African Americans</strong> (~3x higher than Caucasians) and those of <strong>Scandinavian</strong> descent. Peak age of onset is <strong>20-40 years</strong>.</p>
<p>The pathogenesis involves an <strong>exaggerated immune response</strong> to an unknown antigen in genetically susceptible individuals. CD4+ T-cells and macrophages accumulate at sites of disease and release cytokines (IL-2, IFN-gamma, TNF-alpha) that drive granuloma formation. Granulomas are organized collections of epithelioid macrophages, multinucleated giant cells, and lymphocytes that wall off the inciting antigen but also cause tissue damage and fibrosis in chronic disease.</p>
<p><strong>Clinical presentations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Acute (Löfgren syndrome):</strong> triad of <strong>erythema nodosum</strong> (painful red nodules on shins), <strong>bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy</strong>, and <strong>polyarthritis</strong> +/- fever. Excellent prognosis (&gt;80% spontaneous remission within 2 years).</li>
<li><strong>Insidious/chronic:</strong> progressive dyspnea, cough, fatigue over months to years. More likely to progress to fibrosis.</li>
<li><strong>Asymptomatic:</strong> detected incidentally on chest imaging (bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy).</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Extrapulmonary manifestations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Hypercalcemia/hypercalciuria:</strong> sarcoid granulomas express <strong>1-alpha-hydroxylase</strong>, converting 25-OH vitamin D to active 1,25-OH2 vitamin D (calcitriol). This causes increased intestinal calcium absorption -&gt; hypercalcemia, hypercalciuria, nephrocalcinosis, nephrolithiasis.</li>
<li><strong>Ocular:</strong> anterior and posterior uveitis</li>
<li><strong>Cardiac:</strong> granulomatous infiltration causing arrhythmias, conduction blocks, heart failure (leading cause of sarcoid-related death in Japan)</li>
<li><strong>Neurological:</strong> cranial nerve palsies (especially CN VII; Bell's palsy), hypothalamic involvement</li>
<li><strong>Cutaneous:</strong> lupus pernio (chronic violaceous lesions on nose/cheeks/ears; associated with chronic pulmonary disease), erythema nodosum</li>
</ul>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>When to treat:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Asymptomatic Stage I-II:</strong> observation (high rate of spontaneous remission)</li>
<li><strong>Symptomatic disease</strong> (cough, dyspnea) or <strong>progressive radiographic changes:</strong> initiate treatment</li>
<li><strong>Organ-threatening disease</strong> (cardiac, neurological, ocular, hypercalcemia): treat regardless of symptoms</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>First-line:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Prednisone</strong> 20-40 mg/day (or 0.5-1.0 mg/kg/day) for 4-6 weeks, then taper slowly over 6-12+ months</li>
<li>Most patients respond within 4-8 weeks</li>
<li>Relapse rate ~30-50% during/after taper</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Steroid-sparing agents (for relapse or inability to taper):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Methotrexate</strong> 10-15 mg/week (most commonly used steroid-sparing agent)</li>
<li><strong>Azathioprine</strong> 2-3 mg/kg/day</li>
<li><strong>Mycophenolate mofetil</strong> 1000-2000 mg/day</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Refractory disease:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>TNF-alpha inhibitors:</strong> infliximab 3-5 mg/kg q4-8 weeks, or adalimumab 40 mg q2 weeks (limited by side effects; modest benefit)</li>
<li><strong>Antifibrotics:</strong> nintedanib (for progressive fibrotic phenotype; emerging evidence)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Supportive:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Pulmonary rehabilitation</li>
<li>Oxygen therapy for severe hypoxemia</li>
<li>Vaccination (influenza, pneumococcal)</li>
<li>Screen for hypercalcemia and cardiac involvement (ECG, echocardiogram, Holter)</li>
<li>Bone protection (calcium, vitamin D, bisphosphonate) if on chronic steroids; BUT monitor calcium closely given sarcoid-related hypercalcemia risk</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Lung transplantation</strong> for end-stage fibrotic disease</p>
