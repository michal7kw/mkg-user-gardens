<h1>MPO</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Myeloperoxidase (MPO) is a 150 kDa heme-containing peroxidase enzyme stored in the azurophilic granules of neutrophils and monocytes. Upon leukocyte activation, MPO is released into phagosomes (to kill engulfed pathogens) and into the extracellular space (where it can damage host tissues). MPO catalyzes the conversion of hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) and chloride ions to hypochlorous acid (HOCl, the active ingredient in household bleach), as well as the oxidation of tyrosine to tyrosyl radicals and nitric oxide to nitrotyrosine. These reactive oxidants damage lipids, proteins, and DNA -- driving the oxidative modification of LDL that initiates and propagates atherosclerosis.</p>
<p>As a biomarker, serum or plasma MPO has emerged as one of the most powerful independent predictors of cardiovascular events. The seminal Brennan et al. study (<em>NEJM</em> 2003, n=1090) showed that MPO levels independently predicted 30-day and 6-month risk of major adverse cardiac events in patients presenting with chest pain, even after adjusting for troponin and hsCRP. Subsequent studies have extended this to heart failure, atrial fibrillation, and subclinical atherosclerosis. MPO received FDA 510(k) clearance as a cardiovascular risk biomarker (PrognostiX / Cleveland HeartLab assay).</p>
<p>The mechanism linking MPO to atherosclerosis is multi-fold: (1) MPO-generated HOCl oxidizes LDL to atherogenic oxLDL; (2) MPO activates metalloproteinases that destabilize atherosclerotic plaque; (3) MPO consumes endothelial nitric oxide, impairing vasodilation; (4) MPO-modified HDL becomes dysfunctional and pro-inflammatory. Therapeutic strategies targeting MPO (e.g., AZD5904, PF-06282999) are in clinical development.</p>
<p><strong>Evidence Level: 4/5</strong> (multiple large cohort studies; FDA-cleared clinical biomarker; not in primary prevention guidelines yet).</p>

<p><strong>Caveats:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Reference range is assay-specific (Cleveland HeartLab ELISA, BECKMAN DxI, Roche research assays differ)</li>
<li>EDTA plasma preferred over serum (MPO is released ex vivo during clotting)</li>
<li>Hemolysis falsely lowers MPO (erythrocytes contain MPO inhibitors)</li>
<li>Acute infection or inflammation transiently elevates MPO</li>
<li>Age, BMI, smoking status influence baseline</li>
</ul>

<h2>Factors Affecting Levels</h2>
<p><strong>Increase MPO:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Acute coronary syndrome (ACS) -- MPO rises within hours of plaque rupture</li>
<li>Acute heart failure exacerbation</li>
<li>Active atherosclerosis (stable CAD, peripheral arterial disease)</li>
<li>Acute systemic infection / sepsis (transient massive elevation)</li>
<li>Chronic inflammatory diseases (RA, IBD, SLE)</li>
<li>Smoking (acute and chronic elevation)</li>
<li>Hyperglycemia / type 2 diabetes</li>
<li>Chronic kidney disease (impaired clearance)</li>
<li>Obesity (visceral fat drives chronic inflammation)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Decrease MPO:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Statin therapy (especially rosuvastatin, atorvastatin)</li>
<li>Lifestyle interventions: smoking cessation, exercise, weight loss</li>
<li>Antioxidant supplementation (NAC, vitamin C, vitamin E, omega-3 fatty acids)</li>
<li>Mediterranean diet</li>
<li>ACE inhibitors / ARBs (modest reduction via anti-inflammatory effects)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p><strong>Elevated MPO is associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Acute coronary syndrome:</strong> MPO independently predicts 30-day and 6-month MACE in patients presenting with chest pain (Brennan 2003, NEJM); adds prognostic value to troponin</li>
<li><strong>Stable coronary artery disease:</strong> elevated MPO predicts future CV events independently of traditional risk factors</li>
<li><strong>Heart failure:</strong> MPO levels correlate with NYHA class and predict hospitalization and mortality (Tang 2013)</li>
<li><strong>Atrial fibrillation:</strong> elevated MPO predicts incident AF in some cohorts</li>
<li><strong>Subclinical atherosclerosis:</strong> MPO correlates with carotid intima-media thickness and coronary artery calcium</li>
<li><strong>Endothelial dysfunction:</strong> MPO-mediated NO consumption impairs flow-mediated dilation</li>
<li><strong>Type 2 diabetes:</strong> MPO elevation reflects the oxidative stress of hyperglycemia; predicts CV events in diabetics</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Limitations:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Not in primary prevention guidelines</strong> (USPSTF, ACC/AHA cholesterol guideline uses hsCRP as the inflammation biomarker of choice, not MPO)</li>
<li><strong>Acute inflammation confounds:</strong> any infection or active autoimmune disease raises MPO acutely</li>
<li><strong>Limited reimbursement:</strong> not universally covered by insurance; Cleveland HeartLab is the primary commercial pathway</li>
<li><strong>Few intervention trials</strong> powered to show that lowering MPO reduces CV events (vs epidemiologic association only)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Monitoring use cases:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Risk stratification in intermediate-risk patients (after traditional risk factors assessed)</li>
<li>Prognosis after ACS</li>
<li>Heart failure prognostication</li>
<li>Tracking response to lifestyle and pharmacologic interventions (research)</li>
<li>Complementary to hsCRP and Lp-PLA2 in multimarker inflammation panels</li>
</ul>
<h2>Strategies to Improve</h2>
<p>MPO is modifiable through both lifestyle and pharmacologic interventions:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Smoking cessation</strong> -- among the most impactful MPO-lowering interventions</li>
<li><strong>Statin therapy</strong> -- atorvastatin, rosuvastatin reduce MPO by 20-40% via pleiotropic anti-inflammatory effects</li>
<li><strong>NAC (N-acetylcysteine) 600-1800 mg/day</strong> -- meta-analyses show ~25% reduction in MPO</li>
<li><strong>Vitamin C</strong> 500-1000 mg daily -- modest reduction (5-15%) in chronic users</li>
<li><strong>Vitamin E</strong> 200-400 IU daily -- modest reduction in oxidative stress populations</li>
<li><strong>Omega-3 fatty acids</strong> 1-4 g/day EPA+DHA -- 20-30% reduction in MPO in cardiovascular risk cohorts</li>
<li><strong>Mediterranean diet</strong> -- observational evidence for ~15-25% reduction</li>
<li><strong>Aerobic exercise</strong> 150 min/week moderate intensity -- 10-20% reduction via anti-inflammatory effects</li>
<li><strong>Weight loss</strong> in obese patients (5-10% body weight reduction) -- substantially lowers MPO</li>
<li><strong>Treat underlying chronic inflammation</strong> -- optimize RA, IBD, SLE management</li>
<li><strong>Investigational MPO inhibitors</strong> (AZD5904, PF-06282999) -- in clinical trials; not yet approved</li>
</ol>
