<h1>VLDL</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Very-low-density lipoprotein (VLDL) is the triglyceride-rich lipoprotein synthesized and secreted by the liver to transport endogenously produced triglycerides to peripheral tissues. Each VLDL particle carries one molecule of apolipoprotein B-100 along with apoC and apoE. In the circulation, lipoprotein lipase progressively hydrolyzes VLDL triglycerides, converting VLDL into smaller, cholesterol-enriched remnant particles (IDL) and ultimately LDL. Because VLDL is the precursor of LDL and the principal carrier of fasting triglycerides, it sits at the center of the link between triglyceride metabolism and atherogenic lipoproteins.</p>
<p>VLDL cholesterol is the largest contributor to <strong>remnant cholesterol</strong> — the cholesterol carried in triglyceride-rich lipoproteins and their remnants. Remnant particles can penetrate the arterial wall and are taken up by macrophages without requiring oxidative modification, making them directly atherogenic. Mendelian randomization and large cohort studies indicate that remnant/VLDL cholesterol is causally associated with ischemic cardiovascular disease, independent of LDL-C. VLDL cholesterol is most often <em>estimated</em> from a standard lipid panel (commonly triglycerides ÷ 5 when triglycerides are &lt;400 mg/dL), so it rises in parallel with triglycerides and is elevated in insulin resistance, metabolic syndrome, obesity, and high carbohydrate intake.</p>

<h2>Factors Affecting Levels</h2>
<p><strong>Increase VLDL:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Insulin resistance and metabolic syndrome (hepatic triglyceride overproduction)</li>
<li>High refined-carbohydrate, high-sugar, or high-alcohol intake</li>
<li>Obesity and physical inactivity</li>
<li>Type 2 diabetes, hypothyroidism, familial hypertriglyceridemia</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Decrease VLDL:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Weight loss and reduced refined-carbohydrate/alcohol intake</li>
<li>Aerobic exercise (enhances lipoprotein lipase activity)</li>
<li>Omega-3 fatty acids, niacin, fibrates (lower triglyceride production/clearance)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p><strong>Elevated VLDL is associated with:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease</strong> — as the main source of remnant cholesterol, VLDL contributes causal, LDL-independent atherogenic risk.</li>
<li><strong>Metabolic syndrome and insulin resistance</strong> — VLDL overproduction is a hallmark of the insulin-resistant state.</li>
<li><strong>Hypertriglyceridemia</strong> — VLDL tracks triglycerides; very high levels add pancreatitis risk.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Monitoring use cases:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Estimating remnant cholesterol burden alongside triglycerides</li>
<li>Tracking response to triglyceride-lowering lifestyle and pharmacologic therapy</li>
</ul>
<h2>Strategies to Improve</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Reduce refined carbohydrate, sugar, and alcohol</strong> — the most direct way to lower hepatic VLDL/triglyceride output.</li>
<li><strong>Weight loss and aerobic exercise</strong> — improve insulin sensitivity and triglyceride clearance.</li>
<li><strong>Omega-3 fatty acids</strong> — high-dose EPA/DHA substantially lower VLDL triglycerides.</li>
<li><strong>Niacin and fibrates</strong> — lower triglyceride-rich lipoproteins (clinician-supervised).</li>
<li><strong>Berberine</strong> — improves glycemic control and lowers triglycerides that drive VLDL production.</li>
<li><strong>Treat underlying contributors</strong> — glycemic control in diabetes, thyroid replacement in hypothyroidism.</li>
</ol>
