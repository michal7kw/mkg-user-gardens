<h1>TPO Antibodies</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Thyroid peroxidase antibodies (TPOAb) are autoantibodies directed against thyroid peroxidase, the membrane-bound enzyme that catalyzes the iodination of thyroglobulin and the coupling reactions essential for producing T4 and T3. Because the antibody targets the central enzymatic machinery of hormone synthesis, TPOAb both marks and contributes to immune-mediated destruction of thyroid follicular cells. It is the most sensitive and specific serological marker of autoimmune thyroid disease.</p>
<p>Elevated TPOAb is the hallmark of Hashimoto's thyroiditis, the leading cause of hypothyroidism in iodine-sufficient regions, and is present in the large majority of affected patients. It is also detected in Graves' disease and postpartum thyroiditis. Critically, a positive TPOAb result in a person with subclinical hypothyroidism is a strong, evidence-graded predictor of progression to overt hypothyroidism, which is why TPOAb is used to risk-stratify and to guide monitoring frequency. Low or undetectable levels are normal and indicate the absence of an autoimmune attack.</p>
<p>In pregnancy and the postpartum period TPOAb has added prognostic value: positive women have higher rates of gestational thyroid dysfunction and postpartum thyroiditis. The antibody titer does not need to be tracked continuously once autoimmunity is established; instead, TSH and thyroid hormones are followed to detect and treat evolving hypothyroidism. TPOAb is typically interpreted together with TgAb, TSH, and free thyroid hormones.</p>

<h2>Factors Affecting Levels</h2>
<p><strong>Increase TPOAb:</strong> Hashimoto's thyroiditis, Graves' disease, postpartum thyroiditis, other autoimmune disorders, genetic predisposition; titers may rise with active autoimmune disease.</p>
<p><strong>Decrease TPOAb:</strong> treatment that modulates the autoimmune process (and, in some studies, levothyroxine therapy and correction of selenium deficiency may reduce titers); low/undetectable levels are normal in non-autoimmune individuals.</p>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>TPOAb is the primary test for confirming autoimmune thyroid disease and is especially valuable for predicting which patients with subclinical hypothyroidism will progress to overt disease (a level 1a evidence association underpins the elevated-risk relationship to Hashimoto's thyroiditis). A positive result reframes mildly abnormal TSH as autoimmune in origin, supporting closer follow-up and earlier intervention. In women planning or undergoing pregnancy, TPOAb positivity flags increased risk of gestational and postpartum thyroid dysfunction.</p>
<h2>Strategies to Manage</h2>
<p>Management addresses the underlying autoimmunity and any resulting hypothyroidism rather than the antibody in isolation. Selenium supplementation has evidence for lowering TPOAb titers and supporting thyroid antioxidant defenses, and adequate vitamin D status is associated with autoimmune thyroid disease and may aid immune regulation; myo-inositol (often combined with selenium) has shown benefit in some studies for thyroid function and antibody reduction. Overt or progressing hypothyroidism is treated with levothyroxine under medical supervision. All supplementation should be individualized, guided by testing, and overseen by a clinician.</p>
