<h1>Tg Antibodies</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Thyroglobulin antibodies (TgAb) are autoantibodies that target thyroglobulin, the large glycoprotein the thyroid follicular cells use as a scaffold for synthesizing and storing thyroid hormones. Their detection in serum is a marker of autoimmune thyroid disease: the immune system has mounted a response against thyroid tissue. TgAb frequently coexist with thyroid peroxidase antibodies (TPOAb), and the two together define the autoimmune basis of the most common thyroid disorders.</p>
<p>Elevated TgAb is strongly associated with Hashimoto's thyroiditis (chronic autoimmune hypothyroidism) and is also seen in Graves' disease and postpartum thyroiditis. TgAb alone is generally considered a less sensitive and less specific marker of autoimmune thyroiditis than TPOAb, but it adds diagnostic value, particularly in patients who are TPOAb-negative yet clinically suspected of autoimmune disease. Low or undetectable TgAb is the normal finding and indicates absence of an autoimmune attack on thyroglobulin.</p>
<p>TgAb carries special importance in differentiated thyroid cancer management. After thyroidectomy, serum thyroglobulin is used as a tumor marker to detect residual or recurrent disease, but circulating TgAb can interfere with the thyroglobulin assay and produce falsely low or unreliable results. For this reason TgAb is measured alongside thyroglobulin in cancer surveillance, and a rising TgAb trend itself can serve as a surrogate marker of recurrent thyroid tissue.</p>

<h2>Factors Affecting Levels</h2>
<p><strong>Increase TgAb:</strong> Hashimoto's thyroiditis, Graves' disease, postpartum thyroiditis, other autoimmune conditions, presence of residual thyroid tissue in treated thyroid cancer; titers may fluctuate with disease activity.</p>
<p><strong>Decrease TgAb:</strong> immunomodulation and treatment of the underlying autoimmune process, successful ablation/removal of thyroid antigen over time; low/undetectable levels are normal in non-autoimmune individuals.</p>
<h2>Clinical Significance</h2>
<p>TgAb is used to confirm autoimmune thyroid disease, especially as an adjunct to TPOAb, and to interpret serum thyroglobulin in thyroid cancer follow-up. A positive TgAb in a patient with subclinical hypothyroidism signals autoimmune etiology and a higher likelihood of progression to overt hypothyroidism. In cancer surveillance, the assay must be paired with thyroglobulin because TgAb interference is a well-recognized source of false-negative tumor-marker results; the antibody titer trend is monitored as an independent signal.</p>
<h2>Strategies to Manage</h2>
<p>Because TgAb reflects underlying autoimmunity rather than a deficiency, management focuses on supporting immune balance and thyroid health rather than directly &quot;lowering&quot; the antibody. Correcting selenium deficiency has evidence for reducing thyroid antibody titers and supporting thyroid antioxidant defenses, and restoring adequate vitamin D status is associated with autoimmune thyroid disease and may support immune regulation. Antibody titers are followed over time alongside TSH and thyroid hormones, with replacement therapy added when hypothyroidism develops. All supplementation should be guided by testing and a clinician.</p>
