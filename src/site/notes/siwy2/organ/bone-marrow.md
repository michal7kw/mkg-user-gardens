<h1>Bone Marrow</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Soft, spongy tissue inside the central cavity of bones — the body's primary hematopoietic organ, responsible for producing red blood cells (via erythropoiesis), white blood cells (via myelopoiesis and lymphopoiesis), and platelets. Red marrow (in flat bones and proximal long-bone epiphyses) remains hematopoietically active throughout life; yellow marrow stores adipose tissue and reverts to red marrow under sustained anemic stress. Houses the niche where hematopoietic stem cells self-renew and differentiate under signals including erythropoietin, thrombopoietin, and G-CSF.</p>
<h2>Function</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Erythropoiesis</strong> — produces ~2 million RBCs per second under steady-state demand; iron, B12, folate, copper, and vitamin B6 are required cofactors</li>
<li><strong>Granulopoiesis</strong> — produces neutrophils, eosinophils, basophils</li>
<li><strong>Lymphopoiesis</strong> — produces B-cell precursors that mature in marrow; T-cell precursors migrate to the thymus</li>
<li><strong>Thrombopoiesis</strong> — produces megakaryocytes and platelets</li>
<li><strong>Stem cell niche</strong> — hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) reside in perivascular and endosteal microenvironments</li>
</ul>
<h2>Pathology</h2>
<p>Aplastic anemia (marrow failure), myelodysplastic syndromes, leukemias, multiple myeloma, marrow infiltration by metastatic disease, drug-induced suppression (chemotherapy, chloramphenicol).</p>
