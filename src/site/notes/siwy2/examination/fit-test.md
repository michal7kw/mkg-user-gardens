<h1>FIT Test</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>The fecal immunochemical test (FIT) is a stool-based screening examination that uses antibodies specific to human globin to detect occult (hidden) blood in the stool. Because colorectal cancers and many advanced adenomas bleed intermittently, FIT serves as a non-invasive marker for colorectal neoplasia. Unlike older guaiac-based fecal occult blood tests, FIT is specific for human lower-gastrointestinal hemoglobin, is not affected by diet or most medications, and usually requires only a single stool sample.</p>
<p>FIT is a cornerstone of organized population screening programs worldwide (for example, the NHS bowel-screening program and many national programs across Europe), where a positive FIT triggers referral for diagnostic colonoscopy. It is recommended annually for average-risk adults as one of the accepted first-line screening strategies, balancing low cost, convenience, and good performance for cancer detection.</p>
<h2>Performance and Use</h2>
<p>FIT has high specificity and good sensitivity for colorectal cancer, with lower sensitivity for non-bleeding advanced adenomas; quantitative FIT allows a numeric hemoglobin threshold to be tuned to program resources. Its effectiveness as a screening strategy depends critically on adherence — annual repeat testing and prompt colonoscopy follow-up of positive results. Multitarget stool DNA tests add molecular markers to FIT to raise sensitivity at the cost of specificity and price.</p>
<h2>Limitations</h2>
<p>A negative FIT does not exclude colorectal cancer, and the test detects bleeding rather than the lesion itself, so it cannot localize or characterize disease. Interval cancers can occur between negative tests. Any positive result requires diagnostic colonoscopy for confirmation and treatment.</p>
