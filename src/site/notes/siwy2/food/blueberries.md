<h1>Blueberries</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Berry of Vaccinium genus species (V. corymbosum highbush, V. angustifolium lowbush/wild). Among the highest antioxidant-content fruits per serving (ORAC ~9,000 µmol TE/100g), driven by anthocyanin content — particularly malvidin, delphinidin, cyanidin, and petunidin glycosides — the pigments responsible for the blue color. Wild lowbush blueberries are typically 2-3x higher in anthocyanins than commercial highbush.</p>
<p>Devore et al. (Ann Neurol 2012, n=16,010 from the Nurses' Health Study) showed greater intake of blueberries and strawberries was associated with slower rates of cognitive decline in older women. Stull et al. (Diabetes Care 2010) showed blueberry smoothies twice daily improved insulin sensitivity in obese, non-diabetic adults.</p>
<p>Anthocyanin bioavailability is poor (~5%) but their gut-microbiome-derived metabolites (phenolic acids, urolithins) may be the relevant active compounds.</p>
