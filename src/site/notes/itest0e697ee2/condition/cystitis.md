<h1>Cystitis</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Cystitis is inflammation of the bladder, most commonly caused by a <strong>bacterial urinary tract infection (UTI)</strong>. It is one of the most common bacterial infections in humans, affecting <strong>50% of women</strong> at least once in their lifetime. The shorter female urethra (4 cm vs 20 cm in men) allows uropathogens -- predominantly <strong>Escherichia coli</strong> (75-95% of cases) -- to ascend from the perineal flora into the bladder. Other pathogens include <em>Staphylococcus saprophyticus</em> (5-15%, young women), <em>Klebsiella</em>, <em>Proteus</em>, and <em>Enterococcus</em>.</p>
<p>The typical presentation is <strong>dysuria (burning on urination), urinary frequency and urgency, suprapubic pain, and cloudy urine</strong>. Most cases are <strong>uncomplicated</strong> (healthy non-pregnant women with normal anatomy) and can be treated empirically with a short course of antibiotics. <strong>Complicated cystitis</strong> (pregnancy, male sex, catheter, diabetes, stones, immunosuppression) requires longer therapy and workup for underlying causes. <strong>Recurrent UTIs</strong> (3+ per year) affect ~5% of women and warrant prophylactic strategies.</p>
<p><strong>Interstitial cystitis (IC)</strong>, also called bladder pain syndrome, is a distinct <strong>chronic, non-infectious</strong> condition characterized by bladder pain, urgency, and frequency in the absence of infection. It is covered separately in the vault. IC involves dysfunction of the bladder's protective glycosaminoglycan (GAG) layer and is not responsive to antibiotics.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Uncomplicated acute cystitis:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Nitrofurantoin</strong> 100 mg BID x 5 days -- first-line; low resistance; excellent bladder concentrations</li>
<li><strong>TMP-SMX</strong> (Bactrim DS) 1 tab BID x 3 days -- first-line where E. coli resistance &lt;20%</li>
<li><strong>Fosfomycin</strong> 3 g single dose -- convenient single-dose option</li>
<li><strong>Beta-lactams</strong> (amoxicillin-clavulanate) -- less effective, more side effects; reserve for pregnancy or resistance</li>
<li><strong>Fluoroquinolones</strong> (ciprofloxacin) -- reserve for complicated UTI or pyelonephritis (CDC stewardship)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Symptomatic relief:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Phenazopyridine (Pyridium)</strong> 100-200 mg TID x 2 days -- urinary analgesic (orange urine; stains contacts)</li>
<li><strong>Hydration</strong> -- 2-3 L/day to flush bacteria from the urinary tract</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Complicated cystitis:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>7-14 day antibiotic course based on culture and sensitivity</li>
<li>Treat underlying cause (stones, obstruction, catheter)</li>
<li>Urine culture mandatory before treatment</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Recurrent UTI prevention:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Daily prophylactic antibiotics</strong> -- nitrofurantoin 50-100 mg, TMP-SMX, or postcoital dosing</li>
<li><strong>Vaginal estrogen</strong> (topical) for postmenopausal women -- restores vaginal lactobacilli and pH</li>
<li><strong>Cranberry products</strong> (proanthocyanidins prevent E. coli Type 1 fimbriae adhesion)</li>
<li><strong>D-mannose</strong> 2 g daily -- prevents E. coli adhesion (emerging evidence)</li>
<li><strong>Behavioral</strong> -- postcoital voiding, adequate hydration, front-to-back wiping</li>
</ul>
