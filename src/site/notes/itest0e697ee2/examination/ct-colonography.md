<h1>CT Colonography</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>CT colonography (CTC), also called virtual colonoscopy, is a radiological examination that uses thin-section, low-dose multidetector computed tomography to image the cleansed, gas-distended colon and rectum. Specialized software reconstructs the data into two-dimensional cross-sections and three-dimensional endoluminal &quot;fly-through&quot; views, allowing a radiologist to search for polyps and cancers along the entire colon without inserting an endoscope.</p>
<p>CTC requires bowel preparation similar to optical colonoscopy and insufflation of the colon with carbon dioxide, but it does not require sedation and carries no risk of perforation from instrumentation. It is endorsed as one of the acceptable options for average-risk colorectal cancer screening (typically every 5 years), and is especially useful when optical colonoscopy is incomplete or contraindicated. A key limitation is that any significant lesion found must still be removed by subsequent optical colonoscopy.</p>
<h2>Indications and Performance</h2>
<p>CTC is used for screening average-risk adults, for evaluation after incomplete colonoscopy, and in patients who cannot tolerate or are at higher risk from endoscopy. It has high sensitivity for clinically significant polyps (&gt;=10 mm) and cancers, though sensitivity falls for small and flat lesions, and it does not detect non-structural mucosal disease as well as endoscopy. Extracolonic findings on the CT images can be incidental benefits or sources of additional workup.</p>
<h2>Preparation and Risks</h2>
<p>Standard preparation includes a clear-liquid diet, cathartic bowel cleansing, and often oral contrast &quot;tagging&quot; of residual stool and fluid. Risks are low: principally the small radiation dose of a low-dose CT and rare complications of colonic insufflation. Because it is non-therapeutic, a positive CTC mandates referral for optical colonoscopy and polypectomy.</p>
