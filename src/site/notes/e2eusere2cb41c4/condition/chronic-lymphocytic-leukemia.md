<h1>Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) is the <strong>most common leukemia in adults</strong> in Western countries, with an annual incidence of ~5 per 100,000. It is characterized by the accumulation of <strong>monoclonal, mature-appearing but functionally incompetent B-lymphocytes</strong> in the peripheral blood (&gt;=5,000/uL), bone marrow, and lymphoid tissues. The median age at diagnosis is ~70 years, and it is rare before age 40.</p>
<p>CLL is part of a spectrum with <strong>small lymphocytic lymphoma (SLL)</strong> -- they share the same immunophenotype (CD5+, CD19+, CD23+) and molecular features but differ in presentation: CLL is primarily a blood and marrow disease, while SLL is primarily nodal. The WHO 2022 classification considers them a single entity: &quot;CLL/SLL.&quot;</p>
<p><strong>Clinical course is highly variable:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Some patients live for decades without symptoms or treatment (&quot;smoldering&quot; or indolent disease)</li>
<li>Others progress rapidly with symptomatic disease requiring treatment</li>
<li>Median overall survival is now &gt;10 years with modern targeted therapies</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Staging:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Rai staging:</strong> 0 (lymphocytosis only; good prognosis), I (+lymphadenopathy), II (+organomegaly), III (+anemia Hb&lt;11), IV (+thrombocytopenia Plt&lt;100K)</li>
<li><strong>Binet staging:</strong> A (&lt;3 lymphoid areas involved; good), B (&gt;=3 areas), C (+anemia/thrombocytopenia; poor)</li>
</ul>
<p>The treatment paradigm has been <strong>revolutionized</strong> by novel targeted therapies:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>BTK inhibitors</strong> (ibrutinib, acalabrutinib, zanubrutinib) -- oral, continuous therapy, highly effective</li>
<li><strong>BCL-2 inhibitor</strong> (venetoclax) -- oral, time-limited therapy (12-15 months with obinutuzumab)</li>
<li>These have largely replaced traditional chemoimmunotherapy (FCR, BR) in most patients</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Prognostic markers:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>IGHV mutated</strong> (better prognosis; longer treatment-free intervals)</li>
<li><strong>TP53 deletion/mutation</strong> (poorer prognosis; chemo-resistant; but responsive to venetoclax/ibrutinib)</li>
<li><strong>FISH:</strong> del(17p)/TP53 (worst), del(11q)/ATM (intermediate), trisomy 12 (intermediate), normal (intermediate), del(13q) (best prognosis)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p><strong>Treatment initiation (not all CLL patients need treatment):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>iwCLL treatment criteria:</strong>
<ul>
<li>Progressive marrow failure (worsening anemia or thrombocytopenia)</li>
<li>Massive or progressive lymphadenopathy (&gt;10 cm or growing)</li>
<li>Massive or progressive splenomegaly</li>
<li>Progressive lymphocytosis (&gt;50% increase in 2 months or lymphocyte doubling time &lt;6 months)</li>
<li>Autoimmune cytopenias (AIHA, ITP) poorly responsive to steroids</li>
<li>Symptomatic extranodal involvement</li>
<li>Disease-related B symptoms or fatigue</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>First-line therapy (for fit patients without del(17p)/TP53):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Venetoclax + obinutuzumab</strong> (time-limited 12 months; high rate of undetectable minimal residual disease)</li>
<li><strong>Ibrutinib</strong> (continuous therapy; or acalabrutinib as better-tolerated alternative)</li>
<li><strong>FCR</strong> (fit young patients with IGHV-mutated CLL; high cure rate)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>For del(17p)/TP53-mutated:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>BTK inhibitor</strong> (ibrutinib, acalabrutinib) -- continuous therapy</li>
<li><strong>Venetoclax + rituximab</strong> -- time-limited</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Supportive care:</strong> IVIG for hypogammaglobulinemia with recurrent infections; vaccination; tumor lysis syndrome prophylaxis (allopurinol, rasburicase, hydration) for high tumor burden</p>
