<h1>Schizophrenia</h1>
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Schizophrenia is a severe, chronic psychiatric disorder affecting roughly 1% of the population, defined by a triad of symptom domains: <strong>positive</strong> (hallucinations, delusions, disorganization), <strong>negative</strong> (avolition, anhedonia, blunted affect), and <strong>cognitive</strong> (deficits in working memory, attention, and executive function). It is among the most heritable psychiatric conditions, with a highly polygenic architecture interacting with environmental risk.</p>
<p>Its neurochemistry is classically framed by two complementary hypotheses. The <strong>dopamine hypothesis</strong> (version III) holds that presynaptic striatal dopamine dysregulation — mesolimbic hyperdopaminergia producing positive symptoms, mesocortical hypodopaminergia producing negative/cognitive symptoms — is the &quot;final common pathway.&quot; The <strong>glutamate hypothesis</strong> posits NMDA-receptor hypofunction, consistent with the psychotomimetic effects of NMDA antagonists. These converge with the observation that the kynurenine metabolite <a href="/garden/itest8f0d6ee1/metabolite/kynurenic-acid/" class="internal-link">Kynurenic Acid</a> — an endogenous NMDA antagonist — is elevated in schizophrenia, linking <a href="/garden/itest8f0d6ee1/neurotransmitter/glutamate/" class="internal-link">Glutamate</a> hypofunction to tryptophan metabolism.</p>
<h2>Clinical Presentation</h2>
<h3>Symptoms</h3>
<p>Positive symptoms (hallucinations, delusions, thought disorder) typically emerge in late adolescence/early adulthood; negative and cognitive symptoms often precede them and most strongly determine long-term functional outcome.</p>
<h2>Diagnostic Information</h2>
<p>Clinical diagnosis (DSM-5/ICD-11) based on characteristic symptoms persisting ≥6 months with functional decline; no validated biomarker, though plasma/CSF <a href="/garden/itest8f0d6ee1/metabolite/homovanillic-acid/" class="internal-link">Homovanillic Acid</a> has been studied as a dopaminergic activity marker.</p>
<h2>Risk Factors</h2>
<p>High polygenic heritability interacts with obstetric complications, prenatal infection, adolescent cannabis use, urban upbringing, and advanced paternal age.</p>
<h2>Management</h2>
<p>Antipsychotics (all D2-receptor antagonists) treat positive symptoms; clozapine is uniquely effective in treatment-resistant disease. Negative and cognitive symptoms remain poorly addressed, motivating glutamatergic and kynurenine-targeted research.</p>
